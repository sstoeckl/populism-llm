WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows minimal populism: it occasionally invokes citizens' interests against bureaucracy and political interference in courts, but frames politics in institutional, programmatic terms rather than as a moral struggle between a virtuous people and a corrupt elite.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is a detailed policy programme with minimal populist framing; there is a brief anti-corruption reference and a service-to-the-people framing for e-government, but no sustained elite-bashing, people-as-virtuous-bloc rhetoric, or Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto expresses moderate anti-elitism and strong people-centrism by emphasizing individual responsibility and opposition to political interference, with some moralized struggle evident in immigration control policies.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, indicating absence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto exhibits very low overall populism, with only a minor anti-technocratic stance mentioned under state governance.

chunk 2 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The dominant ideological flavour is right-leaning populism: strong emphasis on national identity, conservative immigration/refugee policy, opposition to multiculturalism and refugee quotas, combined with free-market economics rather than left-wing redistribution.

chunk 2 · The manifesto leans mildly right-populist through its emphasis on border control, anti-illegal-immigration measures, and national identity/security, but the overall tone is that of a mainstream conservative policy programme rather than a strongly populist one.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto leans right populist with strong emphasis on Estonian national identity and conservative social policies, moderate centrist populist appeals to individual effort, and low left populist stance opposing expansive welfare state.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the manifesto text; it focuses on policy and governance without populist framing.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, focusing heavily on conservative migration policies, national preservation, and opposition to refugee quotas.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideology in the text, as it focuses on mainstream policy proposals.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal economically (free markets, low taxes, privatisation, capital market development, competition in banking) and politically (parliamentary democracy, rule of law, independent courts, free speech), but scores lower on social liberalism due to opposition to same-sex partnership law and gender/ethnic quotas.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently supports rule of law, independent courts, deregulation, market competition in energy and finance, and basic rights protection, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal (classical/ordo-liberal) orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberalism with clear endorsement of free markets, limited state intervention, rule of law, political freedoms, and market-based financial reforms, while showing moderate conservatism on social issues.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports rule of law, individual rights, economic deregulation, and financial market development, as evidenced by quotes on independent judiciary, data protection, reducing bureaucracy, and capital market activities.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The party strongly supports classical economic liberalism and parliamentary democracy, but scores low on social liberalism due to its opposition to the Cohabitation Act and support for defining marriage strictly between a man and a woman.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal principles, including the rule of law, independent courts, fundamental rights, free enterprise, and market-based financial investments.
