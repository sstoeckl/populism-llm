WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto moderately invokes populist themes by framing the ruling elite as corrupt and self-serving ("vladajuća politička elita ima snažan motiv da preraspodjeljuje resurse"), describing privatization as plunder, and appealing to citizens as the rightful beneficiaries of state resources, but stops short of a fully Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, preferring institutional reform language.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently frames the ruling elite as corrupt and self-serving (hidden alliances of political and economic elites, predatory privatization, party-state capture), invokes the interests of all citizens as the moral standard, and uses moralized language like "economic genocide" and "plunder," indicating moderate-to-strong populist framing.

chunk 3 · The text contains moderate populist elements: it criticises the ruling elite for enriching themselves at citizens' expense ("bogaćenje vladajuće garniture"), invokes the people's will as supreme ("Volja građana na prvom mjestu"), and frames the ruling party as labelling opponents "državni neprijatelj," but the overall tone is predominantly policy-programmatic rather than strongly populist.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · Manifesto expresses moderate anti-elitism by criticizing elite resource redistribution, emphasizes unity of the people for a better Montenegro, and shows some moralized struggle against corrupt governance, reflecting a moderate populist stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the party-state elite system and corruption, shows mixed anti-elitism; it emphasizes unity of 'the people' beyond identity divisions, indicating people-centrism; and it highlights moralized struggles against privileged groups exploiting resources, showing some manichean framing.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly discusses elite opposition and polarization, framing dissenters as enemies, while emphasizing the people's will and avoiding divisive rhetoric, indicating moderate populist traits.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames the ruling political elite as corrupt, self-serving, and using artificial divisions to maintain power and plunder resources.

chunk 2 · The text strongly criticizes the corrupt ruling elite for colluding with privileged individuals to exploit natural resources and carry out predatory privatizations at the expense of the citizens.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits strong anti-elitism by accusing the ruling elite of corruption, self-enrichment, and dividing the people to maintain power, while emphasizing the supremacy of the will of the people.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is primarily centrist-left: the party explicitly rejects "neoliberal economic policy," calls for social equality and redistribution, and advocates state reaffirmation in the economy, while also supporting EU integration and competition, making it a mixed but predominantly centre-left populist profile.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology leans left-of-centre: the manifesto strongly criticises predatory privatisation, corporate/political elite enrichment at workers' expense, and demands redistribution and workers' rights, while explicitly rejecting national/ethnic divisions, making right-wing populist ideology weak.

chunk 3 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist with emphasis on social protection, reducing inequality, and state intervention in housing and healthcare, while also invoking sovereignty and national interest; it lacks strong right-populist nativist or anti-immigration rhetoric, and its centrist appeals to "citizens above divisions" are secondary.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text supports left-leaning social justice and environmental protection, right-leaning national sovereignty, and centrist institutional reforms, indicating a balanced populist ideology with moderate scores across left, right, and centrist dimensions.

chunk 2 · The text combines centrist appeals to unity beyond identity divisions with some left-leaning critique of privileged exploiters and right-leaning emphasis on national unity and patriotism, resulting in a moderate overall populist ideology score.

chunk 3 · The text shows mild left-wing populist critique of elite enrichment and right-wing polarization, but also centrist appeals to unity and avoidance of divisive politics, reflecting a mixed populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology leans left-centrist, focusing on social justice, anti-corruption, and returning plundered resources to the citizens without right-wing nationalist elements.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology leans left, focusing on the protection of public wealth against corporate-style looting and advocating for social equality and workers' rights.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology leans left-centrist, focusing on anti-corporate/anti-elite enrichment and social justice, without right-wing nationalist or anti-immigrant rhetoric.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is moderately pro-liberal: it strongly endorses rule of law, democratic institutions, minority rights, gender equality, and EU integration (high lib_political and lib_social scores), while taking a mixed stance on economic liberalism by rejecting "neoliberalism" yet supporting competition and foreign investment, and noting underdeveloped capital markets without advocating financial liberalization.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports rule of law, gender equality, EU integration, and competition, indicating moderate pro-liberal stances across political, social, and economic dimensions, though it also advocates state intervention in markets (subsidies, state-set credit terms), producing mixed signals on economic liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is moderately liberal: it strongly endorses media freedom and democratic institutions, supports minority education rights and social inclusion, advocates market entry facilitation for investors, and mentions bond issuance and private financing alongside state intervention in housing, yielding a mixed but overall moderately pro-liberal profile.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses political liberalism with emphasis on human rights, rule of law, social liberalism with anti-discrimination and gender equality, and economic liberalism promoting sustainable development, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market policies.

chunk 2 · The manifesto affirms constitutional political rights, promotes social equality and anti-violence policies, supports economic development and regional investment, but lacks explicit discussion of financial market policies, leading to a moderate pro-liberalism overall score.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports constitutional democracy, civil rights, market mechanisms with state intervention, and transparency in public finance, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly advocates for classical liberal principles including the rule of law, independent judiciary, human rights, and market competition, while criticizing neoliberal excesses.

chunk 2 · The text supports classical liberal principles by advocating for the rule of law, gender equality, and the development of small and medium enterprises, though it lacks specific financial market proposals.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism, emphasizing media freedom, religious tolerance, and minority rights, while advocating a mixed approach to the economy with state intervention in housing and social welfare.
