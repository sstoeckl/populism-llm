WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The text consistently invokes "el Pueblo" as a virtuous collective betrayed by financial speculators and usurers ("Los únicos privilegiados son los usureros"), frames politics as a moral struggle between the productive "Patria Productiva" and the parasitic "Patria Financiera," and calls for a national popular mobilization ("Revolución Productiva") against elites who have impoverished Argentina.

chunk 2 · The text invokes "el Pueblo" as a virtuous collective repeatedly, frames financial elites ("patria financiera") and international creditors as corrupt exploiters of ordinary Argentines, and draws a stark moral contrast between productive workers and speculative rentiers, though it stops short of fully conspiratorial framing.

chunk 3 · The text consistently invokes "el Pueblo" as a virtuous collective, frames elites (financial speculators, central bureaucrats, powerful economic groups) as corrupt adversaries, and draws a Manichean contrast between productive Argentina and parasitic/financial Argentina, yielding moderate-to-high populism scores across all three sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text explicitly criticizes a corrupt political class as a separate elite, invokes the people as a unified entity, and frames the national crisis in moral terms of division and solidarity, supporting a moderate populist score.

chunk 2 · The text explicitly critiques financial elites and promotes a productive economy for the people, invoking the people as a central interest and framing a moral struggle around debt and economic sovereignty, evidencing moderate populist themes.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes financial elites and parasitic speculation while emphasizing the power and virtue of the people united in work, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral struggle.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text frames a moralized struggle between a corrupt, speculative elite ('la patria financiera') and the virtuous, working 'Pueblo' of Argentina.

chunk 2 · The text strongly frames a moral struggle between the virtuous 'pueblo' and corrupt financial elites ('patria financiera').

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by contrasting the virtuous working people and local communities against centralist, corrupt metropolitan bureaucrats and financial speculators.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is predominantly left-populist in the Peronist tradition: it champions wage increases ("salariazo"), redistribution of income, reindustrialisation, and social justice against financial elites and IMF-imposed austerity, while also invoking national-Christian values and sovereignty, giving a secondary right-nationalist tinge.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is predominantly left-populist in the Peronist tradition, emphasizing redistribution, social justice, workers' wages, anti-financial-elite rhetoric, and state-led development, while also invoking national sovereignty and economic independence with mild right-populist nationalist overtones.

chunk 3 · The text is predominantly left-populist (Peronist/Justicialista), emphasizing redistribution, workers' rights, anti-financial-speculation, and social justice, with minor communitarian-conservative elements (family, Church) but no significant right-populist nativist or anti-immigration content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto combines left-wing populist themes of anti-speculation and social justice with right-leaning nationalist references to Latin American unity, and centrist appeals to inclusive political participation, reflecting a mixed populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The manifesto combines left-leaning themes of social justice and income redistribution with pragmatic openness to foreign capital under conditions, reflecting a centrist populist ideology with some right-leaning economic nationalism.

chunk 3 · The text promotes a centrist populist ideology focused on federalism and participation, with some left-leaning anti-financial elite rhetoric, but lacks right-wing nationalist or anti-immigration themes.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on redistribution, social justice, and the subordination of capital to labor, while rejecting financial speculation.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing, focusing on social justice, labor rights, and anti-imperialist economic sovereignty.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, emphasizing social justice, labor rights, and the redistribution of wealth against financial elites.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto endorses democratic participation and rule of law ("Democracia Social"), mentions reducing discrimination, and calls for a competitive private sector with some deregulation, but strongly opposes financial-market liberalism, condemning speculative capital, usury, and IMF-driven monetarism, yielding a mixed-to-low overall liberal score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal signals: it supports democracy, decentralization, and some deregulation of private activity, but strongly opposes financial market liberalization (condemning the 1977 reform's interest-rate liberalization and capital mobility as destructive), advocates state-directed credit, and rejects "crude liberal capitalism," yielding a below-midpoint overall liberalism score.

chunk 3 · The text shows mixed liberal signals: it endorses democratic participation, decentralization, and legal security (pro-liberal), supports women's equality (moderately pro-liberal socially), opens doors to productive foreign capital (moderately pro-liberal economically), but explicitly rejects financial speculation and favors state-directed credit and social functions of enterprise (anti-liberal on financial markets), yielding a middling overall liberalism score.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto advocates for democratic openness, social progress, economic development through rational resource use, and criticizes financial speculation, indicating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The text supports democratic institutions, social justice, market-friendly economic policies, and financial reforms that enable productive credit, showing a moderately pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports democratic participation, social equality, market openness, and criticizes financial speculation, indicating a moderately pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial market dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports democratic institutions and some private deregulation, but strongly rejects free financial markets and classical economic liberalism in favor of state-led social pacts and wage increases.

chunk 2 · The text presents a mixed view of liberalism, supporting private risk capital and market integration but strongly advocating for state-guided credit and social protections.

chunk 3 · The text supports democratic participation, decentralization, and private initiative, but rejects financial speculation and advocates for state-directed credit and social functions of enterprise.
