WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The text exhibits strong populist features: the ruling elite ("system Tuska," post-communist nomenklatura) is portrayed as corrupt and self-serving, the Polish people are invoked as a virtuous unified community, and the political landscape is framed in Manichean terms as a struggle between honest patriots and a corrupt, externally-subservient establishment.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits strong populism through sustained anti-elite framing of the "Tusk system" as a corrupt post-communist establishment serving narrow interests against the Polish people, a Manichean good-vs-evil narrative pitting PiS (defenders of democracy and the nation) against PO (destroyers of the state), and repeated invocations of ordinary citizens and the nation as the rightful sovereign being betrayed.

chunk 3 · The text repeatedly frames the ruling PO-PSL coalition under Donald Tusk as corrupt, incompetent, and self-serving elites who neglect ordinary citizens, while invoking the people as victims deserving state protection, and portraying the political situation in near-conspiratorial, good-vs-evil terms (e.g., "elements of a police state").

chunk 4 · The text exhibits moderate populism: it frames the ruling PO-PSL coalition as corrupt elites who "neutralize" oversight institutions and "capture the state," while invoking citizens' access to justice and democratic participation, but stops short of a fully Manichean people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 5 · The text exhibits moderate populism: the ruling PO-PSL coalition is repeatedly framed as corrupt and negligent (e.g., "zdrada interesów polskiej wsi"), ordinary Poles and farmers are invoked as a virtuous collective deserving equal rights, and there is a clear moral contrast between the betraying elite and the suffering people, though the rhetoric remains policy-focused rather than fully Manichean.

chunk 6 · The text repeatedly blames the Tusk government for failures in healthcare, housing, and pensions ("Rząd Tuska doprowadziły nie tylko do jej finansowej zapaści"), frames ordinary Poles as deceived victims ("Polacy w 1998 roku zostali oszukani"), and positions the party as champion of the people against negligent elites, yielding moderate populism scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 7 · The text contains mild populist elements — criticising media bias favouring the government and calling for civic creativity of "the Polish people" — but the dominant register is technocratic-programmatic (education, science, culture, foreign policy reform), with no strong elite-vs-people moral dichotomy.

chunk 8 · The text shows moderate populist tendencies: it invokes "the people" as the true democratic subject against EU bureaucratic elites and frames EU centralization as a threat to popular sovereignty, but the rhetoric is primarily technocratic-nationalist rather than strongly populist.

Sonnet

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is predominantly right-wing, centered on national identity, cultural tradition, historical memory, and sovereignty, with explicit hostility toward "gender ideology," left-wing cultural politics, and foreign (German/Russian) influence, while anti-corporate or redistributive left-populist themes are largely absent.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is primarily right-leaning, emphasizing national interest, strong state institutions, centralized security, and skepticism of EU integration arrangements, with some statist/social elements but no significant left-wing redistributive or anti-corporate framing.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is mixed: left-leaning elements include social solidarity in taxation and support for SMEs and workers, while right-leaning elements include national currency retention, economic sovereignty, and state ownership of strategic sectors, without a dominant single ideological direction.

chunk 5 · The populist ideology is predominantly right-leaning, emphasizing national ownership of land ("polska ziemia dla polskich rolników"), state control of strategic enterprises, opposition to foreign land purchases, and national identity, with only minor redistributive (left) elements such as family benefits and opposition to privatization.

chunk 6 · The dominant populist flavor is left-leaning, emphasizing social justice, wage growth, redistribution, and state-provided services ("kwestią sprawiedliwości społecznej"), with secondary right-leaning national-identity elements (sovereignty, patriotic education), placing the overall ideology as center-left populist.

chunk 7 · The populist flavour is predominantly right-leaning, centred on national identity, patriotic education, Polish historical memory, and sovereignty vis-à-vis the EU, with only marginal anti-corporate or redistributive left-populist elements.

chunk 8 · The populist ideology is predominantly right-leaning, emphasizing national identity, cultural sovereignty, protection of Polish land and currency, and resistance to supranational cultural experiments, with only weak redistributive or left-populist elements.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it endorses democracy, rule of law, and rejects statism (pro-liberal), but opposes "gender ideology," frames post-1989 liberalism as social Darwinism, advocates active state economic policy, and is hostile to financial-market actors (banks, developers) seen as exploiting the system.

chunk 2 · The text presents a mixed liberal profile: it rhetorically endorses free markets and democratic institutions but frames them through a nationalist-statist lens (strong state as friend of the market), explicitly rejects gender/social liberalism, and focuses primarily on restoring state capacity and national sovereignty rather than advancing classical liberal freedoms.

chunk 3 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it invokes constitutional rights and rule of law but simultaneously advocates centralizing prosecutorial power under the executive, expanding ministerial control over courts, and rolling back privatization of public services, reflecting a statist rather than classically liberal orientation.

chunk 4 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it supports deregulation, rule of law, and institutional independence (pro-liberal), but also advocates state interventionism, a bank "domestication" policy, a financial-institutions tax, and state-directed investment funds (anti-liberal), yielding a middling overall score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto scores low-to-moderate on liberalism: it explicitly opposes privatization of state enterprises (Poczta Polska, energy sector), advocates strong state ownership and control in energy and transport, restricts land markets to Polish farmers only, and promotes heavy state intervention in housing and agriculture, though it mentions democratic norms and some market-enabling tax incentives for R&D investment.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it invokes individual choice in pension systems and supports some market mechanisms, but explicitly rejects market logic in healthcare ("Odrzucamy twierdzenie, że mechanizmy rynkowe"), advocates extensive state intervention in labor markets, and proposes renationalization of health financing, yielding mid-range scores across all sub-dimensions.

chunk 7 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal signals: it champions freedom of speech, media pluralism, academic freedom, and checks on government, but simultaneously advocates strong state control over education, public media, and cultural policy, and restricts market mechanisms in schooling and textbook publishing.

chunk 8 · The text endorses the EU single market and free movement of goods and capital as core achievements, but simultaneously resists supranational cultural liberalism, defends national currency sovereignty, and opposes deeper EU integration, producing a mixed liberal profile.
