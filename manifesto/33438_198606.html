WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: it frames the Socialist government as irresponsible and power-concentrating, appeals to 'ordinary citizens' and 'Spaniards in the street,' and uses the slogan 'NOSOTROS PODEMOS HACERLO MEJOR,' but the overall tone is programmatic and policy-oriented rather than deeply Manichean or people-centric.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains mild populist elements: it criticises the Socialist government for expanding bureaucracy and raising taxes without improving services, and frames Coalición Popular as the champion of citizens against an inefficient state, but the rhetoric is primarily technocratic and policy-focused rather than deeply populist.

chunk 3 · The text shows minimal populism: there is a brief criticism of the incumbent socialist government's patronage politics and occasional references to "the Spanish people," but the dominant register is programmatic and institutional, lacking sustained people-vs-elite framing or Manichean moral conflict.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto criticizes concentration of power and arrogance of elites, highlights ordinary citizens as distinct from elites, and frames political power struggles as threats to constitutional balance, indicating moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized conflict.

chunk 2 · The text criticizes the socialist government and bureaucratic elites, emphasizing a rational administration serving all citizens and opposing bureaucratic oppression, reflecting low populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean views.

chunk 3 · No evidence of populist rhetoric or themes was found in the text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits mild populism by defending the 'ordinary Spaniards' against the perceived abuses and threats of the governing socialist power.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits low-to-moderate populism, focusing primarily on criticizing the socialist government's administrative inefficiency and clientelism rather than framing a deep moral struggle between a pure people and a corrupt elite.

chunk 3 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist flavor is predominantly right-leaning, combining law-and-order rhetoric (tougher anti-terrorism and crime penalties), free-market liberalization, privatization, and anti-Socialist government criticism, with only minimal left-populist redistribution elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto's populist flavour leans centre-right: it defends national unity, cultural identity, private enterprise over state intervention, and criticises the Socialist government's bureaucratic expansion, without strong nativist or anti-immigration rhetoric.

chunk 3 · The populist flavour that does exist leans mildly right-wing, centred on national interest, NATO integration, and defence of Spain's sovereignty, with no significant anti-corporate or redistributive left-populist content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text shows moderate left populist critique of socialist reforms, right populist national security measures, and centrist appeals to better governance, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto combines calls for state unity and cultural identity with redistribution and social needs, alongside appeals for efficient administration without political exploitation, indicating a centrist populist ideology with moderate left and right elements.

chunk 3 · The text lacks any explicit populist ideological content, left, right, or centrist.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on the defense of ordinary citizens against state overreach without strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are framed within a center-right, national-conservative ideology that emphasizes state unity, cultural identity, and market-friendly administrative reforms.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideology, as the text focuses on mainstream conservative-liberal policy proposals.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions judicial independence and democratic rule of law, opposes gender discrimination, advocates extensive economic deregulation and privatization, and explicitly calls for free capital flows, deeper capital markets, and reduced state intervention in finance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it advocates administrative streamlining, democratic rule of law, gender equality, free markets with privatisation and deregulation, and market-based financial instruments including mortgage market development and private-sector competition.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is clearly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic institutions and rule of law, social integration of minorities, economic liberalisation (labour market, deficit reduction, deregulation), and free movement of capital and goods within a single European market.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports judicial independence, non-discrimination in education, economic liberalization including market competition and privatization, and financial market development, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through calls for objective, neutral administration and rule of law; social liberalism via gender equality and anti-discrimination; economic liberalism by promoting private initiative and reducing administrative intervention; and financial market liberalism by encouraging industry and regulatory control that enables market function.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports free trade, market integration, and prudent economic policies within the European Community, indicating a pro-liberal economic and political stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly advocates for classical liberal principles, including judicial independence, free-market competition, deregulation, and the free circulation of capital.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including deregulation, privatization, constitutionalism, non-discrimination, and the promotion of market-based financial mechanisms like mortgage secondary markets.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including democratic institutions, free trade, economic liberalization, and the free movement of capital.
