WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto criticises political elites and interest groups for corrupting state banks and economic policy, frames ordinary citizens and entrepreneurs as virtuous, and uses some good-vs-evil language (e.g., "tajkunsko plenjenje"), but the tone is primarily technocratic-analytical rather than strongly populist.

chunk 2 · The text criticises political-economic elites and interest groups for capturing the state and distorting markets, but frames this primarily as a technocratic/liberal argument for privatisation and rule of law rather than a moralistic people-vs-elite populist appeal; the Manichean and people-centric elements are present but secondary.

chunk 3 · The text criticises a corrupt political-economic elite ("politično obarvane interesne skupine") that extracts rents via state-owned enterprises and opaque para-state funds, framing this as a systemic betrayal of the broader economy, while invoking individual freedom and responsibility rather than a unified "people" as the counter-force.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly distances itself from populism ("brez populizma"), warns against "populistične vnaprejšnje obsodbe," and frames its appeals in technocratic, rule-of-law terms. Occasional references to corruption and clientelism are framed institutionally rather than as a people-vs-elite moral struggle, yielding very low populism scores across all sub-dimensions.

chunk 5 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto (NSi, Slovenian Christian Democrats) focused on infrastructure, digital economy, culture, defence, and EU affairs; it contains only minor, incidental criticism of politically motivated decisions and no sustained populist anti-elite or people-vs-elite framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · Manifesto emphasizes belief in the people as dynamic and virtuous, criticizes political interference in business, and rejects corporatist economic policies, reflecting moderate populist themes.

chunk 2 · Manifesto critiques political interference and elite control over the economy, indicating moderate anti-elitism but lacks strong people-centrism or Manichean framing.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly critiques political and economic elites through discussion of economic nationalism, political favoritism, and the concentration of power in parastatal companies, reflecting moderate anti-elitism; it emphasizes collective responsibility and political control over state-owned enterprises, indicating people-centrism; and it frames economic policy debates in moralized terms such as economic xenophobia and political rent-seeking, showing some manichean elements.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes populist tactics and clientelism, emphasizes the centrality of the people and family, and frames societal issues in moral terms, indicating a low to moderate populist stance.

chunk 5 · Manifesto shows mild anti-elitism criticizing political favoritism, emphasizes people-centric national pride in the army, and recalls historical moral struggles, indicating moderate populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits low-level populism, focusing primarily on criticizing political interventions in the economy and state-owned enterprises rather than framing a deep moral struggle between a corrupt elite and a pure people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate anti-elitism by criticizing state capture by interest groups and political elites controlling state-owned enterprises, but lacks strong moralizing or people-centrist rhetoric.

chunk 3 · The text frames state-connected interest groups and political elites as corrupt rent-seekers exploiting the economy at the expense of virtuous, responsible individuals.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits mild anti-elitism by criticizing corruption and clientelism in public systems, but explicitly rejects populism in its judicial section.

chunk 5 · Populism is extremely weak, with only minor anti-elitist references to local power-holders delaying infrastructure projects.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The party's populist appeals are primarily centrist-technocratic (social market economy, anti-corporatist), with mild right-leaning national-cultural references and minimal left-wing redistribution rhetoric; no strong ideological populist anchor dominates.

chunk 2 · The text explicitly rejects socialism, Keynesianism, and state capitalism from a Christian-democratic free-market perspective aligned with the right, but its anti-elite critique targets crony-capitalist interest groups rather than ethnic or immigration-based enemies, placing it at a low-to-moderate right-populist ideological score.

chunk 3 · The populist critique is primarily directed at a rent-seeking political-corporate elite rather than at ethnic/national outsiders or at capitalist class structures; the party's pro-market, pro-privatisation, anti-redistribution programme places it closer to a centrist-liberal anti-establishment stance with only marginal right-populist (economic nationalism critique) and left-populist (anti-corporate elite) overtones.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is a Christian-democratic centre-right programme emphasising free markets, deregulation, rule of law, and family values, with modest social solidarity elements. It explicitly rejects populism and shows no strong left or right populist ideological flavour; overall populist ideology is minimal.

chunk 5 · The manifesto reflects a centre-right Christian-democratic orientation (NSi), emphasising national identity, cultural heritage, social market economy, and EU integration, with no strong populist ideological flavour on either the left or right extreme.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text supports social safety nets and national cultural identity while promoting entrepreneurial freedom, indicating a centrist populist ideology with some left and right elements.

chunk 2 · The text highlights political control over property rights and economic decisions without clear left or right populist programmatic stances, indicating a centrist populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The text shows a moderate left-leaning populist ideology through criticism of income redistribution and state intervention, a mild right-leaning nationalism in economic sovereignty concerns, and a centrist populist stance in its emphasis on collective responsibility and political control, resulting in an overall moderate populist ideological flavor.

chunk 4 · The text combines social justice and family-centered policies (left), national identity protection via criminalization of fascist symbols (right), and centrist appeals to family and social cohesion, reflecting a moderate centrist populist ideology.

chunk 5 · Text reflects centrist populism through calls for unity and cooperation within EU, left-leaning social market economy values, and right-leaning cultural identity pride, showing a balanced populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are centrist and technocratic, opposing political interest groups in favor of individual citizens' aspirations without strong left-wing or right-wing populist rhetoric.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on how political elites and interest groups distort the market and state institutions without adopting left-wing or right-wing populist frames.

chunk 3 · The populist rhetoric is combined with a strong anti-leftist, pro-market stance, rejecting redistribution and state intervention while welcoming foreign capital.

chunk 4 · The populist elements are centrist and focus on anti-corruption without strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

chunk 5 · The text lacks any structured populist ideology, containing only a minor centrist critique of local political actors.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly advocates free markets, competition, rule of law, privatisation of state banks, lower taxes, and deregulation (e.g., pharmacy market liberalisation), placing it firmly in the pro-liberal ordo-liberal tradition, with moderate social safety-net provisions tempering the score slightly.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across political (democratic elections, rule of law), economic (market liberalisation, deregulation, privatisation, tax reduction), and financial-market (bank privatisation, competition in insurance/banking) dimensions, while social liberalism is entirely absent from the text.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it advocates privatisation, free markets, deregulation, tax cuts, rule of law, transparency in legislation, and market-based financial intermediation, explicitly rejecting state intervention, redistribution, and political management of the economy.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it advocates judicial independence and rule of law (political), human/disability rights (social), free markets, deregulation, tax cuts, and privatisation (economic), and opening insurance/health markets to competition (financial), yielding consistently high liberalism scores.

chunk 5 · The manifesto consistently endorses media freedom, minority protections, open markets with foreign investment, and diagnoses the financial crisis as stemming from insufficient financial-market regulation, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal (Christian-democratic/ordo-liberal) stance across all four dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto advocates for rule of law, social protections, a social market economy with entrepreneurial freedom, and addresses financial market issues like bank recapitalization, showing a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports rule of law, social rights within a Christian democratic framework, free market principles, and critiques failures in financial market regulation, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports liberal political principles by advocating transparency and rule of law; liberal social policies through social security reforms; liberal economic policies by proposing tax reductions and market liberalization; and liberal financial market policies by endorsing central bank regulation and rejecting political interference in banking, leading to an overall pro-liberal stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports rule of law with judicial independence, social protections, economic liberalization via tax cuts, and financial market reforms promoting competition, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 5 · Strong emphasis on political rights, media freedom, social rights, social market economy, and acknowledgment of financial market regulation issues demonstrates a pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly advocates classical liberal and ordoliberal principles, emphasizing private property, free market competition, deregulation, lower taxes, and the privatization of state-owned banks and enterprises.

chunk 2 · The text strongly advocates for classical liberal principles, including free-market competition, deregulation, privatization of banks, and the protection of private property rights against state intervention.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including privatization, free-market competition, deregulation, tax reduction, and independent financial market regulation.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal principles, including free markets, deregulation, judicial independence, and private enterprise.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including media freedom, market-based economic policies, and minority protections, while advocating for stronger financial market regulation.
