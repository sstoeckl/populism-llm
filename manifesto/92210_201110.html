WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames financial institutions and large capital groups as adversaries of ordinary citizens, invoking people-centric language and a mild good-vs-evil framing around neoliberal globalization, but stops short of full populist Manicheanism, maintaining a social-democratic programmatic tone throughout.

chunk 2 · The text contains moderate anti-elitism directed at politicized military leadership and right-wing governments, some people-centric framing around the army serving citizens, and limited Manichean rhetoric targeting PiS's xenophobic foreign policy, but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

chunk 3 · The text contains moderate populist elements, primarily framing right-wing governments (PiS, PO) as harmful to Poland's interests and invoking broad public support, but the dominant tone is programmatic and policy-oriented rather than strongly populist.

chunk 4 · The text criticises the ruling right-wing government (PO-PSL, Donald Tusk) for failing citizens on healthcare, discrimination, and education, but the critique is policy-specific and institutional rather than a broad Manichean people-vs.-corrupt-elite frame, yielding only moderate populism scores.

chunk 5 · The text shows moderate populist features: the ruling PO-PSL coalition and its "neoliberal plans" are criticized as harmful to ordinary citizens, and the left frames itself as the only force capable of saving Poland from right-wing mismanagement, but the rhetoric remains largely policy-focused rather than strongly Manichean or people-centric.

chunk 6 · The manifesto criticises the ruling PO government and "neoliberal right" as harmful to Poland, frames SLD as the sole rescuer of the country, and appeals to the welfare of "all Poles," but the rhetoric is primarily programmatic rather than strongly populist; anti-elitism and Manichean framing are present but moderate.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist, explicitly opposing neoliberal globalization, defending the European social model, advocating redistribution and social equality, with no meaningful right-wing nativist or identity-protectionist content.

chunk 2 · The document is primarily a left-leaning (SLD) policy manifesto with proposals for a global capital flows tax and social solidarity, mild national-character references for the army, and generic citizen-service appeals, placing it weakly on the left of the populist spectrum.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is clearly left-leaning, advocating redistribution, social equality, expanded welfare, a global financial transaction tax, and workers' rights, while explicitly rejecting right-wing liberal social policy as favoring the wealthy.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is clearly left-leaning: it advocates public, non-commercial hospitals, state responsibility for education and healthcare, anti-discrimination, gender equality, and LGBT rights, with explicit opposition to privatisation and market-driven public services.

chunk 5 · The text is clearly left-populist in orientation, emphasizing redistribution, workers' rights, public investment, social equality, and opposition to neoliberal market reforms, with minimal right-wing or purely centrist populist elements.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it emphasises redistribution, social cohesion, reducing inequality (Gini target), expanding social protection, and attacking "neoliberal" right-wing policies, with no nativist or right-populist content beyond openness to labour immigration.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is moderately liberal on political and social dimensions (supporting minority rights, civil liberties, separation of church and state, same-sex partnerships), but explicitly rejects financial market primacy and advocates a strong state role in the economy, yielding mixed-to-low scores on economic and financial market liberalism.

chunk 2 · The text endorses democratic values and open foreign policy (pro-liberal politically), supports aviation market deregulation while also advocating state ownership in railways and postal services (mixed economically), proposes a global capital flows tax (anti-liberal financially), and calls for a humane migration policy (moderately pro-liberal socially).

chunk 3 · The manifesto is moderately liberal politically (supporting democratic institutions, rule of law, EU integration, human rights) and socially (gender equality, anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ rights implied), but economically leans toward social-democratic intervention, and explicitly supports a capital flows tax that is anti-liberal in financial market terms.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports civil rights, gender equality, LGBT partnership rights, and anti-discrimination (high lib_social); endorses democratic values and rule of law (lib_political); but explicitly rejects market logic in healthcare and education (lower lib_economic); financial markets are not discussed.

chunk 5 · The text shows mixed liberal signals: it supports media pluralism, artistic freedom, and social liberalization (abortion, sex education), while also advocating strong state intervention in the economy, public ownership of forests and media, and skepticism toward market mechanisms in education and public services.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports business-friendly reforms, transparency, and equal opportunities (pro-liberal), but also advocates a financial-sector tax, state intervention in markets, redistribution, and expanded public spending (anti-liberal), yielding a moderate overall score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto supports political liberalism through emphasis on constitutional rights and public cultural policy, social liberalism via support for childcare and women's labor activation, and economic liberalism by promoting competitive economic growth; financial market liberalism is not addressed.

chunk 6 · The manifesto advocates for strengthening democratic institutions, reducing inequality, simplifying taxes, supporting private enterprise, and prudential financial regulation, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial market dimensions.
