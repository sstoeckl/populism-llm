WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames the governing establishment's discourse as self-congratulatory and misleading ("discurso oficial y sus corifeos"), calls on the organized majority to reclaim political agency, and criticizes the government's economic path as "equivocado, errático e injusto," showing moderate populist anti-elitism and people-centrism without a fully Manichean framing.

chunk 2 · The text shows mild populist tendencies through occasional references to elite interests ("grupos socialmente hegemónicos") and criticism of the PSOE government's failures, but the document is predominantly a detailed policy manifesto on education, health, and sports with little sustained people-vs-elite framing.

chunk 3 · The text criticises financial and political elites (PSOE, PP, business class, financial centres) as corrupt and self-serving, invokes citizens as the legitimate sovereign, and frames politics as a struggle between the people and entrenched power, but the tone is programmatic rather than strongly Manichean, yielding moderate populism scores.

chunk 4 · The text criticises the PSOE government as authoritarian and corrupt, frames citizens as the legitimate sovereign, and draws a moral contrast between the ruling establishment and the people, but the tone is primarily programmatic rather than strongly populist.

chunk 5 · The text invokes 'the people' as the source of legitimate politics and frames elites (commercial interests, hegemonic powers, right-wing economic policy) as corrupt or harmful, with a moderate Manichean good-vs-evil framing around social justice and anti-militarism.

chunk 6 · The text invokes 'the people' as the source of legitimate politics and frames current governance as working against citizens' interests, but the anti-elitism and Manichean framing are moderate rather than extreme, focused on participatory democracy and youth policy rather than a sharp elite-vs-people confrontation.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto criticizes the government and elites for complacency and economic mismanagement, appeals to the people with moral urgency, and highlights social injustices and exclusion, indicating moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and some moral polarization.

chunk 2 · The manifesto criticizes the ruling power for fostering a fragmented society and emphasizes active participation of people, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and strong people-centrism with some moral conflict framing.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elites and the establishment, emphasizes the role of 'the people' through democratic institutions, and shows mixed moralistic conflict framing, supporting a moderate populism score.

chunk 4 · The text explicitly criticizes political elites and highlights citizens' role in justice, showing moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism, with some moralized critique of government authoritarianism, indicating low to moderate populism overall.

chunk 5 · The manifesto expresses moderate anti-elitism through critique of media concentration and mercantilization, emphasizes people-centrism by advocating democratic opinion formation, and shows mixed manichean elements by blaming war promoters and corrupt elites, supporting a moderate overall populism score.

chunk 6 · No evidence found in the text for populism dimensions; the manifesto focuses on social and political participation without anti-elitist or moralizing rhetoric.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the political power for creating a fragmented society and demanding that public services serve the citizens rather than elite interests.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the governing administration as deceptive and out of touch, while moralizing the struggle of marginalized groups against structural capitalist forces.

chunk 4 · The text criticizes the governing PSOE elite for authoritarian policies and cutting liberties, while framing Izquierda Unida as the defender of popular sovereignty and citizens' rights.

chunk 5 · The text exhibits moderate populism by contrasting the interests of 'the people' and 'the many' against corrupting commercial and governmental elites.

chunk 6 · The text exhibits strong populism by framing a divide between the ruling political class and the citizens, advocating for a direct, bottom-up democracy where political programs must emerge directly from the people.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it champions public services over market logic, redistribution, social equality, anti-discrimination, and workers' interests, while explicitly identifying as "izquierda transformadora" and rejecting market subordination of social policy.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist in orientation, emphasizing redistribution, equality, anti-privatization, public services, and social solidarity, while explicitly opposing neoliberal policies and defending the welfare state; there is no right-populist or nativist content.

chunk 3 · Izquierda Unida's manifesto is firmly left-populist: it calls for redistribution, democratic planning of the economy, progressive taxation, strong public sector, opposition to privatisation, and solidarity with marginalised groups, with no right-wing nativist content.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is clearly left-wing (Izquierda Unida), emphasising workers' rights, public services, anti-racism, LGBTQ+ equality, and opposition to privatisation, with no right-wing or nativist populist content.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it explicitly rejects neoliberal and monetarist criteria, advocates redistribution and workers' rights, opposes US hegemony and corporate media concentration, and frames the struggle as workers/people against capitalist elites.

chunk 6 · The text is clearly left-populist: it calls for social transformation, redistribution (housing, employment, public funds), explicitly rejects conservative policies, and frames IU as the vehicle of the working and youth classes against precarity and inequality.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports left-wing populist themes such as social equality, state regulation, and redistribution, while also acknowledging centrist people-elite appeals and some right-wing authoritarian tendencies.

chunk 4 · The manifesto expresses a left-wing populist stance emphasizing defense of liberties and social rights, criticizes government authoritarianism, and calls for ethical politics, reflecting a left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist elements.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports left-wing populist ideology with emphasis on social justice and workers' rights, shows some right-wing populist concern on immigration regularization, and includes centrist populist appeals to citizen participation, justifying a moderately high overall populist ideology score.

chunk 6 · No evidence found for populist ideological stances; the text emphasizes youth participation and social policies without clear left/right/centrist populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is socially liberal (strong support for gender equality, minority rights, LGBTQ+ respect, anti-discrimination) and politically liberal (democratic participation, decentralization), but economically interventionist—explicitly rejecting market logic in social services and proposing extensive state regulation and public enterprise, yielding a mixed overall liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto scores high on political liberalism (democratic governance, transparency, autonomy) and social liberalism (gender equality, multiculturalism, minority integration), but is anti-liberal economically (favoring public provision, restricting private concessions, opposing market-based health reforms) and explicitly hostile to free-market financial policies.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism (separation of powers, democratic rights, rule of law) and social liberalism (anti-discrimination, minority rights), but is explicitly anti-liberal economically (opposing privatisation, favouring state planning) and anti-liberal on financial markets (capital controls, banking secrecy abolition), yielding a mixed overall liberalism score.

chunk 4 · The text is strongly pro-liberal on political rights and social liberalism (democratic institutions, LGBTQ+ rights, anti-discrimination), but anti-liberal on economic liberalism, explicitly rejecting privatisation and prioritising public management of services; no financial-market content is present.

chunk 5 · The text is moderately liberal on political and social dimensions (democracy, minority rights, human rights, media pluralism) but mixed on economic liberalism, favouring public services, state intervention in media, and anti-market cultural policy, while supporting market transparency in limited contexts.

chunk 6 · The text supports parliamentary democracy and individual rights (conscientious objection, anti-discrimination), scoring moderately pro-liberal politically and socially; however, it advocates heavy state intervention in housing and subsidised credit, scoring anti-liberal on economic and financial-market dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto advocates for democratic institutions, political rights, social protections, economic planning, progressive taxation, and financial market regulation enabling market function, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights, social equality, and calls for administrative reforms enhancing political and social liberalism, with economic liberalism present but less emphasized; financial market issues are not addressed.

chunk 5 · The manifesto advocates strong political liberalism with calls for parliamentary control and democratic reforms, robust social liberalism through anti-discrimination measures, and economic liberalism by opposing neoliberal austerity, but lacks explicit financial market discussion, resulting in a high overall liberalism score.

chunk 6 · The manifesto explicitly supports democratic institutions, civil rights, social equality, regulated economic policies, and financial market facilitation, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political democracy and human rights, but rejects market-based economic principles in favor of public services and state intervention.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political democracy, transparency, and social equality (especially gender equality), but rejects free-market economic policies in favor of public services and state regulation.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic reforms and civil liberties, but rejects economic and financial liberalism in favor of state control and market regulation.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic checks and balances and minority rights, but rejects economic liberalism in favor of public control and state management of services.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic reforms and minority rights, but rejects economic and financial liberalism in favor of state intervention.

chunk 6 · While the text supports political representation and fundamental civil rights like conscientious objection, it strongly opposes market-based economic and financial solutions, advocating instead for state-subsidized credit, public housing, and fiscal penalties.
