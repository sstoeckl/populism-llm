WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto criticises the Netanyahu government and capital holders for deepening inequality and concentrating wealth, framing this as deliberate harm to the people, but the anti-elitist and Manichean rhetoric is moderate and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

chunk 2 · The manifesto (Meretz, Israel 2015) contains only mild populist elements: it briefly criticises the political system as captured by interest groups and Netanyahu's government as serving narrow elites, but the dominant register is programmatic policy detail rather than a moralized people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populist tendencies: it frames the dangerous closeness between capital holders and government officials as a serious threat to democracy, notes that the governing elite is involved in corruption, and calls for protecting the public from abuse of power — but it does so in a technocratic, reform-oriented manner rather than through sharp people-vs-elite rhetoric.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly frames elites as corrupt and threatening democracy, emphasizes inclusive people-centrism with equal rights for all citizens, and highlights a moral struggle against racism and discriminatory laws, supporting a moderately high populism score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly critiques the Netanyahu government as an elite establishment managing the Israeli-Palestinian conflict as a status quo, while emphasizing multicultural unity and societal balance, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict framing.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the dangerous closeness between capital owners and government officials, calls for fair treatment of refugees across society, and frames struggles involving migrants and elites as a conflict, indicating moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized struggle.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text contains minor populist elements, specifically criticizing government ministers and Knesset members for inciting racism, but lacks strong people-centrism or Manichean framing.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits low populism, focusing primarily on policy details rather than anti-elite rhetoric, though it criticizes the current government for acting against public interests.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits mild populism focused on anti-elitism, specifically targeting the corrupting influence of capital on political decision-makers.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is clearly left-leaning, advocating for a strong welfare state, re-nationalisation of privatised services, progressive taxation, workers' rights, and redistribution, with no right-wing nationalist or identity-protectionist content.

chunk 2 · Where populist flavour exists it leans left—redistribution in higher education, workers' rights, environmental justice, and social equality for marginalised groups—rather than right-wing identity populism, though the overall ideological intensity is moderate.

chunk 3 · The populist flavor is predominantly left-leaning, focusing on reducing economic concentration, protecting workers' rights (including migrant workers), expanding social services, and challenging corporate influence on government — with no meaningful right-wing nativist or identity-based populist content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text promotes left-wing populist economic justice and welfare state policies, opposes right-wing nationalist and discriminatory laws, and embraces centrist democratic inclusiveness, reflecting a mixed but predominantly left populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The text shows a moderate left-leaning populist ideology with emphasis on anti-occupation, social equality, and multicultural inclusion, alongside centrist appeals to societal balance and some right-leaning nationalist security concerns.

chunk 3 · The text combines left-leaning anti-elite economic critiques with concerns about migration and social inclusion, alongside centrist appeals to shared societal responsibility, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is left-wing, focusing on social welfare, reducing inequality, and criticizing the concentration of capital and corporate elites.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology leans left, targeting private corporations, outsourcing, and privatization in favor of public services and workers' rights.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing, framing the elite threat as a collusion between capital owners and government officials.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores high on political and social liberalism (democracy, minority rights, LGBTQ+ equality, anti-racism) but is anti-liberal economically, favouring re-nationalisation, halting privatisation, imposing financial transaction taxes, and heavy market regulation.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (academic freedom, free expression, civil rights, gender equality, religious pluralism, rule of law), mixed on economic liberalism (supports public services and regulation while criticising neo-liberal overconsumption), and moderately anti-liberal on financial markets (advocates renationalisation of water utilities and state control of natural resources).

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal politically (defending parliamentary democracy, rule of law, constitutional rights, judicial independence, and transparency), pro-liberal socially (promoting equality for women, Arabs, and people with disabilities, protecting refugees), mixed on economic liberalism (supporting agricultural profitability and trade conditions but also state subsidies and regulation), and moderately pro-liberal on financial markets (favoring reduced concentration and transparency but not explicitly endorsing market-based finance).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights, social equality, economic fairness, and prudent financial market regulation, indicating a robust classical liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political rights, social equality, economic accessibility, and prudent financial market regulation, emphasizing democracy, human rights, education access, and environmental-economic balance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports parliamentary democracy, political rights, social freedoms including artistic expression, economic policies favoring agricultural profitability and market conditions, and calls for transparency in lobbying, indicating a broadly pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through minority rights and democratic values, but rejects economic and financial liberalism in favor of state intervention, nationalization, and financial transaction taxes.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through human rights, freedom of expression, and minority protections, while opposing economic liberalism by advocating for state intervention and public ownership.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through human rights and judicial independence, while opposing economic privatization in public services.
