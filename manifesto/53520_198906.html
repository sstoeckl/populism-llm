WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto criticises Fianna Fáil as deceptive and arrogant but frames this primarily as a partisan electoral critique rather than a systemic anti-elite populist appeal, with only modest people-centric and Manichean rhetoric.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains only mild anti-elite rhetoric, primarily criticising Fianna Fáil for abusing Lottery funds and treating Defence Forces callously, but does not construct a sustained people-vs-elite narrative or moralistic Manichean framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the incumbent Fianna Fail government as corrupt, arrogant, and untrustworthy, calling for their removal and emphasizing Fine Gael's commitment to the interests of the Irish people, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized conflict.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains no explicit discussion of populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralized good-vs-evil struggles.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits mild populism by framing the ruling Fianna Fáil party as deceptive and arrogant, while positioning Fine Gael as the true defender of the Irish people's interests.

chunk 2 · The text contains mild anti-elitist criticism of the governing party's abuse of public funds, but lacks a broader moralized or people-centric populist framework.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The document's populist elements are centrist-technocratic in character, focused on competent governance and national development rather than left-wing redistribution or right-wing identity politics.

chunk 2 · The limited populist signals are broadly centrist-reformist, with modest centre-left welfare concerns (poverty traps, social welfare reform) and no strong left-redistributive or right-nativist populist ideology.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text shows a moderate left populist stance through criticism of attacks on social services, a mild right populist concern for national economic interests, and a centrist populist readiness to govern responsibly, indicating an overall moderate populist ideological flavor.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideological stances on the left, right, or centrist populism in the manifesto text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on general democratic accountability and the public interest without strong left-wing anti-corporate or right-wing nativist framing.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are limited to centrist partisan criticism of the ruling party's administration of public funds without a strong left or right ideological populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic debate, individual rights (including for the disabled), free-market competition, tax reduction, privatisation of semi-state bodies, and market-based financial reforms.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly liberal across political (Freedom of Information Act, legal aid, courts reform), social (Equal Status Bill, women's rights), economic (removing job-creation disincentives), and financial-market (Home Income Plan enabling mortgage-based annuities) dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic debate, poverty alleviation, economic competition, and financial market regulation enabling market function, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal political institutions (e.g., Freedom of Information Act), social equality (Equal Status Bill), and economic reforms promoting market efficiency and welfare reform, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing market competition, the incorporation of EC competition law, tax cuts, and institutional democratic reforms.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political reforms like freedom of information, social equality through anti-discrimination laws, and market-enabling financial instruments for the elderly.
