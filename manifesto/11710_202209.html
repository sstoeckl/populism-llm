WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames established parties (both socialist and bourgeois) as having betrayed Swedish citizens by prioritizing other interests, while positioning 'the people' (Swedish citizens) as a unified virtuous group deserving protection, with a clear moral framing of crisis caused by irresponsible elites.

chunk 2 · The text shows moderate populist features: elites (political parties, EU, government) are criticised for ignoring ordinary people's needs and hiding pension problems, while 'vanliga människor' are invoked as the true reference point for policy; however, the rhetoric stops short of a fully Manichean good-vs-evil framing.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populism: established parties (socialist and bourgeois governments) are blamed for dismantling defence, the Swedish people are invoked as the sovereign voice via referendum instruments against Brussels power transfers, but the tone is primarily policy-programmatic rather than strongly moralized good-vs-evil.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly prioritizes 'Swedish interests' and portrays a struggle between 'good citizens' and criminals, emphasizing a moralized conflict and a people-centric approach against elites and outsiders.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes EU and government elites restricting national rights and emphasizes the virtuous Swedish people and their interests, framing conflicts in moral terms such as threats from islamism and terrorism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto expresses distrust of EU elites and loss of national influence, emphasizes Nordic and national identity as a collective, and frames migration and EU policies as threats, indicating moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean views.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text frames previous governments as corrupt elites who ignored the interests of the virtuous Swedish people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the political elite as out of touch and corrupt, while championing the needs of ordinary citizens against elite-driven experiments.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the transfer of power from national democracies to EU elites and advocating for direct democratic instruments like referendums to give the people a voice.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is predominantly right-wing, centered on nativist anti-immigration stances, cultural protectionism, and national identity, with only marginal left-populist redistribution rhetoric and a nominal centrist self-description that is not substantiated programmatically.

chunk 2 · The populist appeals are overwhelmingly right-wing: anti-immigration, national identity protection, cultural conservatism, opposition to EU power transfers, and framing of immigration as a threat to welfare, housing, and youth — hallmarks of right-wing populism.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is clearly right-leaning, centred on anti-immigration/migration control, national sovereignty against EU integration, Nordic identity, and opposition to Islamism, with no redistributive or anti-corporate left-populist content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text rejects traditional left-right labels but strongly emphasizes national priority and restrictive immigration policies, reflecting a right-leaning populist ideology with some centrist appeals.

chunk 2 · The text combines right-wing populist themes like anti-immigration and national protectionism with some centrist appeals to support for farmers and rural communities, and limited left-wing economic intervention.

chunk 3 · The text combines right-wing populist themes of strong border control and anti-immigration with centrist calls for increased voter influence and skepticism of EU power, and some left-leaning critique of EU economic control, resulting in a mixed but predominantly right-leaning populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, focusing heavily on stopping immigration and protecting national identity.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, focusing heavily on national identity, anti-immigration, and the rejection of multiculturalism.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, focusing on national sovereignty, anti-immigration, and putting Swedish interests first against external EU influence.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto endorses market economy, free trade, and competition while also supporting deregulation for business; however, it is mixed on social liberalism (opposing veil/headscarf, restricting immigration) and political liberalism (strengthening rule of law but also restricting civil liberties for non-citizens), yielding a moderate overall liberal score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports rule of law, judicial independence, and free speech (pro-liberal), but also advocates import bans, state intervention in housing and agriculture, cultural restrictions (e.g. banning headscarves in schools), and opposition to EU single market integration, pulling scores toward the middle.

chunk 3 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it endorses free trade and Nordic market integration but opposes EU political union, seeks currency opt-outs (anti-financial-market integration), restricts migration, and frames freedom of expression narrowly against EU censorship, yielding a below-average overall liberal score.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports the Swedish model and market economy with some social protections but shows skepticism towards EU influence and extensive liberal social policies, indicating moderate liberalism with reservations.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports strengthening democratic institutions, rule of law, civil rights including for minorities and HBT+ persons, and economic liberalism such as tax reductions and market competition, but lacks discussion on financial market regulation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports political rights like free speech, values free trade economically, but opposes increased EU economic control and new EU taxes, and calls for conditional aid, reflecting a moderate classical liberal stance with some skepticism toward EU integration.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The platform supports market economy and rule of law, but opposes liberal social pluralism regarding multiculturalism and religious attire.

chunk 2 · The text supports classical liberal democratic institutions and property rights, but takes a restrictive stance on social liberalism regarding gender theories and religious practices.

chunk 3 · The text supports free trade and democratic participation but opposes deeper political integration, rejects the common currency, and advocates for state intervention in preserving cash as a payment method.
