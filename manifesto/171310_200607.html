WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto moderately invokes populist themes by criticizing "élites gobernantes y poderes fácticos" for prioritizing business climate over social needs, calling for citizen empowerment, and labeling the status quo as "necedad corruptocrática," but frames these critiques within a democratic, institutional, and pluralist framework rather than a sharp people-vs-elite binary.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mild populist features—criticizing concentrated capital and an exclusionary development model—but is primarily a technocratic policy document focused on structural reform, sustainable development, and rights, without strong people-vs-elite framing or Manichean rhetoric.

chunk 3 · The text is primarily a policy manifesto with detailed reform proposals; it makes occasional references to the people's aspirations and criticizes vested interests in sport and politics, but lacks sustained anti-elite rhetoric, Manichean framing, or strong people-centrism typical of populist discourse.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly critiques economic and political elites as obstacles to social justice, emphasizes the role of 'citizenship' and democratic participation as central to political life, and acknowledges moralized struggles against paternalistic and authoritarian postures, reflecting moderate populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto critiques the economic elite's neoliberal policies and emphasizes democratic participation and social equity, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moral framing of inequality.

chunk 3 · The manifesto expresses anti-elitism by challenging established political prejudices, emphasizes people-centrism through youth empowerment and community focus, and shows a mild manichean tone by contrasting adult failures with youth potential.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text criticizes ruling elites and factual powers for ignoring social needs, framing politics as a struggle between a self-serving minority and the majority.

chunk 2 · The text does not contain populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits very low populism, only briefly mentioning a desire to break with established political schemes and prejudices without invoking a virtuous 'people' or a moralized struggle.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist flavor is predominantly left-leaning, emphasizing redistribution, anti-corporate elite critique, social equality, gender equity, and workers' rights, with minimal nativist or right-populist content, though the overall populist intensity is moderate given the manifesto's strong institutionalist and liberal-democratic orientation.

chunk 2 · The populist elements lean left, emphasizing poverty reduction, inequality, redistribution, and labor rights, while right-populist or nativist themes are virtually absent; the overall ideological flavor is a moderate center-left reformism rather than strong populism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist through its emphasis on social redistribution, universal social security, workers' rights, and anti-inequality measures, while also invoking national sovereignty; however, the overall tone is social-democratic rather than strongly populist.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-leaning commitments to social justice and equality with centrist calls for political pluralism and consensus-building, while rejecting right-wing religious impositions, indicating a mixed but overall moderate populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The text combines left-leaning concerns about inequality and redistribution with right-leaning nationalist economic concerns, alongside centrist democratic participation appeals, indicating a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 3 · The text supports left-leaning populist ideology by advocating for just wealth distribution and market economy reforms, while centrist populism is reflected in calls to break political prejudices; no right-wing populist evidence is found.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist discourse is strongly left-wing, focusing on reducing economic inequality, combating poverty, and opposing corporate/financial elites.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on programmatic policy proposals without populist framing.

chunk 3 · There is no clear populist ideology present in the text as it lacks the core populist framing of the pure people versus the corrupt elite.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across political and social dimensions—championing democracy, rule of law, civil liberties, anti-discrimination, and LGBTQ+ rights—while taking a mixed economic stance that supports competition and anti-monopoly policy but also advocates significant state investment in education and social programs; financial market liberalism is weakly evidenced, noting only the absence of accessible financial instruments.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on social dimensions (anti-discrimination, gender equality, LGBTQ+ rights, indigenous rights) and moderately pro-liberal politically (democratic participation, rule of law, laicism) and economically (market liberalization, anti-monopoly), with mixed signals on financial markets (pro-competition but also proposing capital-flow controls on speculative investment).

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on social dimensions, explicitly defending LGBTQ+ rights, religious freedom, and anti-discrimination; it supports democratic institutions and rule of law; on economic and financial dimensions it accepts market mechanisms but emphasizes state redistribution and social security, yielding mixed-to-moderate scores.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights, social equality, economic growth with employment, and acknowledges the need for accessible financial institutions, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, human rights, gender equity, market competition, and financial market reforms that enable market functioning, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberalism with explicit defense of secular democracy, minority rights, market economy, and international trade agreements, showing a comprehensive pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly defends civil liberties, human rights, and anti-discrimination, while supporting market competition through anti-monopoly policies and access to credit.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including democratic participation, human rights, gender equality, market competition, and financial sector reforms.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports social liberalism, pluralism, and minority rights, alongside a regulated market economy and democratic institutions.
