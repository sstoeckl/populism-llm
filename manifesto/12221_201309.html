WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames financial speculators and market liberalism as corrupt forces threatening ordinary people's security, invokes "folk flest" as a virtuous collective, and frames the struggle between the people and capital as a moral conflict, yielding moderate-to-high populism scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The text invokes collective ownership ("fellesskapets midler") and frames private welfare profiteers negatively, but the dominant tone is programmatic policy rather than a moralized people-vs-elite struggle, yielding low-to-moderate populism scores.

chunk 3 · The text shows mild populist tendencies through framing of market forces and private profit as threats to the common good ("fellesskapets penger skal gå til velferd og ikke til profitt"), but the dominant register is programmatic social-democratic policy rather than a sharp people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: commercial actors are framed as exploiting public health for profit, and the goal of giving "ordinary people" more power is invoked, but the rhetoric is policy-focused rather than morally Manichean or strongly people-centric.

chunk 5 · The text shows moderate populist tendencies: it frames global financial speculation and elite capital concentration as harmful to ordinary people, calls for radical redistribution of power and resources, and positions the struggle as one between the rich and the poor, but does so within a policy-oriented, institutional framework rather than a strongly Manichean people-vs-corrupt-elite narrative.

chunk 6 · The text frames the economic crisis as caused by decades of right-wing policy (anti-elitism), calls for solidarity with "ordinary people" fighting EU austerity (people-centrism), and draws a moral contrast between capital/elites and the people, though the framing is programmatic rather than strongly Manichean.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing: the manifesto calls for redistribution through progressive taxation, strong public ownership, anti-corporate regulation, workers' rights, and reduction of class differences, with only marginal national-sovereignty framing (EU/EØS opposition) that does not constitute right-wing populism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it advocates strong state ownership, redistribution, labour protections, and anti-corporate regulation, with only minor nativist undertones (Norwegian wage standards), placing it firmly on the left of the populist spectrum.

chunk 3 · The populist elements that do appear are clearly left-coded: anti-privatization, redistribution through progressive taxation, strong welfare state, and anti-corporate framing ("de som eier mest og tjener mest må betale mer til fellesskapet"), with no right-populist or nativist content.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is clearly left-leaning, with strong emphasis on reducing market-based systems in healthcare, expanding public ownership, redistributing power to workers, and combating commercial exploitation of welfare services.

chunk 5 · The populist ideology is clearly left-leaning: the text calls for radical redistribution, taxes on financial transactions, opposition to privatization, support for workers' rights and trade unions globally, and frames multinational corporations and international capital as undemocratic power factors threatening ordinary people.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is strongly left-populist: it explicitly blames deregulation, privatization, and lower taxes for capitalism's problems, advocates redistribution and shifting power from capital to democratic institutions, and supports trade protections for poor countries and workers' rights globally.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal economically and financially—explicitly rejecting market governance of housing, calling for state control of financial markets, financial transaction taxes, nationalization of banks, and banning hedge funds—while showing moderate support for democratic institutions and social equality (gender, minority inclusion), yielding a low overall liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The text scores moderately on political and social liberalism (democracy, equal rights, gender equality) but is anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions, calling for stricter banking regulation, state ownership of key sectors including banking, and market controls in agriculture and energy.

chunk 3 · The text scores moderately on political and social liberalism (democratic values, equal access regardless of gender/ethnicity), but is anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions, explicitly calling for market regulation, rent controls, interest-rate caps, and state-led housing and welfare provision over market solutions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto scores high on social liberalism (LGBTQ+ rights, gender equality, anti-discrimination) and political liberalism (democratic governance, free press, voting rights), but is anti-liberal economically (restricting commercial actors, opposing market mechanisms in healthcare) and mixed on financial markets (supporting cooperative finance institutions).

chunk 5 · The text scores high on political and social liberalism (strong support for democracy, human rights, anti-discrimination, multiculturalism, and religious freedom), but scores low on economic and financial-market liberalism, explicitly opposing unregulated financial markets, calling for financial transaction taxes, opposing privatization, and supporting state monopolies and fixed-price book systems.

chunk 6 · The text scores moderately on political liberalism (supports democracy, human rights, UN) and social liberalism (women's rights, minority protections), but is anti-liberal economically (protectionism, state control over trade) and proposes financial transaction taxes, yielding a mixed overall liberalism score.
