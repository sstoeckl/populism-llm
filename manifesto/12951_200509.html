WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies: bureaucracy and the growing state are framed as threats to individual freedom, and there are passing references to 'folk flest', but the text is primarily a detailed liberal-economic programme without strong people-vs-elite moral framing or conspiratorial rhetoric.

chunk 2 · The text is predominantly a policy manifesto focused on economic liberalization and deregulation, with only minor populist elements: a brief critique of UN climate bureaucrats as politically motivated and a few references to crime affecting ordinary people, but no sustained people-vs-elite framing or Manichean narrative.

chunk 3 · The text shows only mild populist features: occasional anti-elite rhetoric (rich countries' hypocrisy blocking poor nations' trade) and appeals to Norwegian national interests, but no sustained people-vs-elite framing, no Manichean conspiracy narrative, and no invocation of a unified virtuous 'people' as a political subject.

chunk 4 · The text shows minimal populist features: it criticises the current health system's failures and invokes patient choice, but does not frame elites as corrupt enemies of the people, nor does it construct a Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 5 · The text is primarily a policy manifesto focused on education, transport, culture, and research, with minimal populist framing. There are occasional references to "folk flest" (ordinary people) and criticism of bureaucratic inefficiency, but no sustained anti-elite rhetoric, people-as-sovereign appeals, or Manichean good-vs-evil framing.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist flavour is mildly right-leaning: welfare rights tied to citizenship and a preference for national economic interests appear, but the dominant thrust is classical liberal market economics rather than nativist or identity-based populism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto leans slightly right-populist through its emphasis on border control and immigration enforcement, but is primarily a classical-liberal/market-oriented program with minimal populist ideological content overall.

chunk 3 · The dominant populist ideological flavor is right-leaning: strong restrictive immigration policy, national-interest framing, and cultural assimilation demands, with no redistributive or anti-corporate elite framing typical of left populism.

chunk 4 · The text is primarily a market-liberal policy programme with mild right-leaning elements (emphasis on individual responsibility, integration demands on immigrants) but no strong left or right populist ideological framing.

chunk 5 · The text shows weak populist ideology overall. There are minor right-leaning cultural-nationalist elements (protecting Norwegian cultural heritage, skepticism of forced cultural integration) but no strong anti-immigration or anti-corporate elite framing. The dominant thrust is market-liberal rather than populist-ideological.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is explicitly self-described as 'liberalistisk' and consistently advocates free markets, minimal state intervention, flat taxes, free capital markets, independent central bank, free trade, and strong rule-of-law/constitutional protections, placing it firmly in the classical-liberal tradition across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all dimensions: it advocates free trade, deregulation, privatization of state enterprises (including banks), free competition in financial markets, independent courts, and rule of law, consistent with a classical liberal/ordo-liberal orientation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal economically (free trade, free capital flows, market economy for development, privatization of prisons and hospitals, competition in healthcare), moderately pro-liberal politically (rule of law, free speech, democracy), and mixed on social liberalism (anti-discrimination rhetoric alongside restrictive immigration and opposition to same-sex adoption).

chunk 4 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across economic, financial, and political dimensions: it advocates free competition between public and private providers, piece-rate financing, private pension savings, free school choice, and democratic civic education, while also supporting equal opportunities for disabled persons.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions press freedom and democracy (political), opposes gender quotas in admissions (social, mixed), advocates market-based transport deregulation, privatization, and competition (economic), and supports market-rate interest on student loans (financial market).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports constitutional rights, individual freedoms, free markets, economic growth, competition, and financial market liberalization, demonstrating a high commitment to classical liberal principles.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports political democracy, individual rights, economic liberalization, and financial market harmonization, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism with emphasis on rule of law and rights, social liberalism with anti-discrimination, economic liberalism advocating free trade, and financial market liberalism promoting market-based investment mechanisms.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through calls for reorganization and rights-based social insurance, social liberalism via equal opportunities for disabled persons, economic liberalism through market-based financing like stykkprisfinansiering, and financial market liberalism by endorsing insurance and market-based health financing.

chunk 5 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, equal treatment regardless of identity, market competition, and incentives for research funding, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial market dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly champions classical liberalism, advocating for free markets, independent courts, private property, and a highly deregulated financial system.

chunk 2 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including free trade, deregulation, privatization of state-owned banks, independent courts, and individual privacy rights.

chunk 3 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including free trade, deregulation, and private property, though it holds socially conservative views on issues like adoption.

chunk 4 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal principles, emphasizing market competition, private alternatives to public services, and individual choice.

chunk 5 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including free-market competition, deregulation, privatization, media freedom, and non-discrimination.
