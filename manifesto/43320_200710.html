WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames globalization as captured by powerful elites (transnational corporations, financial firms) at the expense of ordinary people, and calls for restructuring it in the service of "all people," exhibiting moderate populist anti-elitism and people-centrism without a strongly Manichean conspiratorial framing.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows only weak populist features: it briefly criticizes the "extremen und populistischen Rechten" and frames asylum policy as playing with people's lives, but the dominant register is programmatic social-democratic policy rather than a people-vs-elite narrative.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elites and transnational corporations as privileging themselves in globalization, emphasizes inclusive political participation of all people, and frames struggles against corporate abuses as a moral fight, reflecting moderate populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes economic elites and emphasizes the role of 'the people' in decision-making, solidarity, and social justice, reflecting moderate populist themes.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text targets corporate elites and top managers as greedy and out of touch, but lacks a strong moralized or people-centric populist framework.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the anti-competitive behavior of economic elites at the expense of ordinary consumers.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it frames elites as corporate/financial actors exploiting workers and the poor, calls for redistribution from capital to labour, opposes privatization, and explicitly rejects right-wing isolationism and anti-immigration stances.

chunk 2 · The text is clearly left-populist in orientation, defending the European social model, demanding redistribution, strong public services, and workers' protections, with no right-wing nativist or centrist-technocratic populist content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports left-leaning populist ideology through calls for international solidarity, debt relief, and social justice, while also showing some right-leaning protectionist stances on public services and centrist appeals for global institutional reform.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports left-leaning populist ideology with anti-corporate and pro-social justice stances, while also showing centrist populist appeals to participation and solidarity; right-wing populism is minimally present in opposition to NATO membership.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are clearly left-wing, focusing on anti-corporate sentiment, redistribution, and fighting neoliberal globalization.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are clearly left-wing, focusing on protecting consumers and workers from corporate interests and economic elites.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores high on political liberalism (democracy, transparency, rule of law) and social liberalism (gender equality, anti-discrimination), but is strongly anti-liberal on economic policy (opposing privatization, favouring state ownership and service public) and financial markets (advocating a currency transaction tax and capital controls for developing countries).

chunk 2 · The manifesto scores high on political liberalism (democracy, rule of law, checks and balances) and social liberalism (full equality for LGBTQ+, anti-discrimination, asylum rights), but is mixed on economic liberalism (supports competition for consumers yet opposes electricity market liberalization and advocates strong public-sector ownership).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto advocates for democratic participation, strong social equality and minority rights, economic policies supporting employment and innovation, and financial market reforms promoting transparency and effectiveness, indicating a broadly pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly endorses liberal democratic principles, social equality, market openness, and regulated financial markets supporting sustainability and transparency, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political democracy and social equality, but advocates for heavy state intervention, financial transaction taxes, and market regulations, resulting in a mixed economic stance.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political liberalism through the separation of powers and social liberalism via LGBTQ+ equality, while opposing economic liberalization in key infrastructure sectors.
