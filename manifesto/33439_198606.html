WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: it contrasts a virtuous ordinary citizenry ("ciudadanos corrientes") against an irresponsible Socialist government that has politicised the judiciary and deprived citizens of security, but the rhetoric is primarily programmatic and institutional rather than deeply Manichean or people-centric.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains mild anti-elitism directed at the Socialist government (criticising bureaucratic expansion and high taxes) and occasional people-centric appeals ("al servicio de todos los ciudadanos"), but the tone is predominantly technocratic and policy-oriented rather than strongly populist; Manichean framing is limited to electoral sloganeering ("NOSOTROS PODEMOS HACERLO MEJOR").

chunk 3 · The text shows mild populist features: the incumbent Socialist government is criticized for ideological cronyism and poor negotiation outcomes, and "the Spanish people" are invoked as the ultimate beneficiary, but the rhetoric is largely programmatic and institutional rather than strongly anti-elite or Manichean.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes concentration of power and arrogance of elites, references ordinary citizens as distinct from elites, and describes irresponsible policies harming common people, indicating a low to moderate populist stance.

chunk 2 · The text emphasizes the administration as a rational instrument serving all citizens and stresses efficiency without bureaucratic oppression, reflecting mild anti-elitism and people-centrism but lacks strong moral dichotomies.

chunk 3 · No evidence of populist rhetoric or themes was found in the text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the ruling socialist government as an arrogant, power-concentrating elite that threatens and ignores ordinary citizens ('españoles de a pie').

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing the ruling socialist government as an inefficient, costly elite while positioning the party as the true servant of the citizens.

chunk 3 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized people-versus-elite divisions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist flavour is predominantly right-leaning, combining law-and-order themes (tougher anti-terrorism and crime penalties), market liberalisation, privatisation, and anti-statism, with no redistributive or anti-corporate left-populist content.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is primarily a centre-right programme (Coalición Popular, Spain 1986) emphasising privatisation, deregulation, free enterprise, and national economic interests vis-à-vis the CEE, with no left-populist redistribution rhetoric; the modest populist flavour is right-leaning (national industry protection, criticism of Socialist government).

chunk 3 · The populist flavour is predominantly centre-right: emphasis on NATO integration, national interest, free trade, and defence, with only token references to social solidarity; no anti-corporate or redistributive left-populist framing is present.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text shows left-leaning populist concern for unemployment and social marginalization, some right-leaning anti-terrorism measures, and centrist appeals to an alternative political path, reflecting a mixed but moderate populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports redistribution and social equality (left), defends state unity and cultural identity (right), and calls for efficient administration without political bias (centrist), indicating a moderate centrist populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The text lacks any explicit populist ideological content, left, right, or centrist.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are combined with a center-right national-conservative ideology that emphasizes traditional values, national security, and regional historical-cultural identities.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on administrative efficiency and citizen service without strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on mainstream conservative-liberal policy proposals.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions judicial independence and rule of law, opposes gender discrimination in education, advocates maximum market competition and deregulation, and explicitly calls for free capital flows, capital-market deepening, and reduced state intervention in finance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently advocates free markets, privatisation, deregulation, administrative reduction, gender equality, constitutional rule of law, and market-based financial instruments (mortgage market, secondary market for mortgages), placing it firmly in the classical-liberal/ordo-liberal tradition across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is clearly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions constitutional democracy and NATO/EU membership for liberty, social integration of minorities, labour-market liberalisation and deficit reduction, and free movement of capital and goods within the single market.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports judicial independence, non-discrimination in education, market competition, and financial market development, indicating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto advocates for effective, less bureaucratic administration, equality of opportunity, reduction of administrative intervention, and support for private enterprise, reflecting moderate classical liberal principles.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports political freedoms, consumer protection, free trade, and economic competitiveness within the European Community, indicating a pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including judicial independence, free-market competition, deregulation, and the free movement of capital.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including market-based solutions, deregulation, privatization, and non-discriminatory social equality.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal principles, advocating for free markets, the free movement of capital, and the reduction of administrative intervention.
