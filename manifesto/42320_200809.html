WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate populism: the ÖVP leadership is framed as self-serving and destructive ("Zerstörungsstrategie"), and the SPÖ positions itself as champion of ordinary Austrians' concerns, but the rhetoric remains within normal party-political competition rather than a full people-vs.-corrupt-elite frame.

chunk 2 · The text shows only mild populist features: it occasionally invokes the people's interests and criticises the conservative-led EU, but does so in a programmatic, institutional register without strong elite-bashing or Manichean framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto criticizes the elite (ÖVP leadership) as dismissive of electoral outcomes and obstructive, invokes the people (Austrians) as central, and frames political conflict in moral terms, indicating moderate populist elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralized good-vs-evil struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits mild populism, primarily targeting the political opponent (ÖVP) as self-serving and destructive, while framing the SPÖ as the true representative of the Austrian people's concerns.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism by advocating for direct citizen participation and protecting public media from political party influence, but lacks a moralizing good-versus-evil narrative.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist flavor is clearly left-leaning, emphasizing redistribution, income and distributional justice, workers' rights, anti-speculation measures, and social equality, with only marginal nativist elements (EU transition rules for labor market access).

chunk 2 · The dominant ideological flavour is centre-left: the text calls for tax harmonisation to stop wage and tax dumping, supports the European Social Model, and advocates redistribution via a financial-transaction tax, with only marginal right-populist signals around the Turkey referendum.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text emphasizes income and distributional justice (left), demands integration with conditions (right), and stresses constructive cooperation without extreme polarization (centrist), reflecting a mixed but moderate populist ideology.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit discussion of left, right, or centrist populist ideological themes in the text, thus no evidence of populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are strongly embedded in a social-democratic, left-wing ideology that emphasizes wealth redistribution, labor rights, and public services over corporate interests.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are framed from a social-democratic left perspective, emphasizing tax harmonization, a financial transaction tax, and opposition to neoliberal deregulation.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is moderately liberal on political and social dimensions (strengthening parliament, anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ partnership rights, media freedom) but economically interventionist (price controls, state ownership of key enterprises, financial transaction tax, opposition to liberalization as an end in itself), yielding a mixed overall liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The text is moderately liberal politically (press freedom, democratic participation, rule of law) and socially (anti-discrimination), but economically mixed—it explicitly rejects liberalisation as an end in itself and proposes a financial-transaction tax, pulling the financial-market score down.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights including minority protections, fair economic conditions fostering innovation, and financial market reforms enabling EU financing, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through media freedom, social liberalism via workers' rights and anti-dumping measures, economic liberalism with cautious stance on liberalization, and financial market liberalism by proposing a financial transaction tax alongside EU financing reforms.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political and social liberalism (minority rights, democratic reforms) but rejects economic liberalism in favor of state intervention, public ownership, and financial transaction taxes.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political liberalism and media freedom, but takes a highly critical stance on economic liberalization and advocates for financial market regulations like transaction taxes.
