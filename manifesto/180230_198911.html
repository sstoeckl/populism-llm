WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames Brazilian history as a struggle between a corrupt, self-serving elite (military, landowners, financial capitalists) and the virtuous working people, invoking Lula's personal trajectory as a metalworker-turned-candidate to embody the people's cause, and portraying every political institution as captured by dominant minorities against the popular majority.

chunk 2 · The manifesto exhibits very high populism across all three sub-dimensions: it frames elites (industrial, financial, agrarian) as corrupt exploiters of workers, invokes 'the people' and the working class as the virtuous collective subject, and constructs a stark moral binary between the popular masses and the dominant class, describing state policy as "antinacional e antipopular."

chunk 3 · The manifesto consistently frames Brazilian elites and landowners as corrupt exploiters of the people, invokes 'the people' and 'workers' as a unified virtuous bloc, and casts the political struggle in Manichean terms of defeating capitalism and building socialism, scoring very high on all three populism sub-dimensions.

chunk 4 · The text consistently frames a corrupt elite (landowners, bankers, oligarchies, military-state) as exploiting and betraying 'the people' (rural workers, camponeses, sertanejos), with a clear moral binary between oppressors and the virtuous popular masses, exemplified by quotes about public money filling elite pockets and workers' collective strength determining their own fate.

chunk 5 · The text consistently frames elites (construction industry, financial agents, real estate capital, reactionary employers) as corrupt exploiters of the working people, invokes 'o povo' as a unified virtuous actor, and draws a sharp moral contrast between exploiters and the exploited working class, including explicit references to 'exploradores e especuladores' threatening democracy.

chunk 6 · The manifesto exhibits strong populism across all three sub-dimensions: it frames capitalist elites and the state as corrupt and murderous ("ganância capitalista, a criminosa ansiedade por lucros"), invokes "the people" and workers as the virtuous collective whose wealth has been stolen, and constructs a Manichean struggle between the exploited majority and the elite that manages national and international capital interests.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is firmly left-populist: it calls for redistribution of income, suspension of foreign debt payments, opposition to neoliberalism and the IMF, workers' participation in state enterprises, land reform, and explicitly frames the struggle as workers and the poor against financial capitalists and landed elites, with a stated orientation toward a socialist future.

chunk 2 · The populism is unambiguously left-wing: the manifesto explicitly calls for building a "sociedade socialista," advocates worker control over the means of production, redistribution of income, suspension of IMF agreements, nationalization of banks, and frames the struggle as one between workers and capitalists.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is unambiguously left-populist: it explicitly calls for building a socialist society, worker control of production, land reform, nationalization of banks, suspension of IMF agreements, and redistribution of wealth, with no right-wing nativist or centrist-technocratic framing.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is strongly left-populist: it calls for land reform, redistribution of wealth and power, nationalization of natural resources, workers' rights, and explicitly opposes multinational corporations, private capital subsidies, and oligarchic elites — hallmarks of left-wing populism with no significant right-wing nationalist or anti-immigration content.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is unambiguously left-populist: it explicitly declares socialism as the goal, calls for redistribution of income, land reform, nationalization of strategic sectors, workers' control over funds, and frames the struggle as working people versus capitalist elites and financial agents who exploit state resources.

chunk 6 · The populism is firmly left-wing: the manifesto explicitly advocates socialism as the historical alternative to capitalism, calls for nationalization of private health services and strategic industries, champions redistribution of wealth to workers, and frames the struggle as one of the working class against capitalist elites and multinational corporations.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores high on political liberalism (strong pro-democracy, civil liberties, rule of law, civilian supremacy) and moderately on social liberalism (racial inequality noted, workers' rights championed), but scores low on economic and financial-market liberalism, explicitly opposing privatization, IMF conditionality, and foreign debt service, and advocating state control of key industries and suspension of international financial agreements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal across economic and financial dimensions, advocating state control over banks (including nationalization), suspension of privatization, capital controls, state-directed investment, and debt moratorium; it shows mixed political liberalism by endorsing democracy and civil participation while also calling for socialist transformation that would subordinate market mechanisms to state planning.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal economically and financially—advocating state planning, nationalization, capital controls, and suspension of external debt payments—while showing moderate pro-liberal stances on social equality (women, race) and mixed signals on political democracy (endorsing elections and rights but also worker-controlled state power).

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports democratic participation and citizenship rights (moderately pro-liberal politically and socially), but is strongly interventionist economically — advocating state control of natural resources, land redistribution, regulation of agro-industry, and criticizing market-based housing finance — yielding a mixed-to-low overall liberalism score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto supports political democracy and anti-discrimination (moderately liberal on political and social dimensions) but is strongly anti-liberal economically: it advocates socialism, state control over financial flows, nationalization of strategic sectors, elimination of private financial intermediaries, and suspension of external debt payments, scoring very low on economic and financial-market liberalism.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it strongly supports civil rights, gender equality, and racial non-discrimination (pro-liberal socially), and endorses democratic institutions with human rights protections (moderately pro-liberal politically), but is explicitly anti-liberal economically and financially, advocating state nationalization of private services, condemning capitalist accumulation, and framing international finance (bankers, multinationals) as exploitative.
