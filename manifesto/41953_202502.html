WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto exhibits moderate populism: it frames citizens as virtuous and beleaguered by "bürgerferne Politik" and a "linker Zeitgeist," casts establishment parties and EU elites as responsible for Germany's decline, and uses some good-vs-evil framing (e.g., "ökosozialistischen Klimaschützer"), but the dominant register is technocratic policy detail rather than pure populist mobilization.

chunk 2 · The text consistently frames a corrupt, self-serving political class ("politische Klasse") as working against "the people" (Volk as sovereign), portrays climate and migration policy as elite-driven conspiracies against ordinary citizens, and invokes a Manichean good-vs-evil struggle between the people and establishment elites/NGOs/international organizations.

chunk 3 · The text consistently frames established parties and EU elites as corrupt and power-hungry ("Parteibuchwirtschaft", "Selbstbedienung der Parteien"), invokes "das Volk" as the sovereign moral authority, and portrays the political establishment in a Manichean good-vs-evil struggle against ordinary citizens.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes 'bürgerferne Politik' and government 'bewusste Panikmache', while promoting 'selbstbewusste und kritische Bürger', indicating moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and a moralized struggle narrative, supporting a moderate populism score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes a political elite pursuing ideological agendas and self-preservation, framing a moral struggle against them and emphasizing the people's distrust, indicating strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean populism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes EU elites and emphasizes direct popular sovereignty and lawmaking rights, reflecting strong anti-elitism and people-centrism, with some moralized critique of EU institutions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits strong populism by framing the political establishment as out-of-touch and corrupt, contrasting them with the virtuous, hard-working citizens who are suffering under their policies.

chunk 2 · The text strongly frames the political class as a corrupt elite destroying the nation, while championing the virtuous people as the true sovereign.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits strong populism by framing the political and European elites as corrupt, self-serving, and detached, while demanding direct democratic power for 'the people' to control them.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is clearly right-leaning: the manifesto combines anti-immigration rhetoric (restricting Bürgergeld for foreigners, limiting migration to skilled workers), national-identity protectionism (Euro exit, national sovereignty over finance and energy), and opposition to multicultural/gender-progressive policies, while simultaneously advocating free-market economics rather than left-populist redistribution.

chunk 2 · The text is strongly right-wing populist: it combines nativist anti-immigration rhetoric (Remigration, mass deportations, restricting citizenship), national sovereignty against EU/supranational bodies, climate-change denial framed as elite conspiracy, and explicit hostility to "linksgrüne Ideologen," with no left-populist redistribution or anti-corporate elite framing.

chunk 3 · The populist appeals are firmly right-wing: national sovereignty, cultural identity ("deutsche Leitkultur"), opposition to immigration, rejection of EU supranationalism, and traditional family values dominate, with no redistributive or anti-corporate left-wing framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates for limiting immigration to qualified workers and emphasizes individual responsibility, while opposing excessive bureaucracy, reflecting a right-leaning populist ideology with some centrist elements and low left-wing populism.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports national sovereignty and rejects EU centralization, while criticizing leftist ideologies and globalism, reflecting a right-leaning populist ideology with some centrist appeals.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports national sovereignty and critiques supranational EU integration, aligning with right-wing populist ideology, while rejecting left-wing redistributionist policies like anti-discrimination laws; no centrist populism evident.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is heavily right-wing, focusing on national identity, anti-immigration rhetoric, and opposition to left-wing cultural and environmental policies.

chunk 2 · The populist appeal is heavily combined with right-wing nationalist and anti-immigration policies, specifically calling for a comprehensive 'Remigration' program.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, focusing heavily on protecting national sovereignty, preserving traditional family structures, and securing borders against illegal immigration.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is economically liberal (deregulation, tax cuts, market competition, free enterprise) but socially conservative (banning gender-affirming care for minors, opposing cannabis legalization, restricting immigration), and shows mixed signals on political liberalism (defending free speech while opposing EU digital regulation and WHO authority); financial-market liberalism is mixed, combining pro-market rhetoric with demands for Euro exit and national banking sovereignty.

chunk 2 · The text shows a mixed liberal profile: it supports free markets, Bitcoin deregulation, and cash freedom (pro-liberal), but rejects anti-discrimination laws, opposes minority protections, advocates state control of water infrastructure, and proposes extensive restrictions on civil liberties for migrants and Muslims (anti-liberal), yielding an overall moderate-to-low liberalism score.

chunk 3 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it champions free speech, judicial independence, market primacy over state planning, and contract freedom, but strongly opposes anti-discrimination laws, LGBTQ+ rights, EU integration, and gender equality measures, yielding a below-average overall liberal score.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports the social market economy, democratic rights, and economic liberalism including tax simplification and market competition, while showing moderate support for financial market liberalization and cautious social liberalism, resulting in a moderate overall liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows limited support for classical liberalism, advocating direct election of the president and deregulation of Bitcoin, but opposes many liberal social policies and EU-driven economic regulations, resulting in a low overall liberalism score.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports rule of law reforms and judicial independence, opposes state intervention in markets and subsidies, rejects anti-discrimination laws, and promotes free market principles, indicating moderate classical liberalism with some reservations.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports classical liberal economic principles, deregulation, and private capital markets, while opposing social liberalism regarding gender identity and state-led environmental regulations.

chunk 2 · The text shows mixed stances on liberalism, supporting free-market principles and deregulation of Bitcoin, but strongly rejecting anti-discrimination laws and state-backed digital currencies.

chunk 3 · The text shows a mixed stance on liberalism, defending free speech and private contract autonomy while strongly opposing anti-discrimination laws, gender equality measures, and state-backed digital currencies.
