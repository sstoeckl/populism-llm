WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist features: the right-wing governing elite is briefly framed as responsible for Spain's problems, and "the people" are invoked as the source of democratic legitimacy, but the overall tone is programmatic and technocratic rather than morally Manichean.

chunk 2 · The text is a detailed technocratic policy manifesto with minimal populist rhetoric; references to 'citizens' and 'the people' are incidental to policy delivery rather than moralized people-vs-elite framing, and the single mild anti-elite reference targets 'monopolies and privileges' in passing.

chunk 3 · The text is a mainstream social-democratic government programme with minimal populist content: it briefly criticises the historical tendency of Spanish political power to become self-serving, but frames this as a historical observation rather than a Manichean elite-vs-people struggle, and consistently addresses citizens through institutional channels rather than invoking a unified virtuous "people."

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the previous ruling elite as responsible for Spain's problems and lacking conviction, while emphasizing the people's trust in PSOE to bring change, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and some moralized struggle.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mild anti-elitism and people-centrism through references to corporativism opposing society and guaranteeing minimum prosperity for all citizens, but lacks strong moral dichotomies.

chunk 3 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, thus no evidence of populism is found.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits very low populism, framing political differences in terms of standard democratic competition and policy choices rather than a moralized struggle between a corrupt elite and a pure people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits very low populism, only occasionally referencing 'the Spanish people' and warning against corporate interest groups pressuring the state.

chunk 3 · The text contains very minimal populist rhetoric, focusing instead on institutional reforms, with only minor references to avoiding corrupt practices and invoking the collective effort of the Spanish people.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist flavour is predominantly left-leaning, emphasising redistribution, social equality, workers' rights, and criticism of the capitalist growth model, with only marginal cultural-nationalist references and no significant right-populist identity politics.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is a mainstream social-democratic (centre-left) programme emphasising redistribution, social welfare, and equality, with very weak populist ideological flavour overall; the mild left-populist elements (redistribution, workers' rights) are embedded in technocratic policy language rather than mobilising anti-elite rhetoric.

chunk 3 · The limited populist flavour that exists leans mildly left, emphasising material equality before the law, protection of weaker social sectors, and redistribution through social services, while right-populist identity or anti-immigration themes are entirely absent.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text shows a left-leaning populist ideology through emphasis on solidarity, social equality, and redistribution, combined with centrist appeals to the people's trust and political change, but no right-wing populist themes.

chunk 2 · The text emphasizes redistribution and social participation, reflecting a left-leaning populist ideology with centrist elements in social integration and participation.

chunk 3 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the text; the manifesto focuses on institutional reforms and governance without populist rhetoric.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are mild and clearly aligned with a social-democratic left-wing ideology that emphasizes wealth redistribution, public services, and solidarity.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are framed from a social-democratic left perspective, focusing on overcoming inequalities generated by the system.

chunk 3 · There is no distinct populist ideology present, as the text is a mainstream social-democratic program focusing on institutional modernization rather than populist division.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political (democracy consolidation, rule of law) and social (pluralism, privacy rights) dimensions, while taking a mixed stance on economic liberalism—supporting market competition and private initiative alongside strong state intervention, public enterprise, and social concertation.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it affirms democratic rights and rule of law, promotes gender equality and minority inclusion, supports market competition and financial market modernisation under transparency and competition principles, while also endorsing state intervention in energy and social sectors consistent with a social-democratic mixed-economy approach.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions a completed system of rights and freedoms comparable to Western Europe, defends human rights and democratic consolidation, calls for removing bureaucratic obstacles to enterprise, and frames administrative modernisation and fiscal coordination as enabling market and civic activity rather than substituting for it.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic consolidation, social rights, economic modernization, and prudent fiscal policies, indicating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial market dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports political rights and democracy, social equality and education, economic competitiveness, and modernized financial markets, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly emphasizes democratic governance, rule of law, citizen rights, administrative modernization, and economic facilitation, with verified quotes on constitutional development, citizen rights, and reducing bureaucratic barriers, indicating a pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports democratic institutions, pluralism, and civil rights, while advocating a mixed economy that combines private enterprise with state guidance and social welfare.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social rights, while advocating for a mixed economy with increased financial market competition alongside state regulation.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles of constitutionalism, judicial reform, individual rights, and reducing bureaucratic barriers for private enterprises.
