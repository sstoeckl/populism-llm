WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames Croatian society as a struggle between the virtuous working majority ("radna većina") and a corrupt elite of oligarchs, tycoons, and foreign capital, using strong Manichean language about those at the top refusing to relinquish their privileges at the expense of the people.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently frames neoliberalism and capitalist elites as corrupt oppressors of the popular majority, invoking a sharp people-vs-elite dichotomy throughout, as evidenced by phrases like "bogatstvo elita s druge" and "Biti za narodnu većinu, a ne bogatu elitu."

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly frames national and global political-economic elites as the root cause of societal problems, invokes 'the people' (workers, women, migrants) as a unified virtuous group, and draws a sharp Manichean contrast between capital/profit logic and the common good, scoring consistently high on all three populism sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · Manifesto explicitly criticizes the capitalist elite's wealth concentration, emphasizes the oppressed majority as 'the people', and frames societal struggle as a conflict between the privileged elite and the working majority, supporting a populist stance.

chunk 2 · The text explicitly criticizes elites as wealthy and responsible for inequality and exploitation, emphasizing the need to support the 'narodnu većinu' (the people) against elites, framing a moral struggle against neoliberal and right-wing forces.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes political-economic elites as the true culprits and emphasizes the power of 'the people' including women, framing a moral struggle against elites, demonstrating strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean populist elements.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text frames politics as a struggle between the 'working majority' and a corrupt 'privatization-tycoon capital and political oligarchy'.

chunk 2 · The text strongly frames politics as a struggle between the wealthy capitalist elites and the working-class majority.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits strong anti-elitism by framing political and economic elites as the true culprits behind societal problems.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is unambiguously left-populist: it calls for a global anti-capitalist movement, nationalization of strategic industries, workers' self-management, redistribution from capital to labour, and explicitly rejects right-wing nationalism and centrist technocratic populism.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is firmly left-wing, explicitly advocating for "demokratski socijalizam," workers' control, renationalization of privatized enterprises, and redistribution against corporate and capitalist elites, while explicitly rejecting right-wing nationalism and centrist technocracy.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly left-populist: it advocates socialism as the solution to ecological crisis, frames capitalism as the systemic enemy, calls for feminist-socialist policies, socialization of reproductive labour, and anti-capitalist redistribution, with no right-wing or nativist populist content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates left-wing populist ideology with emphasis on social equality and democratic planning, rejects right-wing nationalism, and calls for centrist internationalist policies, reflecting a mixed but predominantly left populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports left-wing populist ideology advocating democratic socialism, social ownership, and solidarity with the people, while criticizing right-wing nationalism and centrist technocracy as inadequate or harmful.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports left-wing populist themes such as anti-exploitation feminism and solidarity with the oppressed, while also acknowledging right-wing populist phenomena like rising xenophobia, and centrist populist appeals to democratic participation by 'the people'.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is explicitly radical left, advocating for the overthrow of capitalism and the socialization of production.

chunk 2 · The populist rhetoric is explicitly left-wing, focusing on anti-capitalism, anti-neoliberalism, and democratic socialism.

chunk 3 · The text advocates for a strongly left-wing socialist-feminist ideology, explicitly rejecting right-wing xenophobia and capitalism.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly anti-liberal across all dimensions: it advocates nationalization, workers' self-management, capital controls, protectionism, and state-directed industrial policy, while explicitly condemning neoliberal liberalization, privatization, deregulation, and foreign bank ownership as tools of elite exploitation.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly anti-liberal across economic and financial dimensions, condemning deregulation, liberalization, privatization, and private pension funds as tools of elite enrichment; it shows moderate pro-liberal stances only on political democracy and gender equality.

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores mixed on liberalism overall: it supports democratic participation, LGBTIQ rights, and open access to knowledge (pro-liberal), but is explicitly anti-market, anti-capitalist, and anti-free-trade in economic and financial dimensions, advocating state/social control over production and distribution.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto critiques neoliberal economic policies, privatization, and foreign bank ownership, while supporting international solidarity and progressive political rights, indicating a low to moderate liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The text critiques neoliberal economic policies such as deregulation, liberalization, and privatization, while supporting democratic political control, social rights including feminist policies, but shows skepticism towards current economic and financial market liberalism due to privatization and financial speculation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto advocates for political liberalism through democratic participation and rights, social liberalism via feminist and minority rights, shows mixed economic liberalism by criticizing capitalist profit motives, and lacks explicit financial market discussion.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly rejects classical liberalism, advocating for nationalization, state interventionism, and strict controls over financial markets.

chunk 2 · The text strongly rejects economic and financial liberalism (privatization, deregulation, and private banking) while supporting democratic control and social equality.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports social and political rights (such as abortion and democratic participation) but rejects economic liberalism and market mechanisms.
