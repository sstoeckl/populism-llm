WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly invokes the Québécois people as a unified virtuous group whose democratic will is systematically betrayed by the corrupt Liberal federal elite, framing the conflict in moralized good-vs-evil terms (sponsorship scandal, patronage, "trahison") while positioning the Bloc as the people's defender against Ottawa's anti-democratic practices.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism: the federal Liberal government is repeatedly framed as serving corporate/oil interests ("quelques amis du régime") at the expense of ordinary Québécois taxpayers and workers, and Quebec is cast as the virtuous "meilleur élève" victimized by a corrupt federal establishment, though the rhetoric is primarily regionalist rather than fully populist.

chunk 3 · The manifesto exhibits moderate populism: it frames the Liberal government as corrupt and serving elite friends ("quelques amis du régime") at the expense of ordinary workers and citizens, invokes the Québécois people as a virtuous collective deserving protection, and uses moralized language ("pillage éhonté") to depict the federal government's misappropriation of employment insurance funds as a betrayal of workers.

chunk 4 · The text repeatedly frames the Liberal government of Paul Martin as corrupt and self-serving (pillaging employment insurance surpluses, enabling tax havens for the wealthy), contrasts this with the interests of ordinary Québécois workers, and uses moralized language ("pillage éhonté," "intolérable") characteristic of moderate populism.

chunk 5 · The text frames the federal government as systematically neglecting Quebec's interests—pillaging employment insurance funds, ignoring regional needs, and favouring other provinces—while invoking Quebecers as a unified people deserving better, producing moderate populist scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 6 · The text invokes the Québécois people as a unified virtuous group whose interests only the Bloc can defend, criticises elites (rich countries, neoliberal governments, Paul Martin) for serving narrow interests, and frames globalization as a moral struggle between exploiters and the exploited, yielding moderate populist scores across all three sub-dimensions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is a hybrid: it combines left-leaning elements (fiscal redistribution, social transfers, environmental regulation against corporate polluters) with strong ethno-nationalist/sovereigntist identity politics centred on Québec nationhood, making it neither purely left nor purely right but predominantly a Quebec-nationalist populism with left-leaning economic overtones.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is a blend of left (anti-corporate, anti-oil industry subsidies, worker protections) and right/identitarian (Quebec cultural sovereignty, national identity protection), with the dominant frame being Quebec nationalist rather than a pure left or right populism.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is predominantly left-leaning: the manifesto calls for redistribution of wealth, protection of workers (seasonal, elderly, indigenous), surtaxes on oil company profits, and social programs, while also containing a right-leaning nationalist/protectionist strand (preferring domestic over foreign suppliers, defending Québécois identity and language).

chunk 4 · The populist appeals are predominantly left-leaning, emphasizing wealth redistribution, workers' rights (anti-scab legislation, employment insurance reform), pay equity, social housing, and opposition to corporate tax avoidance, with no significant right-wing nativist or anti-immigration framing.

chunk 5 · The populist ideology is primarily left-leaning (demands for agricultural reinvestment, employment insurance reform, worker support, supply management protection) combined with a strong Quebec-nationalist/autonomist strand, with minimal right-populist identity framing, yielding a moderate left-populist overall score.

chunk 6 · The text is predominantly left-populist: it advocates wealth redistribution, a social development fund, reform of the IMF to include human/social rights, fair trade, and criticism of neoliberal elites and large oil corporations, with a secondary Quebec-nationalist identity dimension but no significant right-populist nativist or anti-immigration content.

chunk 6 · The text shows left-leaning populist ideology through critique of federal resource taxation favoring oil over mining, some right-leaning protectionism in regional market support, and centrist populist appeals to Quebec identity and democratic participation.

Gemini

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is left-leaning, focusing on the protection of taxpayers against big corporate polluters and advocating for public investments and social programs.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on taxing the excess profits of large oil corporations and protecting workers and small local businesses from corporate elites.

chunk 4 · The populist discourse is strongly left-wing, focusing on wealth redistribution, social justice, and protecting workers against corporate and political elites.

chunk 5 · The populist elements are centrist and regionalist, focusing on defending Quebec's regional interests against the federal establishment.

chunk 6 · The populist ideology is left-leaning, focusing on combating the excesses of globalization, tax havens, and corporate greed, particularly targeting oil companies.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it strongly champions democratic institutions, transparency, rule of law, and parliamentary oversight (pro-liberal), while also advocating state intervention in energy markets, surtaxes on oil companies, and fiscal redistribution mechanisms that temper economic liberalism, yielding a moderate overall liberal score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports trade openness and market mechanisms (Kyoto credit markets, Montreal Exchange), opposes discriminatory regulations, and defends individual rights (student discrimination), but also advocates extensive state intervention, subsidies, protectionist measures for textiles/furniture, and government-directed industrial policy, yielding a middling overall liberal score.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports trade openness and free commerce in principle but conditions it on social and labour standards; it opposes bank mergers and advocates surtaxes on oil companies and tighter pharmaceutical regulation, reflecting a preference for state intervention over market-based solutions, while simultaneously defending individual rights, due process for refugees, and democratic institutions.

chunk 4 · The text shows mixed liberal signals: it defends civil liberties against anti-terror overreach and opposes discriminatory labour rules (pro-liberal), supports some tax cuts (pro-liberal), but also advocates heavy state intervention in housing, employment insurance, and closing tax-haven loopholes, yielding a middling overall liberalism score.

chunk 5 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it supports free trade (NAFTA enforcement, free-trade agreement with EU) and democratic accountability (parliamentary approval of treaties), but also defends supply management (protectionist quotas and tariffs), state-directed regional development, and government loan guarantees, producing middling scores across economic and financial dimensions.

chunk 6 · The text is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (democratic oversight, rule of law, human rights), mixed on economic liberalism (endorsing trade openness but insisting on social/environmental constraints), and moderately anti-liberal on financial markets (targeting tax havens, imposing surtaxes on oil companies, advocating IMF reform to subordinate financial decisions to human rights criteria).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto affirms democratic parliamentary traditions, minority representation, economic development through free enterprise like hydroelectric power, and criticizes federal financial opacity and mismanagement, supporting liberal political, social, economic, and financial market principles.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports political rights and regional autonomy, defends cultural diversity, criticizes federal interference in education and research, and calls for economic policies favoring free enterprise and innovation, indicating moderate liberalism overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports political liberalism through demands for respect of language rights and bilingualism, social liberalism via support for families and minorities, economic liberalism by opposing bank mergers and supporting cooperative fiscal measures, and financial market liberalism by calling for fair procurement policies, indicating an overall moderate liberal stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports democratic rights and freedoms, social equality (noting ongoing gender pay disparities), economic concerns about federal underinvestment, and criticizes financial practices like tax havens, reflecting a moderate liberal stance overall.

chunk 5 · The manifesto supports democratic oversight of international treaties, cultural diversity, regional economic incentives, and market-friendly economic policies, but lacks discussion on financial market regulation, indicating a moderately pro-liberal overall stance.

chunk 6 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions and treaty approval processes, social rights and protections, economic openness balanced with social/environmental concerns, and financial market reforms targeting tax havens, indicating a moderately pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly defends democratic institutions, parliamentary democracy, and historical civil rights milestones like the early election of a Jewish representative.

chunk 2 · The text shows a mixed stance on liberalism, supporting international trade and market-based carbon credits while simultaneously demanding protectionist measures, subsidies, and state interventions for local industries.

chunk 3 · The text shows strong support for democratic rights and civil liberties, but takes a highly interventionist, protectionist stance on economic and financial matters, opposing bank mergers and foreign competition.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports democratic rights, civil liberties, and gender equality, but leans interventionist on economic matters by advocating for strict labor regulations and anti-strike-breaker laws.

chunk 5 · The text supports classical liberal principles by advocating for democratic oversight of international treaties and promoting free trade agreements.

chunk 6 · The text supports democratic oversight and human rights, but advocates for strong state regulation of trade and international financial institutions to ensure social justice.
