WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto of ATAKA is maximally populist: it frames all post-1997 governments as traitors serving foreign colonial masters against a virtuous Bulgarian people, draws a stark Manichean divide between "the good" (nationalists/the people) and "the bad" (foreign monopolists, domestic elites, and their political servants), and relentlessly invokes the Bulgarian people as a unified, exploited, morally superior collective.

chunk 2 · The manifesto scores very high on populism across all three sub-dimensions: it frames elites, oligarchs, the IMF, and successive governments as corrupt exploiters of the Bulgarian people, invokes "the Bulgarian people" as a unified virtuous group deserving state protection, and constructs a Manichean narrative of colonial enslavement versus national liberation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto exhibits clear populist features: elites and past governments are labelled "national traitors" who destroyed state institutions, the people/nation are invoked as the rightful sovereign over natural resources and defence, and a stark moral dichotomy is drawn between corrupt ruling elites and the Bulgarian people.

chunk 4 · The manifesto (Ataka party, Bulgaria 2013) exhibits strong populism: it frames successive governments as "national traitors" imposing "colonial slavery" and "genocide" on the Bulgarian people, casts ordinary citizens as virtuous victims of corrupt elites and foreign monopolies, and employs a stark good-vs-evil narrative throughout.

chunk 5 · The text is strongly populist: it frames successive governments as "national traitors" imposing "colonial slavery," portrays financial elites ("turbo-capitalists") as conspiring to enslave Bulgaria in debt, and calls for the state budget to serve "the Bulgarian people" rather than "western colonizers," combining intense anti-elitism, people-centrism, and Manichean good-vs-evil framing throughout.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is overwhelmingly right-wing nationalist: the manifesto champions ethnic Bulgarian identity, attacks Islamic fundamentalism and Turkish influence, demands repatriation of ethnic Bulgarians, and frames foreign capital and immigration as existential threats to the Bulgarian nation, while also incorporating some left-leaning demands for social justice and state ownership of resources.

chunk 2 · The manifesto blends right-wing populism (strong ethnic/national framing, anti-Roma rhetoric, protectionism) with left-wing populist elements (nationalization of utilities, state healthcare, anti-IMF/World Bank stance, redistribution), making it primarily right-populist with significant left-populist economic content.

chunk 3 · The populism is predominantly right-wing nationalist: the manifesto explicitly frames education in a "nationalist vision," promotes ethnic-Bulgarian identity myths, calls for mandatory Orthodox religious instruction, demands withdrawal from NATO, and advocates state ownership of all natural resources under a nationalist rationale, with only minor anti-monopoly rhetoric suggesting left-leaning elements.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is a blend of left (anti-corporate exploitation, wage inequality, state ownership of transport) and right (ethnic framing of crime via "Romani gangs," national identity, anti-immigration undertones), with the right-nationalist strand slightly dominant, yielding a mixed but right-leaning populist profile.

chunk 5 · ATAKA combines right-wing nationalist populism (restricting foreign labour, Bulgarian ethnic identity preferences, anti-EU/euro-zone sovereignty rhetoric) with left-wing economic populism (nationalization of strategic sectors, progressive taxation, redistribution, state-led development), making it a radical-right nationalist party with significant left-economic elements, scoring high on both dimensions.

chunk 2 · The text combines left-leaning anti-corruption and social welfare themes with right-leaning nationalistic and anti-minority crime rhetoric, alongside centrist appeals to serving all social layers, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 3 · The text combines patriotic education and cultural identity emphasis with anti-corruption and social justice elements, indicating a centrist to right-leaning populist ideology with some leftist anti-elite aspects.

chunk 4 · The text shows a left-leaning populist ideology by condemning exploitation by foreign companies and low wages, while also including centrist appeals to legal clarity and citizen rights, but lacks right-wing nationalist or anti-immigration rhetoric.

chunk 5 · The text supports left-wing populist economic redistribution and progressive taxation, right-wing restrictive immigration policies, and centrist appeals to the people against elites, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is deeply anti-liberal across all dimensions: it rejects free markets as a "colonial scheme," frames foreign banks and financial institutions as exploiters, dismisses democracy as a cover for colonization, and advocates strong state control of the economy, nationalization of resources, and exclusionary ethnic nationalism that is hostile to minority rights and pluralism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal across all dimensions: it explicitly rejects market-based healthcare, calls for nationalization of utilities and banks, advocates state-fixed drug prices, frames foreign investment as colonial exploitation, and uses ethnic exclusionary language that contradicts civil rights norms.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal across all dimensions: it advocates state monopoly over natural resources (anti-economic liberalism), state-directed interest-free credit via a state bank (anti-financial market liberalism), mandatory Orthodox religious education with restrictions on other faiths (anti-social liberalism), and a NATO referendum combined with anti-Western framing (mixed political liberalism).

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal: it opposes privatization of railways and transport, advocates majority state ownership of infrastructure firms, proposes taxes on banking transactions, and frames market outcomes as exploitation, while its judicial-reform proposals show only partial alignment with rule-of-law liberalism.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is strongly anti-liberal across all dimensions: it advocates nationalization of strategic sectors, state control of the central bank (citing Hungary as a model), progressive taxation replacing the flat tax, state-set interest rate caps on bank loans, protectionist trade measures, and explicitly condemns "neoliberal governments" and financial deregulation as responsible for Bulgaria's economic decline.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto criticizes violations of political rights, highlights social issues like crime, and condemns economic policies favoring foreign banks and privatization, indicating a largely anti-liberal stance on political, social, economic, and financial market dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows skepticism towards current political and economic institutions, highlighting lack of adequate legal frameworks, social inequalities, and market failures, especially in health care, indicating low liberalism scores; no evidence on financial market liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports constitutional political norms, social protections against organized crime, economic support for small and medium businesses, but lacks discussion on financial market liberalism, indicating moderate classical liberalism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto discusses political liberalism with references to equality before the law and constitutional rights, social liberalism through citizen rights protection, and economic liberalism by addressing monopolies and competition, but lacks evidence on financial market liberalism, resulting in a mixed overall liberalism score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto advocates for political accountability and public control of state finances, social policies supporting families, economic support for domestic production, and state intervention in banking, indicating moderate classical liberalism with some interventionist tendencies.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly rejects classical liberal principles, advocating for state control over the economy, opposing free trade, foreign investment, minority rights, and international financial institutions.

chunk 2 · The text explicitly rejects liberal market economics and displays strong hostility toward minority groups, advocating for state-controlled pricing and nationalization.

chunk 3 · The text rejects classical liberal principles by advocating for extensive state ownership of natural resources, state intervention in the economy, and strict state control over religious and educational institutions.

chunk 4 · The text rejects classical liberal principles by advocating for state-owned enterprises, financial transaction taxes, and restricting minority rights.

chunk 5 · The text strongly opposes classical liberal principles by advocating for state nationalization of industries, heavy regulation of bank interest rates, and discriminatory social policies.
