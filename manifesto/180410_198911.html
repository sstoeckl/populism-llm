WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The text exhibits strong populist features: Collor frames himself as the voice of the oppressed against entrenched oligarchies that have long dominated power, invokes a virtuous unified people against corrupt elites, and employs a moralized good-vs-evil narrative opposing honest citizens to corrupt marajás and speculators.

chunk 2 · The text contains mild populist undertones — criticizing rent-seeking "cartórios" and calling for broad societal mobilization — but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-oriented, addressed to business elites, with no strong people-vs-elite moral framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes entrenched oligarchies and elites, emphasizes representing the oppressed and humble people, and frames political struggle in moral terms, demonstrating strong populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto critiques elite privileges and cartel-like concessions, emphasizes collective societal tasks, and highlights state reform needs, indicating moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral framing.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits strong populism by framing the political struggle as the virtuous, oppressed people against corrupt, long-dominating oligarchies and 'marajás'.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild anti-elitism by criticizing state-protected cartels and bureaucratic privileges that block modernization, though it lacks strong moralizing or people-centric rhetoric.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is primarily left-leaning (anti-elite redistribution, social debt to citizens, workers as partners) with some right-leaning national sovereignty rhetoric, but the overall framing is a centrist-to-left modernizing nationalism rather than a purely ideological left or right populism.

chunk 2 · The speech is primarily a technocratic, market-oriented policy address to business leaders, with mild centrist populist appeals (consensus, societal unity) and no strong left or right populist ideological framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-leaning concerns about workers' rights and social justice with centrist appeals to action and right-leaning regional disparities, reflecting a mixed but overall moderate populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The text shows moderate left-leaning populist ideology through critique of financial speculation and state reform, combined with centrist appeals to society-wide responsibility, with no right-wing populist elements.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is primarily centrist to left-leaning, focusing on social justice, redistribution of wealth to the people, and anti-corruption without right-wing nationalist or anti-immigrant themes.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are centrist and technocratic, focusing on dismantling inefficient state-backed cartels to modernize the economy without clear left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic institutions and rule of law, advocates for minority rights and gender equality, promotes free enterprise and deregulation over state paternalism, and calls for freeing capital from financial speculation and reducing state interference in credit markets.

chunk 2 · The text is strongly pro-liberal economically — advocating competitive openness, private capital, deregulation of industrial policy, and freeing capital from financial speculation — while also endorsing democratic university governance and social inclusion through education.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, social equality, free enterprise, and prudent financial market roles, indicating a clear pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports democratic governance, social inclusion via education, economic competitiveness, and financial market reform enabling private investment, reflecting a generally pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing the essential role of free enterprise, private capital, and democratic institutions, while advocating for state reform to enable market efficiency.

chunk 2 · The text strongly advocates for classical liberal economic reforms, including international competition, private investment in infrastructure, and redirecting capital away from speculation into productive markets.
