WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · Živi zid exhibits very strong populism: it consistently frames HDZ/SDP and banking/corporate elites as corrupt oppressors of ordinary citizens ("pogodovanju krupnom kapitalu"), invokes 'the people' as a virtuous unified mass ("Nama je Čovjek važniji od kapitala"), and constructs a Manichean struggle between a "politička kasta potpuno odvojena od naroda" and suffering citizens.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently frames large corporations (Agrokor, banks, dairy lobbies) and the political establishment as corrupt exploiters of ordinary farmers, families, and students, while invoking "the people" (seljaci, obitelji, studenti) as virtuous victims, with moralized good-vs-evil framing ("Tajkuni reketare proizvođače").

chunk 3 · The manifesto consistently frames corrupt political elites, bankers, pharmaceutical corporations, and EU bureaucrats as enemies of the ordinary people (narod/građani), invoking a strong Manichean good-vs-evil struggle between the virtuous people and predatory elites.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes a political elite detached from the people, emphasizes the primacy of 'the people' and human dignity over capital, and frames politics as a moral struggle against injustice, demonstrating strong populist themes.

chunk 2 · Manifesto criticizes elite favoritism and advocates for the rights of common people such as small farmers and debtors, showing moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with moral framing of injustices like secret evictions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the political elite as part of the problem and emphasizes a unified people-centric political front, reflecting strong anti-elitism and people-centrism with a moralized struggle against corrupt power structures.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames politics as a struggle between a corrupt, detached political caste and the virtuous ordinary people who are being crushed by the system.

chunk 2 · The text strongly frames the political and economic elites, such as 'tycoons' and 'water mafia', as corrupt actors exploiting the common people.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits strong populism by framing political elites and international financial institutions as corrupt forces exploiting the virtuous, sovereign citizens.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The party's populism is predominantly left-leaning: it explicitly attacks neoliberalism, demands redistribution, monetary sovereignty, debt write-offs, higher minimum wages, and social protections, while rejecting both left and right labels in favour of "humanism"; there is minimal nativist or identity-based right-wing content.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is predominantly left-populist: it demands free public higher education, fully public healthcare, redistribution away from large corporations (Agrokor, banks) toward small farmers and vulnerable citizens, and explicitly rejects "poduzetnički kapitalizam"; protectionist elements (import quotas, currency devaluation) are secondary and framed in terms of protecting ordinary producers rather than national identity.

chunk 3 · The dominant populist ideology is left-leaning (anti-corporate, pro-nonprofit healthcare, anti-austerity, debt cancellation, solidarity), with some cross-cutting centrist "beyond left-right" framing and minor national-identity elements, placing it primarily in left-populist territory.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text rejects traditional left-right labels, promotes social equality and anti-elite economic views (left populism), rejects totalitarianism and discrimination (moderate right), and embraces a humanistic, centrist stance, reflecting a mixed but predominantly left-leaning populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The text combines left-wing populist themes of anti-corporate elite and social justice with some right-leaning protectionist stances like opposition to GMOs, and centrist appeals to legal fairness and people’s rights, reflecting a mixed populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The text advocates rejecting traditional left-right divisions to form a broad political front, showing centrist populist ideology with some left-leaning anti-elite and economic justice themes, but limited right-wing nationalist elements.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on anti-corporate, anti-bank, and anti-neoliberal rhetoric while advocating for social protection and redistribution.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is heavily left-wing, focusing on state expropriation of bank properties, anti-capitalist rhetoric, and protecting small farmers against large corporations.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is predominantly left-wing and anti-capitalist, targeting private banks, multinational corporations, and austerity, while rejecting traditional left-right divisions in favor of a unified front.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it endorses democratic equality and anti-discrimination principles but strongly opposes neoliberal free-market economics, advocates heavy state intervention in monetary and banking systems (capital controls, currency clause bans, nationalization of money creation), and frames media freedom as undermined by capital—yielding low scores on economic and financial-market liberalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal signals: it invokes constitutional equality and human rights (pro-liberal), but advocates state confiscation of bank-held properties, import quotas, currency manipulation, and full nationalization of healthcare and higher education, reflecting a predominantly anti-liberal economic and financial-market stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal economically and financially (opposing private banking, import liberalization, TTIP, and market-based healthcare), while showing partial support for political transparency and individual religious freedoms, resulting in a mixed-to-low overall liberalism score.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · While advocating democracy and equality before the law, the manifesto criticizes neoliberal economic policies and foreign bank dominance, showing limited support for classical liberal economic and financial market principles, resulting in a generally low to moderate liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports political liberalism through emphasis on rights and education, social liberalism via advocacy for disabled and vulnerable groups, but shows skepticism towards economic liberalism and financial market liberalism due to criticism of subsidies favoring big capital and opposition to commercialization of education.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports democracy, human rights, and religious freedoms, but criticizes EU economic policies and financial elites, reflecting moderate political and social liberalism but skepticism toward current economic and financial market liberalism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · While supporting basic democratic principles and non-discrimination, the text is highly hostile to classical liberal economics, advocating for heavy state intervention, price controls, and strict financial market regulations.

chunk 2 · While supporting constitutional equality and legal rights, the text strongly rejects free-market economics and financial market mechanisms, advocating for state intervention and expropriation.

chunk 3 · While supporting individual civil liberties like freedom of speech and religious freedom, the text strongly rejects economic liberalism and financial markets, advocating for protectionism, state-run non-profit systems, and the nationalization of key sectors.
