WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is moderately liberal on political rights (minors' legal rights, due process) and social equality (eliminating discrimination against non-legitimate children, inclusive family definitions), while explicitly rejecting a purely classical liberal view of the private sphere in favour of state intervention to support families; financial markets are not discussed.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports democratic participation and youth civic education (lib_political: moderate-high), references openness without discrimination (lib_social: moderate), and mentions globalisation and free markets critically as threats to social cohesion (lib_economic: mixed), while financial market policy is entirely absent.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political dimensions (democratic oversight, rule of law, judicial guarantees) and social dimensions (women's image, children's rights), while showing mixed signals on economic liberalism (criticising free-competition principles in audiovisual policy) and financial markets (regulating but also clarifying minors' access to banking services).

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports parliamentary ratification of international conventions and rule-of-law procedures (lib_political), promotes gender equality and protection of vulnerable groups (lib_social), explicitly rejects commodification of healthcare while defending regulated solidarity systems (lib_economic mixed), and proposes prudential oversight of hospital finances without advocating market-based pricing (lib_financial_market neutral-mixed).

chunk 5 · The manifesto shows mixed liberalism: it acknowledges market liberalisation and calls for democratic debate and individual rights, but simultaneously advocates strong state intervention, rejection of privatisation of social security, and extensive public regulation of markets and bioethics, placing it in a moderate, mixed-liberal position.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal in political terms (calling for democratic debate, rule of law, transparency), socially liberal (anti-discrimination, multicultural integration, voting rights for foreigners), and moderately economically liberal (reducing labor costs, encouraging enterprise training), while financial market policy is entirely absent from the text.

chunk 7 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions — strongly supporting democratic debate, rule of law, gender equality, minority rights, and anti-discrimination — while taking a mixed stance on economic and financial liberalism: it supports trade liberalisation for developing countries and financial transparency/prudential oversight, but also advocates capital controls on short-term flows, harmonised taxation, and regulation of financial markets to curb speculation.

chunk 8 · The manifesto is moderately liberal politically (endorsing democracy and human rights) and socially (anti-discrimination, gender equity in career breaks), moderately liberal economically (pro-enterprise, profit acceptance), but explicitly anti-liberal on financial markets through advocacy of capital controls on short-term flows and a dual exchange-rate system for developing countries, partially offset by support for prudential transparency and credit development.

chunk 9 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions entrepreneurial freedom, tax reduction, administrative simplification, market competition, financial transparency and credit access for SMEs, while also defending institutional independence and non-discrimination, reflecting a consistent ordo-liberal orientation.

chunk 10 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic participation, rule of law, judicial reform, and citizens' rights (lib_political high), affirms fundamental rights (lib_social moderate), proposes mixed economic regulation favouring small business while rejecting pure free-market logic in agriculture (lib_economic mixed), and advocates a regulatory body to prevent banking-fee abuses while enabling market competition (lib_financial_market moderate).

chunk 11 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political dimensions, championing rule of law, checks and balances, parliamentary control, minority rights, and democratic participation; economic and financial-market content is incidental and mixed, yielding a moderately high overall liberalism score driven primarily by the political and social dimensions.
