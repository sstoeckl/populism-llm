WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The text exhibits strong populism: the political elite (Fidesz and MSZP) are repeatedly condemned as thieves and liars who betrayed the virtuous Hungarian people, framed as a Manichean struggle between a colonial-minded corrupt establishment and the nation's true interests.

chunk 2 · The text frames past governments and foreign banks as corrupt elites who betrayed ordinary Hungarians ("globalista neoliberalizmus által szétvert"), invokes the Hungarian people as a unified virtuous group deserving decent livelihoods, and portrays the foreign-bank debt crisis as a moralized good-vs-evil struggle between predatory foreign capital and innocent Hungarian citizens.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits strong populism across all three sub-dimensions: elites (foreign banks, corrupt political class, multinationals) are framed as conspiring against the innocent Hungarian people; the people are invoked as a unified virtuous nation; and a Manichean good-vs-evil narrative pits honest Hungarians against exploitative foreign and domestic elites.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits strong populism: it frames all governing parties as having systematically destroyed Hungary through their "crimes," invokes the suffering Hungarian people as a unified virtuous group, and portrays multinational corporations and the political establishment as corrupt exploiters of ordinary Hungarians.

chunk 5 · The text is saturated with populist rhetoric: the established parties (MSZP and Fidesz) are portrayed as corrupt traitors who have jointly destroyed Hungary's countryside and public institutions, while Jobbik positions itself as the champion of ordinary Hungarians and the nation against a corrupt political elite and international lobbies.

chunk 6 · The text frames the Hungarian nation as a virtuous victim oppressed by corrupt elites (socialist and liberal governments, multinational corporations, a "balliberális kultúrkör"), invoking a moralized good-vs-evil narrative while centering the people's suffering, but the populist rhetoric is embedded within a detailed policy programme rather than being the dominant frame.

chunk 7 · The text repeatedly frames past governments and liberal-left cultural elites as corrupt betrayers of the Hungarian nation, invokes the Hungarian people and youth as a virtuous collective, and draws sharp moral contrasts between national-Christian values and a decadent "balliberális" establishment.

chunk 8 · The text repeatedly blames the past 24 years of governments for failures across transport, defence, and sport, frames the people as victims of elite mismanagement, and uses moralized language about "betrayal" of the nation (honfeladás), yielding moderate-to-high populism scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 9 · The text frames the established parties (Fidesz and MSZP) as corrupt elites who have deceived and robbed the people for 24 years, invokes the unified Hungarian people as the virtuous subject of politics, and draws a sharp moral binary between the corrupt establishment and Jobbik as the only honest alternative.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is predominantly right-wing, emphasizing national ownership of land and resources, economic sovereignty against multinational capital and the EU, and protection of Hungarian national identity, with secondary left-leaning anti-corporate rhetoric against multinationals exploiting Hungarian workers.

chunk 2 · The text is predominantly right-wing populist: it emphasizes national/ethnic protectionism (Hungarian land for Hungarians only, preference for Hungarian suppliers, anti-foreign-capital rhetoric), with secondary anti-corporate/anti-multinational elements that lean mildly left, but the dominant ideological flavor is ethno-national protectionism characteristic of the right.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is predominantly right-wing, combining ethno-national protectionism (anti-Roma rhetoric, land protection from foreigners, restricting voting rights) with some left-leaning redistributive elements (progressive income tax, debt renegotiation, utility renationalization), placing Jobbik firmly in the right-populist camp with secondary left-economic features.

chunk 4 · The dominant ideological flavor is right-wing populism: the text emphasizes national economic sovereignty, protection of ethnic Hungarian identity (including Carpathian Basin Hungarians), hostility to Western dependency, and anti-Roma rhetoric, while also containing some left-leaning redistributive elements (progressive taxation, worker protections) that are secondary.

chunk 5 · The populist ideology is overwhelmingly right-wing: the text invokes ethnic crime categories (cigánybűnözés), frames international organizations (TASZ, Helsinki, Amnesty) as enemy agents, calls for land protection from foreigners, promotes national self-sufficiency, and attacks liberal international lobbies — all hallmarks of right-wing populism.

chunk 6 · The populist ideology is predominantly right-wing, emphasising national-Christian identity, opposition to globalism and liberal cultural elites, and protection of Hungarian national interests; there are also some left-leaning elements (state renationalisation of energy, public healthcare funding) but these are secondary to the nationalist-identitarian framing.

chunk 7 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, centred on national-Christian identity, defence of Hungarian culture and language against globalist and liberal-left elites, with only marginal anti-capitalist rhetoric and no genuine centrist or left-populist programmatic content.

chunk 8 · The manifesto is predominantly right-wing populist: it emphasizes national identity, a national airline, anti-immigration (stopping hidden migration), ethnic Hungarian minority rights abroad, and Eurosceptic sovereignty, with only minor redistributive (left) elements such as criticism of health-spa access being limited to the wealthy.

chunk 9 · The populism is overwhelmingly right-wing: it centers on national sovereignty, protection of Hungarian land from foreigners, ethnic Hungarian minority rights abroad, anti-EU/NATO/IMF sovereignty rhetoric, and an ethno-nationalist foreign policy reorientation, with only marginal anti-corporate or redistributive framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly anti-liberal economically, explicitly rejecting neoliberalism, privatization, and market liberalization as failures, advocating state intervention, protectionism, national ownership, and cooperative banking, while offering only vague references to political accountability with no social liberalism content.

chunk 2 · The text consistently favors strong state intervention over market mechanisms—mandating state housing programs, preferring Hungarian suppliers over foreign ones, forcing conversion of foreign-currency loans at fixed rates, and restricting foreign land ownership—all of which are anti-liberal in the classical/ordo-liberal sense, though some market-enabling elements (competition in ICT, e-commerce promotion) temper the score slightly.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal across all four dimensions: it proposes revoking universal suffrage, eliminating positive discrimination, rejecting free-market capitalism as a failed model, and forcibly converting foreign-currency loans at original exchange rates—all of which restrict rather than enable liberal political, social, economic, and financial-market norms.

chunk 4 · The text is broadly anti-liberal: it advocates state-owned banks, forced profit repatriation from multinationals, progressive taxation, state-directed industrial policy, and opposes positive discrimination for minorities; parliamentary oversight of the central bank is the only mildly pro-liberal political element.

chunk 5 · The text is strongly anti-liberal across all dimensions: it proposes banning civil rights NGOs as foreign agents, frames ethnic discrimination in policing as acceptable, explicitly declares the neoliberal economic model a failure, advocates state renationalization of energy and land, and treats liberalism itself as an enemy ideology that has devastated Hungary.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal across all dimensions: it advocates state renationalisation of energy infrastructure, strong state control of healthcare and education, opposes cultural pluralism (especially LGBTQ+ visibility), and while it opposes internet censorship and some state subsidies, the overall thrust is statist and nationalist rather than liberal.

chunk 7 · The text shows limited liberal tendencies (some media pluralism, tax incentives for patrons, restoring savings-bank autonomy) but is predominantly illiberal: it advocates state-directed cultural policy, restricts financial market freedom via bank monitoring and nationalization of water/telecom assets, promotes religious-national identity over civil pluralism, and frames liberal values as harmful foreign imports.

chunk 8 · The text shows mixed liberal signals: it supports minority rights and some democratic mechanisms (referenda, parliamentary oversight), but also advocates state-directed economic interventions (national airline, administrative steering of procurement toward domestic products), protectionism against EU land sales, and a strong nationalist-statist framework, yielding below-average liberalism scores overall.

chunk 9 · The text is broadly anti-liberal: it advocates renegotiating IMF and EU contracts, protecting Hungarian land from foreign ownership, withdrawing from euroatlantic structures, and pursuing an ethno-nationalist foreign policy, while offering only weak nods to transparency and competition in EU fund management.
