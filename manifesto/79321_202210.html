Country Bosnia-Herzegovina · Party SDP BiH (Social Democratic Party of Bosnia and Herzegovina) · Date October 2022 · Party family Social democratic · Tokens 27,813 · Manifesto id 79321_202210

📄 Full text: the scored text is the Manifesto Corpus version 2026-1, key 79321_202210, available through the Manifesto Project API (free registration), e.g. in R: manifestoR::mp_corpus(data.frame(party = 79321, date = 202210)). The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames systemic failures as burdens on ordinary workers and the poor, invokes the working class as a virtuous collective, and contrasts the wealthy 1% with the impoverished majority, but stops short of strong elite-conspiracy rhetoric typical of high populism.

chunk 2 · The text contains mild populist elements—criticising nepotism and party-based appointments in public institutions and framing youth as victims of a corrupt system—but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-oriented (energy transition, education reform), keeping overall populism low.

chunk 3 · The manifesto contains moderate populist elements — criticising corrupt elites and incumbent authorities who protect criminals, and centring citizens' interests against nationalist power-holders — but the tone is predominantly technocratic and reform-oriented rather than strongly populist.

chunk 4 · The text contains mild anti-elite rhetoric (criticizing the current government as incompetent and uninterested) and some conspiratorial framing around the deliberate dismantling of state projects, but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

chunk 5 · The text is primarily a technocratic policy document on diplomatic infrastructure and foreign affairs reform; it contains mild criticism of patronage appointments and rule-breaking by Presidency members, but frames these as governance failures rather than a populist elite-vs-people struggle.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mixed populist rhetoric with some anti-elitism and people-centrism, combined with a focus on social justice and economic reforms targeting workers and marginalized groups, indicating moderate populist content.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elite corruption and nepotism, emphasizes the plight and unity of 'the people' especially youth, and frames nationalistic conflict in moral terms, supporting a moderate populist score overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto emphasizes reforming the state to serve the interests of citizens against nationalist elites, highlighting both anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moral conflict over corruption and political obstruction.

chunk 4 · Manifesto expresses distrust in elites through claims of planned destruction of state projects and emphasizes unity and patriotism around national sports teams, indicating moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict framing.

chunk 5 · The text contains no explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralized good-vs-evil struggles, indicating absence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text does not contain any populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild anti-elitism by criticizing the ruling parties for nepotism and political patronage in public institutions, but lacks strong manichean or people-centrist rhetoric.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the ruling elites as corrupt, incompetent, and actively working to dismantle state institutions, while positioning the party as the defender of the citizens' interests.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the current ruling elites as corrupt, incompetent, and actively working to dismantle state projects, while positioning the party as the defender of the unified interests of all citizens.

chunk 5 · The text exhibits mild anti-elitism by criticizing political appointments based on party suitability and personal proximity to presidency members rather than professional competence.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is clearly left-leaning populist, emphasizing redistribution, workers' rights, labour-capital rebalancing, social justice, and trade union empowerment, with no right-wing nativist or identity-protectionist content.

chunk 2 · The party explicitly identifies as representing the working class and social-democratic values, opposes nationalism and ethnic segregation, and advocates redistribution and state support for youth, placing it clearly on the left with minimal right-populist or centrist-populist features.

chunk 3 · The populist flavour is mildly left-leaning, emphasising social equality, anti-corruption, and EU-oriented social-democratic reforms, while explicitly condemning nationalist/right-wing goals; no strong right-populist or purely centrist-populist framing dominates.

chunk 4 · The manifesto leans left-of-centre with calls for universal free healthcare, social solidarity, and public enterprise protection, while also invoking patriotism and national sports identity; there is no strong right-wing nativist or anti-immigration content.

chunk 5 · The document's rare people-centric references are generic appeals to citizen interest in the context of administrative reform, with no discernible left, right, or strong centrist-populist ideological framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 2 · The text advocates left-leaning economic regulation and worker-employer harmony, opposes nationalism, and promotes inclusive centrist participation, reflecting a mixed but moderate populist ideological stance.

chunk 3 · The text reflects a centrist populist ideology focused on citizen interests and legal reforms, while criticizing nationalist and elite-driven agendas, showing a moderate left-leaning stance on social justice and economic development.

chunk 4 · The text shows moderate left populist elements via calls for increased public funding and social investment, some right populist concern about regional inequalities, and centrist populist appeals for reform and unity, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 5 · There is no evidence of populist ideological content, left, right, or centrist, in the manifesto text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · There is no evidence of populist ideology in the text, as it focuses on programmatic policy proposals without populist framing.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is framed from a left-wing perspective, explicitly identifying the party as a representative of the working class.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing, emphasizing social and class sensitivity, public funding, and the exclusion of elitism from the education system.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology leans left, focusing on state-provided universal services, public ownership of key enterprises, and social solidarity.

chunk 5 · The populist critique is centrist and technocratic, focusing on institutional inefficiency and nepotism without a clear left-wing or right-wing ideological framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports political pluralism, participatory democracy, anti-discrimination, and market competition through tax reform, but also proposes a financial transaction tax on banks and strong state intervention in the economy, producing a mixed-to-moderate liberal profile overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently promotes depoliticisation of public institutions, anti-discrimination and inclusive education, market competition alongside state regulation, and EU-aligned energy market integration, reflecting a broadly liberal orientation across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly endorses rule of law, democratic institutions, depoliticisation of the judiciary, minority rights, and market economy values aligned with EU/NATO membership, placing it clearly in the pro-liberal camp across political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto endorses rule of law, EU integration, democratic reforms, and citizen equality, but simultaneously opposes privatisation of key public enterprises and advocates state-led healthcare financing, reflecting a mixed but moderately liberal overall stance.

chunk 5 · The text strongly advocates WTO membership to open foreign markets and improve trade conditions, and endorses democratic procedural norms (hearings for ambassador candidates modelled on democratic states), reflecting a pro-liberal orientation on economic openness and political institutions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including democracy, social rights, economic reforms favoring market efficiency, and financial market development, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports political rights and rule of law, social inclusion and anti-segregation policies, and economic regulation favoring market harmony, but lacks explicit financial market discussion, indicating moderate liberalism overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports rule of law, minority rights, economic development, and transparent financial cooperation with EU institutions, indicating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports liberal political principles like rule of law and transparency, social liberal values of universal and equal healthcare, and economic liberalism through promoting ICT sector growth, but lacks explicit financial market policy discussion.

chunk 5 · The manifesto discusses the lack of a stable legal framework for foreign affairs and highlights benefits of WTO membership for economic openness, indicating moderate support for political and economic liberalism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports classical liberal principles including political pluralism, gender equality, and foreign investment, though it proposes some market interventions like financial transaction taxes.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports liberal democratic principles, including the independence of regulatory bodies, non-discrimination, and the development of the private sector alongside state regulation.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports liberal democratic values, including the rule of law, minority rights, and integration into Euro-Atlantic institutions, while advocating for market-oriented cooperation with the economy.

chunk 4 · The text supports democratic norms and equal rights for all citizens, but opposes economic liberalism by advocating for the halt of privatization of public enterprises.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports integration into global free trade systems like the WTO and advocates for democratic institutional practices and rule-based governance.
