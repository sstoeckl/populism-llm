WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

chunk 6 · The text is a mainstream social-democratic programme with minimal populist framing: it invokes "la ciudadanía" as a collective beneficiary but does not construct a corrupt elite, a virtuous people-vs-elite struggle, or a Manichean moral narrative.

chunk 8 · The text contains minimal populist content; the only faint anti-elitist framing is a brief criticism of a prior "superficial" Africa policy, and the sole Manichean element is labelling the 2003 Iraq war "illegal," while the overall tone is technocratic and policy-oriented with no sustained people-vs-elite narrative.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores highly on political liberalism (independent public media, rule of law, democratic dialogue) and social liberalism (same-sex marriage, gender equality, anti-discrimination law), with moderate economic liberalism (tax simplification, fiscal surplus) and no evidence on financial-market liberalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it promotes anti-discrimination and civil rights (lib_social), competitive capacity and social economy (lib_economic), mortgage insurance and housing market transparency (lib_financial_market), and participatory democratic governance (lib_political).

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic citizenship and rule of law, gender equality and minority inclusion in sport, tax cuts and market competitiveness, and financial transparency/anti-fraud measures, while also proposing some redistributive fiscal interventions that temper the economic liberalism score.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic citizenship and rule of law, gender equality and intercultural pluralism, market competition and deregulation, and prudential financial supervision with consumer protection and transparency.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across political (democratic quality, media pluralism, rule of law), social (same-sex marriage, gender equality), economic (market competition in energy), and financial-market (transparency, competition in energy markets) dimensions, reflecting a mainstream social-democratic liberal programme.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political (transparency, rule of law, democratic institutions) and social (anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ rights, gender equality) dimensions, moderately liberal economically (private-sector participation, enterprise support), and shows limited but present engagement with financial-market issues (financing instruments for businesses).

chunk 7 · The manifesto strongly supports judicial independence, rule of law, democratic institutions, and gender equality (high lib_political and lib_social scores), endorses competitive markets and the Lisbon Strategy (moderate lib_economic), but also calls for taxing financial speculation and regulating hedge funds at European level (mixed lib_financial_market).

chunk 8 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across political and social dimensions, repeatedly championing democracy, rule of law, human rights, gender equity, and free trade (Euro-Mediterranean Free Trade Zone), while financial-market policy is entirely absent from the text.
