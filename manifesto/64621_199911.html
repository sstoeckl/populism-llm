WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames politics as a struggle between ordinary New Zealanders and entrenched elites ("vested interests have worked to cripple the MMP process"; "occupy the positions of power"), consistently invokes "New Zealanders" as a unified virtuous people whose interests must come first, and draws a moderate good-vs-evil contrast between the old parties and NZ First, though the tone is more programmatic than stridently populist.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies: it references "failed Government economic policies" harming rural communities and targets "incompetence and corruption" in the IRD, but the overall tone is programmatic and policy-focused rather than strongly people-vs-elite framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes entrenched elites and political establishment, advocates empowering the community and making Parliament more accountable, and highlights political deception and power struggles, indicating moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes incompetence and corruption within the Inland Revenue Department, indicating anti-elitist sentiment, and emphasizes partnership with the people for economic growth, showing moderate people-centrism and some anti-elitism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits strong populist elements by framing established politicians and vested interests as corrupt elites holding onto power against the interests of 'all New Zealanders'.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism, combining a rhetoric of partnering with 'the people' with a distrust of future politicians tampering with public funds.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · NZ First blends left-populist themes (anti-privatisation, anti-financial sector dominance, redistribution via superannuation and minimum wage) with right-populist themes (strict immigration controls, national identity, protecting NZ resources from foreign ownership), making it a mixed but slightly right-leaning populist party overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto blends right-leaning economic nationalism (restricting foreign land ownership, tariff caution, "buy New Zealand first") with some left-leaning redistribution (superannuation increases, student loan relief) and centrist people-partnership rhetoric, yielding a mixed but mildly right-nationalist populist flavour.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto combines left-wing concerns about foreign control and social services decline with right-wing emphasis on immigration control and national interest, alongside centrist appeals for institutional responsiveness, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The text supports left-leaning economic policies like tax incentives for employment and export growth, right-leaning protectionist measures such as reducing foreign land ownership, and centrist appeals to partnership with the people, reflecting a mixed but overall moderate populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is primarily right-wing nationalist, focusing heavily on strict immigration controls and protecting national resources from foreign ownership, combined with some left-leaning anti-privatization stances.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology leans right-nationalist, emphasizing protection of domestic land from foreign buyers and promoting 'buy New Zealand first' policies.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal credentials: it supports democratic reform, accountability, and rule of law (moderately pro-liberal politically), but advocates state asset retention, price controls on electricity, strict immigration limits, foreign investment restrictions, and a development bank, all of which constrain market liberalism in economic and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports reducing compliance costs, trade openness, and tax incentives (pro-liberal), but also opposes privatisation of the TAB and roads, favours state-run superannuation and government-directed banking utilities, and rejects market-model education, tempering its overall liberal score.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions and political accountability, minority and social inclusion (notably Pacific Islanders), export-led economic growth, and prudent monetary policy reforms, indicating a generally pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports liberal political institutions by advocating legislative safeguards, recognizes social diversity and youth inclusion, promotes economic efficiency and tax system reform, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market regulation, resulting in an overall moderate liberalism score.

Gemini

chunk 1 · While the text strongly supports democratic reforms and political accountability, it rejects economic liberalism by advocating for state-owned enterprises, price controls, and state-directed development banking.

chunk 2 · The text shows mixed classical liberal stances, supporting judicial appeals and business development but opposing privatization of key assets and advocating state-directed banking utilities.
