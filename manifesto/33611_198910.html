WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto criticises the Socialist government as arrogant and incompetent ("ejercicio prepotente"), calls on society to mobilise, and frames politics as a contest between a failed governing elite and the broader citizenry, but the rhetoric is moderate and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows minimal populism: it briefly criticises the Socialist government for favouring large public industry over SMEs, but this is a technocratic policy critique rather than a moralistic people-vs-elite frame; there is no sustained anti-elite rhetoric, no unified "people" invoked as a virtuous bloc, and no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 3 · The text contains only mild anti-elitism directed at the Socialist government's failures in justice and culture policy, with occasional people-centric framing around citizens' access to services, but no strong Manichean good-vs-evil narrative; the overall tone is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than populist.

chunk 4 · The text contains only mild populist signals: the incumbent Socialist government is criticised for failing to implement agreed policies, but there is no sustained elite-vs-people framing, no Manichean moral struggle, and no invocation of a unified virtuous people against corrupt elites.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto criticizes the socialist government as an out-of-touch elite, calls for societal mobilization invoking 'force' and unity of the people, and frames political renewal as a moral imperative, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral struggle.

chunk 2 · The text shows mild anti-elitism and people-centrism through government support for domestic industry and investment, but lacks strong moral polarization, resulting in a low populism score.

chunk 3 · No evidence of populist rhetoric or themes was found in the manifesto text, which focuses on detailed policy proposals and institutional reforms without anti-elitist, people-centric, or Manichean framing.

chunk 4 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits low-to-moderate populism, focusing its criticism on the governing socialist party's 'arrogance' and 'conformism' rather than framing politics as an existential struggle between a corrupt elite and a pure people.

chunk 2 · The text contains very mild populist elements, limited to criticizing the ruling socialist government for favoring state-owned enterprises over small and medium businesses.

chunk 3 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil struggles.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence of populist rhetoric, anti-elitism, or people-centrism in the text.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · CiU's populist appeals blend mild centre-left redistribution rhetoric with Catalan autonomist identity politics; there is no strong left-wing anti-corporate framing nor right-wing nativist/anti-immigration content, placing the ideological flavour closer to centrist regionalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto's weak populist signals are ideologically mixed: it combines modest social-protection measures (minimum pensions, unemployment coverage) with strong pro-market, pro-privatisation, and pro-SME stances, and its regional/autonomy emphasis does not map cleanly onto left or right populism; centrist technocratic appeals are slightly more prominent than ideological populism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto blends moderate centre-left social policy (equality of opportunity, welfare expansion) with regionalist/autonomist priorities (Catalan identity, decentralization) and some cross-party consensus appeals, without a strong left or right populist ideological anchor.

chunk 4 · The text blends moderate centre-right concerns (law and order, security, drug trafficking) with some centre-left elements (development aid at 0.7% GDP, social reinsertion), producing a weakly populist, broadly centrist-to-centre-right ideological profile without strong programmatic populist ideology.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text emphasizes social redistribution and critiques central government threats to Catalan autonomy (right-wing populist), while also appealing broadly to society without clear left or right programmatic exclusivity, indicating a centrist populist ideology overall.

chunk 2 · Populist ideology is mildly left-leaning with protectionist and labor-flexibility elements, alongside centrist fiscal investment appeals and some right-leaning national brand promotion.

chunk 3 · The manifesto does not present any clear populist ideological stance, left, right, or centrist; it is policy-focused without populist appeals or identity protectionism.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence in the text of populist ideological stances, whether left, right, or centrist.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are centrist and regionalist, targeting the central government's centralism and inefficiency without adopting a radical left-wing or right-wing populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The populist critique is centrist and regionalist, focusing on the defense of small and medium enterprises and regional autonomy against central government inefficiency.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of a populist ideology as the text focuses on programmatic, regionalist, and institutional reforms.

chunk 4 · The text focuses on institutional, regional, and foreign policy without any populist ideological framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently advocates economic liberalisation, deregulation, tax reform to stimulate savings and investment, financial market modernisation, and political pluralism/democracy, placing it firmly in a pro-liberal direction across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is consistently pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it advocates parliamentary budget controls and judicial oversight (political), union electoral equality (social), privatisation, SME deregulation, and free-trade promotion (economic), and opening capital markets to citizens and private capital participation in public enterprises (financial markets).

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it defends judicial independence and democratic rule of law, promotes gender equality and minority rights, supports freedom of education and market-mixed healthcare, and references credit lines and fiscal incentives consistent with market-enabling finance.

chunk 4 · The text is broadly pro-liberal: it champions democratic regimes, human rights, rule of law, minority inclusion, and competitive agriculture, while proposing institutional reforms that strengthen checks and balances and decentralisation; financial-market policy is entirely absent.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, pluralism, market liberalization, economic modernization, and financial market development aligned with European standards, reflecting a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports liberal political rights, social programs, economic competitiveness, and financial market incentives for innovation, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles such as rule of law, judicial independence, gender equality, and promotion of innovation and economic collaboration, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports constitutional rights, social protections, regional economic participation, and democratic political principles, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly advocates classical liberal principles, emphasizing economic liberalization, private enterprise, financial market modernization, and the protection of pluralism and democratic institutions.

chunk 2 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including market competition, privatization of public enterprises, deregulation, tax incentives for investment, and individual educational freedoms.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing democratic guarantees, individual rights, pluralism, and fiscal incentives.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing individual rights, human rights, and international cooperation.
