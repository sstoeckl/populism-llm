WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto occasionally frames policy opponents (the "old majority," employers' associations, nuclear lobby) as acting against the people's interests, but the dominant mode is programmatic social-democratic policy argumentation rather than populist people-vs-elite rhetoric.

chunk 2 · The text contains moderate populist elements: political elites and industry are framed as corrupt servants of corporate interests ("Lakaien und Lobbyisten"), and there is a Manichaean framing of the political class versus the people, but the document is primarily a detailed policy manifesto rather than a populist appeal, keeping overall populism scores moderate.

chunk 3 · The text frames large corporations and financial elites as exploiting both the Third World and domestic workers, contrasts corrupt establishment behaviour (Swiss banks sheltering dictators' money) with the interests of ordinary citizens, and uses moralized language about dirty money and injustice, yielding moderate populist scores across all three sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elites through references to restrictive social policies and fears of abuse, emphasizes the need for better public information and inclusion of 'the people', and highlights social struggles such as poverty and unemployment, indicating moderate to strong populist elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the political elite as self-serving and eroding public trust, invokes a call to the people to renew passion for life and responsibility, and frames societal challenges in a moral struggle against industrial and environmental threats, indicating moderate populist rhetoric.

chunk 3 · The manifesto criticizes economic and political elites for exploiting developing countries and calls for international solidarity against oppressive regimes, illustrating anti-elitism and a moral struggle against injustice, while invoking the people’s interests against elite-driven foreign policy.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the ruling bourgeois parliament and energy cartels as acting against the interests of the common people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing political elites as lobbyists for corporations and contrasting the interests of the common people against powerful economic actors.

chunk 3 · The text contains minimal populist rhetoric, with only a brief critical reference to the 'political establishment' in the context of constitutional reform.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · Where populist framing appears it is consistently left-flavoured: anti-corporate redistribution, workers' rights, social equality, and criticism of profit-driven elites; there is no right-populist nativist or anti-immigration content.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it frames politics as a struggle between workers/consumers and corporate elites, demands redistribution toward small and medium enterprises, worker co-determination, and criticizes corporate power and financial speculation, with no right-wing nativist or identity-based populist content.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it calls for redistribution from export subsidies to development aid and domestic social investment, criticises corporate exploitation of cheap labour in the Global South, and advocates international solidarity and workers' rights, with only a minor nod to immigration stabilisation that prevents a purely left reading.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports left-wing populist ideology with calls for redistribution, protection of weaker groups, and opposition to privatization of losses, while also showing centrist populist appeals through calls for public information and inclusion.

chunk 2 · The text strongly emphasizes labor rights, social equality, and redistribution, consistent with left-wing populist ideology, while also appealing to a broad people-centered renewal without clear right-wing or purely centrist stances.

chunk 3 · The text reflects a left-leaning populist ideology by emphasizing increased development aid, criticizing export subsidies favoring large corporations, and advocating for improved rights for foreigners, while also showing some centrist appeals to national interest and limited right-wing concerns about immigration levels.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are strongly left-wing, focusing on anti-corporate critique, redistribution, and social welfare expansion.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on protecting workers and consumers against corporate power, financial speculation, and capital interests.

chunk 3 · The populist elements are framed entirely within a left-wing, democratic socialist ideology that critiques corporate power and global capitalism.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores high on social liberalism (gender equality, civil rights) and moderately on political liberalism (democratic accountability), but explicitly favours state intervention over market mechanisms in health, energy, and finance, yielding low scores on economic and financial-market liberalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it strongly supports political democracy, civil rights, and anti-discrimination (pro-liberal), but advocates price controls, land speculation bans, capital restrictions, and state intervention in markets (anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions), yielding a moderate overall liberalism score.

chunk 3 · The text is moderately liberal on political and social dimensions (strong defence of human rights, democratic rule of law, open asylum policy), mixed on economic liberalism (supports competition against monopolies but also state media funding and export regulation), and anti-liberal on financial markets (advocates capital export bans and tighter banking secrecy restrictions).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto advocates for political liberalism through support for democratic reforms and equal rights, social liberalism via gender equality and minority protections, and economic liberalism by promoting free movement and reform of social insurance, though financial market issues are not explicitly addressed.

chunk 2 · The manifesto advocates for democratic political rights, social integration and equality in education, economic policies favoring labor alongside capital, and financial market reforms enhancing investor protection, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports political liberalism through emphasis on human rights and democracy, social liberalism via improved rights for foreigners, economic liberalism by criticizing harmful export subsidies and promoting market-oriented reforms, and financial market liberalism by acknowledging Switzerland’s role in global finance, albeit critically.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through equal rights and democratic participation, but opposes economic liberalism by advocating for state intervention and limits on commercial freedom.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political democracy and social non-discrimination, but advocates for heavy state intervention, price controls, and market restrictions in the economic and financial sectors.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political and social liberal values like human rights and an open asylum policy, but rejects economic and financial liberalism by advocating for capital controls and state intervention.
