WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows minimal populist features: it invokes the individual rather than a collective 'people', does not frame elites as corrupt enemies, and contains no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, reflecting a mainstream centre-right conservative party programme.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows minimal populist features: it occasionally invokes decentralisation of power to local communities and individual freedom, but frames this in a technocratic, market-liberal manner rather than as a people-vs-elite struggle, with no Manichean rhetoric or anti-establishment attacks.

chunk 3 · This is a mainstream centre-right welfare and health policy chapter from Norway's Høyre party; it contains virtually no populist rhetoric — no elite-bashing, no virtuous-people framing, and no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, only technocratic policy proposals.

chunk 3 · The manifesto emphasizes returning people from welfare to work and values each individual's resources, showing mild anti-elitism and people-centrism but lacks strong moral conflict rhetoric.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto has negligible populist ideological content; immigration is framed positively as a resource, there is no anti-corporate rhetoric, and the mild people-vs-state framing is generic liberal-conservative rather than ideologically populist.

chunk 2 · The manifesto's very limited populist signals are neither clearly left nor right in populist terms; it combines mild pro-market anti-monopoly language with moderate immigration management, placing it closer to centrist/liberal than to a distinct populist ideological pole.

chunk 3 · The text is a mainstream centre-right policy programme with minimal populist ideological content; the faint right-leaning signal comes from conservative social stances (anti-drug liberalisation, individual responsibility) rather than from populist identity or nativist framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines centrist appeals to individual freedom and responsibility with moderate left concerns about poverty and moderate right recognition of cultural diversity, reflecting a centrist populist ideology.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence in the text of populist ideological positions on the left, right, or centrist populism; the manifesto focuses on policy without populist framing.

chunk 3 · The text promotes work and participation as foundations of welfare with a centrist populist tone and some left-leaning emphasis on social inclusion and welfare activation, without right-wing nationalist themes.

Gemini

chunk 1 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text is a standard mainstream conservative-liberal manifesto.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on mainstream center-right policy proposals.

chunk 3 · The text does not contain any populist ideological framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions rule of law, free speech, and democratic rights; supports women's equality and minority respect; advocates free markets, competition, deregulation, and tax cuts; and explicitly calls for free capital markets and a deregulated Nordic power market.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions rule of law and democratic institutions, equal treatment regardless of gender/ethnicity/sexual orientation, extensive deregulation and free-market competition (energy, fisheries, agriculture), and market-based pricing without price controls in housing and financial markets.

chunk 3 · The manifesto consistently promotes free-market competition in welfare services, internationally competitive taxation, individual choice, private providers, and market-based pension savings, placing it clearly in the pro-liberal range across economic, financial, social, and political dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · Strong emphasis on rule of law, individual rights, social inclusion, economic liberalization, tax reduction, and financial market competition demonstrates a robust liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles including rule of law, individual rights, social equality, responsible economic policy, and democratic institutions, as evidenced by multiple direct quotes.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports political rights through patient rights, social inclusion of disabled persons, economic competitiveness, and a competitive tax system, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly champions classical liberal principles, including the rule of law, individual freedom, free markets, deregulation, and open capital markets.

chunk 2 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including the rule of law, deregulation, free-market competition, and individual choice.

chunk 3 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including market competition, private sector involvement in public services, individual choice, and equal opportunities for disabled citizens.
