WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

chunk 3 · The text is a detailed governing-party manifesto focused on social policy achievements and plans; it invokes people-centric language ("her bir insan değerlidir") and frames the party as servant of citizens, but contains virtually no anti-elite rhetoric, no Manichean good-vs-evil framing, and no conspiracy narratives, yielding very low populism scores overall.

chunk 5 · The text is a detailed AKP government program chapter covering investment, employment, agriculture, energy, and transport. It uses first-person party framing ("AK Parti olarak") and occasional references to serving "halkımız" (our people), but there is virtually no anti-elite rhetoric, no Manichean good-vs-evil framing, and no construction of a unified virtuous people against corrupt elites. chunk 6 · The text is a technocratic policy manifesto focused on logistics, trade, tourism, R&D, and industry; it contains virtually no populist rhetoric—no elite-bashing, no Manichean framing, and only minimal inclusive "people" language in the context of broad development goals.

chunk 7 · The text is a technocratic policy manifesto focused on infrastructure, regional development, and environmental policy; it references citizens as service recipients and invokes inclusive development, but contains virtually no anti-elite rhetoric, Manichean framing, or strong people-centrism typical of populism.

chunk 8 · The text contains mild populist elements, primarily invoking "millet iradesi" (people's will) against "vesayet odakları" (tutelage centers), but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly populist, with no sustained elite-bashing or Manichean framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses democratic rule of law, judicial independence, individual rights, gender equality, minority protections, and a market-enabling legal environment, placing it clearly in the pro-liberal range across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it supports democratic accountability, transparency, rule of law, equal opportunity in education, open foreign investment, and prudential financial market regulation via the Banking Law, reflecting a consistent ordo-liberal orientation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows moderate liberal scores across dimensions: it endorses gender equality and anti-discrimination reforms (lib_social), references democratic education and freedoms (lib_political), mentions competitive enterprise and labour market flexibility (lib_economic), and uses credit guarantee mechanisms to facilitate market access for women entrepreneurs (lib_financial_market), though the overall orientation is social-democratic rather than classically liberal.

chunk 4 · The manifesto consistently champions free-market economics, property rights, entrepreneurial freedom, rule of law, judicial reform, participatory democracy, and a transparent/competitive financial system, placing it firmly in the pro-liberal camp across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 5 · The manifesto strongly promotes economic liberalism through investment environment reforms (one-day company registration, removal of minimum capital requirements for FDI, deregulation, privatization of energy distribution), market-enabling financial instruments (futures/options markets, licensed warehousing), gender equality in the workplace, and transparency/accountability principles, placing it clearly in the pro-liberal range across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 6 · The manifesto strongly endorses free enterprise, open trade, market competition, deregulation of customs and logistics, capital market access for SMEs, and stock market participation, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal economic orientation; social liberalism is touched on only lightly via women's entrepreneurship.

chunk 7 · The manifesto endorses democratic governance, transparency, and accountability in local government; supports market opening for IT firms and new financial instruments (SUKUK) in urban transformation; and promotes inclusive access for disabled and low-income groups, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal stance across all four dimensions.

chunk 8 · The manifesto consistently endorses democracy, human rights, rule of law, and trade openness, references anti-discrimination efforts, and promotes market-based international economic cooperation including securities markets, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal orientation across all four sub-dimensions.
