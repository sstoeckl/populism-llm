WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies through distrust of political institutions and banks, and appeals to 'ordinary working people,' but frames these within a Christian-democratic, rule-of-law framework rather than a sharp elite-vs-people dichotomy.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is a detailed policy programme of a Christian-democratic party (KDU-ČSL) with only incidental populist elements: corruption of politicians is mentioned in the context of gambling regulation, and citizens' trust is invoked, but there is no sustained anti-elite framing, no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, and no strong people-centrism.

chunk 3 · The text is a policy-oriented transport and energy manifesto with minimal populist rhetoric; it appeals broadly to public benefit (affordable energy for all, public safety) but does not frame elites as corrupt or construct a moralized people-vs-elite struggle.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly expresses distrust towards political and economic elites, emphasizes a virtuous 'people' who value work and family, and frames social rules in moral terms, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly discusses corruption as a serious societal problem and emphasizes the role of citizens and local communities, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moral framing of political corruption.

chunk 3 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralized good-vs-evil struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits mild populism by acknowledging widespread public distrust in political institutions and framing the party as similar to ordinary, hard-working citizens.

chunk 2 · The text contains minor anti-elitist elements, specifically criticizing the corruption of politicians by the gambling lobby, but lacks broader populist framing.

chunk 3 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist claims, or moralizing good-versus-evil framing.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The party explicitly rejects both 'right-wing freedom without justice' and 'left-wing justice without freedom,' positioning itself as centrist Christian-democratic; populist ideological coloring is weak and centrist rather than left or right.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is primarily a mainstream Christian-democratic programme; it shows mild right-leaning tendencies through concerns about immigration and cultural identity (islamisation debate), mild left-leaning tendencies through progressive taxation and solidarity, but no strong populist ideological flavour overall.

chunk 3 · The text shows mild centrist-populist appeals (affordable energy for all, public transport for citizens) alongside some centre-left elements (public funding of infrastructure, support for low-income energy poverty), but no clear right-wing populist or strong left-wing redistributive framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text rejects both right-wing freedom without justice and left-wing justice without freedom, shows moderate right-leaning nationalist caution, and centers on people-elite appeals with a centrist pro-European stance, indicating a mixed populist ideological orientation.

chunk 2 · The text shows a mixed populist ideology with left-leaning anti-corruption emphasis, right-leaning migration control, and centrist appeals to political responsibility and institutional reform.

chunk 3 · There is no explicit content related to left, right, or centrist populist ideology in the manifesto, thus no evidence of populist ideological stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The party explicitly rejects both right-wing and left-wing extremes, positioning itself as a centrist force focused on moral values and family support.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of a coherent populist ideology, as the text focuses on standard programmatic policy proposals.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses entirely on technical transport and energy policies.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democracy, rule of law, judicial independence, and European single market integration (pro-liberal), but opposes liberalization of abortion and registered partnerships and favors public health insurance over privatization, yielding a mixed-to-moderate overall liberal score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently supports rule of law, judicial independence, anti-discrimination, free movement of goods and capital in the EU, and prudential regulation of banking, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal stance across all four dimensions.

chunk 3 · The text endorses market opening in rail transport and competitive tendering for public transport operators, reflecting moderate economic liberalism, while also supporting significant state intervention in infrastructure and energy pricing controls, producing a mixed but moderately pro-liberal overall picture.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights through education and ethical values, free market principles in health and social policy, and acknowledges financial market issues as part of a deeper crisis, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, human rights, free market principles with social responsibility, and transparent financial market practices, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports liberal economic policies such as using pricing as an economic tool and references political concepts aligned with liberalism, but lacks evidence on social and financial market liberalism, resulting in a moderate pro-liberal overall score.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports democratic institutions and market integration but opposes social liberalism regarding abortion and registered partnerships.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal principles, including the independence of the judiciary, human rights, free movement of capital, and a regulated social-market economy.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate support for liberal principles by advocating for market opening to private firms, private financing of infrastructure, and respecting property rights, balanced with state coordination.
