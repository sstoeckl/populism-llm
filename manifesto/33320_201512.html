WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto criticises the PP government's education and culture policies as harmful to citizens, invoking the people's interests against a governing elite, but does so in a programmatic, policy-oriented manner with limited Manichean framing, yielding only moderate populism scores.

chunk 2 · The text explicitly distances itself from populism ("sin concesiones hacia el inmovilismo o el populismo"), frames the people-elite tension in institutional/democratic reform terms rather than moralized conflict, and while it criticises the PP government and corruption, it does so through rule-of-law and transparency arguments rather than Manichean good-vs-evil rhetoric.

chunk 3 · The text occasionally contrasts the socialist government's record against the PP's rights-restricting policies and frames large corporations as advantaged over ordinary citizens, but the overall tone is programmatic and institutional rather than strongly populist; anti-elitism and Manichean framing are present but mild and targeted at a specific rival party rather than a corrupt elite in general.

chunk 4 · The text is primarily a detailed economic and labour policy programme with only mild populist undertones: occasional references to benefits going to "only a few," criticism of the PP government's labour reform as creating precarity, and framing workers as victims of crisis, but without sustained elite-vs-people rhetoric or conspiratorial framing.

chunk 5 · The text shows mild populist features: it frames certain elites (PP government, large energy companies) as having failed citizens and criticises privatisation benefiting "unos pocos," but the dominant register is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than morally Manichean or strongly people-centric.

chunk 6 · The text contains mild populist elements—criticising a narrow network of elites and oligopolies that capture rents—but frames this within a mainstream social-democratic, institutional reform agenda rather than a sharp people-vs-elite moral divide.

chunk 7 · The text criticises the PP government as responsible for welfare cuts and growing inequality, invoking "the people" (ciudadanía) as the virtuous beneficiary of social protection, but the critique is policy-specific and social-democratic rather than strongly populist in a Manichaean sense.

chunk 8 · The text criticises the PP government's management of the crisis as having increased inequality and harmed the poorest, but frames this as partisan policy critique rather than a deep people-vs-corrupt-elite populist narrative; people-centric appeals are present but embedded in social-democratic programmatic proposals.

chunk 9 · The text criticises the PP government for damaging Spain's international standing and dismantling cooperation policy, but the critique is party-political and programmatic rather than a deep populist people-vs-elite framing; people-centrism and Manichean rhetoric are mild.

chunk 10 · The text shows mild populist features: it criticises "neoliberal" EU/government management and invokes "el pueblo español" as sovereign, but the dominant register is institutional, consensus-oriented, and programmatic rather than morally polarising.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it champions democratic values, gender and LGBTQ+ equality, entrepreneurship and innovation-driven competitiveness, and market-enabling financial instruments, while also advocating significant public investment and regulation in education and culture, producing mixed-to-high liberal scores.

chunk 2 · The manifesto scores strongly on political liberalism (democratic reform, rule of law, transparency, checks and balances) and social liberalism (minority rights, gender equality), while taking a mixed stance on economic liberalism (supporting some fiscal incentives but defending public services against privatisation) and a consumer-protection-oriented stance on financial markets.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions—championing rule of law, democratic rights, anti-discrimination, and LGBTQ+ equality—while taking a mixed social-democratic stance on economic liberalism (supporting competition and productivity but also redistribution and regulation of financial abuses).

chunk 7 · The text scores moderately on political liberalism (dialogue, consensus, rule of law), strongly on social liberalism (gender identity, anti-discrimination, equality), and mixed on economic liberalism (criticises privatisation of public services but supports market competition in some areas); financial-market references are limited to sustainability of public finances.

chunk 8 · The manifesto scores moderately pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (democracy, human rights, gender equality, diversity), but is mixed-to-anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions, advocating strong public management of housing, social housing at below-market prices, restrictions on financial entities, and expanded state welfare rather than market-based solutions.

chunk 9 · The manifesto strongly endorses democracy, rule of law, human rights, gender equality, and minority protections (high lib_political and lib_social), shows mixed signals on economic liberalism (supports TTIP with caveats, excludes sensitive sectors), and is anti-liberal on financial markets by advocating a Financial Transactions Tax.
