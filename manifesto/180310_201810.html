WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto exhibits moderate populism: it frames the political establishment and corrupt politicians as enemies of the virtuous Brazilian people ("Políticos que enriquecem com a política são ladrões"), invokes the people as creative and entrepreneurial, and draws a moral contrast between honest citizens and a corrupt, inefficient state, but the dominant register is technocratic reform rather than pure people-vs-elite antagonism.

chunk 2 · The text is predominantly a technocratic policy manifesto with only mild populist undertones, limited to criticism of the PT government's foreign policy as serving partisan rather than national interests, with occasional references to "our youth" and "our families" but no sustained people-vs-elite framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes corrupt elites and an inefficient state, emphasizes the virtuous Brazilian people as entrepreneurs and workers, and frames politics as a moral struggle against corruption and demagoguery, supporting a populism score around 7.

chunk 2 · The manifesto does not provide explicit content related to populism dimensions such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or manichean narratives.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the political establishment as a corrupt, self-perpetuating elite while praising the virtuous, entrepreneurial Brazilian people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits low overall populism, with a specific anti-elitist critique directed at the previous PT administrations for using diplomacy for partisan interests.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist flavor is predominantly centrist-reformist, appealing to anti-corruption and equal opportunity without strong left redistribution or right nativist framing; mild right-leaning elements appear in criticism of PT's foreign policy, while mild left-leaning elements appear in social equality rhetoric, yielding a broadly centrist populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is primarily centrist-technocratic, emphasizing state management, institutional reform, and national interest; the mild populist flavor is neither clearly left (no anti-corporate rhetoric) nor clearly right (no nativist/anti-immigration framing), though there is a slight centrist people-vs-partisan-elite critique of the PT government.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines anti-corruption and social justice themes (left), some openness to immigration (right), and broad people-elite appeals (centrist), reflecting a moderate populist ideology overall.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit discussion of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the manifesto text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are centrist and technocratic, focusing on anti-corruption, state efficiency, and moral renewal without adopting a strong left-wing or right-wing populist ideology.

chunk 2 · There is no systematic populist ideological framing present in the text.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic institutions and rule of law, invokes human rights and pluralism, advocates privatization, trade opening, deregulation, and tax simplification, and explicitly calls for increased competition in financial markets and removal of barriers to foreign banks and capital.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it strongly advocates trade openness, foreign investment, democratic institutions, rule of law, and extensive civil rights protections for minorities and vulnerable groups, while supporting market competition and attracting private capital.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, equality, market competition, privatization, and financial market openness, indicating a pro-liberal stance with scores around 8.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal political rights (justice access), social rights (women's and minorities' rights), and economic openness (trade and services integration), but lacks mention of financial market policies.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly aligns with classical liberalism, advocating for free markets, privatization, trade openness, financial competition, and the defense of democratic institutions and human rights.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including judicial independence, minority rights protections, and economic openness through trade integration.
