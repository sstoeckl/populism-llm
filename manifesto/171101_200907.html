WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies by appealing to popular will (e.g., the majority demanding the death penalty) and criticizing politicians for ignoring citizen demands, but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly anti-elite or Manichean.

chunk 2 · The text is a detailed environmental policy manifesto from Mexico's Green Ecologist Party (PVEM) focused on water, air, soil, biodiversity, forests, and climate change; populist elements are minimal and incidental, limited to brief references to corruption in regulatory bodies and the plight of millions without water access, without constructing a sustained people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows minimal populism: it briefly criticizes state-owned PEMEX and CFE as environmental offenders and invokes "all Mexicans" as beneficiaries, but the dominant frame is technocratic environmental policy rather than a people-vs-elite moral struggle.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows only mild populist features: it briefly warns against serving "grandes corporaciones" at citizens' expense and frames policy around citizen welfare, but the dominant tone is technocratic and institutional, focused on legislative reform, energy policy, and environmental sustainability rather than a moralized people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 5 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies: it occasionally contrasts citizens' needs against large corporations and financial institutions' abuses, but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than strongly populist, with no sustained Manichean framing or explicit elite-vs-people narrative.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The PVEM blends mild left-leaning social equality rhetoric (equal rights, access to health/education) with a right-leaning law-and-order stance (death penalty, tough-on-crime), while its core identity is ecologist/centrist, making its populist ideology weak and mixed.

chunk 2 · The PVEM manifesto is primarily an environmental/green policy document with mild left-leaning tendencies (framing water access as a human right, protecting indigenous communities' genetic heritage), but no strong left-populist anti-corporate rhetoric or right-populist nationalist/anti-immigration content; the dominant ideological flavor is centrist-green technocratic.

chunk 3 · The weak populist signals lean slightly left (critique of inequality and concentration of wealth) with negligible right-populist content; overall the manifesto is primarily a green/ecologist policy document without strong ideological populist coloring.

chunk 4 · The populist flavour that does exist leans slightly left—invoking "Justicia Social," redistribution concerns, and criticism of corporate interests—while also embracing cross-sector (public/social/private) partnerships and centrist institutional reform, making the overall ideological signal weak and mixed.

chunk 5 · The manifesto leans left-populist in its emphasis on social justice, redistribution, combating inequality, protecting rural workers and indigenous communities, and criticizing neoliberal trade models, while also invoking national sovereignty in trade and food policy, giving a modest right-populist tinge.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto affirms democratic processes, gender equality, and economic freedom in principle, while also advocating significant state intervention in health, education, and security, yielding a moderate overall liberal score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports constitutional rights and human rights frameworks (lib_political, lib_social at moderate-liberal levels), shows mixed signals on economic liberalism by criticizing market-driven hydrocarbon consumption while also proposing market mechanisms like water banks and tradeable discharge rights, and briefly endorses access to international financial markets for infrastructure financing.

chunk 3 · The manifesto endorses judicial access, indigenous community inclusion, trade liberalization paired with environmental standards, and market-based financial instruments for environmental policy, reflecting moderate classical-liberal orientations tempered by significant regulatory and interventionist environmental proposals.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political dimensions, strongly advocating democratic reform, rule of law, checks and balances, transparency, and media freedom; it also supports gender equity and youth rights (social liberalism), while on economic and financial dimensions it is more mixed, combining market-friendly language (competitiveness, investment) with state-led energy policy and price controls.

chunk 5 · The manifesto supports democracy, rule of law, and minority rights (pro-liberal), while combining market-friendly tax simplification with significant state intervention (public spending, development banking, price controls on banking fees), yielding a mixed liberal profile overall.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto affirms democratic governance, human rights, social equality, and sustainable economic development, supporting classical liberal principles in political, social, and economic domains.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles such as guaranteeing access to water as a constitutional right, recognizing human rights, and endorsing market economy elements, indicating a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports constitutional rights to a healthy environment, environmental education, and legislative reforms promoting environmental protection and economic instruments for sustainability, indicating a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports strengthening democratic institutions, promoting women's participation and rights, maintaining price controls to protect the population, and emphasizes the rule of law and separation of powers, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 5 · The manifesto advocates for accountable governance, respect for minority rights, tax system simplification, and financial market regulation to enable market function, demonstrating a generally pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports democratic institutions, gender equality, and general economic freedom, though it emphasizes state intervention and regulation for environmental and social goals.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports human rights, community rights, and market-based mechanisms like green certifications and international financial borrowing.

chunk 3 · The text supports market competition and international trade agreements while advocating for public consultation, constitutional rights, and targeted financial support for sustainable projects.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including the rule of law, human rights, democratic checks and balances, and market-based incentives for businesses.

chunk 5 · The text supports democratic institutions and minority rights, but advocates for heavy state intervention in financial markets, including interest rate caps and bank commission limits.
