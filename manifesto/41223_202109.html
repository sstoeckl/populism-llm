WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores very high on populism: it consistently frames elites (large corporations, pharmaceutical companies, real estate capital, the ruling coalition) as corrupt exploiters of ordinary people, invokes 'the people' as a unified virtuous collective left behind, and constructs a sharp moral binary between 'Gemeinwohl' and 'Profite/Bereicherung und Korruption'.

chunk 2 · The text frames private corporations and investors as exploiting public insurance contributions and ordinary people's housing costs, while invoking collective solidarity ("alle zahlen ein, alle werden gut versorgt") and a clear few-vs-many antagonism ("Spekulationskreislauf, an dem sich wenige auf Kosten der Vielen bereichern"), yielding moderate-to-high populist scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 3 · The text consistently frames politics as a struggle between corrupt corporate/shareholder elites and ordinary working people, invoking "the many" against profit-driven elites, with moralized good-vs-evil framing around climate, industry, and social policy.

chunk 4 · The text consistently frames politics as a struggle between ordinary people (workers, East Germans, consumers) and corrupt elites (Konzerne, Finanzkonzerne, Treuhand, western corporations), invoking the people as a unified virtuous group and depicting elites as morally culpable adversaries.

chunk 5 · The text shows moderate populist features: it frames corporations and capitalism as exploitative elites harming ordinary people (anti-elitism), invokes broad people-centred policy goals ("Sicherheit für alle"), and draws a moral contrast between capitalist/patriarchal structures and the common good, but the rhetoric is primarily programmatic rather than strongly Manichean or people-vs-elite in tone.

chunk 6 · The text exhibits moderate populism: it frames conservative politicians and the security industry as self-serving elites ("Machtstreben der Konservativen und den Profiten der Sicherheitsbranche"), invokes a moralized contrast between ordinary people and the powerful ("die Großen lässt man laufen"), and calls for security "for all" — but the rhetoric is grounded in concrete policy proposals rather than pure Manichean mobilization.

chunk 7 · The manifesto consistently frames politics as a struggle between the virtuous many ("die Vielen", "Bürger*innenwille") and corrupt elites (corporations, lobbyists, the rich), with DIE LINKE positioned as the only party free from corporate and lobby interests, producing strong anti-elitist, people-centric, and Manichean populist rhetoric.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is unambiguously left-populist: it explicitly advocates 'demokratischen Sozialismus', calls for overcoming 'Vorherrschaft des Kapitals', demands redistribution from the wealthy, nationalisation of hospitals and housing corporations, and strongly pro-immigration/anti-racist stances — with zero right-wing nativist content.

chunk 2 · The populist appeals are firmly left-wing: the text calls for socializing large housing corporations, nationalizing hospitals, abolishing private health insurance, and placing the pharmaceutical industry under democratic control, with no nationalist or identity-based right-wing framing present.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly left-populist: it calls for nationalization of banks, energy companies, and key industries, redistribution of wealth, workers' co-determination, and frames the struggle as working people versus corporate shareholders and capitalist profit logic.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is firmly left-wing: the text calls for redistribution via wealth taxes, re-municipalization of privatized services, worker rights, anti-corporate regulation, and explicitly opposes market logic and privatization, with no nativist or right-wing identity content.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is strongly left-populist: it explicitly calls for overcoming capitalism, redistribution of wealth, abolition of the low-wage sector, nationalisation of hospitals, and open borders — all classic left-populist positions — with zero right-wing nativist or identity-protectionist content.

chunk 6 · The text is strongly left-populist: it explicitly calls for overcoming "neoliberal capitalism" characterized by deregulation and privatization, demands redistribution and global social justice, opposes corporate elites and arms exporters, and frames the struggle as one for a "Systemwechsel" — with no right-wing nativist or identity-protectionist elements.

chunk 7 · The manifesto is strongly left-populist, combining anti-corporate redistribution demands, public ownership, workers' rights, and climate justice with explicit rejection of right-wing identity politics and open support for migration and universal rights.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political rights and social liberalism (democracy, LGBTQ+, anti-discrimination, press freedom), but deeply anti-liberal on economic and financial-market dimensions, explicitly calling to 'break the power of banks and financial markets', oppose privatisation, nationalise hospitals and housing corporations, and cap rents — yielding a mixed overall liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The text scores low on economic and financial-market liberalism due to explicit calls for nationalization, rent caps, banning hedge funds from real estate, and stopping privatizations, while scoring moderately on political and social liberalism through support for constitutional rights, gender/LGBTQ+ equality, and inclusive participation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores moderately on political and social liberalism (supporting democracy, gender equality, inclusion) but is strongly anti-liberal economically, explicitly rejecting privatization, free markets, and calling for nationalization of banks, energy companies, and key industries.

chunk 4 · The text is strongly pro-democratic and pro-social equality (high lib_political and lib_social), but explicitly anti-market in economic and financial terms, calling for re-municipalization, shrinking financial markets, banning investment banking, and imposing heavy capital controls and taxes, yielding a low overall liberalism score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto scores high on political liberalism (strong defence of democracy, civil liberties, rule of law, and parliamentary control) and very high on social liberalism (extensive LGBTQ+, minority, and gender-equality rights), but is strongly anti-liberal economically (calls for abolishing capitalism, re-nationalising hospitals, abolishing the low-wage sector) and moderately anti-liberal on financial markets (state monetary sovereignty, strict regulation of fintech and digital payment systems, opposition to private currencies).

chunk 6 · The text scores high on political liberalism (strong defense of civil liberties, democratic participation, press freedom, rule of law) and social liberalism (anti-discrimination, minority rights, LGBTQ+ protections), but scores low on economic and financial-market liberalism, explicitly opposing privatization, calling for a financial transaction tax, and seeking to overcome neoliberal capitalism.

chunk 7 · The manifesto scores high on political and social liberalism (democracy, human rights, gender equality, minority rights) but is strongly anti-liberal economically, explicitly rejecting free trade, market radicalism, privatization, and financial market autonomy, while advocating financial transaction taxes, nationalization of banks operating in tax havens, and state direction of the ECB.
