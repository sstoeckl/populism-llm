WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows minimal populism: it briefly invokes community-centrism and pledges to serve all New Zealanders rather than "vocal pressure groups," but there is no sustained anti-elite rhetoric, no Manichean good-vs-evil framing, and no construction of a unified virtuous "people" against a corrupt establishment.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is a mainstream centre-left policy document with minimal populist framing; it occasionally references 'every New Zealander' and criticises past privilege, but does not construct a moralized people-vs-elite narrative or portray politics as a Manichean struggle.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is largely a programmatic policy document with minimal populist framing; there is no sustained anti-elite rhetoric, no strong people-vs-elite dichotomy, and no Manichean narrative — only occasional references to protecting ordinary citizens and cracking down on corporate fraud.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild anti-elitism and people-centrism through emphasis on acting in the interests of all New Zealanders and equitable wealth sharing, but lacks strong moral polarization.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly prioritizes New Zealand's people and interests, emphasizing 'Labour will put New Zealand first' and highlighting the people as the nation's competitive advantage, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism but lacking strong moral dichotomies.

chunk 3 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralized good-vs-evil struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto is largely non-populist, focusing on technocratic governance and market reforms, with only a minor, standard democratic appeal to local community decision-making.

chunk 2 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions, focusing instead on programmatic policy achievements and proposals.

chunk 3 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The faint populist flavour leans mildly left through commitments to equitable wealth-sharing, progressive taxation, and redistribution, but the dominant tone is technocratic centre-left governance rather than ideologically charged populism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist through emphasis on workers' rights, employment equity, state housing, and redistribution to disadvantaged groups, but the overall ideological tone is mainstream social-democratic rather than strongly populist.

chunk 3 · The manifesto reflects a centre-left Labour orientation emphasising welfare state expansion, social equality, and redistribution, but with very low populist ideological intensity — it is primarily a technocratic policy programme rather than a populist ideological manifesto.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto reflects a left-leaning populist ideology by advocating removal of subsidies and protectionism, promoting social equity, and emphasizing collective interests without strong right-wing or centrist populist themes.

chunk 2 · The text shows a moderate left populist stance through support for employment equity and workers' rights, a right-leaning nationalist tone in immigration policy prioritizing New Zealand, and centrist appeals to balanced industrial relations frameworks, indicating a mixed populist ideological flavor.

chunk 3 · There is no explicit evidence of left, right, or centrist populist ideological stances in the manifesto text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · There is no distinct populist ideology present, as the text lacks anti-elitist framing and only contains a mild centrist appeal to local community empowerment.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of a populist ideological framework in the text, as it lacks any anti-elite or people-centric discourse.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on standard social-democratic and liberal policy reforms.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic accountability and rule of law, minority and women's rights, extensive economic deregulation and trade liberalisation, and explicitly credits financial-sector deregulation with enabling free capital flows and banking competition.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it strongly supports democratic reform (proportional representation, open government), expansive anti-discrimination protections, trade liberalisation through GATT and CER, and market transparency in securities law, while also maintaining significant state intervention in housing and health.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it supports parliamentary oversight and democratic accountability, promotes anti-discrimination and gender equality legislation, encourages market competition in transport and aviation, and frames financial market reform as enabling integrity and discipline rather than restricting market function.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberalism with explicit policies on political institutions, social inclusion of minorities, economic deregulation, and financial market liberalization, all promoting market efficiency and individual rights.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political principles such as public legislative input and transparency, social liberalism through employment equity and anti-discrimination, and economic liberalism via trade liberalization, though it lacks explicit discussion of financial market regulation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal political institutions, social rights, economic development, and financial market regulation that enables market function, as shown by detailed policy commitments and legislative reforms.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly aligns with classical and ordoliberal principles, highlighting extensive deregulation of the financial, transport, and telecommunications sectors, tariff reductions, and the promotion of market competition.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal principles, including democratic transparency, anti-discrimination protections, free trade, and market-enabling financial disclosure regulations.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including anti-discrimination protections, parliamentary oversight, market competition in aviation, and prudential regulation of securities markets.
