WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto invokes the Québécois people as a unified virtuous nation whose interests are systematically betrayed by Canadian federal parties beholden to Toronto finance and Alberta oil, while framing Harper's government in starkly adversarial terms as autocratic and anti-democratic, yielding moderate populist scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto exhibits moderate populism: it frames both Liberal and Conservative federal governments as corrupt thieves who stole from unemployment insurance contributors, invokes Quebec workers as a virtuous collective, and uses moralized good-vs-evil language (e.g., "vol estimé à 57 milliards de dollars") against the political establishment.

chunk 3 · The manifesto exhibits moderate populism: it frames the federal government as systematically favouring Alberta oil and Ontario auto industries at Quebec's expense ("tout pour le pétrole albertain et l'automobile ontarienne"), invokes Quebecers as a unified virtuous people whose interests the Bloc alone defends, and portrays Conservatives and Liberals as hypocritical or acting in bad faith — but the tone is primarily technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly Manichean.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows moderate populism: Ottawa is repeatedly framed as ignoring Quebec's legitimate demands (anti-elitism), the people of Quebec are invoked as a collective victim, and both Liberals and Conservatives are portrayed as serving partisan interests over the public good, though the tone is primarily policy-focused rather than strongly moralized.

chunk 5 · The text criticises both Conservative and Liberal governments as corrupt, incompetent, or indifferent to citizens' rights, invoking the people of Québec and Canada as a virtuous collective, but the critique is primarily institutional and policy-based rather than deeply Manichean, yielding moderate populism scores.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto emphasizes the Bloc Québécois' stance against elites tied to financial and industrial powers, strong identification with the Quebec people as a unified nation, and a moralized struggle between Quebec and Canada, reflecting moderate to strong populist traits.

chunk 2 · The text shows moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moralized conflict, reflecting a moderate populist stance focused on Quebec interests against federal policies.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes federal elites for favoring other provinces over Quebec and positions the Bloc Québécois as the defender of Quebec's interests, invoking the nation and people of Quebec as a unified group and framing political opponents as equivalent adversaries, demonstrating moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral dichotomy.

chunk 4 · The manifesto criticizes Ottawa as ignoring industry demands and highlights the dependence of many communities on forestry, reflecting anti-elitism and people-centrism, with some moralized critique of government inaction.

chunk 5 · The manifesto criticizes the conservative government for obstructing parliamentary oversight and transparency, indicating a mixed anti-elitism stance but lacks explicit people-centrism or manichean rhetoric.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text frames the federal government as corrupt, autocratically bafouing laws, and acting against the virtuous will of the Quebec nation.

chunk 2 · The text frames federal elites as corrupt actors who 'stole' billions from the unemployment fund and engage in political tricks against the interests of ordinary citizens.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the federal government as hypocritical and out of touch with the interests of the Quebec people.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits mild anti-elitism by criticizing the federal government in Ottawa for ignoring regional demands, but lacks strong moralizing or people-centric populist rhetoric.

chunk 5 · The text contains mild anti-elitist criticism of the ruling Conservative government's lack of transparency and bypass of parliament, but lacks strong moralizing or people-centric populist rhetoric.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The dominant ideological flavour is left-leaning (social-democracy, solidarity, workers' rights, social housing) combined with a strong ethno-nationalist/identity-protectionist strand (French language supremacy, Quebec sovereignty, opposition to multiculturalism), making it a mixed left-nationalist populism with the left dimension slightly dominant.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is predominantly left-leaning, emphasizing social housing reinvestment, employment insurance reform, wage equity, anti-scab legislation, and support for low-income groups, with a secondary Quebec nationalist/identity dimension (French language protection) but no significant right-wing populist anti-immigration framing.

chunk 3 · The populist flavour leans left: the manifesto champions workers, retirees, SMEs, social economy ("primauté des personnes et du travail sur le capital"), surtaxes on the wealthy and executive bonuses, and attacks on oil-company subsidies and tax havens; a secondary ethno-regional (Quebec nationalist) strand adds a mild right-populist tinge, but the dominant programmatic content is redistributive and pro-labour.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is primarily left-leaning (state intervention, supply management, public services, worker support) with a strong Quebec nationalist/identitarian overlay (food sovereignty, Quebec's fair share), making it a left-nationalist blend rather than purely left or right populism.

chunk 5 · The Bloc Québécois combines left-leaning concerns (workers' rights, redistribution, social equality, anti-corporate dumping) with a mild Quebec-nationalist identity dimension, placing it primarily on the left of the populist spectrum with a secondary identity/nationalist element.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text shows left-leaning populist ideology through defense of social groups and redistribution, right-leaning elements in national and resource protectionism, and centrist appeals to Quebec's unique identity and autonomy, indicating a mixed but distinct populist ideological profile.

chunk 2 · The manifesto combines left-leaning economic development and social justice proposals with some right-leaning critiques of federal fiscal policies, and centrist pragmatic problem-solving, indicating a mixed populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The manifesto combines left-wing economic populism through opposition to subsidies for oil companies and support for social programs with right-wing populist emphasis on Quebec nationhood and protection of Quebec's autonomy, alongside centrist appeals to pragmatic governance, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports left-leaning populist policies like defending supply management and opposes right-leaning privatization, while also emphasizing regional autonomy and centrist people-elite negotiation appeals.

chunk 5 · The manifesto advocates for social justice in trade negotiations and labor rights, reflecting a left-leaning populist ideology focused on protecting workers and fundamental rights.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist appeal is combined with a strong social-democratic, left-wing economic stance and Quebec nationalism.

chunk 2 · The populist rhetoric is combined with a left-wing focus on social housing, public services, and protecting workers against corporate and federal elites.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is primarily left-wing and regionalist, focusing on making the wealthy pay their fair share and defending Quebec's distinct interests against the federal elite.

chunk 4 · There is no clear populist ideological framing, as the text focuses primarily on regionalist and sector-specific economic demands.

chunk 5 · There is no clear populist ideological framing present in the text, as the focus is on standard policy debates and institutional critiques.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly defends democratic institutions, rule of law, and independent oversight bodies (lib_political=7), advocates for women's and minority rights (lib_social=6), supports regulated but fair trade while opposing social dumping (lib_economic=4), and criticises using the SCHL to bail out banks rather than fund social housing, reflecting a sceptical stance toward market-based finance (lib_financial_market=3).

chunk 2 · The manifesto is moderately liberal: it strongly supports civil rights, gender equality, and refugee rights (pro-liberal socially and politically), while also advocating significant state intervention in housing, employment insurance, and energy policy that tempers economic liberalism; financial market references focus on anti-fraud regulation rather than market liberalization.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports a Canada-EU free-trade agreement and carbon-market mechanisms (pro-liberal), but also proposes surtaxes on high incomes, buy-local procurement rules, tighter foreign-investment thresholds, and opposes a pan-Canadian securities regulator on jurisdictional grounds — reflecting a blend of market-enabling and market-restricting stances across dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports democratic process and individual rights abroad, but advocates state intervention in markets (supply management, government loans, opposition to postal privatization, food sovereignty against market liberalization), reflecting a social-democratic rather than classical liberal orientation.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is broadly pro-democratic institutions and rule of law, supports trade openness and investment protection, but tempers economic liberalism with calls for social clauses, safeguard measures, and a financial-transactions tax, producing mixed-to-moderate liberal scores across dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, civil rights, social welfare, and economic policies favoring provincial autonomy and market functions, but shows skepticism toward financial markets linked to external elites, resulting in a moderate pro-liberalism stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports political rights, social equality, and economic liberalism through sustainable development and innovation policies, but lacks discussion on financial market regulation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports liberal principles by advocating for democratic rights and provincial autonomy, social protections and program funding, pragmatic economic management, and financial market regulation respecting Quebec's jurisdiction, indicating a generally pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto advocates for political rights protection abroad, social policies like GMO labeling, and economic support for agriculture, but lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 5 · The manifesto supports democratic processes, political rights, social equality, and free trade balanced with regulation, indicating a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political democracy and social rights, but opposes free-market economic liberalism in favor of state intervention and trade protectionism.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports fundamental civil rights and non-discrimination, while advocating for state-supported economic measures and financial regulations to protect small investors.

chunk 3 · The text shows mixed support for liberalism, endorsing free trade with Europe but opposing a unified national financial regulator and advocating for state intervention and local purchasing preferences.

chunk 4 · The text supports democratic institutions and parliamentary will, but strongly opposes economic deregulation, free trade, and liberalized financial markets in favor of state intervention, subsidies, and protectionism.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports political and social rights, and endorses free trade and investment protection, but advocates for social regulations, trade safeguards, and a financial transaction tax.
