WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows minimal populism: it briefly contrasts Fianna Fáil's competence against "weak Coalition" opponents and invokes the Irish people's support, but the dominant tone is technocratic governance and economic management rather than a moralized people-vs-elite struggle.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains virtually no populist rhetoric: there is no anti-elite framing, no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, and references to 'the people' or 'citizens' are incidental and non-mobilising, amounting to standard governmental policy language.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild anti-elitism and people-centrism through references to opponents and appeals to the Irish people, but lacks strong moral polarization, indicating low populism overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains no explicit discussion or framing of elites, 'the people', or moralized good-vs-evil struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits very low populism, focusing on technocratic national recovery and consensus-building with social partners rather than anti-elite or moralized struggles.

chunk 2 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions, focusing instead on standard policy achievements and proposals.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist flavour is weakly centrist, relying on generic appeals to Fianna Fáil's unique governing competence and national recovery rather than clear left-wing redistribution or right-wing nativist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mild centre-left tendencies through social housing expansion and welfare provisions, but no strong populist ideological flavour of any stripe; it is primarily a technocratic governing record document.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text reflects a moderate left populist emphasis on social equity, a right-leaning pro-European market stance, and centrist consensus-building, resulting in a moderate overall populist ideology.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit evidence of left, right, or centrist populist ideological stances in the manifesto text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is minimal and centrist, appealing broadly to the Irish people without strong left-wing anti-corporate or right-wing anti-immigrant rhetoric.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of a populist ideology, as the text lacks any anti-establishment or people-centric framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions tax reduction, free enterprise, financial services deregulation, parliamentary democracy, and gender equality in social insurance, reflecting a consistent classical-liberal orientation.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it supports democratic norms and human rights internationally, advances gender equality and employment equality legislation domestically, promotes low-interest-rate market conditions and private investment, and explicitly expands building society financial services, all consistent with a moderate classical-liberal orientation.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through accountability and transparency, social liberalism via expanded social benefits, economic liberalism through tax reforms and market growth, and financial market liberalism by promoting international financial services, indicating strong liberalism overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal political rights, social protections against discrimination, economic reforms promoting market functions, and financial market modernization, demonstrating a strong pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal and ordoliberal principles, emphasizing tax reduction, fiscal discipline, market-based financial services, and private investment.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including democratic rights, anti-discrimination protections, market-driven economic growth, and the expansion of financial services through legislative reform.
