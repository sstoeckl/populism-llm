WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames politics as a struggle between "the people" (gente trabajadora, pensionistas, jóvenes) and corrupt elites (bancos, fondos buitre, IBEX35 executives, media owned by banks), with Unidas Podemos positioned as the only force with "manos libres" to fight for the people.

chunk 2 · The manifesto frames elites (political, financial, corporate) as corrupt and self-serving at the expense of ordinary citizens, invokes 'la ciudadanía' as the virtuous collective, and draws a clear moral contrast between the people and privileged minorities, though the tone is more programmatic than apocalyptic.

chunk 3 · The text frames the banking bailout as an elite-benefiting operation at the expense of ordinary workers and families, proposes recovering funds from banks via special taxes, and contrasts "la gente" against large corporations and the political right, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism and people-centrism.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elites and powerful economic actors as corrupt and controlling, emphasizes trust in 'our people' to face challenges, and frames political struggle in moral terms against the extreme right, demonstrating strong populist rhetoric.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the influence of private economic interests and corruption, emphasizes the sovereignty of 'the people', and highlights the moral struggle against corruption, supporting a moderately high populism score.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elite tax avoidance and privileges, advocates for tax justice benefiting the people, and frames the Church and financial elites as privileged adversaries, showing strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames a moralized struggle between 'the people' and corrupt 'powerful' elites, including media spokespersons and financial institutions.

chunk 2 · The text frames a clear conflict between corrupt elites who have expelled citizens and the virtuous people from whom democratic power emanates.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing policies as recovering rights for 'the people' and criticizing political opponents.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populism is strongly left-wing, centered on anti-corporate rhetoric, redistribution, public ownership of strategic sectors, labor rights, and social equality, with explicit attacks on large corporations, banks, and financial elites exploiting workers and the public.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly left-populist: it targets corporate elites, financial institutions, and the wealthy 1% for redistribution, champions workers' rights and public services, and frames the struggle as ordinary people versus privileged minorities, with no significant right-wing nativist or identity-based populist content.

chunk 3 · The text is strongly left-populist: it proposes wealth taxes on large fortunes, progressive income tax reform, a financial transactions tax, a special bank tax, and public enterprise expansion to displace private corporations, with no right-wing nativist or anti-immigration content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-wing populist themes of anti-corporate privilege and defense of working people with right-wing populist references to combating the extreme right, and centrist populist critiques of mainstream parties, showing a mixed but predominantly left populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The manifesto advocates for left-leaning populist economic policies such as taxing large fortunes and corporations, while also showing centrist populist appeals to the people versus elites, but lacks right-wing populist themes.

chunk 3 · The text supports left-wing populist ideology through progressive taxation and redistribution, shows centrist populist appeals to territorial unity and dialogue, but lacks right-wing nationalist or anti-immigration content.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist discourse is heavily left-wing, focusing on anti-corporate rhetoric, taxing the wealthy, and protecting working-class people from financial elites.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on ending the fiscal privileges of a wealthy minority and redistributing corporate wealth.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on taxing large fortunes, corporations, and banks to redistribute wealth.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores high on social liberalism (LGBTQ+ rights, feminist agenda, civil rights) and moderately on political liberalism (electoral reform, anti-corruption, democratic participation), but is anti-liberal economically (public ownership, state intervention, financial transaction taxes, capital controls) and in financial markets (bank nationalization, transaction taxes, debt restructuring).

chunk 2 · The manifesto scores high on political and social liberalism (judicial independence, anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ rights, democratic reform) but is strongly anti-liberal on economic and financial-market dimensions, advocating rent controls, market interventions, financial transaction taxes, bank levies, and nationalization of housing stock.

chunk 3 · The text is moderately anti-liberal economically: it proposes heavy progressive taxation, a financial transactions tax, a special bank levy, and expansion of public enterprises to compete with private corporations, while showing some pro-liberal political stances (democratic conflict resolution, territorial reform) and social equity measures.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions and media freedom, promotes strong social rights including gender equality, advocates for economic competition and anti-monopoly laws, and calls for recovering public funds from financial bailouts, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports liberal democratic institutions, social equality laws, progressive economic policies, and financial market regulations that enable market function, indicating a strong liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto promotes liberal principles by advocating for democratic reforms in tax administration, civil equality (removal of Church tax exemptions), progressive economic policies, and financial market regulation enabling market function.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic reforms and LGBTQ+ rights, but rejects economic and financial liberalism in favor of state intervention and public ownership.

chunk 2 · The text shows a mixed stance on liberalism, strongly supporting political and social rights while heavily opposing economic and financial market freedoms through price controls and targeted taxes.

chunk 3 · The text is highly interventionist and anti-liberal economically, proposing heavy financial transaction taxes, bank taxes, and wealth taxes, while supporting democratic processes for territorial conflicts.
