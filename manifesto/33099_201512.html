WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The text consistently invokes the democratic mandate of "the people of Catalonia" as a unified virtuous group against the Spanish State's systematic refusals, framing the conflict as a clear democratic struggle, though the anti-elitist and Manichean elements are directed at institutional actors rather than a corrupt elite per se.

chunk 2 · The manifesto frames the Spanish central government (PP) as attacking Catalan democracy and breaking fundamental democratic norms, invoking the Catalan people's will to self-determination, but the anti-elitism and Manichean framing are moderate and primarily directed at the Spanish state rather than a generalized elite.

chunk 3 · The manifesto frames the Spanish central government (PP) as deliberately hostile and financially strangling Catalonia, invoking moderate anti-elite and Manichean rhetoric, but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

chunk 4 · The text shows mild populist tendencies: it criticises the central government for favouring large monopolistic firms and acting in a partisan manner on security, but the dominant register is technocratic-programmatic (tax reform, financial regulation, infrastructure), with no sustained people-vs-elite moral framing.

chunk 5 · The text criticises the central Spanish government for partisan abuse of security and justice policy and frames reforms as returning power to citizens, but the rhetoric is primarily institutional/autonomist rather than strongly populist in the POPPA sense.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text explicitly frames the Spanish state as obstructing dialogue and negotiation, denying the Catalan people's democratic right to decide, invoking a unified 'people' with a clear mandate, and describing a struggle against an obstructive state, indicating moderate to strong populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict.

chunk 2 · The text explicitly criticizes the central government (PP) for obstructing Catalonia's democratic self-determination, framing elites as obstructive and violating democratic norms, while emphasizing the people's right to decide their political future, indicating a strong populist stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto expresses distrust and opposition towards the Spanish State and its elites, highlighting financial oppression and cultural hostility, while emphasizing the Catalan people’s struggle and grievances, indicating moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict.

chunk 4 · No evidence of populist rhetoric or themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moral dichotomies was found in the text.

chunk 5 · The text criticizes elite institutions like the Tribunal Constitucional and the central government for partisan misuse and undermining popular sovereignty, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text frames a conflict between the virtuous Catalan people and the Spanish state elites who systematically deny their democratic rights.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the Spanish state's actions as a direct attack on democracy and the citizens' right to decide.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate anti-elitism by framing the central Spanish government as hostile and deliberately suffocating Catalonia financially.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits mild populism primarily through anti-elitist criticism of central state elites and political clientelism, without strong moralizing or people-centrist rhetoric.

chunk 5 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the central government's partisan use of security institutions and calling to return politics to the citizens.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is primarily ethno-territorial/nationalist (Catalan independence), with minor left-leaning social welfare references and centrist democratic-regeneration appeals, making it a mixed but predominantly right-nationalist populist flavor in the European sense.

chunk 2 · The manifesto combines a left-leaning social agenda (reducing inequality, welfare state expansion, workers' rights) with a strong ethno-territorial/nationalist dimension (Catalan independence), placing it at the intersection of left-populism and right-identitarian populism, with the independence agenda being the dominant frame.

chunk 3 · The manifesto blends centre-left redistributive demands (fairer wealth distribution, worker protections, minimum wage increases) with Catalan nationalist identity claims, producing a mixed left-leaning populist ideology without strong right-wing nativist content.

chunk 4 · The manifesto's populist flavour is primarily centrist-technocratic (good governance, consumer protection, SME support) with a mild left-leaning tilt on tax fairness and labour income, but no strong left redistributive or right nativist programme.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is primarily a Catalan autonomist/pro-independence programme; its populist flavour is centrist-reformist (anti-corruption, democratic regeneration) with mild left-leaning social policy elements and minimal right-wing identity framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto combines left-leaning economic grievances about fiscal oppression with right-leaning identity defense and a centrist inclusive movement narrative, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports left-leaning populist themes such as reducing inequalities and increasing social cohesion, while also emphasizing Catalonia's national independence agenda and negotiation with Spain and the EU, reflecting a mix of left and right populist ideology with centrist appeals.

chunk 3 · The text combines left-leaning concerns about social justice and redistribution with right-leaning nationalist grievances and centrist appeals to institutional reform, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 4 · The text does not present any clear populist ideological stance, left, right, or centrist, as it focuses on administrative, fiscal, and institutional reforms without populist framing.

chunk 5 · The manifesto combines left-leaning social policies like universal healthcare recovery with right-leaning critiques of immigration expulsions and centrist appeals to popular will, indicating a mixed populist ideological stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist appeal is centrist and nationalist, focusing on the collective will of the Catalan people against the Spanish state without a distinct left-right economic focus.

chunk 2 · The populist framing is combined with a center-left socio-economic agenda focused on reducing inequalities and expanding the welfare state.

chunk 3 · The populist elements are centrist-regionalist, focusing on the defense of Catalan institutions and resources against the central state without a strong left-right populist ideology.

chunk 4 · The populist elements are centrist and regionalist, focusing on defending Catalan institutions and local SMEs against central state monopolies and political clientelism.

chunk 5 · The populist elements are centrist and regionalist, focusing on Catalan self-determination and democratic regeneration without strong left-right economic populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The text defends democratic institutions, separation of powers, and fundamental rights against the PP government's centralization, while criticizing financial intervention in Catalan governance and unfair fiscal policies, yielding a mixed liberal profile that is pro-liberal in political terms but focused on autonomy rather than classical economic liberalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on social issues (LGBTQI+ rights, gender equality) and shows mixed signals on political liberalism (defending separation of powers while pursuing independence unilaterally) and economic liberalism (market efficiency invoked but also strong welfare-state and redistributive commitments); financial market references are limited to credit access for the third sector.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it defends LGBTQI+ rights and gender equality, university autonomy, consumer protection in financial markets, and local political autonomy, while also calling for progressive taxation and labour market reforms that temper pure economic liberalism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it supports judicial independence and rule of law, proposes corporate-tax cuts and SME deregulation, promotes banking competition and consumer protection in financial markets, and raises individual rights (dying with dignity), yielding a moderate-to-high overall liberalism score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto strongly defends rule of law, separation of powers, and individual rights (high lib_political/lib_social), supports EU single market freedoms (moderate lib_economic), but advocates a Financial Transactions Tax which is mildly anti-liberal for financial markets.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text documents weakening democracy under the Spanish government, linguistic and cultural rights being undermined, economic and financial controls imposed on Catalonia, all indicating a low to moderate liberalism stance with verified evidence in political, social, economic, and financial market dimensions.

chunk 2 · The text shows limited support for liberalism, criticizing central government overreach and defending social rights and equality, but also highlighting economic centralization and underuse of financial market instruments, indicating mixed liberal economic and political stances.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports political liberalism through recognition of language rights and institutions, social liberalism via gender equality policies, economic liberalism by promoting employment and fiscal reform, and financial market liberalism by advocating competition and consumer protection in banking.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles such as transparency and good governance laws, fiscal reforms favoring SMEs, and financial market regulation promoting competition and consumer protection.

chunk 5 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, civil rights, social protections, and some economic regulation reforms, showing a moderately pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text defends democratic rules, fundamental rights, and regional economic autonomy, while criticizing state interventions and centralizing financial regulations.

chunk 2 · The text strongly defends liberal-democratic principles, such as the separation of powers and fundamental civil rights, while advocating for social equality.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including minority rights, institutional autonomy, tax reductions for small businesses, and investor protection frameworks.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including judicial independence, market competition, investor protection, and individual social rights.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports liberal political and social rights, including the separation of powers and human rights, while showing mixed economic views by supporting the EU single market alongside financial transaction taxes.
