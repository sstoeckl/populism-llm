WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly opposes populism ("להילחם בפופוליזם המסוכן") and frames its welfare and economic policies in technocratic, programmatic terms with no anti-elite rhetoric or Manichean people-vs-elite framing, yielding very low populism scores across all sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains mild populist elements—invoking "the silent majority" of taxpayers against corrupt politicians and large corporations—but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-oriented, with anti-elitism framed narrowly around anti-corruption rather than a broad people-vs-elite struggle.

chunk 3 · The manifesto focuses on anti-corruption governance reforms and institutional transparency, invoking "the public" as beneficiary of clean government, but does not frame politics as a Manichean struggle between a virtuous people and a corrupt elite; anti-elitism is mild and procedural rather than populist.

chunk 4 · The manifesto (Yesh Atid, Israel 2021) scores low on populism: it criticises political corruption and self-interest ("האינטרס הפוליטי קודם לטובת הציבור") but frames this in technocratic, institutional terms rather than as a Manichean people-vs.-elite struggle; the "people" are invoked mainly as service recipients, not as a virtuous collective pitted against a corrupt establishment.

chunk 5 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto (Yesh Atid, Israel 2021) focused on healthcare, transport, disability rights, and digital equity; it invokes citizens' welfare and occasionally frames public money as belonging to the people ("הכסף הזה צריך לחזור לציבור"), but contains virtually no anti-elite rhetoric, no Manichean framing, and no unified "people vs. corrupt elite" narrative — populism scores are minimal.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is a detailed policy platform (Yesh Atid, Israel 2021) focused on environment, innovation, minority integration, and elderly welfare; it contains virtually no populist rhetoric — no elite-bashing, no moralized people-vs-corrupt-establishment framing, and no Manichean narrative, scoring near zero across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 7 · The manifesto is a detailed policy platform (Yesh Atid, Israel 2021) focused on eldercare, energy, small business, immigration, agriculture, and sport; it contains only mild, incidental criticism of past governments' discriminatory policies toward small businesses and no sustained populist framing of a virtuous people versus a corrupt elite.

chunk 8 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto (Yesh Atid, Israel 2021) focused on water management, sports, education, periphery development, and culture; it contains virtually no populist rhetoric — no sustained elite-bashing, no moralized good-vs-evil framing, and only incidental references to "public corruption" in local government and an "injustice" in the culture sector, yielding very low scores across all three populism sub-dimensions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is centrist-liberal with mild social-democratic elements (poverty reduction, welfare expansion) and mild nationalist security positions, but explicitly rejects populism and frames itself as a responsible governing party, resulting in very low ideological populism scores across left, right, and centrist dimensions.

chunk 2 · The populist flavor is weakly centrist-liberal: appeals to the middle class and periphery residents without strong left redistributive or right nativist framing; the anti-corruption agenda and pro-SME stance reflect a centrist, good-governance orientation rather than a clear left or right populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is centrist-liberal in orientation, combining good-governance technocracy with social equality and Jewish-democratic identity, without strong left-wing redistributive or right-wing nativist populist framing.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is centrist-liberal with mild left-leaning social-equity concerns (reducing health and geographic gaps) and a mild civic-nationalist framing (Israel as the Jewish national home with equal rights for all citizens), but no strong left-populist redistribution rhetoric or right-populist nativist/anti-immigration content; the dominant ideological flavour is good-governance centrism.

chunk 5 · The manifesto's mild populist flavour leans slightly left-centrist through emphasis on reducing social gaps, expanding public services, and protecting disability rights, but it lacks strong class-based or redistributive anti-corporate rhetoric; no right-wing nationalist or identity-based populism is present.

chunk 6 · The manifesto leans centrist-liberal with mild centre-left social equity themes (reducing gaps between Jewish and non-Jewish citizens, welfare expansion) and a mild centre-right affirmation of Israel's Jewish-democratic character, but no strong populist ideological flavour dominates.

chunk 7 · The manifesto is centrist-liberal in orientation, combining market-friendly reforms (credit market liberalization, deregulation, tax relief for SMEs) with moderate social-welfare extensions; it does not exhibit strong left-populist redistribution rhetoric or right-populist nativist framing.

chunk 8 · The manifesto is predominantly centrist-liberal in orientation (Yesh Atid), combining mild social-democratic elements (reducing gaps, subsidizing education, supporting periphery) with pro-market and pro-democratic values; there is minimal nationalist-right content (one reference to Israeli flag at competitions) and no strong left-populist anti-corporate framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses liberal democracy ("לחזק את הדמוקרטיה הליברלית בעולם"), LGBTQ+ rights, free-market competition through import reform and deregulation, and financial market access for small businesses, reflecting consistently high liberalism scores across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions rule of law and anti-corruption (political), gender equality and women's rights (social), deregulation and SME competition (economic), and opening credit markets to non-bank competitors (financial market).

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly endorses checks and balances, judicial independence, LGBTQ+ rights, civil marriage, minority protections, and market competition in kashrut, reflecting high scores across political, social, and economic liberalism dimensions; financial-market policy is entirely absent.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across political, social, and economic dimensions: it champions checks and balances, judicial independence, a written constitution, equal rights for all citizens including minorities and LGBTQ+ (surrogacy for the LGBTQ+ community mentioned), market competition in kashrut and other sectors, and decentralisation; no financial-market policy is discussed.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it champions individual rights and legal entrenchment of disability rights (lib_social), calls for deregulation to enable shared-transport markets (lib_economic), recognises internet access as a human right within a rule-of-law framework (lib_political), and mentions accessible banking/financial services in the context of digital inclusion (lib_financial_market).

chunk 6 · The manifesto strongly affirms democratic and civic equality principles (Jewish-democratic state, full civil equality regardless of religion/race/gender), promotes deregulation and competition in the hi-tech and business sectors, and addresses financial institutions with ESG-oriented prudential requirements, yielding consistently pro-liberal scores across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 7 · The manifesto consistently promotes deregulation, market competition, anti-discrimination, and institutional oversight mechanisms across energy, credit markets, small business, and minority rights, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 8 · The manifesto strongly endorses liberal values across political (democratic discourse, rule of law, decentralization), social (explicit non-discrimination by religion, race, sex, disability, sexual identity; inclusion of special-needs children), and economic (fair competition, OECD-aligned pricing, support for SMEs and hi-tech) dimensions; financial-market policy is entirely absent from the text.
