WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly frames the political and economic elite as corrupt captors of Hungarian democracy ("kartell-pártok gazdasági érdekcsoportjai foglyul ejtették a magyar politikát"), invokes responsible citizens reclaiming democracy, and draws a moral contrast between the corrupt establishment and the people, yielding moderate-to-high populism scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The text shows moderate populist features: it frames large landowners, banks, and global capital as exploiters of ordinary people and local communities, and calls for redistribution from the rich to the poor, but does so within a policy-reform framework rather than a strongly Manichean or people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populism: it frames the ruling party as distributing public money to its "clients and oligarchs," invokes local communities as virtuous actors, and contrasts the people's interests against corrupt elites, but the dominant tone is technocratic-programmatic rather than strongly populist.

chunk 4 · The text contains mild anti-elitist references to elite groups benefiting from the education system and party-connected oligarchs dominating decisions, but the dominant tone is policy-programmatic rather than populist; people-centrism is present but framed in terms of equal opportunity rather than a virtuous unified people versus corrupt elites.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the political and economic elite's corrupt entanglements, emphasizes the role of responsible citizens reclaiming democracy, and highlights moral conflicts in politics, demonstrating strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elite dominance and legal power imbalances in financial contracts, highlights the plight of ordinary people, and points to exploitative practices by banks, indicating moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the ruling party for favoring its own clients and oligarchs, supports local communities as key actors, and emphasizes reducing social inequalities, indicating a moderate populist stance with anti-elitism and people-centrism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elite favoritism in education and advocates for equal opportunities, emphasizing the community and social justice, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moralized critique of political decisions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames the political and economic elites as corrupt cartels exploiting the country, while calling for citizens to reclaim democratic politics.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing banks and oligarchs as corrupt elites exploiting vulnerable citizens.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the ruling political class as corrupt actors who distribute public funds to their own oligarchs and clients, while championing the local communities and small-scale producers.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing party-affiliated oligarchs and political elites while advocating for putting resources like renewable energy back into the hands of the people.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The party is explicitly "Globalizáció- és kapitalizmus-kritikus," frames global capitalism as exploitative, advocates redistribution and social justice, and calls for an eco-social market economy—placing it firmly on the left-populist spectrum, with only minor nativist elements regarding Hungarians abroad.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is predominantly left-leaning, emphasizing reduction of exploitation, poverty, and territorial inequality, redistribution of land from large estates to small family farms and local communities, and strong social protections for women and disabled persons, with only minor national-protectionist elements regarding EU land rules.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is primarily left-leaning, emphasizing redistribution (progressive income tax, taxing the wealthy), anti-corporate elite critique, and social equality, with minor protectionist/national-product elements but no strong right-wing identity politics.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is primarily a green/ecological programme (LMP – Politics Can Be Different) with left-leaning social elements (free tuition, adult education, energy poverty relief, redistribution of cultural resources) and a mild national-minority dimension; it does not fit a strongly left-populist or right-populist mould but leans modestly left-green.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates for social justice and critiques economic growth paths, supports community and cultural identity while rejecting exclusion, and emphasizes citizen participation, reflecting a left-leaning populist ideology with centrist elements and some right-leaning cultural concerns.

chunk 2 · The text advocates for reducing poverty by limiting elite privileges, empowering local communities over resources, and supports left-leaning redistribution and social equality themes, with some centrist localism and right-leaning community protection elements.

chunk 3 · The text advocates for social equality and support for local economies (left populism), sets higher quality standards for domestic products (right populism), and emphasizes local community interests without a clear programmatic bias, reflecting a centrist populist ideology overall.

chunk 4 · The text supports social justice and equal access to education (left populism), acknowledges national identity and minority rights (right populism), and appeals broadly to the community (centrist populism), indicating a mixed but moderate populist ideological stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is heavily left-wing, focusing on anti-capitalist, anti-globalization, and environmentalist critiques of the elite.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is predominantly left-wing, focusing on redistribution from the rich to the poor and protecting local communities from big capital.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is predominantly left-wing, focusing on anti-corporate sentiments, criticizing strategic agreements with multinationals, and advocating for local, green, and cooperative economic structures.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is primarily left-wing, focusing heavily on social justice, free education, and green transition, though it also includes a supportive stance on national minority rights for Hungarians abroad.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports restoring rule of law, checks and balances, and participatory democracy (pro-liberal politically), promotes gender equality and minority rights (pro-liberal socially), advocates regulated but fair market competition (mixed economically), and calls for stronger financial consumer protection and banking transparency (mixed on financial markets), yielding an overall moderate liberal score.

chunk 2 · The text is strongly pro-liberal on social dimensions (gender equality, anti-discrimination, civil rights for disabled persons, women's political representation) and moderately pro-liberal on political institutions (parliamentary oversight, due process), but explicitly anti-liberal on economic and financial-market dimensions by advocating land redistribution, caps on bank interest rates, restrictions on free capital flows, and state intervention in credit markets.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is moderately liberal: it supports democratic participation, competition policy, and market-correcting green taxes, while also advocating state-led investment in education and community economies, reflecting a mixed but generally pro-liberal orientation.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports institutional independence, transparency, rule of law, minority rights, and competitive economic development, reflecting moderate classical-liberal values, though it also advocates significant state investment in green industries and free higher education, producing a mixed but moderately pro-liberal overall picture.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto discusses the erosion of the rule of law, advocates for women's social equality, calls for regulated fair competition, and supports financial consumer protection, indicating moderate support for classical liberal political, social, economic, and financial market principles.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports political liberalism through advocating gender quotas and political representation, social liberalism via gender equality and anti-discrimination, economic liberalism by promoting affordable rural credit and sustainable development, and financial market liberalism by calling for stronger consumer protection and banking competition.

chunk 3 · The manifesto promotes democratic principles through education, social equity via early development services, economic support for small businesses, and mixed signals on financial market regulation, indicating a generally pro-liberal stance with some reservations on financial market policies.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports political rights and environmental protections, emphasizes cultural pluralism, and promotes a green economy with market-based solutions, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall, though financial market policy is not addressed.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports democratic institutions and social equality, but advocates for heavy state regulation of markets and financial interest rates, rejecting pure free-market liberalism.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports social and political liberalism through gender equality and democratic oversight, but rejects economic and financial liberalism by advocating for market restrictions and interest rate caps.

chunk 3 · The text supports democratic education and social equality, but takes a highly interventionist stance on economic and financial matters, advocating for state-backed credit institutions and market-distorting environmental regulations.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports political liberalism through checks and balances and minority rights, while taking a more interventionist, state-supported approach to green economic development.
