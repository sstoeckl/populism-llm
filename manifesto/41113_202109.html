WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows minimal populism: it invokes 'the people' and their needs as a policy reference point and mildly criticises profit-driven economic models, but frames politics in terms of democratic accountability and expert-guided governance rather than a corrupt elite vs. virtuous people dichotomy.

chunk 2 · The text shows only mild populist features: it occasionally frames large corporations and the wealthy as needing to be held accountable, but does so within a technocratic, policy-oriented framework rather than through a moralized people-vs.-elite narrative; the people are not invoked as a unified virtuous bloc, and there is no Manichean good-vs.-evil framing.

chunk 3 · The text contains mild populist elements—criticising oligopolies, large corporations shifting profits to tax havens, and calling for social rights for "every person"—but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than morally charged people-vs-elite rhetoric, yielding a low overall populism score.

chunk 4 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto (Greens, Germany 2021) focused on housing, education, science, and social cohesion; it contains only very mild populist signals—occasional references to speculators and democracy's enemies—but no sustained elite-bashing, no unified 'pure people' rhetoric, and no Manichean good-vs-evil framing.

chunk 5 · The text shows minimal populist features: it references protecting politics from opaque lobbying influence and invokes citizen trust, but frames these within institutional reform rather than a people-vs-corrupt-elite narrative; there is no Manichean good-vs-evil framing or strong people-centrism.

chunk 6 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto focused on international cooperation, EU reform, human rights, and sport policy; it contains only isolated, mild references to corruption among sports officials and citizen participation, with no sustained anti-elite rhetoric, people-as-virtuous-bloc framing, or good-vs-evil populist narrative.

chunk 7 · The text shows minimal populist features: it invokes serving "the people" and appeals to civic participation, but frames politics as cooperative and pluralist rather than as a moral struggle between a virtuous people and a corrupt elite.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it affirms democratic accountability, gender equality and pluralism, supports market-based instruments (ETS, CO2 pricing, competition), and endorses market-enabling regulation, though it also calls for significant state intervention in energy and transport markets, producing a mixed economic-liberal picture.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (upholding democratic primacy, anti-discrimination, gender equality), moderately liberal economically (endorsing fair competition and markets but with strong regulatory and redistributive interventions), and mixed on financial markets (supporting ESG transparency and investor protection but also proposing a financial transaction tax, separation banking, and leverage caps that restrict market-based finance).

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores moderately on liberalism overall: it strongly supports civil rights and anti-discrimination (lib_social high), upholds institutional independence (lib_political moderate-high), but is mixed on economic liberalism—opposing further privatisation, proposing wealth and financial-transaction taxes, and expanding state social provision—while supporting a digital euro with data security and market-enabling rules.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly endorses liberal democracy, rule of law, and civil/social rights (LGBTQ+, anti-racism, minority protections), scoring high on political and social liberalism; economic liberalism is mixed due to extensive market regulation in housing (rent caps, speculation bans, social housing mandates); financial-market liberalism is moderate, with transparency measures (property register, cash ban) balanced against anti-speculation interventions.

chunk 5 · The text is strongly pro-liberal across political and social dimensions—emphasising democracy, rule of law, parliamentary control, anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ rights, and minority protections—while also supporting market competition and prudential financial oversight, yielding a high overall liberalism score.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions—consistently advocating democratic institutions, rule of law, minority rights, and gender equality—while showing mixed signals on economic liberalism (supporting fair market access but also debt restructuring and food speculation controls) and mildly anti-liberal on financial markets (endorsing capital controls for Global South countries).

chunk 7 · The text strongly endorses parliamentary oversight, rule of law, democratic civil society, and gender equality, reflecting a clearly pro-liberal stance across political and social dimensions, while economic references are mixed between market use and state-led investment.
