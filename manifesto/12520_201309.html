WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto blends mild left-leaning redistribution concerns (opposing large economic inequalities, fair distribution of resources) with conservative social positions (defending traditional marriage, restricting abortion), while explicitly positioning itself as a centrist 'third way' alternative to both left and right systemic ideologies.

chunk 2 · The manifesto blends modest centre-right elements (Christian values, family, anti-drug, minority crime mention) with centre-left elements (public responsibility for health/dental care, welfare expansion), reflecting a Christian-democratic centrist orientation with no strong populist ideological flavour.

chunk 3 · The manifesto blends mild centre-right cultural conservatism (emphasis on Christian heritage, religious freedom) with support for the welfare state and ideelle (non-profit) sector, without strong left or right populist programmatic content; the overall populist ideological flavour is weak.

chunk 4 · The text blends modest centre-right elements (Norwegian ownership in fisheries, predictable business frameworks) with mild centre-left concerns (combating social dumping, worker rights), reflecting a Christian-democratic centrist profile with no strong populist ideological flavour.

chunk 5 · The manifesto blends modest centre-left social welfare commitments (support for disadvantaged groups, housing subsidies) with mild centre-right immigration regulation, but neither strand is framed in populist ideological terms; the overall populist ideological flavour is very weak.

chunk 6 · The manifesto's modest populist signals lean slightly left (redistribution, aid to poor countries) but are embedded in a mainstream Christian-democratic programmatic framework with no strong ideological populist identity.

chunk 7 · No populist ideological framing (left, right, or centrist) is detectable; the text is a technocratic defence policy document focused on military readiness, veterans, and civil preparedness.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto affirms democracy and rule of law as protections against power abuse (pro-liberal politically), endorses universal human equality and minority dignity but restricts LGBTQ+ rights such as same-sex marriage (mixed socially), and combines free-market subsidiarity with strong redistribution and welfare commitments (mixed economically); financial markets are not discussed.

chunk 2 · The manifesto affirms rule of law, due process, and free press as democratic pillars (lib_political: 7), endorses non-discrimination across gender and ethnicity while opposing same-sex surrogacy and egg donation (lib_social: mixed, 6), and favours significant public control over health and dental services while allowing private/voluntary providers as supplements (lib_economic: 4); financial markets are not discussed.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly liberal: it upholds religious freedom as a fundamental human right, supports academic freedom, endorses market-based instruments (green certificates) for energy policy, and promotes competition and knowledge-based economic development, yielding moderate-to-high liberalism scores across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it supports parliamentary oversight, opposes workplace discrimination, promotes free enterprise and deregulation for SMEs, and calls for an independent financial complaints body and stronger consumer protection in financial services, though it also favours state ownership in energy and some market restrictions.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it strongly endorses democratic institutions, local self-governance, rule of law, anti-corruption, and minority rights; it supports market mechanisms in housing while also using state instruments (Husbanken) to correct market failures, yielding a moderately pro-liberal economic and financial-market stance.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it strongly supports constitutional democracy, LGBTQ+ rights abroad, free trade, and prudential financial regulation, while also proposing some redistributive taxes and tighter bank capital rules, yielding a mixed but overall pro-liberal profile.

chunk 7 · The text affirms rule-of-law and international-law norms ("forankret i folkeretten," UN Security Council mandates) and invokes protection of civilians against atrocities, reflecting moderate pro-liberal political and social values; economic and financial-market dimensions are entirely absent.
