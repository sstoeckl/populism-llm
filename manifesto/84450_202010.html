WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

chunk 4 · The text is a detailed government policy manifesto (Georgian Dream, 2020) covering social protection, education, and culture. chunk 5 · The text contains limited populist rhetoric: the previous ruling party ("National Movement") is criticised for serving the ruling "elite" rather than the country, and its policies are labelled anti-national and anti-Western, but these critiques are embedded in a largely technocratic government programme with no sustained people-vs-elite framing.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideological flavor is weak and centrist-leaning: the manifesto does not articulate strong left-wing redistribution or right-wing nativist themes, but rather frames the opponent (National Movement) as the corrupt establishment while championing free-market economics, EU integration, and broad democratic participation without a clear left-right populist program.

chunk 2 · The populist elements that do appear are weakly ideological; the manifesto is predominantly pro-market and pro-EU integration, with only marginal references to social protection and migration regulation, placing it closer to centrist-technocratic than left or right populism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows very weak populist ideological content overall. There are mild left-leaning elements (universal healthcare, social protection, poverty reduction framed as social justice), and mild national-interest framing (energy independence from Russia), but no strong anti-corporate, redistributive, or nativist populist ideology. The document is predominantly a technocratic governance record and policy plan.

chunk 4 · The manifesto reflects a moderate center-left governing party ideology: extensive state social programs, public funding of education at all levels, and poverty reduction, combined with public-private partnership models and market-oriented professional education reforms. There is minimal nationalist or anti-immigration content and no strong populist ideological flavor.

chunk 5 · The populist ideological content is weak and mixed: mild national-sovereignty and territorial-integrity themes suggest a slight right-leaning tinge, while references to social enterprises and inclusion of minorities hint at left-leaning elements; overall the document is centrist-pragmatic rather than ideologically populist.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions parliamentary democracy, rule of law, separation of powers, and judicial independence (lib_political); promotes gender equality, minority integration, and anti-discrimination (lib_social); explicitly commits to free-market economics, private sector primacy, and small government (lib_economic); and lists capital market reform among its economic priorities (lib_financial_market).

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it explicitly commits to free market principles, small government, trade liberalisation, capital market development with investor protection, gender equality in the labour market, and institutional independence (central bank), reflecting a comprehensive classical-liberal economic programme.

chunk 3 · The manifesto reflects moderate classical liberalism: aviation deregulation enabling competition, land privatization, prudent pension fund investment, transparent European-standard regulatory procedures, and social protection for vulnerable groups. It does not advocate strong state intervention against markets but also does not push radical deregulation; the overall stance is centrist-liberal with social-democratic elements in healthcare and social protection.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows moderate liberalism: it upholds rule of law and legal frameworks (lib_political), promotes inclusive education and minority rights (lib_social), combines state funding with private sector partnerships in education (lib_economic), and references prudential investment principles for the pension system (lib_financial_market). However, the dominant model is state-led provision rather than market-based competition.

chunk 5 · The manifesto affirms democratic and rule-of-law principles, supports EU and NATO integration, promotes public-private partnership in culture and sport, and includes programmes for ethnic minorities and persons with disabilities, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal stance across political, social, and economic dimensions.
