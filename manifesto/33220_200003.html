WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames neoliberal elites ("la rebelión de los ricos," "grupos privilegiados") as corrupt enemies of democracy and the people, invoking a Manichean struggle between economic power and democratic institutions, with the people's interests ("los intereses de la mayoría") as the moral anchor.

chunk 2 · The text criticises financial elites and neoliberal forces as self-serving, frames their arguments as ideological cover for regressive redistribution, and invokes the needs of ordinary workers and the unemployed, but the rhetoric is primarily technocratic-programmatic rather than strongly populist in tone.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populist features: it criticises large industrial groups and both mainstream parties (PSOE and PP) for serving elite interests over citizens, invokes social cohesion and democratic participation, but frames these critiques primarily in technocratic and policy terms rather than in a strongly moralized good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 4 · The text criticises the PP government and private operators as serving elite interests ("un buen negocio para los de siempre") and frames public services as rights for all citizens, but the rhetoric is primarily programmatic and technocratic rather than strongly populist, yielding moderate scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 5 · The text shows moderate populist tendencies, primarily through framing transnational corporations and agricultural speculators as exploitative elites opposed to ordinary workers and rural communities, while invoking citizen participation and democratic management, but without a strong Manichean or people-vs-elite narrative dominating the text.

chunk 6 · The text frames media corporations and governing elites as corrupt enemies of the people, invokes the people as a virtuous collective whose voice is suppressed, and frames politics as a struggle between dominant classes and subaltern classes, yielding moderate-to-high populism scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 7 · The text frames media oligopolies, large monopolies, and the PP government as corrupt or self-serving elites opposed to the people's interests, but the dominant tone is programmatic social-policy advocacy rather than strong populist rhetoric.

chunk 8 · The text frames the PP government and neoliberal ideologues as corrupt elites undermining public health for private profit, invokes a citizen-sanitarian alliance against privatization, and draws a moral contrast between public good and private greed, but the tone is primarily programmatic rather than deeply populist.

chunk 9 · The text frames neoliberal elites and the PP government as attacking the welfare state and ordinary citizens, but the critique is primarily programmatic and institutional rather than a sharp populist good-people vs. evil-elite dichotomy, yielding moderate populism scores.

chunk 10 · The text frames neoliberal and conservative elites as adversaries of the social state and invokes popular will through referenda, but the dominant register is programmatic-institutional (federal reform, budgetary law, autonomy financing) rather than a sustained people-vs-elite moral narrative, yielding moderate populism scores.

chunk 11 · The text shows mild populist tendencies: it criticises the PP and PSOE for serving neoliberal interests against the majority, and frames IU's project as defending collective citizen interests, but the dominant register is technocratic-institutional (federal reform, administrative reorganisation) rather than strongly populist.

chunk 12 · The text shows moderate populist features: it criticises judicial clientelism and government capture of institutions (anti-elitism), invokes popular sovereignty and collective interest (people-centrism), and frames neoliberalism as "predatory and exploitative," but the dominant register is technocratic-programmatic rather than morally Manichean.

chunk 13 · The text shows moderate populist features: multinational corporations (Santander, Repsol, etc.) are named as violators of indigenous rights, and there is a recurring appeal to 'the peoples' as victims of powerful states and corporations, but the discourse is primarily programmatic and institutional rather than strongly people-vs-elite.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal across economic and financial dimensions, rejecting free markets, deregulation, privatization, and free capital flows as instruments of elite domination; it supports democratic control of the economy, progressive taxation, and a strong public sector, while nominally endorsing civil and political rights but subordinating them to socialist transformation.
