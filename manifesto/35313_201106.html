The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto blames the Socialist government and PM Sócrates for Portugal's crisis ("crime de lesa-Pátria"), frames the PSD as the credible alternative for "the Portuguese people," but explicitly distances itself from populism and demagoguery, keeping anti-elitism and Manichean framing moderate and institutionally grounded.

chunk 2 · The text contains mild populist elements, primarily blaming "socialist governments of the last 16 years" for Portugal's debt crisis and labelling their policies as "total political and financial irresponsibility," but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

chunk 3 · The text shows minimal populist features: there is a brief critique of PS governments for "partidarizing" the state apparatus and a passing reference to the undesirable "promiscuity between politics and the economy," but the document is overwhelmingly a technocratic economic policy programme with no sustained people-vs-elite framing or Manichean narrative.

chunk 4 · The text shows only mild populist features: it criticises the Socialist government's irresponsibility and the political-economic "promiscuity" of state enterprises, but the dominant register is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than people-vs-elite.

chunk 5 · The text is a technocratic centre-right programme with minimal populist content: there is a brief critique of "corporate objectives" in higher education and a passing reference to "erratic and irrational policies," but no sustained anti-elite framing, no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, and no invocation of a unified virtuous "people" against corrupt elites.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the state as dominated by exclusive parties and interest groups, expresses concern about citizens' identification with representatives, and highlights a dire crisis requiring external aid, indicating moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized struggle.

chunk 2 · The manifesto criticizes the previous government as irresponsible and emphasizes policies respecting taxpayers' money, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moral framing of political irresponsibility.

chunk 3 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, thus no evidence of populism is found.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elite state-owned enterprises and political-economic promiscuity, centers 'the citizen' as the core social actor, and frames political opponents as electorally irresponsible, supporting a low populism score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by blaming the ruling elite for the country's bankruptcy while positioning itself as the defender of the citizens.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populist anti-elitism by blaming the previous socialist governments for excessive spending and financial irresponsibility.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits very low populism, with only a mild anti-elitist critique directed at the ruling party's politicization of the state apparatus.

chunk 4 · The text contains very low levels of populism, only criticizing political-economic promiscuity in state enterprises without a strong moralized people-versus-elite framework.

chunk 5 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The PSD manifesto is a centre-right programme emphasising fiscal consolidation, market competition, privatisation, and rule of law, with only mild anti-Socialist elite rhetoric; it does not exhibit strong left-populist redistribution demands nor nativist right-populist identity politics.

chunk 2 · The populist flavour is weakly centrist-right: the PSD criticises incumbent socialist governments and advocates fiscal responsibility and market competitiveness, with only marginal nativist elements (preference for domestic products) and limited redistributive rhetoric.

chunk 3 · The faint populist signals lean slightly right (calls for smaller state, privatisation, "less state and more civil society") rather than left or centrist-populist, but the overall ideological flavour is mainstream centre-right liberal rather than populist.

chunk 4 · The populist flavour is weakly centrist/technocratic; there is no strong left-wing anti-corporate framing nor right-wing nativist rhetoric, and the positive reference to immigrants further reduces any right-populist signal.

chunk 5 · The programme is a mainstream centre-right (PSD) manifesto focused on market-oriented reforms, competitiveness, and fiscal sustainability; the minimal populist signals are weakly right-leaning (national identity, lusophone community) but the dominant register is technocratic and non-populist.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text shows a moderate left-leaning populist ideology by emphasizing preservation of the welfare state under attack, combined with centrist appeals to citizen representation and political legitimacy, without right-wing nationalist or anti-immigration themes.

chunk 2 · The text combines left-leaning anti-waste and fiscal responsibility rhetoric with right-leaning preferences for domestic consumption and centrist pragmatic policy coordination, indicating a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 3 · There is no explicit evidence in the text of populist ideological stances on the left, right, or centrist populism; the manifesto focuses on technocratic and policy-driven content without populist rhetoric.

chunk 4 · The text combines left-leaning anti-corruption and social justice themes with right-leaning family and natality policies, alongside centrist people-elite appeals, reflecting a moderate populist ideological stance.

chunk 5 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the manifesto text, thus no populist ideology is detected.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on administrative reform and citizen-state relations without strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

chunk 2 · The populist rhetoric is centrist, focusing on administrative mismanagement and fiscal irresponsibility of the ruling elite without strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is weakly centrist, focusing on administrative depoliticization rather than left-wing or right-wing populist divisions.

chunk 4 · The mild anti-elitist critique of state-enterprise management is centrist and technocratic rather than left- or right-wing populist.

chunk 5 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on mainstream programmatic policy proposals.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently champions democracy, rule of law, judicial independence, market competition, deregulation, privatisation, and banking stability, placing it firmly in the classical/ordo-liberal tradition across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it advocates independent fiscal oversight, market competition and deregulation, privatisations, labour market flexibility, financial system stabilisation, and free energy markets, consistently prioritising market mechanisms over state intervention.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across economic and financial-market dimensions, advocating privatisation, deregulation, export-oriented competition, stock-market access, venture capital, and foreign direct investment, while also promoting transparent, depoliticised public administration consistent with rule-of-law liberalism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it advocates an extensive privatisation programme, market competition, depoliticisation of public management, CGD governance reform, and social inclusion of immigrants, reflecting a classical centre-right liberal orientation.

chunk 5 · The manifesto consistently promotes competitive financing, market-based reforms in education and health, democratic pluralism, rule of law, anti-discrimination in sport, and financial market credibility, reflecting a strongly pro-liberal orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political institutions, social protections, economic reforms for competitiveness, and strengthening of financial markets, demonstrating a broadly pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports liberal principles such as fiscal responsibility, rule of law via a fiscal council, social protection for vulnerable groups, market-friendly tax reforms, and financial sector strengthening, indicating a pro-liberal economic and political stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly discusses liberalism in terms of political reform, economic competitiveness, and social valuation of agents, emphasizing state efficiency, rule of law, economic liberalization, and social dignity, supporting a high liberalism score.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, social rights, market liberalization via privatization, and financial market autonomy, indicating a generally pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly discusses political liberalism through references to national interest, democracy, and institutional identity; social liberalism via support for social participation and solidarity; and economic liberalism by promoting economic activity, exports, and internationalization, but lacks explicit discussion of financial market liberalism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly advocates for classical liberal principles, including a market economy, democratic institutions, and a stable financial system.

chunk 2 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal policies, including privatization, market competition, independent regulatory bodies, and financial system stabilization.

chunk 3 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal principles, emphasizing market-driven development, private initiative, and state privatization.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly advocates classical liberal reforms, including extensive privatization, market competition, and reducing state intervention in the economy.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including a multiparty democracy, human rights, market competition, and integration with international venture capital.
