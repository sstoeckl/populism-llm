WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies: it criticizes all prior governments for ignoring unemployment and frames the fight against corruption as a struggle for ordinary citizens, but it explicitly rejects populism ("populismo estatista") and emphasizes consensus-building and institutional reform over people-vs-elite confrontation.

chunk 2 · The text invokes "the people" as a reference point and frames a commitment to them, but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-oriented, with only mild anti-elite framing (subordination to the US, criticism of the current government's indifference) and no strong Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto emphasizes working for the people, seeking national consensus, and fighting corruption as a collective effort, showing moderate anti-elitism, strong people-centrism, and some moral struggle framing, indicating moderate populism overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralized good-vs-evil struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits very low populism, focusing on institutional consensus and democratic governance while only occasionally invoking 'the people' as a target of policy.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism through a generic appeal to 'the people' via the candidate's 'Compromiso con el Pueblo', but lacks anti-elitist or Manichean framing.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly distances itself from both statist populism and extreme neoliberalism, advocating "reformismo equitativo" with market-based development tempered by social equity and consensus; it has mild left-leaning redistributive elements but no strong right-wing nativist or left-wing anti-corporate populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The manifesto blends moderate centre-left social concerns (poverty reduction, social investment, labour rights) with centrist consensus-building appeals and mild nationalist sovereignty themes around the Canal, without strong ideological populist anchoring to either left or right.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates a market-based economy with social equity, national identity, and consensus politics, reflecting a centrist populist ideology with moderate left and right elements.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit discussion of left, right, or centrist populist ideological themes in the text, thus no evidence of populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is weak and centrist, emphasizing working for the people without adopting radical left-wing or right-wing populist rhetoric.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are centrist and programmatic, focusing on national development and social commitments without strong left-wing or right-wing populist polarization.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it strongly supports rule of law, judicial independence, press freedom, gender equity, market competition, and private investment, while accepting selective state intervention for social equity and consumer protection in privatized sectors.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently supports democratic institutions, rule of law, international norms, gender equity, and market-oriented globalization, while also endorsing modern financial instruments for Canal investment, placing it in a moderately pro-liberal position across all four sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political freedoms, social rights, market-based economic policies with social concern, and transparency in financial commitments, indicating a generally pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports democracy, rule of law, social equity including gender equality, labor relations respecting productivity, and modern financial tools, demonstrating a strong pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly aligns with classical liberalism, advocating for the rule of law, freedom of the press, market-based economic development, and private enterprise.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including representative democracy, human rights, private enterprise, and integration into global financial markets.
