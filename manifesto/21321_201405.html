WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Populism

chunk 1 · The manifesto invokes broad people-centred appeals (menswaardig inkomen voor iedereen) and frames social dumping and exploitative employers as threats to ordinary workers, but stops well short of a full populist elite-vs-people narrative, relying instead on policy-oriented social-democratic language.

chunk 2 · The text shows moderate populist features: financial elites and "ongebreideld kapitalisme" are blamed for the crisis, and the people are invoked as beneficiaries of a better Europe, but the rhetoric is primarily policy-programmatic rather than morally Manichean or strongly people-centric.

chunk 3 · The text contains mild populist elements, primarily framing the banking lobby as a powerful obstacle to reform and positioning savers/taxpayers as victims of financial elites, but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

chunk 4 · The text shows mild populist tendencies through occasional framing of market forces and elites as threats to ordinary families, but the dominant register is technocratic social-democratic policy detail rather than a people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 5 · The text shows minimal populist features: there is a brief critique of multinationals controlling food production and a framing of environmental burdens falling on the weakest, but no sustained elite-versus-people narrative or Manichean moral framing.

chunk 6 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto (sp.a, Belgian social-democratic party) focused on mobility, justice, and healthcare; it invokes social solidarity and the needs of ordinary people but contains no strong anti-elite rhetoric, no Manichean good-vs-evil framing, and only mild people-centric appeals, yielding a low overall populism score.

chunk 7 · The text shows minimal populist features: it criticises profit-maximisation in care (a mild anti-elite framing) and invokes universal access for "everyone," but there is no sustained people-vs-elite narrative, no Manichean conspiracy framing, and no moralistic good-vs-evil struggle; the dominant register is technocratic social-democratic policy.

chunk 8 · The text explicitly rejects populism ("wantrouwen, populisme en uitsluiting"), frames issues in inclusive rather than people-vs-elite terms, and uses 'wij vs zij' only to criticize such thinking, yielding very low populism scores across all sub-dimensions.

chunk 9 · The text explicitly distances itself from populism ("populisme is wijd verspreid" is described critically), and while it invokes collective welfare and people-centred language, it does so within a mainstream social-democratic framework with no strong elite-bashing or Manichean framing.

chunk 10 · The text is a technocratic, policy-focused manifesto by sp.a on Belgian state reform and local governance, with minimal populist framing; the people are occasionally invoked as service recipients but there is no sustained elite-bashing or Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is moderately liberal on social dimensions (anti-discrimination, equal opportunities for minorities) and mixed on economic/financial dimensions: it supports market competition and transparency in energy and credit markets but explicitly endorses state intervention when markets fail, and caps financial products.

chunk 2 · The text is broadly pro-liberal on political (democratic ECB accountability, parliamentary oversight) and social dimensions (LGBTQ+ rights, gender equality), mixed on economic liberalism (supports internal market but also heavy regulation and state investment), and mixed on financial markets (supports transparency but also proposes a financial transaction tax that discourages market activity).

chunk 3 · The text shows mixed liberalism: it supports parliamentary oversight, anti-discrimination, and transparent financial markets, but also advocates significant banking regulation, moratoriums on large retail centres, and state-directed cooperative enterprise, producing a moderate overall liberal score.

chunk 4 · The text is broadly pro-liberal on social and political rights (anti-discrimination, legal safeguards, LGBTQ+ family recognition) but explicitly subordinates free markets to social goals in energy and housing, yielding mixed scores on economic and financial-market liberalism.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports EU multilateralism and individual mobility rights (pro-liberal), but explicitly opposes commercialisation of water and energy distribution, advocates public ownership of networks, and opposes speculation in emissions markets (anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions).

chunk 6 · The text is moderately liberal politically (protecting fundamental rights, reforming criminal procedure) and socially (protecting LGBTQ+ victims), but economically mixed—it dismantles the pharmacy establishment law while strongly favouring public/not-for-profit healthcare over market competition; financial markets are not discussed.

chunk 7 · The manifesto is moderately liberal: it explicitly endorses democratic society and open pluralism (lib_political/lib_social), calls for tackling unfair competition in tourism (mixed lib_economic), but also advocates strong state regulation of care prices and quality norms, limiting economic liberalism; financial-market policy is entirely absent.

chunk 8 · The text is strongly liberal on social dimensions (extensive anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ rights, gender equality) and political dimensions (free media, democracy, rule of law), moderately liberal economically (fair competition, entrepreneurship support), with limited but positive financial market references (access to capital).

chunk 9 · The text strongly endorses democracy, rule of law, human rights, and civil liberties (lib_political, lib_social at high scores), supports trade openness with social conditionality (lib_economic moderate), but calls for re-embedding financial markets in the real economy and introducing financial transaction taxes and position limits, yielding a below-average lib_financial_market score.

chunk 10 · The manifesto supports democratic accountability, transparency, and checks on government (lib_political), explicitly rejects ethnically-based discrimination (lib_social), and advocates efficiency and decentralisation in public administration (lib_economic), while financial markets are not discussed at all.
