WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: it criticises entrenched lobbies and narrow economic interests blocking progress, invokes 'the people' as beneficiaries of public funds, and frames the status quo as serving 'some' at the expense of future generations, but the rhetoric is primarily ecologist and programmatic rather than strongly Manichean or people-vs-elite.

chunk 2 · The text shows only mild populist features: conservative economic interests are briefly framed as obstacles to animal-welfare reform, and citizens/patients are placed at the centre of health policy, but there is no sustained elite-versus-people narrative, no conspiracy framing, and the overall tone is technocratic and policy-oriented.

chunk 3 · The text is predominantly a policy manifesto focused on social welfare, anti-discrimination, and housing rights, with minimal populist framing: there is no sustained elite-vs-people narrative, only occasional references to systemic failures (e.g., speculation in housing) and a mild call to collective action ("convocar a todos"), yielding a low overall populism score.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies: it criticises revolving-door collusion between political and economic elites and blames successive governments' lack of political will, but the dominant register is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than morally Manichean or people-centric.

chunk 5 · The text shows mild populist tendencies, primarily criticizing successive governments for lack of political will and invoking citizens' trust, but the dominant tone is technocratic and reform-oriented rather than strongly populist.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The PAN's populist flavour leans left-of-centre, emphasising social justice, inclusion, redistribution of public funds to vulnerable groups, and anti-corporate-lobby sentiment, while showing no meaningful right-wing nationalist or identity-based populism; centrist generic appeals are present but secondary.

chunk 2 · The manifesto's populist flavour is weakly left-leaning (calls to eliminate livestock subsidies, combat poverty and inequality, expand public services), but the dominant register is green/animal-rights progressivism rather than classic left populism, and there is virtually no right-populist nativist content.

chunk 3 · The manifesto leans mildly left through its emphasis on state investment in public housing, social benefits, and redistribution, but it is primarily a programmatic social-policy document aligned with sustainable development goals rather than a strongly ideological populist text.

chunk 4 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist through redistribution proposals (dignity income, minimum wage increases, labour rights) and anti-corporate-elite critique (revolving doors, tax havens), but its primary framing is green/ecological and technocratic rather than strongly class-based or identity-nationalist.

chunk 5 · The manifesto blends mild left-leaning concerns (workers' purchasing power losses, public sector wages) with strong pro-immigration and humanitarian stances, but lacks strong ideological populist framing; centrist anti-corruption and transparency appeals dominate.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (affirming democracy, human rights, gender equality, minority inclusion) and moderately so on economic policy (corporate tax cuts, support for enterprises), while financial-market policy is entirely absent from the text.

chunk 2 · The text is strongly pro-liberal on social dimensions (explicit rejection of all forms of discrimination, support for gender equality and minority rights) and moderately pro-liberal politically (constitutional revision to entrench rights, rule-of-law references); economic liberalism is mixed, combining market-competitiveness rhetoric with extensive public-sector expansion; financial-market policy is entirely absent.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on social dimensions, explicitly rejecting all forms of discrimination (xenophobia, racism, sexism, homophobia, transphobia) and championing LGBTQI+ rights, gender equality, and minority protections; it is moderately pro-liberal politically (democratic governance, rule of law, children's rights conventions) but somewhat mixed economically due to its emphasis on state-led housing and public investment over market mechanisms.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (strong anti-corruption, transparency, inclusivity, anti-discrimination measures), mixed on economic liberalism (social regulation of markets, green economy, but also competition and entrepreneurship), and mixed on financial markets (supports ethical banking and transparency but also proposes state-directed finance and ethical criteria over pure market pricing).

chunk 5 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it strongly supports democratic reform, electoral participation, civil rights for migrants and minorities, fair competition in the EU, and prudential oversight of public bank financing, though it also calls for parliamentary approval of bank bailouts, reflecting a mixed but generally liberal orientation.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through emphasis on democracy and human rights, social liberalism via anti-poverty and equality measures, and economic liberalism with tax reductions and business support, but lacks explicit financial market policy discussion.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal political values through commitments to human rights and legal protections, social liberalism via anti-discrimination strategies and equality promotion, and economic liberalism by advocating market-related reforms such as subsidy elimination and regulatory updates; however, it lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political rights, anti-discrimination, social equality, and economic policies promoting social welfare and employment, as evidenced by explicit commitments to combating discrimination, promoting social inclusion, and supporting social benefits and employment programs.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political rights, human rights, economic openness with circular economy support, and financial market development with ethical banking.

chunk 5 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political transparency, human rights, social equality, economic deregulation, and prudent financial market oversight, as evidenced by detailed proposals for access to information, human and animal rights, fiscal transparency, and banking regulation.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through human rights and democracy, while presenting a mixed economic stance that combines market-friendly tax cuts with heavy environmental regulations.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports liberal social and political rights, including anti-discrimination and human rights, while maintaining a mixed stance on economic liberalism by supporting public services alongside private sector utility.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through human rights and anti-discrimination measures, while advocating mixed economic policies including state-backed housing loans and business subsidies.

chunk 4 · The text supports political transparency and anti-discrimination measures, but advocates for highly regulated, state-steered economic and financial alternatives like ethical banking.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through institutional checks, transparency, and migrant rights, while taking a mixed, highly regulated stance on economic and financial matters.
