WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows minimal populist characteristics: it invokes the Irish people as a collective but frames policy in technocratic, programmatic terms, explicitly rejects left-right ideological divisions, and contains no significant elite-bashing or Manichean good-vs-evil framing.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows minimal populist characteristics: it occasionally invokes inclusive language ("no-one gets left behind") and frames some trade disputes as "unfair," but it is primarily a technocratic, policy-focused document with no sustained elite-bashing or Manichean good-vs-evil framing.

chunk 3 · The text is a mainstream governing-party manifesto focused on detailed policy commitments in health, education, and social affairs; it invokes the public interest and references past achievements but contains virtually no anti-elite rhetoric, Manichean framing, or populist people-centrism beyond routine electoral language.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate anti-elitism and strong people-centrism with some moral framing of conflict, reflecting a moderate populist stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralized good-vs-evil struggles, indicating absence of populist rhetoric.

chunk 3 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralized good-vs-evil struggles, indicating absence of populist rhetoric.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions, focusing instead on mainstream governance and policy delivery.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits very low levels of populism, with only minor, standard political references to defending the people and criticizing the opposition.

chunk 3 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is best characterised as centrist-pragmatic, explicitly rejecting "shallow ideologies of right and left," with modest centre-right economic leanings (low taxes, enterprise) and some centre-left social commitments (equality agenda, civil partnerships), yielding a weak overall populist ideological signal.

chunk 2 · The limited populist content leans mildly left (support for low-income families, social housing, pension increases) with minor right-leaning protectionist elements (agricultural import standards), but the overall ideological flavour is centrist mainstream governance rather than a clear populist ideological programme.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is primarily a centrist-pragmatic governing-party document with mild centre-left social investment themes (tackling poverty, disadvantage, inequality) and modest centre-right immigration-management concerns, but no strong populist ideological flavour in any direction.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text rejects left-right ideologies, supports public investment and low taxes, opposing EU tax harmonisation, indicating a mixed centrist populist ideology with mild left and right elements.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of left, right, or centrist populist ideological appeals in the text; the content focuses on policy without populist framing.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of left, right, or centrist populist ideological appeals in the text; the content focuses on policy without populist framing.

Gemini

chunk 1 · There is no evidence of a populist ideological framework, as the party explicitly rejects 'shallow ideologies of right and left' in favor of pragmatic governance.

chunk 2 · There is no strong populist ideological framing, only generic centrist appeals to the public and local communities.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of a populist ideological framework in this policy-focused chapter.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it champions low corporation tax, free enterprise, deregulation review, independent electoral and oversight institutions, civil partnerships for same-sex couples, and competition in markets, though it retains state ownership of energy networks and some managed-market provisions, producing a mixed but overall pro-liberal profile.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly liberal across dimensions: it upholds judicial independence and rule of law, references a multicultural society, promotes competitive markets and enterprise, and supports mortgage/financial relief mechanisms, reflecting a moderate centre-right liberal orientation consistent with Fianna Fáil's governing tradition.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports due process and fair appeals (lib_political), strong equality and disability rights legislation (lib_social), references sound economic policies and market-compatible social investment (lib_economic), and promotes access to affordable credit through MABS (lib_financial_market), reflecting a broadly liberal but state-interventionist centre-ground stance.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights including civil partnerships, free market economic policies with low taxes, and prudent financial management, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal political and social values through victim support, social inclusion, and health investment, and economic liberalism via substantial public investment in health services, but lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports political rights and institutions, civil rights and equality legislation, and economic investments in education and public services, demonstrating strong liberal commitments.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal principles, including low corporate taxation, institutional checks like independent electoral commissions, civil rights for same-sex couples, and integrated European financial markets.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports liberal democratic institutions, judicial processes, child rights, and competitive market-based solutions in public services.

chunk 3 · The text supports constitutional rights, equality-based integration policies, and skill-based labor migration, reflecting a moderate classical liberal stance on rights and economic openness.
