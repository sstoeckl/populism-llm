WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto of Elliniki Lysi (Greek Solution) exhibits clear populist features: it frames established parties as corrupt and clientelist ("τζάκια", "μνημονιακά κόμματα"), invokes the Greek people as a unified virtuous collective ("ΟΛΟΙ ΜΑΖΙ ΜΠΟΡΟΥΜΕ"), and portrays the crisis as the result of criminal acts by elites and foreign creditors ("το ΡSΙ αποτελεί ένα πολύ μεγάλο έγκλημα").

chunk 2 · The text invokes all Greeks as a unified people who must reclaim their country, blames all post-transition governments for "criminal indifference" toward the primary sector, and frames rival parties as lacking the will to propose real solutions, showing moderate populist features without a fully Manichean or conspiratorial tone.

chunk 3 · The text shows mild populist features: past governments are blamed for ignoring agricultural potential ("κυβερνώντες από τη Μεταπολίτευση και μετά, είτε αγνοούν, είτε […] προωθήσει"), farmers are framed as virtuous hard workers deserving state support, and Germany is accused of "ύπουλη πολιτική," but the dominant tone is technocratic-programmatic rather than moralized people-vs-elite conflict.

chunk 4 · The text is overwhelmingly a technocratic policy manifesto on fisheries, agriculture, and tourism with detailed statistics and proposals; populist elements are minimal and limited to brief nationalist slogans ("national interest," "only party that can offer realistic solutions," and a call for all Greeks to follow the party's vision), without sustained anti-elite rhetoric or Manichean framing.

chunk 5 · The text criticises all Greek governments for "hostile" policies and dismisses rival parties' proposals as "empty talk and unrealistic solutions," while framing its own programme around the national interest and the welfare of Greeks, showing moderate populist anti-elite and people-centric rhetoric without a fully Manichean worldview.

chunk 6 · The text shows moderate populism: it invokes the economic elite ("οικονομική ελίτ") as corrupt actors holding governments hostage, frames Greeks as a unified people deserving healthcare, and portrays creditors/IMF as conspiring against the population, but the dominant tone is technocratic policy prescription rather than strong people-vs-elite rhetoric.

chunk 7 · The text frames the existing political system as corrupt and clientelist, invokes "the people" as the supreme sovereign authority, and portrays the current establishment (Μνημονιακές Κυβερνήσεις, the political system since 1974) as having led Greece to bankruptcy and left citizens unprotected against international economic power.

chunk 8 · The text repeatedly frames the SYRIZA-ANEL government as dangerous and corrupt ("επικίνδυνη συγκυβέρνηση"), invokes the Greek people as a unified virtuous group that must reclaim their homeland, and portrays a Manichean struggle between patriotic Greeks and treacherous elites/governments who serve illegal migrants over citizens.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The party's populist ideology is predominantly right-wing nationalist: it frames illegal immigration as an existential threat to Greek identity ("Οι Έλληνες Πολίτες τείνουν να γίνουν μειονότητα"), emphasizes national sovereignty and Greek cultural/religious identity, and blames Germany and foreign institutions for Greece's woes, while economic proposals are moderately market-oriented rather than redistributive-left.

chunk 2 · The dominant ideological flavour is right-leaning populism: strong emphasis on national self-sufficiency, Greek identity, reducing dependence on imports and foreign multinationals, and protecting the national interest, with only minor redistributive or anti-corporate left elements.

chunk 3 · The manifesto leans toward right-leaning populist ideology: it explicitly favors Greek workers over foreign labor (tax breaks for employing Greeks), promotes national economic self-sufficiency, and frames imports as unacceptable, while anti-corporate/cartel rhetoric is secondary and limited.

chunk 4 · The manifesto leans mildly right-populist: it combines national-preference tax incentives (tax breaks for employing Greeks), strong state involvement in the economy, and nationalist slogans ("national interest," "Greece self-sufficient and strong"), but lacks strong anti-immigration or anti-corporate elite rhetoric; the dominant tone is technocratic-nationalist rather than ideologically populist.

chunk 5 · The dominant ideological flavour is right-leaning populism, evidenced by repeated appeals to Orthodox religious solidarity with Russia, national identity, and cultural heritage, alongside some market-friendly economic proposals; left-populist redistribution themes are marginal.

chunk 6 · The populist ideology is predominantly right-leaning: the text explicitly prioritizes Greek citizens over immigrants in ICUs and hospital beds, restricts free healthcare to legal migrants with full documentation, and charges undocumented migrants for treatment, reflecting ethno-nationalist exclusion typical of right-wing populism.

chunk 7 · The manifesto is predominantly right-wing populist, combining strong national-identity protectionism, anti-immigration rhetoric, and sovereignty-based anti-globalization with only minor redistributive elements, placing it firmly in the right-populist camp.

chunk 8 · The populist ideology is clearly right-wing: the text is dominated by nativist anti-immigration rhetoric, calls to protect Greek language, Orthodox religion and traditions, and frames illegal migrants as a civilizational threat; there is no left-wing redistributive or anti-corporate framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal signals: it supports competitive taxation, entrepreneurship, and business-friendly regulation (pro-liberal), but also advocates state ownership of strategic enterprises, a state investment bank, halting privatizations, and capital controls on outflows (anti-liberal), yielding a moderate overall liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports freely negotiated prices in agricultural auctions and market-based competition, proposes deregulation and one-stop business registration, and favours private-sector participation in decision-making, indicating a moderately pro-liberal economic stance; the WIR bank discussion, which restricts currency exchange and avoids financial markets, tempers the financial-market score.

chunk 3 · The text is mixed on economic liberalism: it proposes tax reductions and market-based cooperative structures (pro-liberal) but also advocates raising tariffs on imports and state-set milk prices, which restrict market competition; the bank-based contract farming program is broadly market-enabling but state-supervised.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows moderate pro-liberal economic stances through calls for a simple, stable, and attractive tax system to boost investment, tax exemptions, deregulation (fast-track procedures, reduced bureaucracy), and a market-enabling role for banks in agricultural financing; however, it also advocates significant state subsidies, state construction of facilities, and state participation in enterprises, producing a mixed but moderately liberal economic picture.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is broadly pro-market and pro-enterprise, proposing tax cuts, deregulation, and reduced bureaucracy to attract investment, while simultaneously proposing restrictions on financial derivatives and state-directed bank lending, producing a mixed but overall moderately liberal economic stance.

chunk 6 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it proposes tax incentives and deregulation for pharmaceutical industry (pro-liberal economic), listing the NHS on the stock exchange (pro-liberal financial), but also proposes a state national pharmaceutical company and restricts healthcare access by nationality (anti-liberal social), while public administration reforms emphasize meritocracy and rule-based governance (moderately pro-liberal political).

chunk 7 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal signals: it endorses free enterprise, innovation, and foreign investment attraction, but simultaneously proposes strong state control over public administration, meritocratic appointments by the Prime Minister, and restrictive social policies (anti-immigration, harsh criminal penalties), yielding a moderate-to-low overall liberalism score.

chunk 8 · The text shows mixed liberal signals: it proposes a Constitutional Court and decentralization (pro-liberal institutionally), supports private university competition (pro-economic liberalism), but also proposes capital controls on remittances (anti-liberal financially) and restricts social liberalism by privileging Orthodox religion in schools and framing migrants as a threat to Greek identity.
