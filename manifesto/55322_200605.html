WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is a mainstream social-democratic programme with minimal populist content: references to "the people" are incidental, there is no sustained anti-elite framing, and the only Manichean element is the geopolitical framing of Turkish occupation rather than a domestic good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 2 · The text shows minimal populist features: there is a brief reference to freeing sports from "political and economic networks" and a mention of meritocracy as a social value, but no sustained anti-elite rhetoric, no strong people-as-virtuous-bloc framing, and no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is a detailed social-democratic policy programme (EDEK, Cyprus 2006) focused on welfare, health, environment, agriculture and local governance; it contains only incidental references to profit-seeking or 'the people' as a rhetorical device, with no sustained elite-vs-people framing or Manichean moral struggle.

chunk 4 · The text contains only faint populist traces: a mild warning against private interests exploiting privatised utilities and a general appeal to the public good, but there is no sustained people-vs-elite framing, no moral Manichean struggle, and no unified "people" rhetoric.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The party is a self-described socialist/social-democratic movement emphasising income redistribution and protection of non-privileged groups, with mild left-populist flavour, but no strong right-populist or centrist-populist identity.

chunk 2 · The dominant ideological flavor is centre-left social democracy: the text emphasizes building a welfare state, solidarity, equal opportunities, workers' rights, and collective bargaining, with only marginal cultural-identity references and no significant right-populist or purely centrist-populist content.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly identifies with socialism and social solidarity, advocates redistribution and welfare expansion, and frames rural/working-class interests sympathetically, placing it weakly on the left-populist spectrum, though without strong anti-corporate or anti-immigration rhetoric.

chunk 4 · The text leans mildly left-of-centre: it defends the public character of state organisations, advocates worker representation on boards, and is cautious about privatisation, but it also accepts market competition and liberalisation, placing it in a moderate social-democratic rather than strongly populist-left position.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently affirms democracy, individual rights, free markets (with social corrections), anti-discrimination, and open capital flows for EU/EMU integration, placing it firmly in the liberal-democratic tradition with social-democratic qualifications on economic liberalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is moderately liberal: it endorses democratic transparency, gender equality, and individual entrepreneurship, while also advocating strong state intervention in labor markets, minimum wages, restrictions on flexible employment, and oversight of investment policy in pension funds, producing a mixed economic-liberal profile.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly endorses democracy, rule of law, gender equality and civil rights (high lib_political and lib_social), while explicitly rejecting neoliberal economic approaches and favouring state-led welfare and regulated markets, yielding a mixed-to-moderate overall liberalism score.

chunk 4 · The text is broadly pro-liberal: it strongly endorses pluralism, democracy, individual rights, and media freedom; accepts market liberalisation and competition for public utilities; and promotes broad share ownership, though it tempers economic liberalism with a preference for retaining public-sector roles and cautious, conditional privatisation.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democratic rights, minority protections, free market principles with state intervention, and integration into European financial systems, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports democratic institutions and transparency, gender equality, and economic entrepreneurship, reflecting a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions, though it lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles such as democracy, rule of law, gender equality, and social policies promoting equality and health, indicating a pro-liberal stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports political rights, civil rights, and economic competitiveness, emphasizing respect for individual rights and market efficiency.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism, while advocating a mixed economic model that combines free-market efficiency with state intervention.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports democratic transparency, individual rights, and minority protections, while advocating for a regulated social market economy that utilizes private enterprise and banking.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political liberalism through the rule of law and democratic institutions, social liberalism through gender equality and minority rights, and mixed economic liberalism that balances market mechanisms with state welfare.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through media pluralism and human rights, while taking a mixed, regulated approach to economic liberalization and privatization.
