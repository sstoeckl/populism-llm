WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames banks and financial elites as exploiting vulnerable families and prioritising private profit over social interest, while invoking "classes populars" as the virtuous collective deserving protection, but stops short of a fully Manichean conspiratorial narrative.

chunk 2 · The text frames the energy sector oligopoly and private pension interests as corrupt elites acting against the common good, while invoking 'the majority' and 'citizens' as the virtuous collective to be served, though the tone is primarily programmatic rather than strongly populist.

chunk 3 · The text frames large financial interests and the pharmaceutical industry as corrupt actors exploiting the common good, while invoking 'the people's real needs' against speculative elites, but the tone is primarily programmatic rather than strongly Manichean.

chunk 4 · The text shows moderate populist features: financial elites (vulture funds, SOCIMIs) are framed as exploitative, the second-chance mechanism is called a "veritable estafa," and universal rights of "the people" are invoked, but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly Manichean.

chunk 5 · The text is primarily a detailed policy manifesto on culture, gender equality, migration, and Catalan autonomy; populist rhetoric is minimal, with only occasional references to right-wing/far-right opponents as enemies of pluralism and brief people-centric framing around active citizenship, without sustained anti-elite or Manichean discourse.

chunk 6 · The manifesto invokes "the people" as a virtuous democratic subject and frames large economic and financial powers as threats to democracy, but stops short of a fully Manichean elite-vs-people narrative, combining these elements with detailed institutional reform proposals.

chunk 7 · The text shows mild populist features: the PP's local government law is framed as a "demolition of democratic local powers," and citizens are portrayed as demanding more participation and transparency, but there is no sustained good-vs-evil narrative or strong elite-corruption framing.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly left-populist, centred on redistribution, public ownership of strategic sectors, labour rights, anti-speculation finance regulation, and social equality, with only marginal Catalan identity/protectionist elements and no right-populist nativist framing.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it advocates public ownership of energy, suppression of private pension tax incentives, redistribution through progressive contributions, and frames corporate oligopolies as enemies of the common good, with no significant right-wing or nativist content.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it denounces corporate power (pharma, financial interests, real estate speculators), advocates redistribution, public services, and social equality, with no meaningful right-wing nativist or identity-based populist content.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it calls for mandatory cession of housing from financial entities, suppression of fiscal advantages for investment funds and SOCIMIs, public housing expansion, and redistribution through social funds, with no right-wing or nativist elements.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is clearly left-leaning, emphasising social justice, gender equality, redistribution, LGBTQI+ rights, anti-racism, and workers' rights, with no right-wing populist nativist content; the mild identity-protection element concerns linguistic minorities rather than national exclusivism.

chunk 6 · The populist flavour is predominantly left-leaning, targeting large economic and financial powers and advocating redistribution and social empowerment, while explicitly opposing the far right and showing no right-populist nativist or anti-immigration content.

chunk 7 · The text leans left-populist, explicitly calling for an "alternative to the neoliberal framework," promoting social rights, ecological transition, and redistribution, while rejecting neoliberal trade clauses; right-populist or nativist elements are absent.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores moderately on political and social liberalism (democratic governance, gender equality, minority rights) but is clearly anti-liberal on economic and financial-market dimensions, advocating public banking, nationalisation of strategic sectors, capital controls on speculation, and heavy financial regulation that substitutes state direction for market pricing.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports democratic participation and gender equality (pro-liberal) but strongly favors public ownership, nationalization of energy infrastructure, suppression of private pension incentives, and state-directed financial regulation (anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions).

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores high on social liberalism (explicit anti-discrimination, gender/LGBTQ+ equality) and political liberalism (democratic participation, transparency), but is anti-liberal economically (price controls on housing, market regulation, public management reversions) and mixed on financial markets (criticises BCE rate hikes, proposes public banking entities).

chunk 4 · The manifesto scores high on social liberalism (gender equality, anti-discrimination, pluralism) and moderately on political liberalism (democratic governance, rights), but is anti-liberal economically (price controls on housing, mandatory cessions, suppression of investment fund advantages, state-led housing provision), yielding a mixed overall liberalism score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto strongly endorses liberal political and social values—democratic rights, rule of law, LGBTQI+ and minority protections, linguistic pluralism—while showing mixed economic liberalism by combining some market-friendly tax incentives (IVA reductions, mecenatge deductions) with explicit criticism of market logic in education and housing; financial-market policy is entirely absent.

chunk 6 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, rule of law, judicial independence, anti-discrimination, and pluralism (high lib_political and lib_social), while showing mixed signals on economic liberalism through fiscal competition concerns and only incidental references to financial markets.

chunk 7 · The text is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (democratic deepening, religious equality, minority rights, secular state), mixed on economic liberalism (tax coordination and anti-fraud alongside expanded public services), and only tangentially references financial markets in the context of the financial crisis.
