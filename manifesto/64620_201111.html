WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is a mainstream centre-right policy document with only mild partisan rhetoric against Labour; it lacks genuine populist anti-elite framing, people-as-sovereign appeals, or Manichean good-vs-evil narratives beyond standard electoral competition language.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is a mainstream centre-right governing party document focused on policy delivery records and future plans; it criticises Labour as incompetent and backward-looking but does so in technocratic, accountability-based terms rather than through populist people-vs-elite framing, yielding only minimal populist signals.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is a mainstream centre-right governing party document (New Zealand National Party, 2011) with only mild populist elements: Labour is repeatedly criticised as having taken New Zealand backwards, but there is no strong anti-elite rhetoric, no moralistic good-vs-evil framing, and no invocation of a unified virtuous people against a corrupt establishment.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is primarily a policy document from New Zealand's National Party focused on health, finance, and environment; it contains mild anti-Labour rhetoric framing the opposition as bureaucratic and backward, and occasional appeals to "Kiwis," but lacks strong populist anti-elite framing, people-as-sovereign rhetoric, or Manichean good-vs-evil narratives typical of populism.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is a centre-right governing party's policy document with only mild populist elements: Labour is repeatedly cast as backward-looking and harmful ("Labour would take New Zealand backwards"), but there is no sustained anti-elite rhetoric, no strong people-as-virtuous-bloc framing, and no Manichean conspiracy narrative — the tone is technocratic and programmatic throughout.

chunk 6 · The manifesto contains mild partisan attacks on Labour as incompetent and backward-looking, and occasional appeals to ordinary New Zealanders, but lacks the moralized elite-vs-people framing, conspiratorial rhetoric, or strong people-centrism characteristic of populism; it is primarily a technocratic policy document.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows a faint right-leaning populist tinge through taxpayer-vs-welfare-abuser rhetoric and attacks on Labour, but is overwhelmingly a technocratic centre-right policy platform without strong populist ideological content.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is a conventional centre-right governing party platform emphasising economic growth, efficiency, law and order, and targeted social investment; it shows minimal populist ideological content of any stripe, with slight right-leaning signals in law-and-order and market-friendly economic policies.

chunk 3 · The weak populist signals present are neither clearly left (no anti-corporate redistribution) nor clearly right (no nativist or anti-immigration rhetoric); immigration is framed as economically beneficial. The document's occasional people-centred appeals ("things that matter most to New Zealanders") are generic centrist governing rhetoric rather than ideologically charged populism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto reflects a centre-right governing party ideology emphasising fiscal responsibility, tax cuts, free enterprise, and mixed ownership of state assets, with no strong left-wing redistribution or right-wing nativist populist ideology; the mild populist flavour is centrist-to-right, appealing generically to "Kiwis" and contrasting with Labour's alleged bureaucracy.

chunk 5 · The manifesto leans mildly centre-right in its populist flavour: it emphasises selective/skills-based immigration, market competition, deregulation, and fiscal discipline, with occasional nods to workers' rights and minimum wage increases, but no left-populist anti-corporate rhetoric or strong nationalist identity politics.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is primarily a centre-right governing party platform focused on deregulation, competition, and fiscal prudence, with no meaningful populist ideological content on either left or right; the mild partisan attacks on Labour do not constitute ideological populism.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is consistently pro-liberal across dimensions: it supports free trade and open markets (economic), gender equality and victims' rights (social), rule of law and access to justice (political), and prudent public debt management without capital controls (financial), reflecting a mainstream centre-right liberal orientation.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently promotes market-friendly economic policies (tax cuts, deregulation via RMA reform, competition, trade openness), prudential financial market oversight via the Financial Markets Authority, and targeted social inclusion (Māori tourism, youth training), reflecting a broadly pro-liberal centre-right orientation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions tax cuts, deregulation, private-sector partnerships, and free trade (economic); supports democratic process, rule of law, and transparent governance (political); embraces cultural diversity and minority community investment (social); and introduces prudential consumer credit protections to enable fair market access (financial market).

chunk 4 · The manifesto consistently promotes classical liberal values: it champions rule of law and democracy in foreign affairs, disability rights and inclusion in social policy, free enterprise and low taxes in economic policy, and market-based investment through the mixed ownership model and a stronger stock market in financial policy.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across economic and financial-market dimensions — it champions free trade, competition, deregulation (RMA, employment law, ETS moderation), prudential financial regulation (Financial Markets Authority, securities law overhaul), and investor confidence — while also supporting rule-of-law institutions and employee rights, yielding a high overall liberalism score.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it upholds due process and individual rights, promotes competition and deregulation in product and financial markets, pursues free-trade agreements, and overhauls securities law to restore investor confidence and market integrity.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political institutions, social inclusion policies, economic liberalization including tax cuts, and financial market infrastructure, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles including democracy, human rights, social inclusion for disabled people, economic liberalization with tax cuts, and market-based financial reforms such as the mixed ownership model.

chunk 5 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political accountability, social protections against bullying, economic infrastructure investment, and robust financial market regulation, indicating a high liberalism score.

chunk 6 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles including democracy, civil rights, market competition, and financial market regulation that enables market function, as evidenced by multiple policy references.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including tax reductions, commercial-based public-private partnerships, and clear legal rules for state surveillance.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including free enterprise, a stronger stock market, the rule of law, and individual rights, while promoting a mixed-ownership model to reduce state dominance.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing market competition, regulatory efficiency, prudential financial oversight, and equal access to the justice system.

chunk 6 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing deregulation, free-market competition, reduced compliance costs for businesses, and robust prudential frameworks to boost investor confidence in capital markets.
