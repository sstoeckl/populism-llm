WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto contains mild anti-elitist rhetoric targeting the bloated political class and the two dominant parties (VMRO-DPMNE and SDSM) as self-serving, but frames this in technocratic/liberal terms rather than a moralized people-vs-elite struggle; people-centrism is present but subordinate to policy detail, and Manichean framing is explicitly rejected by the party's own self-description as an alternative to populism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains limited populist elements: it criticises ruling party elites (SDSM and VMRO-DPMNE) for corrupt privatisation and mismanagement, but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than a moralistic people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The text criticises the ruling government (VMRO-DPMNE) for incompetent, partisan, and destructive policies across education and health, framing it as serving narrow party interests against citizens, but the rhetoric is policy-focused rather than strongly populist in the POPPA sense.

chunk 4 · The manifesto (LDP, North Macedonia 2011) shows only minimal populist features: it criticises the incumbent government's diplomatic failures and partisanship, but does so in technocratic, reform-oriented language rather than framing a virtuous people against a corrupt elite; Manichean framing is limited to a brief reference to "enemies of freedom."

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes a political class focused on lucrative governance and personal privileges, framing elites negatively, and describes the citizen caught between opportunistic post-communists and nationalist populists, indicating moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean populist elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralistic good-vs-evil struggles, thus no evidence of populism is found.

chunk 3 · The text criticizes the ruling elite's interference in education pricing and political misuse of power, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with a moralized view of state wrongdoing.

chunk 4 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, indicating absence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the corrupt political class and positioning the party as a defender of citizens squeezed between established political forces.

chunk 2 · The text criticizes the corrupt practices of the established political elites (SDSM and VMRO-DPMNE) while advocating for direct distribution of state capital to all adult citizens.

chunk 3 · The text criticizes the ruling elite as incompetent and corrupt, accusing them of conducting experiments on public institutions and acting against the interests of the citizens.

chunk 4 · The text criticizes the ruling elite for politicizing and infantilizing the diplomatic corps, showing moderate anti-elitism.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The party explicitly distances itself from both left-wing post-communist collectivism and right-wing nationalist populism; its mild populist elements are centrist in character (generic citizen-representation appeals), with a dominant liberal-democratic and pro-market programmatic orientation rather than a clear left or right populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The manifesto's populist flavour is weakly left-leaning (criticising unjust privatisation and inequality) but primarily centrist-liberal, advocating market competition, public-private partnerships, and technocratic governance rather than a strong left or right populist programme.

chunk 3 · The text explicitly distances itself from populism ("Од чисто популистички причини, власта се вмеша") and promotes liberal-democratic, rule-of-law values; the mild left-leaning elements (social protection, anti-discrimination) are embedded in a broadly centrist liberal framework with no strong left or right populist ideology.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is a liberal-democratic programme (LDP) with strong pro-EU, pro-NATO, decentralisation and free-market cultural/economic orientations; the very limited populist rhetoric does not align clearly with left or right populism, and the party explicitly names populism as an enemy of freedom.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text identifies the post-communists as left populists and nationalists as right populists, while also calling for a unifying option, reflecting a mixed centrist populist ideology with both left and right populist elements.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit evidence in the text of populist ideological stances on the left, right, or centrist spectrum; the manifesto focuses on policy details without populist framing.

chunk 3 · The manifesto opposes party-based patronage and nationalism while promoting citizen rights and participation, indicating a centrist populist ideology with mild left and right elements.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence of populist ideological content, left, right, or centrist, in the manifesto text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are centrist, framing the party as a liberal alternative to both post-communist opportunism and nationalist populism.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on anti-corruption and distributing state assets to all citizens without a strong left-wing or right-wing nationalist framing.

chunk 3 · The populist rhetoric is centrist, focusing on citizen empowerment and anti-corruption without strong left-wing redistribution or right-wing nationalist exclusion.

chunk 4 · The populist critique is centrist, focusing on the ruling party's incompetence and politicization of state institutions without a strong left or right ideological framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it advocates democratic revitalization, rule of law, and institutional reform (political); gender equality and social inclusion (social); zero corporate tax, deregulation, free enterprise, and competition (economic); and improved access to credit, fiscal transparency, and financial oversight enabling market function (financial market).

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently advocates rule of law, tax reduction, market competition, privatisation of state capital, stock market activation, and human rights protections, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal orientation across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly advocates for checks and balances, judicial independence, anti-discrimination including sexual orientation, university autonomy, secular state, and some market-oriented education reforms, reflecting a consistently pro-liberal stance across political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is explicitly liberal-democratic (LDP), championing freedom of culture and art from political interference, rule of law, minority rights, EU integration, fiscal decentralisation, support for private initiatives and market competition, and media freedom, all of which are strongly pro-liberal across all four sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, individual rights, a dynamic private sector, and enhanced capacities to combat informal economy, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial market dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports rule of law, protection of human rights, market-oriented economic policies, and education aligned with labor market needs, indicating a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 3 · Strong emphasis on rule of law, anti-discrimination, and rational economic policies supports a pro-liberal stance, though financial market specifics are absent.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political and social values, including democracy, rule of law, minority rights, and decentralization of economic powers, reflecting a pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including individual rights, private sector development, free enterprise, and market-based financial access.

chunk 2 · The party strongly advocates for classical liberal principles, including the rule of law, equal opportunities, market-based solutions, and commercial financial mechanisms.

chunk 3 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including the separation of powers, anti-discrimination protections for sexual minorities, and institutional autonomy.

chunk 4 · The text strongly advocates for liberal democratic values, including the rule of law, minority protections, and decentralization, while maintaining a balanced view on free markets.
