WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames Mexican society as a virtuous majority ("grandes mayorías") oppressed by corrupt elites—domestic and international—including neoliberal governments, transnational corporations, and the IMF, employing a stark good-vs-evil narrative ("socializar las pérdidas y privatizar las ganancias") and calling for popular sovereignty over economic and political decisions.

chunk 2 · The text frames the ruling elite (oligarquía nacional, capitalismo corporativo) as corrupt beneficiaries of neoliberal globalization, contrasts them with the virtuous working classes and popular masses, and casts the crisis in moralized terms of ethical/moral degeneration, yielding moderate-to-high populist scores across all three sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes corruption and authoritarianism, emphasizes the democratic aspirations of the people, and calls for active societal participation, reflecting strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and some moral struggle framing.

chunk 2 · The text explicitly criticizes the 'degeneración ética y moral del capitalismo corporativo' and highlights the role of 'las clases trabajadoras y de las masas excluidas de […] neoliberal,' showing anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized struggle.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames politics as a struggle between the virtuous 'pueblo' and a corrupt, self-serving 'elite nacional' or 'minorías' that control economic and political power.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing corrupt elites, oligarchical gains, and advocating for the defense of the popular classes.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populism is overwhelmingly left-wing: the manifesto calls for reversing neoliberal privatizations, restoring state control over strategic sectors, redistributing income from capital to labor, renegotiating free-trade agreements, and championing workers, peasants, and indigenous peoples against domestic and foreign capital elites.

chunk 2 · The Partido del Trabajo explicitly identifies itself with the left, calls for state regulation, control of speculative capital, redistribution of public resources to health/education, and direct government housing construction, placing it firmly in left-populist territory with only a minor nationalist-protectionist strand.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates for left-leaning redistribution and anti-neoliberal economic policies, defends public control over strategic sectors, and promotes broad democratic participation, showing a predominantly left populist ideology with some centrist appeals and limited right-leaning protectionism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto references left-wing populist themes like 'emancipación de las clases trabajadoras,' right-wing elements such as 'El racismo antimexicano,' and centrist critiques of political actors' incapacity, indicating a mixed populist ideological stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist discourse is heavily left-wing, focusing on anti-neoliberalism, wealth redistribution, state intervention, and opposition to corporate and financial elites.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on the emancipation of the working class and criticizing neoliberal globalization and corporate capitalism.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores moderately on political liberalism (democratic reform, judicial independence, civil rights) and social liberalism (anti-discrimination, gender equality), but is strongly anti-liberal on economic and financial-market dimensions, explicitly rejecting free markets, financial liberalization, and calling for state control of banking, capital flows, and strategic industries.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal economically: it rejects market-led housing allocation, calls for state control of speculative capital, and frames neoliberal integration as harmful; it shows mixed signals on political liberalism (criticizing archaic domination but not championing institutional checks) and invokes social equality without detailed civil-rights content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions and political rights, minority and social group protections, but critiques free market and financial liberalization policies, favoring state intervention and regulation, indicating moderate liberalism in political and social dimensions but skepticism towards economic and financial market liberalism.

chunk 2 · The text discusses institutional paralysis due to neoliberal economic integration, mentions racism and social issues, and critiques financial excesses, reflecting moderate liberalism in political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports political liberalism (checks and balances, judicial independence) and social rights, but strongly rejects economic and financial liberalism in favor of state control and protectionism.

chunk 2 · While supporting basic social and political rights, the text strongly rejects free-market mechanisms and financial capital, advocating instead for state-led development and regulation.
