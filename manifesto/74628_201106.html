WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly rejects populism ('popülizme asla tevessül etmedik') and is primarily a centre-right governing party platform combining national-unity rhetoric, Islamic-conservative cultural references, and pro-market economic goals; the people-centric appeals are moderate and the ideological flavour is mildly right-of-centre rather than strongly left or right populist.

chunk 2 · The populist flavor is centrist-pragmatic with modest left-leaning social spending rhetoric; there is no strong anti-corporate or nativist/anti-immigration framing, and the party positions itself as a competent governing force rather than a clear left or right populist movement.

chunk 3 · The manifesto blends mild centre-left elements (workers' rights, social justice, disability employment) with mild nationalist-economic themes (yerli otomobil, domestic defence industry), but is primarily a technocratic governing-party programme without strong populist ideological flavour.

chunk 4 · The manifesto blends centre-right elements (family values, national culture, strong state) with social-democratic delivery (free healthcare, education subsidies, social justice), and generic people-centred appeals, without a dominant left or right populist ideological thrust.

chunk 5 · The text blends mild left-leaning redistribution themes (poverty reduction, income equality, social support) with conservative-nationalist cultural values (family protection, traditional arts, national heritage), placing it in a moderate center-right populist ideological space with no dominant single ideological strand.

chunk 6 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist through its emphasis on poverty alleviation, income redistribution, social housing, and support for disadvantaged groups, but also contains mild right-cultural elements (Ottoman-Seljuk architectural heritage, family-centred welfare). Neither dimension is strongly populist in ideological terms.

chunk 7 · The manifesto blends moderate left-leaning social welfare elements (social housing for the poor, disability inclusion) with conservative-nationalist cultural references (Ottoman-Seljuk architecture, national identity) and centrist governance appeals, yielding a mixed but low-intensity ideological populism.

chunk 8 · Mild right-leaning populist ideology is evident through strong emphasis on ethnic/cultural kinship (soydaş, akraba topluluklar, Türk coğrafyası) and national identity, while left-populist redistribution themes are minimal and centrist pragmatism ("kazan-kazan") is present but secondary.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal in the political dimension, emphasising pluralist democracy, rule of law, judicial independence, individual rights, and a new civil constitution; social liberalism is supported through gender equality legislation and minority rights; economic and financial-market liberalism are referenced in the table of contents and economic philosophy sections but elaborated less in this chapter.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly endorses free capital and trade flows, market-based finance with prudential oversight, democratic governance, rule of law, and inclusion of disadvantaged groups, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 3 · The text consistently promotes competition, deregulation, reducing administrative burdens on businesses, improving the investment climate for foreign capital, facilitating SME access to financial markets, and aligning labour and trade law with EU/ILO standards, all of which are strongly pro-liberal across political, social, economic, and financial-market dimensions.

chunk 4 · The text supports market liberalisation in transport (railway sector opening to private operators), energy market competition (Energy Exchange, private sector participation), public-private partnerships, e-democracy, and equal access to education and health, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal orientation across all four dimensions.

chunk 5 · The text is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions, evidenced by constitutional amendments for gender equality, anti-discrimination commitments, parliamentary gender equality commissions, and support for entrepreneurship and youth participation; financial market liberalism is entirely absent from the text.

chunk 6 · The text shows moderate liberal orientation: it references constitutional equality amendments, women's and children's rights, private-sector incentives, capital-market-based housing finance, and alignment with EU environmental and regulatory standards, indicating a broadly pro-liberal but state-interventionist welfare stance.

chunk 7 · The manifesto consistently promotes democratic local governance, transparency and accountability, disability inclusion and equal access, private sector incentives, and capital-market-based housing finance models, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 8 · The text endorses democracy, transparency, good governance, free trade expansion, and individual rights (Uygur Turks' fundamental freedoms), reflecting a broadly liberal orientation across political, social, and economic dimensions; financial market policy is not discussed.
