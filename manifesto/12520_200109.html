WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows minimal populist features: it briefly notes that large multinationals can overshadow democratic governance and references 'folkelige bevegelser', but it does not frame politics as a Manichean struggle between a virtuous people and a corrupt elite; instead it consistently advocates balanced cooperation between state, market, and civil society.

chunk 2 · The text shows minimal populist features: it invokes solidarity and people-centred democracy but frames issues in Christian-democratic, institutional terms rather than as a corrupt elite vs. virtuous people struggle.

chunk 3 · The text is a mainstream Christian-democratic foreign/security/development policy manifesto with minimal populist framing; there is mild concern about multinational corporate power and a brief reference to the 1994 referendum result, but no sustained elite-bashing, people-as-hero rhetoric, or Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 4 · The text is a policy-oriented Christian democratic manifesto with virtually no populist rhetoric: it does not frame elites as corrupt, does not invoke 'the people' as a unified virtuous bloc, and contains no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative; the closest it comes is calling for a simpler Norway with less bureaucracy for small businesses.

chunk 5 · The text is a policy-oriented Christian democratic manifesto focused on education, culture, and research with virtually no populist framing: no elite-bashing, no virtuous-people rhetoric, and no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative beyond mild criticism of commercialisation.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The party explicitly positions itself as a 'third way' distinct from both socialism and liberalism, with Christian-democratic values; its mild redistributive concern and brief immigration reference do not constitute strong left- or right-populist ideology, making centrist the dominant (but still weak) populist ideological flavour.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is centrist Christian-democratic with mild centre-left welfare emphases (solidarity, social care) and some conservative-communitarian elements (alcohol restrictions, family values), but no strong left or right populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The text is primarily a Christian-democratic (KrF) policy programme; the modest left-leaning signals (Tobin tax, debt relief, redistribution to developing countries) are embedded in a broadly centrist, internationalist framework with no strong right-populist or left-populist ideological thrust.

chunk 4 · The manifesto blends mild centre-right elements (national ownership, family businesses, competitive conditions) with mild centre-left elements (fair distribution, green taxes, welfare), but lacks strong populist ideological framing in either direction, placing it as weakly centrist-populist at most.

chunk 5 · The manifesto blends modest centre-right Christian-democratic values (preservation of Christian cultural heritage, religious freedom) with some centre-left welfare commitments (increased student grants, equal access to education), yielding a very low overall populist ideological score with slight right-leaning cultural emphasis.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto affirms democratic institutions and human rights (lib_political=7), upholds equal human dignity regardless of gender and sexual orientation while opposing same-sex marriage equality (lib_social=6), supports good framework conditions for private enterprise while calling for regulated markets (lib_economic=5), and views large uncontrolled capital flows with concern rather than endorsing open financial markets (lib_financial_market=4).

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports democracy, rule of law, human rights, and minority protections (high lib_political and lib_social), while taking a mixed stance on economic liberalism (cautious market competition in welfare services alongside state regulation), and makes no mention of financial markets.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly endorses democracy, rule of law, and human rights both domestically and internationally, supports open markets and competition for Norwegian business, but tempers economic liberalism with calls for national ownership, a Tobin tax on capital flows, and redistribution to developing countries, yielding a moderately pro-liberal overall profile.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is moderately liberal: it supports market competition (telecom, free enterprise for SMEs), women's workplace equality, local self-governance, and removal of distortionary taxes, but tempers this with state ownership requirements in strategic sectors and significant welfare-state commitments.

chunk 5 · The text is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (religious freedom, minority rights, media pluralism, press freedom) and mixed on economic liberalism (tax relief for R&D but state-set student loan interest rates and heavy public funding commitments), producing a moderate overall liberalism score.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through emphasis on democracy and human rights, social liberalism via respect for diversity and freedoms, and economic liberalism by valuing private sector and responsible market regulation; financial market policy is not explicitly discussed.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal democratic principles, including independent judiciary, free media, anti-discrimination laws, social equality, and economic policies favoring work incentives, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including democracy, rule of law, human rights, international economic cooperation, debt relief, and reform of global financial institutions, as evidenced by explicit references to strengthening democracy, human rights, and economic liberalization.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports liberalism with emphasis on political rights via housing and social policies, social liberalism through gender equality efforts, economic liberalism by advocating good conditions for key industries, and financial market liberalism through prudent management of the Petroleum Fund, resulting in a solid liberalism overall score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly supports democratic institutions, media freedom, minority rights, cultural pluralism, and economic policies favoring market incentives and research investment, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports democratic institutions and human rights, but advocates for a highly regulated market economy balanced with strong public welfare and social responsibility.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political democracy, human rights, and minority protections, while taking a mixed, cautious approach to economic marketization and public tenders.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal political institutions, human rights, and market-based economic reforms, though it expresses skepticism toward unregulated global financial flows by proposing a Tobin tax.

chunk 4 · The text supports classical liberal principles such as market competition, human rights, and democratic values, though it advocates for some state ownership in key sectors like finance.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports classical liberal values such as religious freedom, democratic education, and market competition in service delivery.
