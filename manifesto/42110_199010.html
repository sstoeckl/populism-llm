WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: it frames environmental destruction as the result of "Oligarchien" and a hegemonic economic system that suppresses citizen protest, invokes "the people" (Bürgerinnen, Bevölkerung) as virtuous resisters, and frames the struggle as cultural resistance against a brutalizing capitalist order, though the tone is more programmatic than starkly Manichean.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently frames established parties, economic elites, and the Sozialpartnerschaft as corrupt and anti-democratic, invoking 'the people' and citizens as the rightful sovereign, with a moderate Manichaean contrast between rulers and the ruled.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populist tendencies: it criticises entrenched elite alliances (doctors' chamber, pharma, social insurance) and frames social policy as a democratic right of the people, but the dominant register is technocratic-programmatic rather than morally Manichean or strongly people-vs-elite.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly critiques elite-driven economic and scientific systems as exploitative and destructive, emphasizes the central role of 'the people' in democracy, and frames resistance as a cultural and human dignity struggle, reflecting moderate to strong populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the concentration of power in parties and elites, emphasizes the central role of the people in democracy, and highlights resistance against power abuse, reflecting moderate to strong populist themes.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly critiques the ruling economic system as based on an "unendliche" growth spiral and calls for redistribution of wealth, showing anti-elitism and people-centrism with moralized conflict over exploitation, supporting a moderate populism score.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits strong populist elements by framing citizens as the virtuous defenders of the environment against corrupt, ignorant political and economic oligarchies.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits strong populist elements by framing the political system as corrupt and out of touch, while advocating for direct democracy and power to the people.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the established 'social partnership' and state authorities for causing social inequalities, while advocating for the rights of ordinary citizens and marginalized groups.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist flavor is predominantly left-leaning: the manifesto explicitly critiques capitalism's profit-maximization logic, corporate monopolization of seeds and technology, and calls for subordinating the economy to democratic politics, with no significant right-populist nativist or anti-immigration content.

chunk 2 · The populist framing is clearly left-leaning: demands for income redistribution, minimum wages, workers' co-determination, and anti-corporate critique dominate, while right-wing nativist populism is explicitly rejected.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it demands wealth redistribution, a basic income, equal rights for foreign workers, and explicitly rejects anti-immigration framing, while attacking corporate and institutional elites (pharma, employers) rather than ethnic or national outgroups.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text criticizes capitalist economic structures and their alliance with science and politics as exploitative, supports democratic participation, and rejects right-wing nationalist themes, indicating a left-leaning populist ideology with centrist elements.

chunk 2 · The text strongly advocates for left-wing populist themes such as wealth redistribution and social justice, while also showing some right-wing populist elements like criticism of xenophobia; it emphasizes centrist populist appeals to democratic participation and empowerment of citizens.

chunk 3 · The text advocates for wealth redistribution and social justice (left populism), rejects xenophobic claims (low right populism), and seeks broad political alliances (centrist populism), indicating a mixed but overall moderate populist ideological stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing its critique on capitalist profit maximization, corporate power, and the commodification of nature and humans.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on redistribution, workers' self-determination, and social justice, while explicitly rejecting right-wing xenophobia.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing heavily on wealth redistribution, anti-corporate sentiment, and the rights of workers and minorities, while explicitly rejecting right-wing anti-immigrant stances.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is moderately liberal politically (strong emphasis on democratic participation, citizens' rights, rule of law, parliamentary oversight) and socially (gender equality for farmers), but economically mixed—it calls for market liberalization in agriculture while simultaneously advocating heavy state regulation, environmental taxes, and public control of energy policy; financial markets are not discussed.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (democracy, minority rights, gender equality, press freedom), but critical of neoliberal economic deregulation and shows only mixed/weak support for financial market liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores high on political and social liberalism (democratisation, LGBTQ+ equality, minority rights) but low on economic and financial-market liberalism, advocating redistribution, land-speculation restrictions, a capital-gains tax on savings, and a value-added levy on firms rather than market-enabling measures.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, constitutional rights, environmental protections, and market-oriented economic reforms, including regulatory oversight and transparency in energy policy, reflecting a pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political principles including political participation, civil rights protections, and social equality, alongside economic liberalism emphasizing fair competition and deregulation; financial market policy is not explicitly discussed.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports democracy and social justice (political liberalism), gender equality (social liberalism), critiques economic growth models (economic liberalism), but lacks explicit financial market discussion, resulting in a moderate overall liberalism score.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social rights through democratization and constitutional protections, but takes a highly critical stance on capitalist market dynamics, advocating for heavy state regulation and intervention.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through minority rights and separation of powers, but rejects economic liberalism in favor of market intervention and regulation.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political democracy and social minority rights, but rejects classical economic liberalism in favor of heavy state intervention, price controls, and financial taxation.
