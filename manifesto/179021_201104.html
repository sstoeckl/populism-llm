WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores very high on populism: it consistently frames corrupt elites, transnational corporations, and neoliberal governments (Fujimori, Toledo, García) as enemies of the virtuous Peruvian people, employs a stark good-vs-evil narrative between defenders of the "vergonzante pasado" and champions of a new nation, and repeatedly invokes "las mayorías" and "el pueblo peruano" as the moral center of politics.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently frames a corrupt, predatory neoliberal elite ("élites económicas y políticas que capturan el Estado") as oppressing the virtuous popular majority ("la gran mayoría"), invoking a Manichean struggle between the people and oligarchic/neoliberal forces that have governed Peru for 180 years.

chunk 3 · The manifesto consistently frames neoliberal elites and foreign capital as corrupt exploiters of the Peruvian people, invokes 'the people' as a unified virtuous group deserving sovereignty, and constructs a Manichean struggle between nationalist development and the desnationalizing neoliberal model.

chunk 4 · The text frames neoliberal elites and captured regulators as corrupt adversaries of consumers and the Peruvian people, invoking a moderate populist good-vs-evil narrative, but the dominant content is technocratic infrastructure and economic policy rather than sustained populist rhetoric.

chunk 5 · The text repeatedly frames economic regulators and neoliberal elites as corrupt captives of powerful economic groups exploiting ordinary consumers, while invoking 'the people' (consumers, campesinos, small producers) as the virtuous center of the economy deserving state protection.

chunk 6 · The text shows moderate populism: it frames neoliberal elites and globalisers as corrupt or misguided adversaries of the people, invokes the population's welfare as the central goal, and draws a moral contrast between the current model and a transformative alternative, but the tone is largely technocratic and policy-oriented rather than strongly Manichean.

chunk 7 · The manifesto exhibits moderate populism: it frames neoliberal governments (Fujimori, Toledo, García) as having betrayed workers and the poor, invokes the people's collective rights against powerful lobbies and elites, and frames politics as a struggle between the vulnerable majority and entrenched economic interests, though it stops short of a fully Manichean conspiratorial narrative.

chunk 8 · The text frames global inequality as a structural injustice where industrialized elites concentrate 80% of world wealth at the expense of developing peoples, invokes the Peruvian people's sovereign rights over their resources, and calls for rejecting market-only decision-making — showing moderate populist framing without a fully Manichean narrative.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is overwhelmingly left-leaning: the manifesto calls for redistribution, state regulation of markets, renationalization of natural resources (gas via Petroperú), universal public services, labor protections, anti-corporate rhetoric, and Latin American solidarity, while explicitly identifying with "fuerzas de izquierda y progresistas"; nationalist/sovereignty themes are present but subordinate to the left-redistributive program.

chunk 2 · The populism is predominantly left-leaning, centered on anti-neoliberal redistribution, state planning, social justice, and anti-corporate elite rhetoric, with only marginal nationalist-right elements (subordination of individual to patria) and some generic people-centrism.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is predominantly left-leaning, emphasizing redistribution (windfall taxes, progressive taxation), nationalization of strategic sectors, labor rights restoration, and anti-corporate elite rhetoric, with only minor nationalist-identity elements and no significant right-populist content.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is predominantly left-leaning populist, emphasizing state-led industrialization, renationalization of strategic enterprises (Petroperú, Electroperú, ports, airports), redistribution through infrastructure, and anti-neoliberal framing, with only minor nationalist/sovereignty overtones.

chunk 5 · The populist framing is predominantly left-leaning, attacking corporate monopolies, privatization legacies, and neoliberal ideology in favor of redistribution, consumer protection, and state intervention, with only minor nationalist/protectionist elements around TLC renegotiation.

chunk 6 · The text is predominantly left-populist: it calls for redistribution, criticises neoliberal labour precarisation, advocates state-led development, social rights, and renegotiation of free-trade agreements, with only minor nationalist/identity elements.

chunk 7 · The populist ideology is clearly left-leaning: the manifesto calls for reversing neoliberal labour reforms, restoring the solidary and redistributive character of pensions, expanding universal public health and social programmes, and critiquing corporate elites and financial speculation, with only marginal nationalist/identity framing.

chunk 8 · The text leans left-populist by explicitly rejecting "neoliberal dogma," calling for redistribution of global benefits, state-led development, and solidarity with Cuba and Palestine, while sovereignty/national-interest rhetoric adds a mild right-populist undertone.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto presents a mixed liberal profile: it endorses democratic institutions, separation of powers, gender equality, and indigenous rights (moderately pro-liberal on political and social dimensions), but is explicitly anti-neoliberal on economic policy—rejecting deregulation, free trade agreements, and privatization—while supporting a state-regulated "economía nacional de mercado" and developing a domestic capital market, yielding low-to-moderate scores on economic and financial-market liberalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports political democracy, pluralism, and rule of law (pro-liberal), and endorses a "national market economy" with private initiative, but simultaneously advocates strong state planning, regulation, and rejection of the neoliberal model, yielding moderate overall liberal scores.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports democratic institutions, gender equality, and a domestic capital market, but simultaneously advocates capital controls, nationalization of strategic sectors, state-led industrial policy, and rejection of the neoliberal model, producing moderate scores across all sub-dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it explicitly promotes capital market development, consumer rights, and anti-discrimination, but simultaneously advocates renationalization of strategic sectors, rejection of neoliberalism, and strong state intervention in markets, yielding a moderate overall liberal score.

chunk 5 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it supports market competition and anti-monopoly enforcement (pro-liberal) but also advocates strong state price regulation, restructuring of financial institutions, and renegotiation of free trade agreements (anti-liberal), resulting in a moderate overall score.

chunk 6 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it supports democratic participation, social rights, and capital-market access for SMEs (pro-liberal), but also advocates renegotiating free-trade agreements, state-directed credit, and heavy public intervention in health, education, and housing (anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions).

chunk 7 · The manifesto is moderately liberal on political and social dimensions (strong support for democracy, human rights, gender equality, LGBTQ+ rights, indigenous rights), but economically mixed-to-statist: it rejects pure market logic, advocates strong state intervention in health, pensions and housing, and calls for regulation of financial speculation and capital flows, yielding a below-midpoint score on economic and financial-market liberalism.

chunk 8 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it supports democratic institutions, human rights courts, and cultural pluralism (pro-liberal), but calls for heavy regulation of financial markets, state-led industrial policy, capital-flow controls, and reform of Bretton Woods institutions to reduce market autonomy (anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions).
