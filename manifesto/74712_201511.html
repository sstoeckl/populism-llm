WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames the AKP government as corrupt, incompetent, and treasonous elites who have betrayed the virtuous Turkish nation, while MHP positions itself as the sole defender of the people's true interests, creating a clear populist good-vs-evil binary.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies through anti-corruption rhetoric targeting political and business elites (17-25 Aralık references), citizen-centric governance language, and framing of justice as protecting the righteous over the powerful, but these are embedded in a technocratic, institutional reform framework rather than a strong people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The text shows mild populist features: a brief critique of AKP-era economic mismanagement, appeals to participatory development for "the whole of society," and calls to remove political patronage from public administration, but the dominant register is technocratic economic policy rather than a moralized people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 4 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto focused on economic development, SME support, energy, agriculture, and social protection; populist rhetoric is minimal, with only occasional references to rent-seeking elites and vague people-centered welfare promises, without sustained anti-elite or Manichean framing.

chunk 5 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: it targets rent-seeking elites and exploitative labour arrangements, invokes the people's welfare broadly (no one left hungry), and frames some issues in moral terms (slavery-like labour), but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

chunk 6 · The text exhibits moderate populism through strong Manichean framing of the PKK peace process as "treason" (ihanet), calls to punish all who "supported" it, and nationalist people-centric appeals, though much of the document is technocratic health/urban policy.

chunk 7 · The text invokes the nation/people as a unified virtuous group and frames certain media and foreign actors as misleading or harmful, but stops short of a full populist elite-vs-people dichotomy; the dominant tone is nationalist-statist rather than strongly populist.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is overwhelmingly right-wing populist, centered on Turkish-Islamic national identity, anti-separatism, strong unitary state, and anti-PKK militarism, with only minor redistributive social welfare elements that do not constitute a left-populist program.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is predominantly right-leaning populist in ideology, strongly emphasizing Turkish national identity, unitary state structure, opposition to ethnic minority rights and regional autonomy, and resistance to constitutional changes that would fragment national unity, while also incorporating moderate social justice and redistribution concerns.

chunk 3 · The text blends mild left-leaning redistribution (tax relief for low-income earners, debt restructuring for SMEs and farmers) with mild nationalist-protectionist elements (protecting the "national character" of financial markets, prioritising domestic investors), placing it in a weakly left-of-centre nationalist-developmentalist space rather than a strongly ideological populist camp.

chunk 4 · The manifesto blends mild left-leaning redistribution (income equality, social protection, SME support) with nationalist economic themes (national energy policy, domestic production, reducing foreign dependency), without a dominant populist ideological flavor.

chunk 5 · The manifesto blends left-leaning economic policies (minimum wage increases, social transfers, worker protections, redistribution) with right-leaning nationalist-conservative themes (national-spiritual values, family institution, Turkish cultural identity, national unity), characteristic of a nationalist-conservative party (MHP) with social-welfare commitments.

chunk 6 · The populist content is predominantly right-wing, centered on nationalist identity, anti-separatist rhetoric, military strength, and opposition to Kurdish peace negotiations, with minimal left redistributive content.

chunk 7 · The manifesto is clearly right-wing populist, grounding its appeal in Turkish-Islamic civilizational heritage, national unity, and ethnic-national identity (MHP's nationalist ideology), with no left-wing redistributive or anti-corporate framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto endorses free enterprise, competitive markets, rule of law, judicial independence, and individual rights, but tempers these with strong nationalist restrictions on political freedoms (e.g., anti-separatism conditions), creating a mixed liberal profile across all dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently supports rule of law, judicial independence, democratic institutions, free-market competition, and financial market development, scoring moderately liberal across all dimensions, though social liberalism is mixed due to the strong emphasis on national unity over minority/ethnic rights.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal in its support for competitive markets, fiscal transparency, parliamentary oversight, and open trade, but tempers this with state-directed industrial policy, selective capital controls on short-term flows, public bank mandates, and protectionist elements in financial markets, producing a mixed overall liberal score.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal signals: it supports competitive markets, transparency, and financial market infrastructure (energy exchange, SME capital markets access), but also advocates significant state intervention through price guarantees, subsidies, state-owned energy companies, and social transfer programs.

chunk 5 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal signals: it endorses gender equality and democratic university governance (pro-liberal), but also emphasises state-led social protection, wage controls, and nationalist cultural restrictions; economic policy mixes market-enabling public-private cooperation with heavy state intervention in labour markets and social transfers.

chunk 6 · The text shows mixed liberalism: it promotes private health institutions and free zones (pro-liberal), but also extensive state control over pricing, public provision of services, and strong nationalist restrictions on political pluralism (anti-liberal).

chunk 7 · The manifesto endorses democratic standards, rule of law, and human rights rhetorically, and supports trade openness via the Customs Union reform, but frames these within a strong nationalist-statist vision that conditions liberal values on national interest, yielding a mixed liberal profile.

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal democratic principles including rule of law, judicial independence, political rights, civil rights protections, free market economy, and international economic engagement, indicating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial market dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights including women's protections, market economy principles, and financial market strengthening, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly discusses political liberalism through references to rule of law and institutional stability, social liberalism via public financial management and social policies, economic liberalism through capital market development and investment facilitation, and financial market liberalism with risk-based deposit insurance and competitive banking system reforms, supporting a moderate pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 4 · Metin, demokrasi, haklar, azınlık koruması, serbest piyasa, rekabet, finansal piyasa düzenlemeleri gibi liberal unsurları açıkça desteklemekte ve bu alanlarda iyileştirme ve teşvik vaat etmektedir.

chunk 5 · The manifesto advocates political rights and institutional responsibility, social equality especially for women, economic policies favoring tax base expansion and relief, but lacks explicit financial market discussion, indicating moderate liberalism overall.

chunk 6 · Metin, sağlık hizmetlerinde vatandaşın yükünün hafifletilmesi, insan haklarına saygı, özel sektörün teşviki ve sağlık turizminin geliştirilmesi gibi liberal politikaları destekleyen ifadeler içermektedir.

chunk 7 · The manifesto explicitly references democracy, human rights, rule of law, and justice, indicating a moderately pro-liberal political and social stance, but lacks explicit discussion of economic or financial market liberalism.
