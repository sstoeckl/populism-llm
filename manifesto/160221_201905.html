WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto exhibits maximal populism across all three sub-dimensions: it frames a corrupt oligarchic elite (political and economic) as the enemy of a virtuous, unified people, employs stark Manichean good-vs-evil rhetoric (the state as "a great farce," democracy as a "myth"), and consistently centers "the people" as the sole legitimate sovereign whose will has been systematically betrayed by traditional parties and transnational capital.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently frames Panamanian society as a struggle between a corrupt elite (Canal executives, neoliberal governments, multinational capital) and the exploited working people, invoking strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and a Manichean good-vs-evil narrative throughout.

chunk 3 · The manifesto consistently frames elites, the "partidocracia," and transnational capital as corrupt adversaries of "the people," invoking a clear good-vs-evil struggle between the popular majority and a dominant class that controls justice, energy, and politics.

chunk 4 · The FAD manifesto exhibits strong populism: it consistently frames economic elites, transnational capital, and the 'partidocracia' as corrupt adversaries of the Panamanian people, invokes the people as a unified virtuous subject, and frames politics as a Manichean struggle between the people's needs and elite greed ('Al Dios dinero nada le importa más que a […] mismo').

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the oligarchic elite controlling the state and emphasizes the people's role as sovereign, framing a moral struggle against corrupt elites, demonstrating strong populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes economic and political elites privileging minority sectors and highlights the plight of the working people and middle sectors, framing corruption as an abuse of hegemonic power, thus evidencing strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized struggle consistent with populism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly critiques the justice system as serving the interests of the dominant class and calls for broad public participation in judicial appointments, reflecting anti-elitism and people-centrism, while also framing economic and political struggles in moral terms.

chunk 4 · The text explicitly criticizes economic elites and the traditional party system as controlling the country and prioritizing their own interests, framing the rest of the population as obstacles, reflecting anti-elitism and people-centrism with a moralized struggle against economic greed.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames politics as a struggle between corrupt political and economic oligarchies and the virtuous majority of the people.

chunk 2 · The text strongly frames the political and economic elites as corrupt, hypocritical, and exploitative, contrasting them with the suffering of the working people.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits strong populism by framing political and economic elites as corrupt actors serving the 'dominant class' against the virtuous 'people'.

chunk 4 · The text strongly frames politics as a struggle between a corrupt 'partidocracia' allied with transnational capital and the virtuous 'pueblo panameño' whose needs are ignored.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populism is overwhelmingly left-wing: the manifesto explicitly attacks neoliberalism, multinational corporations, the IMF/World Bank, free-trade agreements, privatizations, and capital concentration, while advocating state planning, redistribution, agrarian reform, progressive taxation, nationalization of strategic enterprises, and workers' rights — a classic left-populist program with minimal nativist or right-populist content.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is firmly left-wing, calling for progressive taxation, re-nationalization of privatized enterprises, redistribution of land, state-directed credit, price controls, and explicitly denouncing neoliberal capitalism as the root cause of inequality.

chunk 3 · The populism is firmly left-wing: the manifesto explicitly rejects neoliberalism, calls for redistribution, state-led public services, anti-privatization, and frames corporate and political elites as exploiters of the working poor and marginalized communities.

chunk 4 · The populism is firmly left-wing: the manifesto explicitly opposes neoliberalism, calls for renationalization of privatized enterprises, redistribution, social equality, indigenous rights, gender equity, and frames transnational corporations and the IMF/World Bank as enemies of the people.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates for structural economic, political, and social changes with a leftist orientation, while also emphasizing participatory and independent state roles, reflecting a predominantly left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist elements.

chunk 2 · The text advocates for progressive redistribution policies, increased state revenues for wealth distribution, and emphasizes citizen participation against corruption, reflecting a left-leaning populist ideology with centrist elements in people-elite appeals.

chunk 3 · The text emphasizes fighting inequality and social exclusion as core to its security and social policies, indicating a left-leaning populist ideology, with no evidence of right-wing populism and some centrist appeals to broad participation.

chunk 4 · The manifesto critiques the neoliberal economic model based on consumerism and energy waste, while also highlighting the control of traditional parties and elites, indicating a left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist appeals and right-leaning elements in elite protectionism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist discourse is explicitly left-wing, focusing on anti-neoliberalism, anti-corporate elites, and wealth redistribution.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is explicitly left-wing, focusing on anti-neoliberalism, wealth redistribution, and the struggle of workers and peasants against corporate and financial elites.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on anti-neoliberalism, anti-corporate interests, and the redistribution of wealth.

chunk 4 · The populist discourse is heavily left-wing, focusing on anti-neoliberalism, anti-corporate elites, environmental sustainability, and state-led redistribution.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal across all four dimensions: it rejects free markets and neoliberalism in favor of state planning and regulation, proposes state control of banking and credit rates, advocates price controls and nationalizations, and while invoking human rights and democratic participation, frames these within a majoritarian constituent-power model that subordinates institutional checks to popular sovereignty.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal across all dimensions: it advocates state price controls, bank regulation aligned to government planning, re-nationalization of privatized firms, and a constituent assembly with full powers, while acknowledging discrimination against minorities but framing solutions through state intervention rather than individual rights.

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores mixed-to-low on liberalism: it supports democratic institutions and social rights for marginalized groups, but strongly opposes market-based economic models, privatization of health and social security, and neoliberal policies, reflecting a predominantly statist, anti-market orientation.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is largely anti-liberal economically (calling for renationalization, state control of energy, rejection of neoliberalism) and financially (hostile to IFIs and transnational capital), but scores moderately on social liberalism (gender equity, minority rights, afrodescendant inclusion) and shows mixed political liberalism (criticizing restricted democracy while proposing democratic reforms).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto acknowledges the lack of genuine political independence and rule of law, supports human rights but with mixed commitment, and calls for state economic control and planning, indicating limited endorsement of classical liberal principles.

chunk 2 · The manifesto discusses political rights such as freedom of expression and press, acknowledges social discrimination issues, critiques neoliberal economic policies and deregulation, and calls for regulation of banking aligned with development goals, indicating a moderate liberal stance with critical views on current economic models.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports rule of law and democratic justice, human rights protections, and criticizes economic practices harming the poor, showing a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions, though financial market specifics are not addressed.

chunk 4 · The text acknowledges restricted political democracy, discrimination issues, and critiques neoliberal economic policies, indicating low scores for political, social, economic, and financial market liberalism dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text rejects classical liberal economics, advocating for price controls, state regulation of banking, and nationalization of strategic sectors, while supporting basic social rights.

chunk 2 · The text supports political checks and balances and minority rights, but strongly rejects economic liberalism and free financial markets in favor of state control, price regulation, and banking restrictions.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through the rule of law and human rights, but rejects economic liberalism by opposing privatization and market-based solutions.

chunk 4 · While supporting social equality and democratic rights, the text strongly rejects classical liberal economic principles, advocating for nationalization, price controls, and heavy state intervention.
