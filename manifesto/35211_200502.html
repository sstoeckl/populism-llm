WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly frames the right-wing governing coalition as corrupt and incompetent elites who betrayed the people, uses good-vs-evil (left vs. right as "civilização") framing, and repeatedly invokes the suffering of workers and the poor as the virtuous people wronged by political and economic elites.

chunk 2 · The text shows moderate populism: it frames the petroleum lobby and market rules as corrupt forces harming ordinary citizens, invokes the people as victims of elite-driven privatisation, and uses some good-vs-evil framing (e.g., "fraude contra o ambiente e contra as pessoas"), but the dominant register is policy-programmatic rather than strongly populist.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populist features: it frames media conglomerates and political elites as colluding against the public interest, uses the label "FRAUDE" against rival parties, and invokes democratisation for ordinary citizens, but the dominant register is programmatic policy rather than a moralistic people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 4 · The text frames dominant elites as having delayed necessary national development strategies, invokes popular struggles and civic participation, and draws a moral contrast between a social Europe serving ordinary people versus a Europe of powerful governments and market liberalisation, yielding moderate-to-high populist scores across all three sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes political elites as fragile and out of touch, emphasizes the need for a new social policy cycle created by the people, and frames political conflict as a moral struggle between right and left, supporting a populist overall score of 7.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes economic and political elites such as the 'lobby petrolífero' and highlights popular movements, indicating moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism, with some moralized struggle against environmental injustice, reflecting moderate populism overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes media monopolies and their influence on political power, showing anti-elitism, and supports limiting media concentration, reflecting a moderate populist stance overall.

chunk 4 · The text criticizes elites as 'bons alunos' obedient to decision-makers, invokes 'pessoas, associações, movimentos' as the people, and frames political defeat as a moral loss, supporting moderate populist scores.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the ruling political elites as fragile and out of touch, while positioning itself as the defender of the people's interests.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing corporate lobbies and political elites as corrupt forces acting against the interests of the people.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the collusion between political power and large private media conglomerates while championing the rights of the marginalized majority.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits moderate anti-elitism by criticizing the 'elites dominantes' for delaying national development, but lacks strong people-centrism or Manichean framing.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The Bloco de Esquerda manifesto is firmly left-populist: it advocates nationalization of energy, redistribution through progressive taxation and minimum income, workers' rights, immigrant legalization, and explicitly opposes right-wing and xenophobic politics, with no right-populist or centrist-populist content.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it opposes market liberalisation, privatisation of public services (health, water, energy), and corporate power, while advocating redistribution, public ownership, and social equality; right-wing or nativist populist elements are essentially absent.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it explicitly names neoliberal policies as the source of social and economic inequality, advocates strong public services, redistribution, and anti-corporate media regulation, with no right-wing nativist or identity-protectionist content.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it opposes privatisation and market liberalisation, calls for public investment, social protection, and workers' rights, and explicitly identifies as a socialist left engaged in popular struggles, with no meaningful right-wing or nativist content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports left-wing populist ideology with clear anti-corporate and social equality policies, opposes right-wing immigration stances, and shows some centrist people-elite appeals, justifying a left score of 9 and overall populist ideology score of 7.

chunk 2 · The text emphasizes social inequality, poverty, and public service defense, including strong support for public education and health, rejecting privatization, indicating a left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist appeals and limited right-wing elements.

chunk 3 · The manifesto combines left-wing populist policies such as state funding for education and opposition to neoliberal reforms with centrist appeals to democratic control and some right-leaning concerns about media monopolies and national market concentration, resulting in a moderate populist ideological profile.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports left-wing populist policies like public investment and social protections, with centrist appeals to trust and no evidence of right-wing populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, combining anti-elite rhetoric with socialist, anti-corporate, and redistributive economic demands.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on anti-market, anti-privatization, and state-controlled public services.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, explicitly framing its struggle against neoliberal policies and economic inequalities.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, explicitly identifying as a socialist left committed to popular struggles and social equality.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is socially liberal (expanding LGBTQ+ rights, abortion rights, immigrant rights) but strongly anti-liberal economically (nationalization, capital controls, Tobin tax, opposition to privatization and market liberalism), yielding a mixed overall liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The text is broadly anti-liberal economically and in financial-market terms (opposing privatisation, market pricing of energy, PPPs, and shareholder primacy), while showing pro-liberal stances on political pluralism/media freedom and social rights (gender equality, minority inclusion, reproductive rights).

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores high on political and social liberalism (defending press freedom, democracy, LGBT rights, gender parity), but is anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions, opposing market liberalisation, neoliberal orthodoxy, and financial austerity, yielding a mixed overall liberalism score.

chunk 4 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it supports democratic reform and gender equality (pro-liberal) but strongly opposes market liberalisation, privatisation, and orthodox financial policy, advocating state-directed investment, interest-rate cuts by the ECB, and rejection of market-based service provision (anti-liberal).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto advocates for democracy, political opposition, social rights including minority protections, economic reforms including tax justice, and financial transparency measures, supporting a high liberalism overall score of 8.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through constitutional rights and public health services, social liberalism via universal health and social protections, economic liberalism in defending public education and opposing privatization, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through constitutional guarantees of media freedom, social liberalism via expansion of rights for marginalized groups, and economic liberalism through decentralization and opposition to clientelism, though financial market issues are not addressed.

chunk 4 · The text critiques limited parliamentary power, minimal social equality, market liberalizations, and restrictive financial policies, indicating a low pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political and social liberalism (civil rights, LGBTQ+ equality, and democracy) but rejects economic and financial liberalism, advocating for nationalizations and banking restrictions.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social rights but rejects economic liberalism, privatization, and market-based solutions in public services.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic pluralism and LGBTQ+ rights, but rejects economic liberalism by advocating state intervention and anti-monopoly regulations.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports democratic institutions and social equality, but rejects economic liberalism and financial market autonomy in favor of state intervention and regulation.
