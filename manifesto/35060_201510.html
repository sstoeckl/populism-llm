WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto contains only mild populist elements: the previous government is briefly blamed for incompetence ("a incompetência e a leviandade de outros"), and the Portuguese people are praised for their resilience, but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than populist.

chunk 2 · The text is a detailed centre-right policy manifesto focused on competitiveness, health, youth, sport, and economic reform; it contains virtually no populist rhetoric—no sustained anti-elite framing, no moralized good-vs-evil narrative, and only incidental references to "all Portuguese" rather than a unified virtuous people opposed to corrupt elites.

chunk 3 · The text is a centre-right governing party manifesto focused on technocratic reform, fiscal discipline, and market liberalisation; populist rhetoric is virtually absent, with only a passing reference to removing "excessive rents" and defending against "financial irresponsibility," neither of which frames a corrupt elite versus virtuous people.

chunk 4 · The text is a mainstream centre-right governing coalition manifesto focused on technocratic policy detail; it explicitly distances itself from populist rhetoric ("retóricas, frequentemente populistas"), invokes citizens mainly in the context of democratic participation and institutional trust, and frames politics as a delegated mandate rather than a people-vs-elite struggle.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideological content is weak and mixed: there are modest left-leaning references to fighting inequality and social protection, minor national-identity references regarding emigrant return, and some generic people-centred appeals, but no strong left- or right-populist programmatic framing.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is a mainstream centre-right programme emphasising market competitiveness, private investment, deregulation, and fiscal consolidation; it shows no meaningful left-populist redistribution rhetoric and only marginal national-identity references, placing it firmly outside populist ideological territory.

chunk 3 · The minimal populist signals lean slightly centrist-technocratic (social concertation, balanced budgets) with a faint left tinge (positive discrimination for vulnerable workers) and no meaningful right-populist identity framing; overall populist ideology is negligible.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows no strong populist ideological flavour; it explicitly rejects populist rhetoric, pursues market liberalisation, EU integration, and rule-of-law consolidation, with only mild social-equality language that does not constitute left-populist redistribution framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it celebrates Portugal's return to international financial markets at low interest rates, champions private investment and export-led growth, invokes equality of opportunity and social inclusion, and frames the democratic welfare state as a key achievement, while also supporting market-based competition and prudential fiscal management.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently promotes free-market principles—corporate tax cuts, energy market liberalisation, capital market deepening, privatisations (TAP, CP Carga), deregulation, and competition—while affirming democratic institutions and social inclusion, placing it firmly in the pro-liberal range across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions privatisation (CP Carga, ANA, TAP), market liberalisation of transport, an IPO for infrastructure, fiscal consolidation with tax cuts (IRC, IRS surcharge, IMT), democratic transparency, and equal opportunities, reflecting a consistent classical-liberal orientation.

chunk 4 · The manifesto consistently endorses liberal democratic institutions (rule of law, separation of powers, parliamentary scrutiny), gender equality and minority integration, free-market competition and deregulation, and energy-market liberalisation with transparency, placing it firmly in the pro-liberal range across all four sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political institutions, social equality, market-based economic growth, and financial market integration, reflecting a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles including political rights and democratic institutions, social inclusion and youth empowerment, economic competitiveness and enterprise promotion, and financial market support for business and innovation, as evidenced by multiple detailed policy commitments.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles including transparency and accountability in government, equality of access to public services, fiscal reforms promoting investment and competitiveness, and market-based financial strategies such as IPOs, indicating a strong pro-liberal stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including the consolidation of the rule of law, promotion of equality and non-discrimination, endorsement of market economy principles, and calls for financial regulatory reforms that enable market functioning.

Gemini

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing market competitiveness, private enterprise, financial market development, and democratic institutions.

chunk 4 · The text strongly endorses classical liberal principles, explicitly stating that the market is the most efficient way to organize the economy, while championing the rule of law, non-discrimination, and financial integration.
