WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows only mild populist features: it criticises ideological blindness of authorities and frames a moral struggle between Christian and secular values, but it does not systematically pit 'the people' against corrupt elites nor employ strong Manichean rhetoric; the dominant frame is religious-conservative rather than populist.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows minimal populist features: there is a brief criticism of the Balkenende IV cabinet's budget cuts and some framing of secular elites as harmful, but the text is overwhelmingly a detailed policy programme grounded in Christian-democratic values rather than a people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The text contains only mild populist signals: a brief critique of government overspending driven by political motives, a call to protect the weak, and a rhetorical flourish about a "possessed world," but there is no sustained people-vs-elite framing, no conspiracy narrative, and no moralized good-vs-evil struggle typical of high-populism texts.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto expresses distrust of secular elites and emphasizes a spiritual 'people' needing revival, framing societal conflict as a moral struggle, indicating moderate populist elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto expresses moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism through critique of secular elite morality and emphasis on national identity, with a moralized worldview reflecting some Manichean elements.

chunk 3 · The manifesto expresses distrust of elites through criticism of tax evasion, emphasizes fair distribution of burdens and rewards for the people, and frames the world in terms of crises and moral challenges, indicating low to moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean views.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text does not contain populist elements such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or a Manichean worldview, focusing instead on a traditionalist, theocratic, and conservative Christian worldview.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits low overall populism, with minor anti-elitist elements criticizing secular politicians and opinion leaders for imposing their values.

chunk 3 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or appeals to a pure people against a corrupt elite.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto's populist flavour is predominantly right-leaning, centred on national-Christian identity, opposition to Islam in the public sphere, and restrictive immigration policy, with only minimal redistributive or anti-corporate rhetoric.

chunk 2 · The manifesto's populist ideological flavour is predominantly right-leaning, driven by restrictive immigration stances, national-identity emphasis, and opposition to Islam-related issues, while left-populist redistribution themes are largely absent.

chunk 3 · The SGP manifesto leans mildly right-populist through its emphasis on controlling immigration, national sovereignty over EU integration, and Christian-conservative values (anti-abortion, pro-family), but the overall populist ideological intensity is low as the text is primarily a policy programme rather than a populist appeal.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text shows right-leaning populist ideology through emphasis on national identity, Christian values, and opposition to Islam, with some left-leaning critique of government overreach, and centrist appeals to individual responsibility.

chunk 2 · The text shows a right-leaning populist ideology by opposing Turkey's EU membership on cultural and religious grounds, combined with centrist and mild left-leaning elements emphasizing regulation reduction and national sovereignty.

chunk 3 · The text combines left-leaning calls for fair distribution and economic justice, right-leaning national and cultural protectionism (e.g., opposition to Turkey joining the EU), and centrist pragmatic appeals to national interest and international cooperation, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · There is no evidence of populist ideology in the text, as the party's appeals are structured around religious and historical-cultural conservatism rather than a people-versus-elite dynamic.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is predominantly right-wing, focusing on restricting immigration, preserving national identity, and opposing multiculturalism.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideology, as the text focuses on traditional Christian-democratic and conservative policy positions.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto defends the rule-of-law state and limited government (mixed political liberalism), explicitly opposes same-sex marriage and LGBTQ+ equality (strongly anti-liberal socially), and accepts market mechanisms only under strict public-interest conditions (mixed economic liberalism), while financial-market policy is entirely absent.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports representative democracy and anti-discrimination but restricts civil liberties for LGBTQ+ groups and religious minorities; it favours deregulation and market competition but also proposes a bank tax and financial-sector controls; overall it reflects a Christian-conservative rather than classically liberal orientation.

chunk 3 · The SGP shows moderate economic liberalism (tax cuts, reducing regulation, entrepreneurship support) but tempers this with calls for stricter financial market oversight and frames social liberalism negatively (opposing minority rights for LGBTQ+, restricting abortion), yielding a mixed overall liberal profile.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports rule of law, minority rights, limited government economic intervention, and market principles, but lacks discussion on financial market specifics, reflecting moderate classical liberalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports liberal political institutions and rule of law, advocates anti-discrimination measures, calls for regulatory simplification, and demands better financial sector oversight, reflecting moderate liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports democratic oversight and rule of law, expresses conservative social views limiting some civil rights expansions, favors market-friendly economic policies with tax reductions and subsidy cuts, and calls for improved financial market regulation that enables market function, resulting in a moderate liberalism score overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text presents a mixed stance on liberalism, supporting constitutional checks and balances and economic deregulation, while strongly opposing social liberal values such as LGBTQ+ rights and gender equality.

chunk 2 · The text presents a conservative, Christian-democratic view of liberalism, supporting representative democracy and market elements while advocating for state regulation of morals and financial oversight.

chunk 3 · The text displays a mixed stance on liberalism, supporting rule of law and deregulation for businesses, but opposing liberal social values and calling for tighter financial supervision.
