WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows only mild populist features: it criticises large corporations and the wealthy for tax avoidance at the expense of ordinary citizens, and invokes communal solidarity ("wij met en voor elkaar"), but explicitly rejects scapegoating and polarisation, framing its appeal in Christian-democratic rather than people-vs-elite terms.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mild anti-elitist sentiment directed at dominant health insurers and top executives, and invokes community/people-centred values in care and labour, but does not frame politics as a Manichean struggle between a virtuous people and a corrupt elite; the tone is policy-oriented and Christian-democratic rather than populist.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is a detailed Christian-democratic policy programme with minimal populist framing; the only notable elite-people contrast appears in the EU chapter describing tensions between citizens and political elites, but the text does not systematically invoke a virtuous people against corrupt elites or employ Manichean rhetoric.

chunk 4 · The text is a policy-oriented defence and infrastructure chapter with minimal populist framing; there is no sustained elite-bashing or people-vs-elite narrative, only brief references to past budget cuts and societal obligations toward soldiers.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly critiques elites and large corporations for tax avoidance while emphasizing collective responsibility and unity among all citizens, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and strong people-centrism with some moral framing of societal challenges.

chunk 2 · The text criticizes corporate elites for self-enrichment while harming workers, emphasizes inclusive society ideals, and moral concerns about economic fairness, indicating low-level anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral framing.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly discusses elite-population tensions, people-centrism, and moralized struggles in the EU context, reflecting moderate populist themes.

chunk 4 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralized good-vs-evil struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by contrasting the 'ordinary citizen' with 'big corporations and the wealthy' who avoid taxes, though it explicitly rejects polarization and scapegoating.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing large, institutionalized healthcare organizations and advocating for the 'human scale' and community-based care.

chunk 3 · The text notes a tension between the population and the political elite in Europe, but does not develop a strong, moralized populist narrative.

chunk 4 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist claims, or appeals to the pure people.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist elements are mixed: there are modest left-leaning critiques of corporate tax avoidance and inequality, alongside right-leaning concerns about migrant loyalty and national identity, but the dominant frame is Christian-democratic communitarianism rather than a clear left or right populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The manifesto has a mild left-leaning populist flavour through concern for income inequality, top salary caps, and worker protections, but is primarily a Christian-democratic programme emphasising solidarity, community care, and responsible entrepreneurship rather than a clear left or right populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is primarily a Christian-democratic programme; it contains modest left-leaning elements (fair trade floors, redistribution, development aid) and mild right-leaning elements (EU enlargement scepticism, Turkey accession rejection), but neither dominates; overall populist ideological content is very low.

chunk 4 · The text shows mild right-leaning populist ideology through references to radical Islamist threats and protection of Christian minorities, but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than ideologically populist.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-leaning concerns about economic inequality and tax justice with right-leaning emphasis on national identity and centrist appeals to societal unity and responsibility, indicating a balanced populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto promotes left-leaning policies like fighting poverty and fair wages, right-leaning protectionism in trade (no TTIP with food), and centrist inclusive appeals, reflecting a moderate populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The text shows a balanced populist ideology with left-leaning emphasis on fair trade and social standards, right-leaning emphasis on subsidiarity and sovereignty, and centrist calls for EU reform.

chunk 4 · There is no explicit evidence of left, right, or centrist populist ideology in the manifesto text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements lean slightly left-wing through critiques of tax-avoiding corporations and globalized free trade, combined with right-leaning concerns about national identity and integration.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are centrist and communitarian, focusing on local communities and civil society rather than left-wing or right-wing populist ideologies.

chunk 3 · The populist sentiment is centrist and limited to noting the gap between citizens and European elites without a strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence of a populist ideology as the text focuses entirely on defense, foreign policy, and regional infrastructure.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political freedoms (democratic rule of law, freedom of religion, speech, and education), moderately pro-liberal economically (lower labour taxes, tax reform, anti-tax-avoidance), mixed on social liberalism (expanding anti-discrimination provisions but opposing some social-liberal positions), and cautiously pro-market on finance (favouring normalised interest rates while warning against debt bubbles).

chunk 2 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports rule of law, democracy, and disability rights, lowers labour taxes and reduces regulatory burdens for SMEs (pro-liberal), but also restricts profit distribution in healthcare, caps top salaries, limits financial market freedoms, and opposes extensive trade liberalisation in food, reflecting a Christian-democratic balance between market and social solidarity.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, parliamentary oversight, and minority/human rights (pro-liberal), while taking a mixed stance on economic liberalism—explicitly cautious about liberalising food trade and favouring regulated markets—and only tangentially addressing financial markets in the context of EU exit criteria.

chunk 4 · The text supports rule-of-law building, protection of minorities and women's rights, and removal of economic barriers for cross-border workers and entrepreneurs, reflecting moderate pro-liberal stances across political, social, and economic dimensions; financial markets are not discussed.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights, social inclusion, and economic fairness through tax reform, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions, though it lacks explicit discussion on financial market regulation.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic values, minority rights, market-friendly economic policies, and prudent financial regulation, showing a clear pro-liberal stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports political rights, social protections including religious freedom, inclusive economic policies, and financial market regulation enabling transparency and fairness, reflecting a pro-liberal stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports democracy, rule of law, minority protections, and economic investments linked to free enterprise and regional economic development, indicating a moderately pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports the democratic rule of law, constitutional checks, and social protections for minorities, while advocating for moderate economic reforms and a return to normal interest rates.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights for disabled individuals, and entrepreneurship, but favors significant state intervention, regulation of financial markets, and limits on market forces in healthcare.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports democratic values, human rights, and religious freedom, while expressing skepticism toward the liberalization of agricultural trade.

chunk 4 · The text supports classical liberal values by advocating for the rule of law internationally, protecting religious minorities, and reducing regulatory barriers for cross-border entrepreneurs.
