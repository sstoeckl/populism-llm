WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames a corrupt, self-serving elite ("kleine elite") whose power must be broken, invokes broad popular participation and decentralisation as the democratic corrective, and draws a moral contrast between the powerful and the powerless, though the Manichean framing is moderate rather than conspiratorial.

chunk 2 · The text shows only mild populist features: it criticises political elites and calls for political renewal, but frames this in procedural and democratic terms rather than as a sharp people-vs-corrupt-elite struggle.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes a small elite's power, emphasizes empowering 'the people' and their struggles, and frames politics as a moral battle between conflicting interests, demonstrating strong populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto criticizes political elites as detached 'vakidioten' and emphasizes politicians as ordinary people representing the masses, showing mild anti-elitism and people-centrism but lacks strong moral polarization.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing a 'small elite' as holding disproportionate power and calling for their power to be broken, while advocating for the 'people' to have direct decision-making power.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits very mild populism, focusing on citizen-oriented politics and criticizing parliamentary majorities that ignore deep-seated convictions, but lacks strong Manichean framing.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The PPR's populist appeals are firmly left-wing: anti-corporate elite, redistribution of income and work, workers' self-management, basic income, and solidarity with the Global South, with no nativist or right-wing identity content.

chunk 2 · The PPR's populist flavour is predominantly left-leaning, evidenced by calls to shift tax burdens from labour to capital, raise corporate taxes, and redistribute paid and unpaid work, with no right-wing nativist or anti-immigration content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates for left-leaning populist ideology with emphasis on economic redistribution, social equality, and anti-racist policies, while also showing centrist populist appeals through broad critique of societal structures; right-wing populism is minimally present and mainly in opposition to racism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto advocates left-leaning economic policies like shifting tax burdens from labor to capital and progressive tariffs, while also promoting centrist cooperation and programmatic politics without rigid stances, indicating a left-centrist populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on the 'socialization' of the economy, redistribution of wealth, and opposition to corporate and financial elites.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are embedded in a left-wing ideological framework that emphasizes redistribution, labor rights, and shifting tax burdens from labor to capital.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The PPR scores high on political and social liberalism (strong democratic institutions, anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ equality) but is markedly anti-liberal on economic and financial-market dimensions, advocating planned economy, workers' co-determination over banks, and state direction of investment.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on social dimensions (gender equality, LGBTQ+ rights, anti-discrimination) and political dimensions (parliamentary democracy, civil disobedience rights), but moderately anti-liberal economically through higher corporate taxes, state monopolies (PTT), and extensive public regulation of markets.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal democratic principles including parliamentary democracy, political rights, gender equality, social protections, economic freedom with regulation, and some mixed views on financial market power, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic openness, political freedoms, equal social rights, and economic policies favoring market mechanisms and tax fairness, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market regulation, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic reforms and anti-discrimination protections, but rejects economic and financial liberalism in favor of state planning and market regulation.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism, advocating for individual rights, gender equality, and privacy, while taking a highly interventionist, non-liberal stance on economic planning.
