WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames both centre-right (Berlusconi's "polo delle impunità") and centre-left ("polo della palude e dell'opportunismo") as corrupt elites betraying citizens, while pledging to "mettere il cittadino al primo posto" and "dare voce a chi non ne ha," exhibiting moderate populist anti-elitism and people-centrism without a fully Manichean worldview.

chunk 2 · The text contains mild populist undertones—invoking citizens against powerful interests (banks, mafias, corrupt elites) and calling for equality before the law—but the dominant register is technocratic and policy-oriented, with detailed legislative proposals rather than a moralized people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The text invokes anti-elite rhetoric (privileges of dominant elites, the rich and powerful benefiting from the justice system, media oligopolies) and frames citizens/consumers as victims of concentrated power, but stops short of a fully Manichean populist narrative, blending these appeals with detailed policy proposals.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes political elites as corrupt and impunity-ridden, emphasizes putting citizens first, and frames politics as a moral struggle for values like legality and justice, indicating strong populist elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the state bureaucracy and elites as obstacles and calls for freeing the state from unnecessary burdens, while emphasizing the need to empower 'the people' especially in the South and fight corruption and illegal work, reflecting moderate populist themes.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the current justice system favoring elites and calls for equality before the law, reflecting anti-elitism and people-centrism, while highlighting democratic deficits and power struggles indicative of a manichean worldview.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames both major political coalitions as corrupt, self-serving elites who have abandoned the moral question, contrasting them with the virtuous citizens whose sovereignty must be restored.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the political and business class as corrupt actors who feed on illegal building amnesties and exploit citizens.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits strong populism by framing political and economic elites as corrupt oligarchies that collude against the interests of ordinary citizens.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly draws on Catholic, socialist, and liberal traditions simultaneously, positioning itself as a cross-ideological morality movement that attacks both left and right establishments equally, making centrist populism the dominant ideological flavour with only mild left-leaning social solidarity elements.

chunk 2 · The text blends mild right-leaning elements (anti-illegal immigration, law-and-order) with mild left-leaning consumer/saver protection concerns, but neither dominates; the overall ideological flavor is centrist-technocratic with generic appeals to citizen equality and legality rather than a strong populist ideological program.

chunk 3 · The populist elements lean centrist-reformist (anti-partitocrazia, anti-corruption, pro-transparency) with mild left-leaning undertones (criticism of multinationals, worker safety, consumer protection), but no strong right-wing or nativist content is present.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-leaning themes of social justice and equality with right-leaning cultural protectionism and centrist appeals to citizen primacy, reflecting a mixed but overall populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The text combines left-leaning calls for fiscal reform and redistribution with right-leaning regional autonomy demands and centrist appeals for broad social pacts, indicating a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 3 · The text advocates for labor protections and legal reforms addressing social inequalities (left populism), while also expressing concerns about concentrated economic power and regional identity issues (right populism), with a centrist emphasis on equal legal treatment for all citizens.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist appeal is strictly centrist, explicitly rejecting both the left and right coalitions as indistinguishable in their moral corruption and power-seeking behavior.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is mixed, combining right-wing anti-immigration stances like criminalizing illegal immigration with left-leaning concerns about worker exploitation in the underground economy.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology leans left-wing, focusing on dismantling corporate media duopolies and resisting the power of large financial and industrial conglomerates.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently advocates for free markets, privatisation, a regulatory state that enables competition, investor/saver protection, democratic checks and balances, and minority rights, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The text consistently advocates market liberalization, deregulation, private financing of infrastructure, flexible labour markets, anti-corruption rule of law, judicial efficiency, and financial market transparency, placing it firmly in a pro-liberal (classical/ordo-liberal) orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 3 · The text is broadly pro-liberal: it defends democratic institutions, pluralism, rule of law, press freedom, anti-trust regulation to enable market competition, deregulation of professions, and consumer rights, while also calling for transparency in financial/public entities.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights, market-based economic reforms including privatization, and prudent financial market regulation, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto advocates for political equality, rule of law, market liberalization, protection of property rights, and financial market transparency, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports democratic principles, rule of law, consumer rights, market competitiveness, and regulatory reforms that enable market function, demonstrating a broadly pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses classical liberal principles, including the separation of powers, market-based competition, privatization, and a regulatory state that ensures fair play in financial and economic markets.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, advocating for market liberalization, private sector involvement in infrastructure, property rights protection, and transparent financial market regulations.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing the rule of law, equal treatment under the constitution, and robust antitrust enforcement to ensure free market competition.
