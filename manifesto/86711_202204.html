📄 Full text: the scored text is the Manifesto Corpus version 2026-1, key 86711_202204, available through the Manifesto Project API (free registration), e.g. in R: manifestoR::mp_corpus(data.frame(party = 86711, date = 202204)). The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto exhibits maximal populism across all three sub-dimensions: it frames both the Fidesz and the "balliberal-globalist" establishment as corrupt betrayers of the Hungarian people, invokes the unified Hungarian nation as the virtuous sovereign, and constructs a stark Manichean struggle between the pure Hungarian people and a conspiratorial elite of oligarchs, multinationals, and foreign bankers.

chunk 2 · The manifesto exhibits maximal populism across all three sub-dimensions: it frames a conspiratorial globalist financial elite as the corrupt enemy of the Hungarian people, invokes the unified nation as the virtuous subject of politics, and constructs a stark Manichean struggle between the people and a shadowy "Great Reset" orchestrated by billionaires and multinational corporations.

chunk 3 · The manifesto exhibits very high populism across all three sub-dimensions: it frames globalist elites, multinational corporations, and banker dynasties as corrupt oppressors of the Hungarian people, invokes "the working Hungarian people" as a unified virtuous group, and constructs a stark Manichean struggle between the nation and internal/external enemies.

chunk 4 · The manifesto exhibits very strong populism across all three sub-dimensions: it frames multinational corporations, oligarchs, and international financial elites as corrupt exploiters of the Hungarian people ("adósság-rabszolgaság"), invokes the virtuous Hungarian people as the central political subject, and constructs a Manichean narrative of betrayal and "hazaárulás" (treason) by successive governments serving globalist interests.

chunk 5 · The text consistently frames the Hungarian nation as a virtuous but exploited people oppressed by corrupt elites, oligarchs, multinational corporations, and foreign powers, while calling for radical reforms to break free from this "servitude," reflecting strong populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and Manichean good-vs-evil framing.

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly criticizes the political elite and globalist multinationals as corrupt and harmful, emphasizing trust in the Hungarian people and framing a moral struggle against global elites, reflecting high populist scores.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly criticizes globalist elites and multinational corporations as controlling and exploiting Hungary, framing a moral struggle against them and emphasizing the unity and independence of the Hungarian people.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes globalist elites and secretive organizations like the Bilderberg group, emphasizes the rights of 'the people' to security and order, and frames political struggle as a fight against internal and external enemies, demonstrating strong populist themes.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the profit-centered economy and globalist elites while emphasizing respect and prioritization of the Hungarian people, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elites such as oligarchs and multinational corporations as manipulative, invokes the nation and patriotic people as central, and frames societal problems in terms of corruption and exploitation, indicating moderate populist rhetoric.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is overwhelmingly right-wing: the manifesto combines ethno-nationalist appeals (Hungarian land, Christian heritage, anti-Roma demographic alarmism, anti-immigration), irredentism (Trianon revision), and anti-globalist economic nationalism, with only marginal redistributive (left) or purely centrist elements.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is overwhelmingly right-wing: the manifesto combines ethno-nationalist anti-immigration rhetoric (zero tolerance for immigration, border fence), cultural conservatism (opposition to LGBTQ+ rights framed as "sexual deviance"), national sovereignty against globalism, and protection of Christian/traditional Hungarian identity, with only marginal anti-corporate left-populist elements.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is overwhelmingly right-wing: the manifesto combines ethno-nationalist identity politics, anti-immigration stances, rejection of LGBTQ+ rights, explicit anti-Roma ethnic framing, Christian-national cultural protectionism, and sovereignty-based Euroscepticism, with only marginal anti-corporate rhetoric that does not constitute a left-populist program.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is predominantly right-wing populist: it strongly opposes LGBTQ+ rights, promotes ethnic-national identity, advocates segregation in schools, calls for border guards against illegal immigration, and champions traditional family values and Christian culture. Some left-populist elements appear (wealth tax on billionaires, pandemic solidarity tax on multinationals), but these are secondary to the dominant right-nationalist framing.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is predominantly right-wing populist, combining strong ethno-nationalist identity politics, anti-immigration stances (shoot-to-kill orders at the border), ethnic scapegoating of Roma, and irredentist references to Trianon, with secondary left-populist elements such as worker protections against multinationals and pension redistribution.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-wing anti-globalist economic nationalism with right-wing national protectionism and centrist appeals to the people, showing a mixed but clear populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The text combines left-wing populist themes like anti-globalist economic elites and cultural left critiques with right-wing populist themes such as national identity protectionism and anti-immigration stances, alongside centrist calls for regulation and sovereignty, reflecting a mixed populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The text combines left-leaning economic critiques of globalist capitalism and low wages with right-wing nationalist and anti-multicultural rhetoric, rejecting liberal multiculturalism and emphasizing national identity and sovereignty, reflecting a right-leaning populist ideology overall.

chunk 4 · The text advocates for social justice and fair taxation (left populism) alongside strong national identity and pride (right populism), indicating a mixed but clear populist ideological stance.

chunk 5 · The text combines left-leaning anti-corruption and social justice themes with right-leaning national security and border control measures, alongside centrist appeals to patriotic unity, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is heavily right-wing, focusing on national sovereignty, anti-globalism, and ethnic protectionism against minority population growth.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is heavily right-wing, focusing on protecting national, Christian, and traditional family values against globalist, 'cultural Marxist', and immigrant influences.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is heavily right-wing, focusing on protecting national identity against immigration and addressing ethnic demographic shifts.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is heavily right-wing, focusing on national identity, anti-immigration, and traditional family structures, combined with some left-wing anti-corporate rhetoric.

chunk 5 · The populist ideology is heavily right-wing, focusing on national defense, anti-immigration measures, and preserving national-christian culture, while also incorporating anti-corporate rhetoric.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly anti-liberal across all dimensions: it advocates heavy state intervention in the economy against multinationals, rejects social liberalism (opposing "culture-Marxist" and LGBTQ+ norms), proposes state-directed media regulation and a national social media platform, and frames financial markets and banks as instruments of foreign exploitation rather than as institutions to be enabled.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly anti-liberal across all dimensions: it advocates state intervention in the economy and media, explicitly rejects LGBTQ+ rights, proposes restricting voting rights, endorses capital controls on foreign ownership, frames foreign banks as criminal actors, and repeatedly attacks "liberal opinion dictatorship" as destructive to Hungarian society.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly anti-liberal across all four dimensions: it advocates withdrawing from the European Court of Human Rights, explicitly rejects LGBTQ+ rights and multiculturalism, frames free-market globalism as colonial exploitation, and proposes state-set interest rate caps and financial controls, leaving virtually no pro-liberal content.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal across all four dimensions: it rejects the "globalist-liberal" economic order, explicitly opposes LGBTQ+ rights and pluralism, advocates state control of financial institutions and the central bank in national interest, and calls for renationalizing financial flows away from international markets, scoring low on all liberalism sub-dimensions.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal: it explicitly rejects liberal opinion leaders, advocates state nationalization of the airport, shoot-to-kill border orders, ethnic-based education, mandatory vaccination rejection, and caps on pensions, while only marginally supporting some market-enabling measures like reducing payroll taxes and transitioning to a funded pension system.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows skepticism towards liberal political institutions and media freedom, criticizes social liberalism, supports economic intervention favoring national businesses over multinationals, and notes state financial support to large firms, indicating low liberalism scores overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto expresses skepticism toward liberal democratic institutions, criticizing media freedom limitations and government influence, opposes social liberalism on cultural issues, and supports state intervention in the economy, indicating a generally low pro-liberal stance.

chunk 3 · While the manifesto supports political rights and freedoms for certain groups, it opposes multiculturalism and immigration, and criticizes globalist economic control and financial elites, reflecting a generally low to moderate endorsement of classical liberalism, especially economically and financially.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows moderate support for political liberalism through calls for parliamentary oversight, but opposes social liberalism regarding LGBTQ+ issues, and expresses skepticism towards economic and financial liberalism due to high taxation and calls for national control over finance.

chunk 5 · The manifesto discusses political institutions and rights, social issues like student rights and discrimination, and economic policies favoring workers over multinationals, but lacks explicit discussion of financial market policies, indicating a low to moderate liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text rejects classical liberal economic principles in favor of state interventionism, protectionism, and anti-globalist restrictions on foreign capital and social pluralism.

chunk 2 · The text rejects liberal social values, advocates for extensive state intervention in the economy and financial sectors, and opposes globalized market structures.

chunk 3 · The text explicitly rejects liberal practices, advocates for ethnic segregation in education, opposes multinational capital, and supports state-imposed interest rate caps.

chunk 4 · The text rejects liberal social values, opposes global financial markets, and advocates for state intervention and segregation in education.

chunk 5 · The text rejects classical liberal principles by advocating for state intervention in markets, restricting multinational corporations, and promoting exclusionary social policies.
