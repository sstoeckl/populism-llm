📄 Full text: the scored text is the Manifesto Corpus version 2026-1, key 88320_202010, available through the Manifesto Project API (free registration), e.g. in R: manifestoR::mp_corpus(data.frame(party = 88320, date = 202010)). The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist features: it invokes the people as a unified community deserving a welcoming "Tautos namai," mildly criticises the ruling coalition for dividing society, but frames its critique in institutional/policy terms rather than a sharp elite-vs-people moral struggle.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains mild populist undertones — framing low-tax policy as "voter deception" and criticising tax loopholes that let top earners pay almost nothing — but the dominant register is technocratic social-democratic policy, not a strong people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows minimal populist features: it briefly criticises private-sector analysts as self-interested and notes a negative democratic tendency among elites, but frames these as institutional problems rather than a moral people-vs-elite struggle; people-centric and Manichean rhetoric is virtually absent.

chunk 4 · The text is a detailed Social Democratic policy manifesto focused on governance reform, rule of law, human rights, and foreign/defence policy; it contains minimal populist framing — private-sector analysts are mildly criticised for serving their own interests, and the Lithuanian Nation is invoked once in a constitutional context, but there is no sustained elite-vs-people narrative or Manichean moral framing.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly discusses anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral struggle in the context of tax fairness and fighting tax evasion, showing a low to moderate populist stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

chunk 3 · The text does not contain any populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The dominant ideological flavour is left-populist: the programme centres on welfare-state construction, solidarity-based taxation, income protection, and reducing inequality, with explicit rejection of ethno-nationalist slogans ("Lietuva – lietuviams"), placing it firmly on the social-democratic left.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist in flavour: it calls for progressive income taxation, redistribution, expanded social protection, reduction of inequality, and stronger trade union rights, with only a marginal restrictive note on labour immigration quotas.

chunk 3 · The faint left-leaning tinge (concern for income inequality, poverty, workers' co-operatives, redistribution of farm subsidies toward small farmers) is the dominant ideological flavour, but it is embedded in a broadly technocratic, green-growth programme rather than a strongly populist left frame.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is clearly Social Democratic in orientation — it advocates financial-transaction taxes to curb speculation, stronger social protections, redistribution via EU budgets, and workers' rights, placing it on the left; right-populist or centrist-populist elements are negligible.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the manifesto text; it focuses on policy proposals without populist framing.

chunk 3 · Populist ideology is moderately present with emphasis on social inequality and community cooperation, reflecting a centrist-left populist flavor without strong right-wing nationalist elements.

chunk 4 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the manifesto text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on reducing income inequality, social exclusion, and building a welfare state.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideology or populist framing in the provided text.

chunk 4 · The mild populist elements lean left, emphasizing that the economy and EU mechanisms should serve the common welfare of the nation rather than private interests.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses constitutional rule of law, individual human rights, minority protections, gender balance, and pluralism, while also supporting market competition and entrepreneurship, yielding high liberal scores across political, social, and economic dimensions; financial-market policy is not addressed.

chunk 2 · The manifesto scores moderately liberal overall: it strongly promotes gender equality and minority rights (lib_social high), supports democratic transparency and rule of law (lib_political moderate-high), takes a mixed stance on economic liberalism (pro-investment incentives but also higher taxes and re-regulation), and is mildly anti-liberal on financial markets (proposing a financial transactions tax).

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports rule of law, deliberative democracy, and constitutional reform (lib_political: high); endorses gender equality and social responsibility standards (lib_social: moderate); mixes pro-market tax incentives for investment with significant state intervention and cooperative promotion (lib_economic: mixed); and proposes a financial-transaction tax while also supporting a development bank and transparent capital markets (lib_financial_market: mixed).

chunk 4 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political (rule of law, constitutional compliance, judicial independence) and social dimensions (LGBTQ+ partnership law, minority rights, equality policy); it is moderately pro-liberal economically (SME tax incentives, open EU single market) but scores lower on financial markets due to explicit support for a financial-transactions tax to curb speculation.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses liberal political values such as democracy and rule of law, social liberalism through human rights and equality, economic liberalism via support for entrepreneurship and fair taxation, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market liberalism, leading to a high overall liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles such as constitutional rights, gender equality laws, and progressive economic policies including tax reform and social protections, indicating a pro-liberal stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including democratic governance, social inclusion, economic incentives for entrepreneurship, and regulated financial markets, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles including constitutional democracy, human rights, social equality, economic support for small and medium businesses, and financial market protections, demonstrating a strong pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political democracy and human rights, but is critical of unregulated market relations, advocating instead for social democratic welfare policies.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social rights, but advocates for high state intervention, tax increases, and financial transaction taxes, which limits its economic liberalism.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports democratic institutions, constitutional rule of law, and market-based green growth, though it advocates for some market interventions like a financial transaction tax.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports political liberalism through constitutionalism and social liberalism through minority and LGBTI rights, while taking a mixed, social-democratic stance on economic and financial markets by advocating for financial transaction taxes and state subsidies.
