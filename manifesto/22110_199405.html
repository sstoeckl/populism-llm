WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies: it calls for reducing the power of "zittende politieke elites" and empowering citizens, but explicitly includes GroenLinks itself in that critique and avoids a sharp good-people vs. evil-elite framing, keeping overall populism low.

chunk 2 · The manifesto (GroenLinks 1994) shows only mild populist features: it criticises market orthodoxy and growing inequality between 'haves' and 'have-nots', and invokes citizen participation, but frames these in technocratic, policy-oriented language rather than a sharp people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The text contains mild populist elements — criticising a "closed political culture," invoking citizens as the central reference point, and lamenting the gap between politicians' promises and actions — but these are embedded in a constructive, reform-oriented democratic discourse rather than a sharp people-vs.-elite antagonism.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate anti-elitism with calls to reduce elite power, emphasizes people-centrism through democratic participation, and contains some moralized conflict references, reflecting a moderate populist stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, thus no evidence of populism is found.

chunk 3 · The manifesto expresses distrust and criticism of political elites and institutions, highlighting voter alienation and calls for more direct citizen influence, indicating low to moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moralized political conflict.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the power of political elites and advocating for direct citizen participation, though it explicitly rejects simplistic anti-political stances.

chunk 2 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil struggles between the people and the establishment.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing the closed political culture and the gap between politicians and citizens, while emphasizing that citizens should be central.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is clearly left-leaning populist in ideology, emphasizing redistribution of global wealth, social equality, ecological justice, and workers' rights, while explicitly rejecting right-wing nationalism and xenophobia.

chunk 2 · The populist flavour that does exist is clearly left-leaning, centred on income redistribution, labour rights, anti-discrimination, and ecological correction of market failures, with no right-populist or nativist content.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist in orientation: it advocates worker ownership of capital, wealth redistribution, solidarity levies, and strong social rights, while explicitly rejecting right-wing extremism and framing the elite critique in terms of corporate and political insiders rather than national/identity concerns.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates left-leaning socio-ecological policies, acknowledges right-wing nationalism and xenophobia as threats, and promotes centrist democratic participation, resulting in a balanced populist ideological profile.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the manifesto text; it focuses on policy proposals without populist framing.

chunk 3 · The text supports left-leaning redistributive policies, right-leaning anti-racism stances, and centrist calls for increased citizen participation, reflecting a mixed but moderate populist ideological stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are strongly left-wing, focusing on shifting tax burdens from labor to capital and promoting a multicultural society while rejecting right-wing nationalism.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of a populist ideological framework in the text, as it focuses on programmatic green-left policy proposals without populist framing.

chunk 3 · The populist elements are strongly embedded in a left-wing ideology focused on wealth redistribution, environmental taxes, and expanding the welfare state.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic deepening, civil and minority rights, and market-based ecological incentives ("marktconforme prikkels"), while also advocating state intervention in markets for ecological goals, producing a mixed but overall moderately pro-liberal profile.

chunk 2 · The manifesto accepts market mechanisms as useful but requiring correction; it strongly supports anti-discrimination and gender equality (high lib_social), endorses democratic legitimacy, but proposes significant market interventions including financial-transaction taxes, capital controls on pension funds, and extensive state regulation, yielding mixed-to-moderate overall liberalism scores.

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores high on political and social liberalism — strongly defending democratic institutions, civil rights, anti-discrimination, and minority protections — but scores low on economic and financial-market liberalism, explicitly opposing housing liberalisation, advocating state-directed pension fund investment, mandatory minimum corporate tax rates, and worker buyout of capital.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism via democracy and participation, social liberalism through family and minority rights, economic liberalism with market incentives, and financial market liberalism via investment funds, indicating a robust liberal orientation.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles including political rights and democracy, social equality and individual rights, economic liberalism with market mechanisms and tax reform, and financial market regulation enabling market function, as evidenced by multiple detailed policy proposals.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights protections, and economic reforms favoring market efficiency and redistribution, but lacks explicit discussion of financial market regulation, indicating a generally pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic reforms and human rights, but advocates for substantial state intervention in economic and financial markets to achieve ecological goals.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political democracy and social equality/anti-discrimination, but advocates for heavy state intervention, regulation, and correction of both free markets and financial markets.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic reforms and anti-discrimination measures, but rejects economic and financial liberalism by advocating for heavy state intervention, wealth redistribution, and state direction of investment capital.
