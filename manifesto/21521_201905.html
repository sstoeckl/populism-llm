The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The text is a detailed CD&V policy manifesto focused on family, education, and leisure policy with virtually no populist framing: there is no anti-elite rhetoric, no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, and references to 'the people' are incidental and policy-oriented rather than mobilizing.

chunk 2 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto from CD&V (a Belgian Christian-democratic party) focused on media, culture, health, and welfare policy. It contains virtually no populist rhetoric: no systematic elite-bashing, no invocation of a unified virtuous 'people', and no Manichean good-vs-evil framing. The only mild critical language targets large international tech/media companies (e.g., 'internationale giganten', 'winner takes it all'), which is a market-regulation concern rather than populist anti-elitism.

chunk 3 · The text shows virtually no populist framing: it is a detailed policy programme by CD&V addressing health, housing, mobility and energy with technocratic, evidence-based language, no elite-bashing, and only mild people-centred rhetoric such as universal access to mobility for "every Fleming."

chunk 4 · The text is a detailed Christian-democratic policy manifesto (CD&V) focused on energy, housing, labour market, and social protection, with virtually no populist framing: elites are not attacked, 'the people' are not invoked as a unified virtuous bloc, and there is no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative; the only mild anti-elite signal is a call to combat oligopolies in regulated markets.

chunk 5 · The text is a detailed Christian-democratic policy programme (CD&V) focused on pensions, self-employment, poverty reduction, and economic competitiveness; it contains virtually no populist framing—no corrupt elite narrative, no unified "people" rhetoric, and no Manichean good-vs-evil struggle.

chunk 6 · The text shows minimal populist features: it briefly frames tax fraud as unfair to honest citizens and notes that not everyone pays taxes equally, but it does not systematically pit a virtuous people against a corrupt elite, nor does it employ strong Manichean rhetoric.

chunk 7 · The text explicitly distances itself from populism ("het populisme het hoofd te bieden"), mentions political scandals only to call for ethical reform, and frames governance in technocratic, cooperative terms — showing minimal populist rhetoric overall.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild centre-left tendencies through targeted support for low-income families and vulnerable groups, but no strong left or right populist ideological framing; it is primarily a mainstream Christian-democratic policy programme.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mild centre-left tendencies through its emphasis on universal solidarity, social protection, and redistribution in healthcare, but these are expressed in mainstream Christian-democratic policy language rather than populist ideological framing. There is no right-wing populist identity/immigration content and no strong left-populist anti-corporate rhetoric.

chunk 3 · The text contains modest centre-left social-policy elements (poverty reduction, anti-discrimination, progressive universalism) alongside some regional/Flemish identity markers, but neither strand is framed in populist ideological terms; the overall ideological flavour is mainstream Christian-democratic centrism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows mild centre-left tendencies through emphasis on social dialogue, redistribution via social security, and combating inequality, but these are embedded in a mainstream Christian-democratic framework rather than a populist ideological one; no right-wing identity or anti-immigration populism is present.

chunk 5 · The minimal populist signals present are cross-cutting: mild social-protection expansionism (left-leaning) is balanced by concerns about social-fraud and national solidarity mechanisms (right-leaning), within an overall centrist Christian-democratic framework with no strong populist ideological flavour.

chunk 6 · The text's limited populist elements lean slightly centrist-left, calling for fair taxation of multinationals and internet giants and a financial transaction tax, but without strong class-based or identity-based populist framing.

chunk 7 · The text reflects a mainstream Christian-democratic (centrist) ideology with modest social-market leanings and some migration-control elements, but no strong left or right populist framing; overall populist ideological content is very low.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly liberal on social dimensions (gender equality, non-discrimination, inclusive education) and political dimensions (freedom of education, institutional autonomy), with moderate economic liberalism (tax incentives, business cooperation), but does not address financial market policy.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly liberal in its political and social dimensions, supporting local democracy, media pluralism, non-discrimination, and individual rights. On economic and financial-market dimensions it is mixed: it supports market functioning and transparency (e.g., scrutiny of non-market-conform remuneration, market pricing oversight) while also advocating strong public regulation, universal healthcare, and state subsidies, reflecting a typical Christian-democratic ordo-liberal balance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it defends anti-discrimination in housing, individual freedom of choice in housing tenure, market-conform green bonds, and local democratic autonomy, while also accepting significant state intervention in social housing and energy, producing a mixed but moderately liberal overall profile.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it supports free-market energy services, anti-discrimination and gender equality measures, market-conform financial instruments (green bonds, transparent pension products), and democratic social dialogue, while accepting regulated markets and a strong welfare state as compatible with liberal order.

chunk 5 · The manifesto consistently supports free markets, competition, entrepreneurship, consumer protection, transparency in financial products, and individual rights (including gender equality and human rights for AI governance), placing it solidly in the pro-liberal range across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it strongly supports anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ rights, democratic checks and balances, market competition, and consumer protection, while showing mixed signals on financial markets by advocating a financial transaction tax alongside consumer-oriented financial regulation.

chunk 7 · The text consistently endorses democratic rule of law, anti-discrimination, free trade, and EU financial market deepening (Banking Union, Capital Markets Union), reflecting a broadly pro-liberal stance across all four sub-dimensions.
