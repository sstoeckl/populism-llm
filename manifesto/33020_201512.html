WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto exhibits very high populism across all three sub-dimensions: it frames financial elites, private banks, and transnational corporations as oppressors of "the great social majorities," invokes the people as a unified virtuous class, and frames the political situation as a moral struggle against a corrupt "crisis-estafa" system.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: it frames the financial system as oligarchic and deceptive ("El sistema financiero oligárquico quiere participar"), invokes citizens as the rightful beneficiaries of public services, and frames labour reforms as attacks on workers' rights, but the dominant register is programmatic policy rather than moralized people-vs-elite rhetoric.

chunk 3 · The text contains occasional anti-elite framing (e.g., "los poderosos" hoarding resources, banks and financial entities as exploitative actors) and people-centric language, but the dominant register is technocratic policy detail rather than populist mobilization; Manichean framing is present but mild and issue-specific.

chunk 4 · The text contains only one brief populist passage referencing "los poderosos" reserving scraps for the people, but the overwhelming bulk of the document is detailed policy proposals on migration, social services, and animal rights with no sustained people-vs-elite framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes dominant elites such as financial capitalism, private banks, and transnational corporations, while emphasizing the unity and sovereignty of the popular classes against these powers, framing a moral struggle against false defenders of the people, thus strongly evidencing populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the capital-labor income disparity, advocates worker participation in economic democracy, and denounces financial oligarchy, indicating moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict.

chunk 3 · The manifesto does not explicitly discuss populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, providing no direct evidence for populism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean themes, thus no evidence of populism overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames politics as a struggle between the virtuous popular classes and corrupt, servile elites serving financial powers.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the financial system as an oligarchy that deceives the public to extract profit.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing a struggle between 'the people' and 'the powerful' who control resources.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing elites as 'powerful' actors reserving crumbs for the people, though the overall tone is highly policy-focused.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is unambiguously left-populist: it explicitly identifies as "claramente de izquierdas," calls for nationalization of energy and banking, redistribution of wealth, workers' rights, and anti-capitalist transformation, with no right-wing nativist or anti-immigration content.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are clearly left-leaning: the manifesto calls for worker co-management ("democracia económica, entre en las empresas"), redistribution through minimum wages and guaranteed income, public ownership of energy and health, and attacks on financial oligarchy, with no nativist or right-populist content.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it advocates public banking, dissolution of speculative financial vehicles (SAREB, SOCIMIs), extensive redistribution, strong social rights, and anti-corporate housing policy, with no nativist or right-wing identity content.

chunk 4 · The text leans mildly left-populist through references to exploitation by developed countries and the powerful hoarding resources, but the dominant content is technocratic social policy rather than ideological populism.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates a left-wing populist ideology by calling for a united front of working and popular classes, opposing neoliberal austerity policies, promoting social equality, peace, and sovereignty, while rejecting right-wing nationalist or exclusionary themes, and including centrist appeals to popular sovereignty and democratic participation.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports left-wing populist themes such as labor rights restoration, anti-corporate elite measures, and worker empowerment, with no evidence of right-wing populism and some centrist populist appeals.

chunk 3 · There is no explicit evidence in the text for left, right, or centrist populist ideology; the manifesto focuses on social rights and equality without populist framing.

chunk 4 · The text shows a mixed populist ideology with left-leaning emphasis on fighting labor exploitation and social inequality, some right-leaning acknowledgment of xenophobia, and centrist calls to eliminate criminalization of immigrants, reflecting a moderate populist ideological stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist discourse is explicitly and intensely left-wing, focusing on anti-capitalism, redistribution, feminism, and ecology.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on the struggle of labor against capital and advocating for public ownership and redistribution.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, explicitly aiming for a socialist, anti-patriarcal society with high redistribution.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing, focusing on anti-exploitation, anti-racism, and the expansion of social and political rights for migrants.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports civil rights, feminism, and some democratic freedoms (lib_social high), but is strongly anti-liberal economically—advocating nationalization, state control of banking, financial transaction taxes, capital controls, and explicitly rejecting market-based economic governance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto scores low on economic and financial-market liberalism (state control of energy, bans on fracking, hostility to private pensions and financial oligarchy), moderately on political liberalism (democratic values in education), and higher on social liberalism (gender equality, anti-discrimination, reproductive rights), yielding a below-midpoint overall liberal score.

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores high on political and social liberalism (strong democratic participation, media freedom, extensive LGBTQ+ and gender equality measures) but is markedly anti-liberal on economic and financial-market dimensions, advocating public banking, dissolution of market investment vehicles, heavy housing market regulation, and capital controls on real estate.

chunk 4 · The text is strongly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions—expanding immigrant voting rights, fighting gender discrimination, and protecting civil rights—while offering only modest economic liberalism through targeted fiscal incentives; financial markets are not discussed.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports liberal principles including strong guarantees of political rights and democratic participation, feminist social equality, economic policies favoring reindustrialization and public sector involvement, and financial market regulation aimed at transparency and control, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto promotes democratic political participation and social liberalism in education and civil rights, opposes fossil fuel extraction and fracking, and criticizes private pension advantages, reflecting a mixed but overall moderate liberal economic and financial stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political rights (e.g., guaranteeing social services as fundamental rights), social equality (e.g., gender equality policies), and economic liberalism (e.g., municipal financing to reduce land speculation), though it lacks explicit discussion of financial market liberalism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports political rights protections, social equality measures against discrimination, and economic incentives for vulnerable groups, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall, though it lacks discussion on financial market policies.

Gemini

chunk 1 · While supporting political and social rights, the text strongly rejects classical economic and financial liberalism in favor of state ownership and market regulation.

chunk 2 · While supporting democratic values and social equality, the text strongly rejects economic liberalism by advocating for nationalizations, price controls, and the prohibition of private employment agencies.

chunk 3 · While supporting political and social rights, the text strongly opposes economic and financial liberalism through heavy market regulations and public banking.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through the expansion of voting rights and anti-discrimination measures, while advocating for state-led economic interventions.
