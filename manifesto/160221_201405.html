WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The FAD manifesto exhibits strong populism across all three sub-dimensions: it repeatedly frames a corrupt elite ("partidocracia," "grupos de poder económico") as exploiting "el pueblo panameño," invokes the people as a unified virtuous group owed a "deuda social," and constructs a Manichean narrative in which elite wealth is built directly on mass poverty.

chunk 2 · The text repeatedly frames a corrupt, self-serving "partidocracia" allied with transnational capital as the enemy of the Panamanian people, invoking a clear good-vs-evil struggle between ordinary Panamanians and economic/political elites who govern solely for their own enrichment.

chunk 3 · The manifesto of the Frente Amplio por la Democracia (FAD) exhibits strong populism across all three sub-dimensions: it frames a corrupt "oligarquía política y económica" as the enemy, repeatedly invokes "el pueblo" as the sole legitimate sovereign, and portrays Panamanian politics as a Manichean struggle of elite manipulation and betrayal against popular interests.

chunk 4 · The text frames the justice system as captured by a dominant class and political elites who manipulate appointments for their own benefit, while the poor and low-income sectors suffer selective injustice, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism and people-centrism without a fully Manichean narrative.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the corrupt elite and oligarchic control, emphasizes an inclusive 'Patria para Todos' and highlights the poverty caused by elite enrichment, showing strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized struggle.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the corrupt 'partidocracia' elite and contrasts their interests with those of the majority of the Panamanian people, indicating anti-elitism and people-centrism, while framing a moral struggle between the elite and the people.

chunk 3 · The text explicitly criticizes the unequal control of the state by political and economic oligarchies, emphasizes the sovereign will of the people, and describes repression by state forces, indicating strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict typical of populism.

chunk 4 · The text explicitly critiques elite control over judicial appointments and political interference, showing moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moral framing of justice issues.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames the political system as a corrupt 'partidocracia' serving economic elites at the expense of the virtuous 'pueblo panameño'.

chunk 2 · The text frames a clear struggle between the corrupt 'partidocracia' and the virtuous 'pueblo panameño'.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits strong populism by framing politics as a struggle between a corrupt oligarchy and the virtuous, sovereign people.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing state laws for historically serving the interests of the dominant class rather than the general population.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is overwhelmingly left-wing: the manifesto explicitly labels itself "anti-neoliberal," calls for strong state regulation, progressive taxation, redistribution, nationalization of productive priorities, price controls, and solidarity-based economics, with only a minor food-sovereignty/nationalist strand that does not constitute right-wing populism.

chunk 2 · The FAD manifesto is strongly left-populist: it explicitly opposes neoliberalism, calls for renationalization of privatized enterprises, redistribution of wealth, universal public services, and frames transnational corporations and domestic economic elites as exploiters of the Panamanian people.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is overwhelmingly left-wing: the manifesto explicitly attacks neoliberalism, free-trade agreements, transnational corporations, privatization, and wealth concentration, while calling for state planning, redistribution, workers' rights, and a Constituent Assembly to restructure power in favor of popular sectors.

chunk 4 · The populist framing leans left, emphasizing class-based critique ("intereses de la clase dominante") and protection of low-income sectors, with no right-wing nativist or anti-immigration content, though the critique of political elites also has a centrist anti-corruption dimension.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text strongly opposes neoliberalism and promotes anti-neoliberal economic policies, while also rejecting some right-wing resource concessions, and presents a comprehensive, integrative approach, indicating a left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist elements.

chunk 2 · The text criticizes neoliberal economic models as consumptive and exclusionary, supports indigenous struggles, and denounces the corrupt political elite, reflecting a left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist and right-leaning elements.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly opposes neoliberal economic policies and advocates for a plurinational state recognizing diversity, reflecting a left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist appeals to popular sovereignty and unity.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows a left-leaning populist stance by calling to repeal laws violating human rights, a right-leaning concern about political appointments, and a centrist populist critique of elite influence without clear programmatic solutions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist discourse is explicitly left-wing, focusing on anti-neoliberalism, wealth redistribution, and state regulation of the economy.

chunk 2 · The populist discourse is strongly left-wing, explicitly attacking neoliberalism, privatization, and corporate interests.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing, focusing on anti-neoliberalism, anti-corporate elites, and redistribution.

chunk 4 · The populist framing leans left, as it critiques the legal system for protecting the interests of the dominant class at the expense of lower-income sectors.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores low on economic and financial-market liberalism due to its explicit anti-neoliberal, state-interventionist stance (price freezes, heavy taxation of banks and capital, trade protectionism), while scoring moderately on political and social liberalism through its emphasis on human rights, anti-corruption, transparency, and non-discrimination.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly rejects neoliberalism, free markets, and privatization, calling for renationalization of energy companies and state-led economic management; it supports democratic participation and cultural/ethnic pluralism but frames market liberalization as the root cause of social and environmental problems.

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores mixed-to-low on liberalism overall: it strongly supports civil rights and anti-discrimination (lib_social moderate-high), but explicitly rejects neoliberal economic models, advocates state planning over markets (lib_economic low), criticizes international financial institutions (lib_financial_market low), and frames the existing constitution as antidemocratic while calling for a Constituent Assembly (lib_political low).

chunk 4 · The text is strongly pro-rule-of-law and judicial independence (lib_political), and calls for repealing laws that violate human rights (lib_social), but contains no discussion of economic markets, trade, or financial-market policy.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports political rights and human rights strongly, endorses state regulation of the economy (anti-neoliberal), and criticizes foreign financial influence, showing mixed but present liberal political and social values, limited economic liberalism, and skepticism toward financial market liberalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto advocates for state-led health and social security as human rights, emphasizes universal access and regulation, criticizes privatization effects, and calls for economic models prioritizing life over neoliberalism, indicating moderate liberalism with social emphasis.

chunk 3 · The text reveals skepticism about political institutions, describing the independence of powers as a myth and highlighting widespread social discrimination and economic inequality, indicating a low score for liberalism dimensions, with no evidence on financial market policies.

chunk 4 · The manifesto emphasizes the importance of judicial independence, rule of law, and human rights protections, indicating moderate support for political and social liberalism but lacks economic or financial market policy discussion.

Gemini

chunk 1 · While the text strongly supports human rights and democratic participation, it rejects free-market economics, advocating instead for price controls, state intervention, and financial market restrictions.

chunk 2 · While supporting democratic and minority rights, the text strongly rejects economic liberalism, advocating for nationalization and state intervention.

chunk 3 · The text supports political liberalism (checks and balances, independence of powers) and social rights, but strongly rejects economic liberalism in favor of state regulation.

chunk 4 · The text strongly advocates for classical liberal political principles, emphasizing the rule of law, judicial independence, and the protection of human rights.
