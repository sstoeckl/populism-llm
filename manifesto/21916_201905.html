WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto invokes the Flemish people as a unified virtuous community and frames a binary choice between N-VA's responsible governance and a French-dominated tax government, but stops well short of a full elite-corruption narrative, yielding moderate populism scores.

chunk 2 · The text shows minimal populist features: it occasionally invokes "the Flemish people" as a virtuous group and criticises past governments (Di Rupo), but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-oriented, with no sustained elite-vs-people narrative or Manichean framing.

chunk 3 · The text shows minimal populist features: mild criticism of EU misgovernance ("te veel slecht bestuur in Europa") and occasional references to citizens as the locus of legitimacy, but no sustained anti-elite rhetoric, no Manichean good-vs-evil framing, and no unified "people" invoked against a corrupt establishment.

chunk 4 · The text shows mild populist tendencies through criticism of EU mismanagement and calls for citizen-grounded governance, but the overall tone is technocratic and institutional rather than strongly populist, with no clear elite-vs-people moral dichotomy.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly frames elites as opponents and emphasizes a strong, virtuous Flemish people and a moral struggle to protect Flemish identity, demonstrating clear populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to anti-elitism, people-centrism, or manichean populist themes, indicating no evidence of populism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles.

chunk 4 · The manifesto criticizes elite governance as 'te veel slecht bestuur in Europa' and emphasizes a shared ownership of Europe, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moral framing of crises.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism, framing political choices as a struggle between the Flemish people and a tax-heavy, French-dominated establishment.

chunk 2 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the 'bad governance' of past decades and emphasizing that international cooperation must serve the citizens rather than globalist elites.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits mild populism through criticism of European governance and a call for policies rooted in the citizens, but lacks a strong moralizing or Manichean tone.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The dominant populist flavour is right-wing: strong emphasis on Flemish national identity, cultural sovereignty, strict immigration control, and resistance to supranational technocratic structures, with virtually no anti-corporate or redistributive left-populist content.

chunk 2 · The text leans mildly right-populist through strong Flemish identity/autonomy rhetoric and economic nationalism (Vlaams kopen, verankering), but is primarily a mainstream centre-right programme emphasising free markets, entrepreneurship, and fiscal responsibility rather than a clear populist ideological profile.

chunk 3 · The dominant ideological flavour is centre-right: strong emphasis on Flemish identity, language integration policy in the Rand, national sovereignty, free trade, and controlled migration, with only marginal redistributive elements (energy poverty support), placing the text clearly on the right side of the populist spectrum.

chunk 4 · The text leans toward right-leaning populist ideology through its emphasis on strict migration control, national/regional identity, and opposition to supranational social transfers, while rejecting left-wing redistribution at the European level.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines right-wing nationalist emphasis on Flemish identity and sovereignty with some left-leaning social activation policies and centrist cooperation rhetoric, reflecting a mixed populist ideology with a right-leaning dominance.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit evidence of left, right, or centrist populist ideology in the manifesto text.

chunk 3 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances on the left, right, or centrist populism in the text.

chunk 4 · The text advocates a confederal Europe with subsidiarity and responsibility, opposing a European superstate and emphasizing member states' autonomy, showing a centrist to right-leaning populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are strongly aligned with right-wing regionalism, focusing on protecting Flemish identity, culture, and borders against external influences.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on mainstream programmatic policy proposals.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, focusing on preserving Flemish identity, language, and integration, while opposing globalism and uncontrolled migration.

chunk 4 · The populist elements are combined with a right-wing focus on controlling migration and protecting national/regional sovereignty.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal economically (free market, deregulation, lower taxes, entrepreneurship) and affirms rule-of-law and separation of powers, but restricts social liberalism through tight immigration/integration conditions and frames civil rights in a citizenship-based rather than universal-rights framework, producing mixed scores on political and social dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions free markets and competition, advocates privatisation of state holdings and lower taxes, upholds equality of rights regardless of background/sexuality/disability, and supports democratic reform including abolishing the Senate and moving toward a republic.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it champions free trade agreements, market mechanisms for renewable energy, subsidiarity and democratic rule of law in the EU, property/contract protection in development policy, and minority protections abroad, while also supporting some state intervention in energy and housing that moderates the overall score.

chunk 4 · The text is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions the internal market, capital markets union, democratic legitimacy, human rights conditionality, and fiscal responsibility, while opposing state-led financial solutions like euro-bonds and uniform European social policy.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through rule of law and justice, social liberalism via rights and freedoms, economic liberalism by promoting entrepreneurship and reducing regulation, and some financial market liberalism through innovation and market-based defense industry support.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports rule of law, equal rights, free market principles, and prudent financial market policies, as evidenced by explicit references to reliable government, equal opportunities, free market guarantees, and financial market participation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports democratic governance, fundamental rights, free and fair trade, and prudent financial oversight, indicating a pro-liberal stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, bilingual rights, free trade, and capital market integration, indicating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal economic principles and free markets, while maintaining a conservative, state-centric view of political and social rights.

chunk 2 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including free-market competition, individual rights, and reducing state debt and financial market risks.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal economic principles, including free trade, market mechanisms, and technological neutrality, alongside democratic sovereignty and minority protections.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including free enterprise, the completion of the internal market, and market-based financial integration through the Capital Markets Union.
