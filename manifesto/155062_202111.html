WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames a corrupt, ideologically-driven elite (academic circles, media, bureaucracy, traditional parties) as having betrayed the Chilean people, while invoking "los chilenos" as a unified virtuous group and portraying the left as an existential enemy bent on destroying freedom and the family — a moderately populist but primarily ideological-right framing.

chunk 2 · The manifesto frames the political establishment and international bureaucracies as corrupt and self-serving, invokes Cuban/Venezuelan regimes as existential threats in a good-vs-evil framing, and appeals to ordinary Chileans as victims of elite capture, yielding moderate populist scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows minimal populist features: it briefly frames the left's education proposal as self-serving state expansion and criticises large corporations for distorting free markets, but it does not construct a sustained people-vs-elite narrative, moralistic Manichean framing, or a unified virtuous "people" as its central rhetorical device.

chunk 4 · The text is a highly technical energy and environment policy manifesto with minimal populist content; the only notable populist moment is a brief criticism of authorities prioritizing minority pressure groups over the general population, but this is isolated and not a sustained anti-elite or people-centric narrative.

chunk 5 · The text shows only weak populist features: a brief critique of a cultural "elite" dependent on the state apparatus and vague references to Chilean people's culture, but lacks sustained anti-elite rhetoric, people-as-sovereign framing, or Manichean good-vs-evil narratives typical of populism.

chunk 6 · The text frames the political elite as complacent and out of touch on immigration ("Desde la elite política y social, se repiten frases genéricas") and portrays ongoing urban violence as a moral catastrophe consuming Chile's soul, with a clear good-vs-evil framing between law-abiding citizens and violent agitators.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes an irresponsible elite and emphasizes a unified Chilean people, framing a moral struggle against leftist ideologies, demonstrating clear populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes unelected officials interfering in Chilean affairs, emphasizes national unity, and frames international bodies and leftist groups as threats, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and a moral struggle.

chunk 3 · The manifesto expresses anti-elitist criticism of leftist education policy, centers families as the virtuous people, and frames ideological conflicts in moral terms, indicating low to moderate populism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, thus no evidence of populism is found.

chunk 5 · No evidence of populist rhetoric or themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moralization was found in the text.

chunk 6 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes political and social elites, emphasizes the need for government action on immigration and public order, and frames social conflict in moral terms, indicating moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and a strong manichean worldview.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits strong populism by framing the political class as a privileged caste of bureaucrats abusing power, while calling on the virtuous, patriotic citizens to rebel against this establishment.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing international bureaucracies and domestic left-wing actors as corrupting forces undermining national sovereignty and the will of the Chilean people.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits mild populism by warning against politicians using citizens' savings for their own ends and advocating for direct citizen ownership of state resources to bypass state bureaucracy.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits very low populism, only criticizing political interference in technical state agencies without moralizing or invoking a virtuous 'people'.

chunk 5 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing a state-dependent 'cultural elite' and emphasizing a culture rooted in the common people.

chunk 6 · The text exhibits strong populism by framing the political and social elite as disconnected from the realities of ordinary citizens, particularly regarding immigration and public order.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly right-populist: it defends national sovereignty, secure borders, traditional family values, Christian heritage, and free-market capitalism against a left-wing elite, with no redistributive or anti-corporate framing typical of left populism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly right-wing populist: it combines strong national sovereignty rhetoric, anti-immigration and anti-left framing, hostility toward Cuba/Venezuela, defense of free markets, and opposition to international institutions, with no left-redistributive populist content.

chunk 3 · The manifesto leans right-of-centre in its populist flavour, opposing same-sex marriage and adoption on traditional-family grounds, while its primary programmatic thrust is pro-market and pro-private enterprise rather than a strong left or right populist identity.

chunk 4 · The manifesto leans mildly right-of-centre through its emphasis on property rights, private sector participation, and market competition, but populist ideological content is minimal and does not clearly dominate any single ideological direction.

chunk 5 · The text leans mildly right-populist through its emphasis on national identity, rejection of gender language, defense of traditional family values, and opposition to abortion, but these are embedded in a broadly technocratic, policy-oriented program rather than a strong populist ideological frame.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is predominantly right-populist, combining strong anti-illegal-immigration rhetoric, law-and-order demands, and national-cultural conservatism, with pro-private-sector and pro-market measures (mecenazgo law, TVN privatization) and no redistributive left-populist content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text shows a right-leaning populist ideology defending private enterprise and property, criticizing leftist socialism, while also acknowledging centrist populist appeals through anti-establishment rhetoric and leftist critiques.

chunk 2 · The text shows a right-leaning populist ideology emphasizing national sovereignty and opposition to leftist groups, while also containing centrist appeals to constitutional supremacy and some left critiques of extreme left groups.

chunk 3 · The text shows left-leaning populist critique of state expansion, right-leaning defense of traditional family values, and centrist appeals to family centrality, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 4 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the text; the content focuses on technical and policy aspects without populist framing.

chunk 5 · The text lacks explicit populist ideological content on the left, right, or centrist dimensions; no populist ideological stance is evident.

chunk 6 · The text combines right-wing populist themes of immigration control and national security with some left-leaning concerns about equality and social programs, alongside centrist calls for government responsibility, reflecting a mixed but overall moderate populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, focusing on national sovereignty, anti-immigration, and opposition to left-wing transnational agendas.

chunk 3 · The populist elements lean right, focusing on protecting traditional family values against state-imposed 'ideologies' and opposing same-sex marriage.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is weak and centrist, focusing on technocratic efficiency and reducing political interference in state administration.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal economically (free markets, tax cuts, deregulation, private enterprise) and moderately pro-liberal politically (rule of law, institutional checks), but explicitly anti-liberal socially, rejecting gender ideology, LGBTQ+ framing, and minority-rights discourse as neo-Marxist deconstruction.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal economically (deep tax cuts, deregulation, privatization, free markets) and pro-competitive in financial markets, but scores low on social liberalism due to explicit opposition to same-sex marriage and adoption, and shows mixed political liberalism by championing democracy abroad while withdrawing from international human rights bodies.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal economically (free enterprise, free trade, competition, deregulation, private pension administration, market-based health insurance) and politically (rule of law, property rights), but scores low on social liberalism due to explicit opposition to same-sex marriage and homosexual adoption.

chunk 4 · The manifesto consistently favours free competition, private sector participation, and market-based pricing in energy (explicitly "sin subsidios ni beneficios"), while also calling for depoliticised regulatory institutions and transparent market governance, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal economic and political orientation.

chunk 5 · The text is broadly pro-liberal economically (competitive markets, private sector involvement, credit lines, FOGAPE reform, export promotion) and politically (democratic stability), but is mixed on social liberalism due to its explicit proposal to repeal abortion rights and its conservative stance on family and gender, while supporting inclusion of disabled persons and women in the workforce.

chunk 6 · The text shows mixed liberalism: it supports private initiative, tax incentives, and TVN privatization (pro-liberal economically), but simultaneously advocates strict immigration controls, expanded police/military powers, protest exclusion zones, and sanctions on NGOs, which constrain individual rights and civil liberties.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through respect for rule of law and democracy, economic liberalism via free markets and property rights, and financial liberalism by promoting free trade and modernizing economic treaties, while showing limited support for social liberalism due to criticism of leftist identity politics.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports constitutional rule, minority cultural promotion, economic liberalization via tax cuts and deregulation, and financial market reforms enabling market function, indicating moderate classical liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism (constitutional rights), economic liberalism (free market, private property), and financial market transparency, while showing limited support for social liberalism due to opposition to LGBTQ+ rights.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly promotes political liberalism through transparent participation and regulatory independence, social liberalism via environmental protection and rights, and economic liberalism by advocating free competition and market-based energy policies, though it lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 5 · The manifesto emphasizes protection of political rights, cultural heritage, social rights of children, and some economic social support programs, indicating a moderately pro-liberal stance overall, though financial market topics are absent.

chunk 6 · The manifesto supports democratic rule of law and public order, expresses concern for equality and cultural rights, and promotes economic incentives for private cultural initiatives, but lacks discussion on financial market policies, indicating moderate liberalism overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports classical liberal economic principles like tax reduction and deregulation, but scores low on social liberalism due to its rejection of gender and minority identity politics.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports free-market competition, deregulation, and financial market openness, while taking a socially conservative stance against LGBTQ+ rights.

chunk 3 · The text strongly champions classical liberal economic principles, free markets, and private property, while taking a conservative stance on social issues like marriage equality.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing free competition, private sector participation, and market-based financial instruments.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports market mechanisms, privatization, and financial credit tools, but scores low on social liberalism due to its opposition to abortion rights.

chunk 6 · The text shows mixed support for liberal principles, advocating for private enterprise and equality before the law, while simultaneously proposing highly restrictive measures on immigration and protests that challenge civil liberties.
