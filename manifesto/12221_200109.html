WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly populist: it frames society as a Manichean struggle between "folkemakt" (people's power) and "pengemakt" (money power), portrays market-liberal elites and multinational corporations as corrupt usurpers of democratic power, and consistently invokes "the people" as a unified virtuous collective whose sovereignty must be restored.

chunk 2 · The text shows moderate populist features: financial speculators and capital owners are framed as exploiting workers and society, but the rhetoric is primarily programmatic (left-wing policy) rather than a strong people-vs-elite moral narrative.

chunk 3 · The manifesto frames global capitalism and political elites (US, multinationals, IMF/WTO) as corrupt forces exploiting ordinary people, invoking 'folk flest' as the virtuous majority, with a clear moral binary between powerful capitalist interests and the people.

chunk 4 · The text shows mild populist tendencies, primarily framing powerful capital interests as opposed to ordinary people's interests, but the dominant discourse is policy-oriented rather than populist.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly frames elites as powerful and corrupt, emphasizes 'folkemakt' (people power) against 'pengemakt' (money power), and portrays a moral struggle for a just society, demonstrating strong populist elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, thus no evidence of populism is found.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly critiques the concentration of political, economic, and military power among elites and multinational corporations, contrasts this with the desires of the majority for a more just and democratic world, and frames the struggle as one between powerful interests and the people, indicating moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict.

chunk 4 · The text does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, thus no evidence of populism is found.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames politics as a struggle between 'folkemakt' (people power) and 'pengemakt' (money power), depicting unelected economic elites as corrupting democratic institutions.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing financial speculators who profit at the expense of workers and society, while advocating for local, democratic control over resources.

chunk 3 · The text frames a struggle between the 'people' and 'powerful capitalist interests' or 'political elites' who control global systems.

chunk 4 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil struggles between the people and the establishment.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populism is unambiguously left-wing: the manifesto explicitly identifies SV as a socialist party, calls for replacing market liberalism with a socialist society under democratic control, advocates redistribution, strong public ownership, and anti-corporate policies, with no significant right-wing or nativist populist elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it calls for weakening financial capital's role, expanding state ownership in banks and industry, redistribution, workers' rights, and anti-speculation measures, with minimal right-wing or purely centrist populist content.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing: anti-corporate, pro-redistribution, pro-labour unions, anti-IMF/WTO, advocating social equality and welfare state expansion, with no meaningful right-wing nativist or identity-based populism.

chunk 4 · The text leans left-populist, emphasizing welfare state protection, redistribution, gender equality, and anti-corporate sentiment, with minimal right-populist or nativist framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text strongly advocates for a socialist society without class divisions and social injustice, criticizes multinational corporations, and calls for alliances with popular movements, indicating a left-wing populist ideology with some centrist appeals and minor right-wing elements.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the text; the manifesto focuses on policy proposals without populist framing.

chunk 3 · The text emphasizes left-wing populist themes such as fighting poverty, oppression, and environmental destruction, while opposing militarization and EU defense cooperation, reflecting a left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist elements in foreign policy consultation.

chunk 4 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the text; the content focuses on social equality and cultural policies without populist framing.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist appeal is explicitly socialist and left-wing, focusing on eliminating class divisions, redistributing wealth, and fighting corporate capitalism.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are strongly left-wing, focusing on anti-corporate sentiment, critique of speculation capital, and advocating for state ownership and workers' rights.

chunk 3 · The populist framing is explicitly left-wing, focusing on building counter-power against global capitalism and multinational corporations.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence of populist ideology in the text, as it focuses on programmatic social, cultural, and legal reforms without populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it strongly supports political rights, media freedom, LGBTQ+ rights, and minority protections (pro-liberal), but is explicitly anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions, calling for state control over markets, public ownership, and replacement of market liberalism with socialism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto scores low on economic and financial market liberalism (advocating state ownership, capital controls, anti-deregulation), moderately on social liberalism (gender equality, disability rights), and moderately on political liberalism (democracy promotion), yielding a below-average overall liberalism score.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports political democracy, civil rights, and anti-discrimination (pro-liberal), but explicitly opposes market liberalisation, privatisation, deregulation, and free capital flows, and advocates a Tobin tax and capital controls (anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions).

chunk 4 · The text is strongly pro-liberal on social dimensions (LGBTQ+ rights, anti-discrimination, gender equality) and political freedoms (democracy, free speech), but moderately anti-liberal economically through state intervention, media regulation, and market restrictions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democratic rights such as freedom of expression and local self-government, promotes social equality, but opposes market liberalism and multinational corporate power, resulting in a mixed stance on liberalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports democracy, political rights, social equality, free markets, competition, and financial market openness, indicating a strong pro-liberal stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports international institutions like the UN for political rights and peace, advocates against discrimination and for social rights, critiques unregulated economic globalization, and calls for international financial oversight, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall with some skepticism about current financial market liberalization.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal political institutions (democracy, media freedom), social liberalism (anti-racism, minority rights), and economic liberalism (support for cultural funding and market-based cultural policies), but lacks discussion on financial market policies.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism (such as free speech, minority rights, and democratic reforms) but heavily rejects economic and financial liberalism in favor of state control and anti-market policies.

chunk 2 · The text supports political democracy and gender equality, but strongly opposes classical liberal economic principles by advocating for state ownership, market regulation, and weakening financial capital.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports democratic institutions and minority rights, but rejects free-market economics and financial deregulation in favor of state control and market regulation.

chunk 4 · The text shows strong support for social and political rights, but advocates for state intervention in markets, such as price controls on books and bans on risk-based insurance pricing.
