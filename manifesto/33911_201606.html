WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames the Spanish state as a corrupt, centralizing elite that systematically oppresses the Catalan people, invoking a unified Catalan 'people' with a democratic mandate against an unresponsive establishment, while portraying the conflict in moralized terms of a premeditated offensive against Catalan self-governance.

chunk 2 · The text frames the Spanish central government (PP) as authoritarian and anti-democratic, invoking the Catalan people's democratic mandate against a corrupt elite, but the populist framing is primarily instrumental to the Catalan independence cause rather than a thoroughgoing people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The manifesto criticises the Spanish central government (PP) for austerity policies that harm Catalonia and its citizens, frames the relationship between Catalonia and the Spanish state as unjust and disloyal, and invokes citizens as victims of elite mismanagement, but the rhetoric is primarily regionalist/autonomist rather than strongly populist in the classic people-vs-elite sense.

chunk 4 · The text criticises the PP government's economic mismanagement and austerity policies, but does so in a technocratic, policy-oriented register rather than a morally charged populist one; references to "the people" are incidental and there is no sustained elite-vs-people narrative or Manichean framing.

chunk 5 · The text shows moderate populist features, primarily framing the Spanish central government (PP) as corrupt and self-serving ("clientelismes polítics"), invoking citizens' interests against elite mismanagement, but the dominant frame is Catalan autonomy rather than a pure people-vs-elite populist narrative.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the Spanish state as an obstructive elite denying Catalonia's democratic right to decide, emphasizes the centrality of the Catalan people as a unified actor, and frames the political struggle in moral terms of denial and injustice, supporting moderate to high populism scores.

chunk 2 · The text explicitly criticizes the 'govern absolutista' and lack of dialogue, emphasizes the voice of the Catalan people, and highlights breaches of democratic norms, indicating moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean framing.

chunk 3 · The manifesto expresses distrust and accuses the Spanish state of disloyalty and financial oppression, reflecting anti-elitism and a moralized conflict, while emphasizing Catalonia as a distinct people facing adversity, indicating moderate populist themes.

chunk 4 · The text criticizes the central government for failing to leverage economic growth opportunities and highlights austerity policies lacking social sensitivity, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized struggle.

chunk 5 · The text shows mixed populist elements including criticism of political centralization by elites, appeals to the will of the Catalan people, and moralized critiques of the ruling party's actions, indicating moderate populist discourse.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text frames the Spanish state as an oppressive elite attacking Catalan democracy and institutions, while championing the Catalan people's democratic will.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the Spanish central government as an authoritarian, non-dialogue-seeking elite attacking democracy, while championing the democratic mandate of the Catalan people.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits mild anti-elitism directed specifically at the central Spanish government's financial and political actions against Catalonia, rather than a generalized populist worldview.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits mild populist elements by criticizing the abuse of power by large corporations and monopolies.

chunk 5 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the central Spanish government as corrupt and out of touch, while advocating for returning power directly to the citizens.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is primarily ethno-territorial (right-leaning, identity-based nationalism focused on Catalan independence and language/culture protection), with secondary left-leaning elements around reducing inequality and expanding the welfare state, making it a mixed but predominantly right-populist nationalist profile.

chunk 2 · The manifesto blends centre-left social policy (minimum wage increases, welfare expansion, anti-poverty measures) with Catalan nationalist identity politics, making it primarily a centrist-to-left populist document with a strong ethno-territorial dimension rather than a purely left or right populist one.

chunk 3 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist with calls for increased welfare spending, redistribution, gender equality, and labour protections, but is primarily driven by Catalan nationalist/autonomist demands rather than a clear left-right populist ideology; right-populist elements are minimal and mostly absent.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is primarily a Catalan autonomist/pro-independence programme with moderate centre-left economic elements (redistribution, social protection, SME support) but no strong left-populist or right-populist ideological framing; the dominant axis is Catalan self-governance rather than a classic left-right populist ideology.

chunk 5 · The populist ideology is predominantly centrist-regionalist, focused on Catalan national interests and self-determination rather than a clear left or right economic program, with only minor references to inequality (left) or territorial identity (right).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-leaning social justice goals like fighting poverty and inclusion with right-leaning nationalist defense of Catalonia and centrist appeals to pluralism and broad participation, reflecting a mixed but overall populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports left-leaning populist themes such as reducing inequality and poverty, while also showing some right-leaning elements like support for law and order measures; it also contains centrist appeals regarding the Catalan question's importance.

chunk 3 · The text supports workers' rights and social protections (left), defends Catalan linguistic identity (right), and calls for broad political agreements (centrist), showing a mixed but moderate populist ideological stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto promotes combating inequalities and progressive fiscal policies (left), opposes central market unification laws (right), and appeals to broad people-centered economic reform (centrist), indicating a mixed populist ideology.

chunk 5 · The manifesto combines left-leaning environmental fiscal policies, right-leaning local autonomy defense, and centrist transitional negotiation stances, reflecting a mixed populist ideological profile.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist appeal is centered on Catalan regional nationalism and self-determination, positioning the Catalan people against the Spanish state without a strong left-right economic populist focus.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is left-leaning, focusing heavily on reducing inequalities, strengthening the welfare state, and protecting vulnerable groups against austerity.

chunk 3 · The populist elements are centrist and nationalist-regionalist, focusing on Catalan self-determination and defending regional institutions against central state elites.

chunk 4 · The populist framing is oriented towards the left, focusing on corporate abuse of power and protecting small businesses and workers.

chunk 5 · The populist appeals are centrist and regionalist, focusing on Catalan self-determination and anti-centralist elites without a strong left-wing or right-wing populist ideology.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto endorses democratic quality, transparency, anti-corruption, LGBTQI+ equality, gender equality, competitive enterprise, and free trade (TTIP), reflecting moderate classical liberal values across political, social, and economic dimensions, though the strong statist welfare and autonomy-protection framing tempers full liberal scoring.

chunk 2 · The text shows mixed liberal signals: it defends democratic institutions and separation of powers, promotes social equality and reproductive freedom, and supports market competition, but also advocates extensive state intervention in housing, energy, and social services, yielding a middling overall liberalism score.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports rule of law and constitutional court compliance (lib_political), strongly endorses LGBTQI+ rights and gender equality (lib_social), mixes market flexibility with labour protections and welfare expansion (lib_economic), and facilitates credit access for civil society while also proposing state-directed financial mechanisms (lib_financial_market), yielding a moderately pro-liberal overall profile.

chunk 4 · The manifesto consistently supports market competition, SME access to credit, anti-fraud measures, consumer protection in financial markets, and non-discrimination, reflecting a moderate pro-liberal stance across all four dimensions, tempered by calls for stronger social protection and state intervention in energy and infrastructure.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is moderately pro-liberal: it strongly defends judicial independence, rule of law, and democratic checks (lib_political); upholds human rights and civil liberties (lib_social); promotes market competition in telecoms (lib_economic); but partially undermines financial market liberalism by supporting a financial transactions tax (lib_financial_market).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · While the manifesto supports democratic quality, transparency, social equality, and economic competitiveness, it also highlights strong financial control and intervention by the central government, indicating limited liberalism especially in financial market autonomy.

chunk 2 · The text shows moderate support for liberal social policies with emphasis on social protection and rights, some political liberalism in democratic concerns, moderate economic liberalism in housing market critique, and limited financial market liberalism in credit access for social entities.

chunk 3 · The manifesto advocates universal political rights like healthcare access, promotes social rights including LGTBI recognition, supports some economic liberalization measures, but lacks discussion on financial market policies, resulting in a moderate liberalism score.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports democratic rights, anti-discrimination labor clauses, industrial competitiveness, and financial consumer protection, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal democratic institutions, human rights, market efficiency, and financial transparency reforms, demonstrating a moderate pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports democratic mandates, minority rights (LGTBI, gender equality), and business competitiveness, though it does not detail financial market policies.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports liberal political institutions, the separation of powers, and fundamental rights, while advocating for regulated market competition and access to credit.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports social equality, gender rights, and local political autonomy, while advocating for a balanced economic approach that supports business competitiveness alongside social protections.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports market competition, consumer protection, and non-discrimination, aligning with classical liberal principles.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports liberal democratic institutions, judicial independence, and market competition, though it supports some financial regulation like the financial transaction tax.
