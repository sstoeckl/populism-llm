WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The text criticises the PSD/PP government's mismanagement in fairly standard partisan terms ("forma desastrada como o País foi conduzido") and appeals to national mobilisation, but frames the conflict as a policy failure rather than a moral elite-vs-people struggle; populist features are weak and incidental to a technocratic reform programme.

chunk 2 · The text contains mild populist elements, primarily in its criticism of the PSD/PP governments as fraudulent and its framing of neoliberal policies as disorienting, but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than strongly populist.

chunk 3 · The text shows minimal populist features: it briefly criticises the PSD/PP government's energy policy as a failure and frames some social policies around 'the people', but there is no sustained anti-elite rhetoric, no Manichean good-vs-evil framing, and no invocation of a unified virtuous people against corrupt elites.

chunk 4 · The text is a mainstream social-democratic programme with minimal populist content: it briefly criticises corporatism and corruption but frames politics institutionally, not as a moral struggle between a virtuous people and a corrupt elite.

chunk 5 · The text is a policy-oriented defence and security manifesto chapter with no anti-elite rhetoric, no moralized good-vs-evil framing, and only incidental references to citizens as beneficiaries of security policy; populism is essentially absent.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is a mainstream centre-left social-democratic programme: it emphasises inequality reduction and social cohesion with mild left-populist flavour, but is primarily technocratic and pro-EU, with negligible right-populist or purely centrist-populist content.

chunk 2 · The text leans left-populist in its emphasis on combating poverty and exclusion, social solidarity, workers' rights, and redistribution, while also embracing market competition and social dialogue, placing it in a moderate centre-left ideological space.

chunk 3 · The text leans mildly left-populist through references to social justice, redistribution, and gender equality, while also endorsing regulated immigration and market competition, making any populist ideological flavour weak and predominantly centre-left.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows a mild left-leaning flavour by explicitly opposing a "neo-liberal" drift of the Lisbon Strategy and defending the European social model, but it lacks strong redistributive or anti-corporate populist rhetoric.

chunk 5 · No populist ideological content is present: the text contains no left-wing redistributive appeals, no right-wing nativist or anti-immigration rhetoric, and no centrist people-vs-elite framing; it is a technocratic defence policy document.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly endorses market competition, independent regulation, corporate governance transparency, democratic quality, and equality of opportunity, placing it firmly in a pro-liberal (classical/ordo-liberal) tradition, tempered by a social-democratic emphasis on public investment and fiscal consolidation.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it supports democratic institutions and civic participation, gender equality and minority inclusion, market competition and enterprise competitiveness, and prudential private-sector management of public pension reserves.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly liberal: it strongly endorses democratic deepening, human rights, gender equality, abortion decriminalisation, and non-discrimination; it supports market competition in energy and housing rental markets, though it also advocates significant state regulation and public investment, producing a mixed but overall pro-liberal economic stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly endorses democratic checks and balances, separation of powers, and non-discrimination, while also supporting market openness and transparency in financial disclosures, yielding consistently pro-liberal scores across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 5 · The text shows moderate liberal orientation: it affirms rule of law and international legal order, supports market-oriented enterprise activity, references defence-sector industrial and financial development, and includes social support for excluded groups, all consistent with a mainstream centre-left liberal programme.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political principles such as democratic modernization, political participation, and rule of law; social liberalism through equality and non-discrimination in sports and society; economic liberalism via reforming local government financing and reducing dependency on construction revenues; but lacks explicit discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly affirms the importance of international organizations in maintaining legality, order, and peace, reflecting a pro-liberal political stance.
