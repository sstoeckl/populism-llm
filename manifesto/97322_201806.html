WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto invokes the people as a central reference point and contains mild anti-elite rhetoric (lobbies controlling healthcare, wealth concentrated in few hands), but the overall tone is programmatic and technocratic rather than strongly populist, with no clear good-vs-evil framing of a corrupt elite versus a virtuous people.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows only mild populist tendencies: it invokes 'the people' and workers in a general sense and frames cooperative/worker ownership as an answer to 'ruthless corporate capitalism,' but does not systematically attack elites or construct a Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 3 · The text shows only faint populist signals: occasional references to interest groups threatening public good and past decisions benefiting narrow groups at collective expense, but no sustained elite-vs-people framing or moralized Manichean narrative.

chunk 4 · The text is a policy-oriented manifesto chapter focused on national security, cybersecurity, disaster response, and minority communities, with no anti-elite rhetoric, no moralized good-vs-evil framing, and only incidental references to 'the people' in the context of protection and services.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · Manifesto expresses moderate anti-elitism and moralized conflict through references to corruption and societal distrust, while emphasizing people-centrism by focusing on citizens' welfare and social solidarity.

chunk 2 · The manifesto does not provide explicit content related to populism dimensions such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean narratives.

chunk 3 · Manifesto does not explicitly discuss populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moral dichotomies; no direct evidence found.

chunk 4 · The text contains no explicit references to anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralized good-vs-evil struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by highlighting how 'the people' feel cheated and out of touch with corrupt elites, though it frames this within a structured social-democratic program.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism, combining a critique of bureaucratic elites and corporate capitalism with calls for direct democratic mechanisms to determine the will of the people.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits mild populism through people-centric rhetoric regarding public administration and warnings about special interest groups, but lacks a strong moralizing Manichean worldview.

chunk 4 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist claims, or moralized good-versus-evil framing.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is clearly left-leaning populist in ideology, emphasising progressive taxation, redistribution, strong public services, workers' profit-sharing, and reduction of inequality, with only marginal nods to national identity and no right-wing populist anti-immigration content.

chunk 2 · The dominant ideological flavor is centre-left: the manifesto advocates worker shareholding, profit-sharing, cooperative models, progressive taxation, and social partnership, with only marginal right-populist elements (economic migration strategy framed as selective integration).

chunk 3 · The mild populist elements lean slightly left (anti-energy poverty, social inclusion of Roma, worker protections) with minimal right-populist content (migration distinctions), placing the overall ideological flavor as weakly centre-left.

chunk 4 · The text shows mild left-leaning tendencies through emphasis on social inclusion of Roma, anti-discrimination measures, and multicultural coexistence, but lacks strong populist ideological framing of any kind.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-leaning social justice and redistribution themes with centrist appeals to inclusive governance and moderate right-leaning concerns about nationalism, reflecting a balanced populist ideology.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit evidence in the text indicating a populist ideological stance on the left, right, or centrist spectrum.

chunk 3 · No explicit references to populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) are present in the text; no direct evidence found.

chunk 4 · There is no explicit discussion of left, right, or centrist populist ideological themes in the text, thus no evidence of populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on social democracy, reducing inequality, and criticizing corporate/neoliberal structures.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are strongly left-wing, framing economic democracy and worker ownership as direct solutions to social inequality generated by the capitalist system.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology leans left, prioritizing consumer rights and public welfare over corporate and agricultural economic interests.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on national security, minority rights, and diaspora relations without populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (democracy, rule of law, equality, minority rights), mixed on economic liberalism (open economy but strong state intervention, progressive taxation, mandatory profit-sharing), and only tangentially addresses financial markets through the bank bailout reference without taking a clear pro-market finance stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it supports democratic institutions, media freedom, gender equality, deregulation for SMEs, and prudential banking regulation, while also proposing some market-correcting measures (worker ownership mandates, cooperative banking) that introduce a mixed signal on economic and financial liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto consistently supports democratic institutions, rule of law, anti-discrimination, competitive markets, transparent public finances, and prudential corporate governance of state assets, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 4 · The text affirms human rights and fundamental freedoms in the context of cybersecurity, promotes anti-discrimination and minority rights (Roma, national communities), and encourages economic cooperation, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal stance across political, social, and economic dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal democratic institutions, social rights, economic innovation, and prudent financial management, indicating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles including media freedom, gender equality, economic openness, and prudent financial market regulation, as evidenced by commitments to independent journalism, equal rights, SME internationalization, and banking credit facilitation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto emphasizes rule of law, political rights, social equality, economic growth, and democratic governance, reflecting a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports political rights and protections for ethnic minorities, social inclusion policies, and economic integration efforts, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports political and social liberalism through rule of law and equal opportunities, but advocates for strong state intervention and public services over free-market economic liberalism.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political democracy, media freedom, and gender equality, but takes a highly interventionist, state-driven, and cooperative-based approach to economic and financial matters.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal political institutions, the rule of law, minority protections, and international trade, while advocating for state-guided economic coordination.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports liberal political and social values, emphasizing human rights, fundamental freedoms, and the social inclusion of marginalized ethnic minorities.
