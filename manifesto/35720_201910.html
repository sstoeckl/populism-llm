WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames the political establishment ("elites", "establishment de esquerda", founding parties) as corrupt usurpers who have drained citizens of power and resources through an "extortion system", while invoking citizens as the rightful sovereign, but the anti-elitism is directed primarily at the left-wing constitutional order rather than a pure people-vs-elite populist frame, and the text explicitly warns against tyranny by "the people" as well, moderating overall populism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows moderate populism: it frames elites (oligarchies, lobbies, supranational organisations, globalists) as corrupt adversaries of the Portuguese people, invokes a Manichean struggle between a virtuous nationalist right and a destructive globalist left, and centres policy around the needs of "Portugal and the Portuguese."

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elites as corrupt and contrasts them with the people, framing power struggles in moral terms and emphasizing opposition to arbitrariness and abuse of power, indicating moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly opposes elites and oligarchies, emphasizes the people as a unified group, and frames a moral struggle against globalism and cultural Marxism, demonstrating clear populist rhetoric.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text attacks the established political cartel and 'self-proclaimed elites' as corrupt forces running an 'asphyxiating system of extortion' against citizens.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits clear populist elements by contrasting the needs of the Portuguese people against corrupting oligarchical interests and framing political struggles as a moralized battle on opposite sides of a barricade.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly right-wing populist: it champions national/cultural identity, opposes immigration ("invasão maciça"), attacks Marxist cultural influence, defends traditional family structures, opposes LGBTQ+ rights in public policy, and frames the political struggle as the authentic right against a left-wing constitutional order imposed by force.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is unambiguously right-wing populist: it explicitly identifies with the "identitarian Right," champions national sovereignty, opposes immigration, promotes conservative family values, and frames its enemies as the globalist-Marxist left and supranational institutions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text strongly aligns with right-wing populist ideology, emphasizing conservative liberalism, national sovereignty, and opposition to leftist constitutional legacies, while rejecting leftist ideologies and promoting a conservative cultural identity.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports right-wing populist themes such as anti-LGBTI propaganda, national sovereignty, and conservative cultural values, while also showing centrist populist appeals through bilateralism and some limited left critique of elites.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is explicitly right-wing, framing its struggle against Marxist cultural hegemony and globalism while defending national traditions and a 'modern Euro-American Right' platform.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is explicitly right-wing, identifying with a 'New Right' that defends national identity, traditional family values, and strict immigration controls against globalism.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores high on economic and political liberalism—championing free markets, private property, flat taxes, rule of law, and separation of powers—but scores low on social liberalism, explicitly opposing LGBTQ+ rights in education and public funding for abortion and gender-reassignment surgery, yielding a mixed overall liberal profile.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly economically liberal (free markets, privatisation, state as mere arbiter), moderately pro-rule-of-law on judicial independence, but sharply anti-liberal on social issues (banning LGBTI content in schools, opposing gender ideology, restricting immigration rights), yielding a mixed overall liberalism score.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal principles such as rule of law, separation of powers, individual political rights, economic freedoms, and limited state intervention, while advocating for social equality in terms of non-discrimination and equal protection under the law.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports political liberalism through calls for judicial independence, but opposes social liberalism by banning LGBTI propaganda; economically it favors free markets with state as regulator, but shows skepticism towards financial market liberalism with demands for fund recovery.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports classical economic liberalism, private property, and limited state intervention, but rejects social liberalism by opposing LGBTQ+ rights and gender equality initiatives.

chunk 2 · The text presents a highly mixed stance on liberalism, strongly advocating for free-market economic policies and privatization while simultaneously rejecting social liberalism, minority protections, and LGBTQ+ rights.
