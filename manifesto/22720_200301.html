WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames politics as a moral struggle between "good and not good, old and new," invokes the citizen as the rightful sovereign against a "regentencultuur" (patrician elite culture), and positions the LPF as the voice of ordinary people against an unresponsive establishment.

chunk 2 · The text shows mild populist tendencies through appeals to the citizen ("overheid ten dienste van de burger"), some anti-establishment rhetoric (criticising "megablunders" and bureaucracy), and a pragmatic framing, but lacks strong elite-bashing or moralized good-vs-evil narratives.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly critiques political elites as disconnected and emphasizes 'the people' as a collective, framing politics as a struggle between good and not good, indicating moderate populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto emphasizes government service to the people and progressive tax burden, indicating mild anti-elitism and people-centrism with limited moral polarization.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits strong populism by framing politics as a struggle between the citizens and a corrupt 'regentencultuur' (regent culture), emphasizing that the government must serve the people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by emphasizing that the government must serve the citizen and criticizing wasteful state spending.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is predominantly right-leaning, centered on strict anti-immigration policy, national identity protection, and cultural assimilation demands, while explicitly rejecting a left-right framing and championing free markets over redistribution.

chunk 2 · The LPF manifesto blends modest right-leaning populism (cutting integration budgets, pragmatic nationalism on environment) with some left-leaning solidarity rhetoric (progressive taxation, "sterkste schouders"), resulting in a weakly ideological, largely centrist-pragmatic populist profile.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text rejects traditional left-right divisions, stresses national identity, limits immigration, and calls for integration based on Dutch norms, reflecting a strong right-leaning populist ideology with centrist appeals and some left critiques of bureaucracy.

chunk 2 · The text combines centrist appeals to government service with left-leaning tax solidarity and some right-leaning environmental concerns, reflecting a mixed populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, focusing heavily on national identity, strict immigration limits, and rejecting multiculturalism.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology leans rightward, as evidenced by proposals to cut integration and development aid budgets.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses free market economics, parliamentary democracy, and individual freedoms (including gender and sexual equality), but tempers liberal social scores with restrictive integration demands and cultural assimilation requirements for minorities.

chunk 2 · The text supports economic liberalism through tax reduction, deregulation, and stimulating entrepreneurship, while showing mixed signals on financial markets (mortgage regulation) and limited political liberalism through citizen participation; social liberalism is entirely absent from the text.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, individual rights including equality for minorities, free market economy, and parliamentary democracy, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market regulation, indicating a broadly pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, social protections, and economic incentives for entrepreneurship and home ownership, showing moderate classical liberalism, but lacks financial market discussion.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports classical liberal principles including parliamentary democracy, individual responsibility, equal rights for women and homosexuals, and a free market economy.

chunk 2 · The text supports classical liberal economic principles, such as stimulating the business climate and reducing administrative burdens, while advocating for some state oversight in key infrastructure.
