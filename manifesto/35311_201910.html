The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly rejects populism ("soluções simples e populistas não funcionam") and frames itself as a technocratic, evidence-based governing party, showing virtually no populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean framing.

chunk 2 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto of the Portuguese Socialist Party (PS) that explicitly opposes populism ("luta contra os populismos e os nacionalismos xenófobos"), shows no anti-elite rhetoric, and frames its appeals in technocratic, institutional terms rather than people-vs-elite narratives.

chunk 3 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto (PS, Portugal 2019) focused on environmental, agricultural, demographic, and housing policy; it contains virtually no populist rhetoric—no elite-bashing, no Manichean framing, and only incidental references to 'the people', with an explicit rejection of xenophobia and demagoguery.

chunk 4 · The text shows minimal populism: it briefly references "movimentos populistas" as a social ill to be avoided, frames the PS as a party of equal opportunity, and opposes racist/demagogic positions, but does not construct a people-vs-elite narrative or moralized good-vs-evil struggle.

chunk 5 · The text is a technocratic policy manifesto with minimal populist rhetoric; the only near-populist moment is a brief dismissal of higher education as an "elite redoubt," but there is no sustained anti-elite framing, no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, and no invocation of a unified virtuous "people" against corrupt elites.

chunk 6 · The text is a mainstream centre-left party programme (PS Portugal) focused on digital rights, culture, and labour regulation; it invokes "the people" broadly and defends workers' rights but shows virtually no anti-elite rhetoric, Manichean framing, or populist people-vs-elite narrative.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild centre-left tendencies (reducing inequality, social cohesion, public services) but explicitly opposes right-wing nationalism and populism, and does not frame politics as a people-vs-elite struggle, placing it at very low populist ideology overall.

chunk 2 · The PS manifesto is a mainstream centre-left social-democratic programme with mild social-policy references and explicit rejection of right-wing populism and xenophobic nationalism; it carries no meaningful populist ideological charge in any direction.

chunk 3 · The manifesto leans mildly centre-left through emphasis on combating labour precarity, social dialogue, and public housing, but avoids strong ideological populist framing; there is minimal right-populist content beyond balanced migration management.

chunk 4 · The text leans mildly left-populist through emphasis on redistribution of income and wealth, combating salary inequality, and strengthening social protection, but is embedded in a mainstream social-democratic programmatic framework rather than a strongly populist ideological one.

chunk 5 · The faint left-leaning tinge (fairer labour relations, greater labour share of national income) is embedded in a broadly pro-market, pro-innovation, pro-internationalisation programme, yielding a very low overall populist-ideological score with a slight left inflection.

chunk 6 · The limited populist signals present lean mildly left (worker protections, social protection, gig-economy regulation) with no right-wing identity or anti-immigration content and only weak centrist people-centrism.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across political (democratic quality, rule of law, anti-corruption), social (gender equality, anti-discrimination), economic (tax reduction, competitiveness, free enterprise), and financial (prudential supervision reform, competitive financial sector) dimensions, reflecting a mainstream social-democratic pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly affirms rule of law, judicial independence, and democratic checks and balances; supports gender equality in the armed forces; promotes trade openness and internationalisation of the economy; and endorses completing the Banking Union and EU financial integration, all consistent with a moderately pro-liberal stance across all four dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it upholds fundamental rights and access to justice (political), combats discrimination (social), promotes competitive markets and private investment in forestry (economic/financial), while also advocating significant state intervention in housing and labour markets, producing a mixed but overall moderately pro-liberal profile.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on social dimensions (anti-discrimination, LGBTI rights, gender equality, minority protections) and moderately pro-liberal politically (democracy, rule of law references), while on economic and financial dimensions it shows a mixed stance—supporting market regulation and consumer transparency in banking but also advocating significant state intervention in housing markets and labour regulation.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions an open economy, export targets, free competition, digital market integration, democratic oversight of AI, LGBTI rights, and prudential (transparency-based) financial regulation, with only modest state-interventionist elements such as public development finance and consumer protection rules.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political (democratic oversight of AI, digital rights charter) and social (gender equality, anti-discrimination) dimensions, and moderately pro-liberal economically (competitiveness, internationalisation of culture); financial-market policy is entirely absent.

chunk 3 · O manifesto contém várias passagens que defendem direitos políticos, inclusão social, combate à discriminação, promoção da coesão social e regulação econômica, refletindo uma orientação liberal moderada, embora sem menção explícita a regulação financeira de mercados.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports constitutional rights, anti-discrimination, social equality, employment, and economic justice, reflecting a high pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 5 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including consumer rights protection, cultural inclusivity, promotion of fair labor relations, innovation financing, and digital economy development, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 6 · The manifesto explicitly supports political rights and democratic oversight, social equality measures such as gender equality in culture, and economic policies including increased state cultural spending, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance.
