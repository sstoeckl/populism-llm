WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is primarily a policy programme for the Hungarian minority in Romania (UDMR), focused on autonomy, identity preservation, and social/economic policy; it invokes community solidarity and mild resistance to central state power but lacks strong anti-elite rhetoric, Manichean framing, or classic populist people-vs-elite narratives.

chunk 2 · The text shows minimal populism: it mildly criticises centralised state power and bureaucracy as sources of corruption, and invokes the Hungarian community as a collective subject, but frames demands through rule-of-law institutions rather than a virtuous-people-vs-corrupt-elite narrative.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles.

chunk 2 · The text does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, thus no evidence of populism is found.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits very low populism, focusing on minority rights and decentralization rather than a moralized people-vs-elite struggle, though it criticizes state centralism.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits very low populism, only criticizing state centralization and bureaucratic power as sources of corruption without invoking a moralized struggle of the pure people against a corrupt elite.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto blends minority-rights nationalism (identity/cultural preservation for Hungarians in Romania, right-leaning) with social solidarity and welfare policies (left-leaning), without a dominant populist ideological flavour; it is best characterised as a moderate ethnic-minority interest party programme.

chunk 2 · The populist flavour is weak and mixed: there are mild left-leaning social-welfare concerns (fighting pauperisation) alongside ethno-national identity protection for the Hungarian minority in Romania, without a dominant left or right populist programme.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances on the left, right, or centrist populism in the text.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit discussion of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the text, so no populist ideology is evidenced.

Gemini

chunk 1 · There is no clear populist ideology present, as the discourse is structured around ethnic minority representation and classical liberal-democratic reforms.

chunk 2 · There is no clear populist ideology present in the text, as the focus is on minority rights, regionalism, and European integration rather than populist mobilization.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently supports democratic rule of law and autonomy, gender equality, free-market economics with privatisation and tax reduction, and banking privatisation with transparency — all broadly pro-liberal stances, though tempered by significant social-welfare and state-subsidy commitments.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal politically (judicial independence, rule of law, anti-corruption, parliamentary democracy) and socially (equal rights for religious communities, minority language rights), with moderate economic liberalism (fiscal incentives, EU integration) and only marginal financial-market content (VAT on medicines).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly supports democratic principles, equality of opportunity, a functional market economy, privatization, and transparent financial market reforms, indicating a strong pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports rule of law and parliamentary democracy, fundamental political rights, and social rights such as freedom of expression and minority language media, as well as regional economic development aligned with European integration, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal principles, including the rule of law, individual rights, a functional market economy, and privatization of the banking sector.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing the independence of the judiciary, the rule of law, religious freedom, and support for private enterprise and credit.
