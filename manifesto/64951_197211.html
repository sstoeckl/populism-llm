WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames private financial institutions as illegitimately profiting from credit that "rightfully belongs to the nation as a whole," portrays the people as the virtuous sovereign whose credit has been usurped, and casts the struggle as one between ordinary citizens and a "financial dictatorship," yielding strong but not maximally conspiratorial populism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies, criticising the "Party System" and the current government's disregard for public servants, and invoking the primacy of "the New Zealand people," but the tone is predominantly technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly opposes political and financial dictatorship, monopoly, and bureaucracy, emphasizing opposition to elites and advocating sovereignty of the people over money creation, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral struggle.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the gap between true democracy and the parliamentary system, emphasizing constituent representation and financial reform, indicating moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with limited moral polarization.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate-to-high populism by framing the financial system as a corrupt 'financial dictatorship' and advocating for the return of monetary sovereignty to 'the people' as a unified, virtuous group.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing the established party system and advocating for direct constituent representation, though it lacks a strong moralized good-versus-evil narrative.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly rejects left-right categorisation, positioning Social Credit as a technocratic monetary-reform movement; its anti-monopoly, pro-redistribution of credit stance has mild left-populist overtones, but its defence of private enterprise and individual freedom tempers this, placing it closest to centrist populism with a slight left lean.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is centrist-leaning with minor left elements (restricting foreign investment, state-assisted industries) and minor right elements (preference for NZ-owned services), but no strong programmatic left or right populist framing dominates; the party presents itself as a pragmatic reform movement above traditional party lines.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto states Social Credit is distinct from left or right ideologies but focuses on arresting capitalist centralization and opposes foreign land ownership, indicating a centrist populist ideology with some left and right elements.

chunk 2 · The text shows a moderate left populist stance through financial reform aims, a right-leaning protectionist approach to foreign investment, and centrist appeals to constituent representation, reflecting a mixed populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The ideology is explicitly centrist, rejecting both left and right conventional philosophies in favor of a unique monetary reform theory designed to preserve private enterprise through state-controlled credit.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology leans slightly right-nationalist through its protectionist stance against foreign investment and ownership of domestic assets.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-individual rights (Bill of Rights, anti-discrimination, democratic reform) and pro-private enterprise, but its core financial proposals—state-controlled credit creation, interest-free government lending, and a National Credit Authority—are substantially anti-liberal in the financial-market dimension, pulling the overall liberalism score to moderate.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal politically (electoral reform, PR-STV, rule of law, ombudsman, judicial review) and socially (decriminalising homosexuality, civil rights protections), but economically mixed—combining free trade rhetoric with state-directed finance, import controls, and government-set interest rates that temper its liberal credentials.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports liberal political rights via a Bill of Rights, opposes racial discrimination, endorses competitive enterprise with fair government rules, and advocates state-controlled credit to enable market function, showing a generally pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports political transparency, civil rights reforms, economic support via low-interest finance, and judicial oversight of prices, indicating a generally pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial market dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through a proposed Bill of Rights and decriminalization of homosexuality, but scores low on financial liberalism due to its advocacy for state-controlled credit and banking.

chunk 2 · The platform strongly supports liberal political reforms and social liberalization (such as decriminalizing homosexuality), but takes a mixed approach to economic liberalism by combining support for competition with state-directed credit and interest rate controls.
