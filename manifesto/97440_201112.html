WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto contains only mild populist elements: the managerial elite is criticised for "predatory takeovers" instead of investing in R&D, but this critique is embedded in a technocratic, policy-heavy programme rather than a moralistic good-vs-evil narrative, and "the people" are not invoked as a unified virtuous bloc.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows minimal populist features: there is a passing critique of 'elitno' art and a general reference to citizens' quality of life, but no sustained anti-elite rhetoric, no Manichean good-vs-evil framing, and no invocation of a unified virtuous 'people' against corrupt elites.

chunk 3 · The text contains moderate populist elements, notably framing economic and political elites as corrupt networks that capture the state (TEŠ 6 example), but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than strongly populist; people-centrism appears mainly through calls for active citizenship and public participation rather than a moralized people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 4 · The text frames economic and political elites as corrupt networks ("kapitalsko-politična omrežja") that capture the state (e.g., TEŠ 6), manipulate media, and finance politicians, while calling for accountability and transparency — moderate populist framing without a fully Manichean people-vs-elite narrative.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto criticizes elite economic mismanagement and emphasizes empowering citizens to face challenges, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral framing of crisis.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports cultural inclusion and reducing elitism, emphasizes active engagement with minority groups as a unified people, and critiques consumerist elite systems, indicating moderate populist themes.

chunk 3 · The manifesto critiques economic and political elites' corrupt influence, emphasizes active citizen participation, and highlights moralized struggles against elite capture, reflecting moderate populist themes.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes economic and political elites for corruption and their undue influence, showing anti-elitism and people-centrism with a moralized struggle against corrupt networks, supporting a moderate populism score.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the 'managerial elite' for 'predatory takeovers' instead of investing in development, though it lacks strong people-centrism.

chunk 2 · The text contains very minimal populist rhetoric, only briefly criticizing state-funded 'elite' art in favor of grassroots cultural creators.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing corrupt political-capital networks and advocating for citizen participation against out-of-touch elites.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing political and economic elites for capturing the state and colluding in corrupt networks, while advocating for closer communication with citizens.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is predominantly a centrist-liberal programme (Zares is a centre-left liberal party); the mild critique of the managerial elite and support for social inclusion give a faint left-populist tinge, but there is no nativist or right-populist content beyond a skills-based immigration reference.

chunk 2 · The manifesto's populist flavour, where present, leans mildly left through support for the welfare state, social cohesion, and workers' rights, with only a marginal right-leaning element in domestic-producer protection; overall populist ideological content is very weak.

chunk 3 · The populist flavour leans mildly left, with references to fighting "kapitalsko-politična omrežja" (capital-political networks) and corporate elite capture of politics, while protectionist references to domestic producers are minor; centrist technocratic appeals to broad consensus are also present.

chunk 4 · The populist framing is predominantly left-leaning, targeting corporate-political elite capture, opposing privatisation of prisons due to "vpliv kapitala," and criticising financial market deregulation, with only minor national/identity elements (Slovenes abroad).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text supports social assistance and entrepreneurship, promotes adjusted immigration policy for skilled workers, and advocates an open society, indicating a centrist-leaning populist ideology with mild left and right elements.

chunk 2 · The text shows a centrist-left populist ideology by advocating for minority rights, social inclusion, and reducing elitism without strong right-wing nationalist elements.

chunk 3 · The text supports protection of domestic producers and rural development (left-leaning populism), environmental and identity concerns (right-leaning populism), and emphasizes citizen engagement without clear ideological bias (centrist populism).

chunk 4 · The text emphasizes anti-corruption and critiques elite capture with a left-leaning populist flavor, while also showing centrist people-elite appeals without strong right-wing nationalist elements.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements lean leftward, focusing on corporate elite greed and advocating for employee profit-sharing and social entrepreneurship.

chunk 2 · There is no clear populist ideological framing present in the text, as the focus remains on standard policy proposals.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is left-leaning, focusing on the negative influence of capital on public policy and criticizing corporate-political networks.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology leans left, focusing on the corrupting influence of capital, corporate elites, and financial markets over politics and public services.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it explicitly champions open markets, competition, rule of law, transparent governance, minority protections, and market-based financial instruments such as venture capital, while framing political and economic freedom as mutually reinforcing.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly liberal: it explicitly champions democratic processes, transparency, anti-discrimination, and minority rights (high lib_political and lib_social scores), while on the economic side it mixes market-friendly elements (competition in health, public-private partnerships) with significant state intervention, and on financial markets it envisages a time-limited state venture fund that ultimately defers to market survival.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it strongly supports judicial independence, rule of law, checks and balances, anti-discrimination, and minority rights; economic liberalism is mixed due to state interventions in energy and agriculture alongside competition-enabling measures; financial market regulation is framed as corrective oversight after the financial crisis rather than outright anti-market.

chunk 4 · The text is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (democracy, rule of law, human rights), mixed on economic liberalism (supports market competition but calls for stronger financial market regulation and criminal sanctions for market manipulation), and moderately pro-liberal on financial markets (transparency and oversight framed as enabling fair markets).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic education access, social equality in schooling, free enterprise, and market-based financial mechanisms like venture capital, demonstrating a broadly pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto promotes democratic processes, minority rights, social inclusion, and economic investment with public-private partnerships, reflecting a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions, though financial market specifics are absent.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, minority rights, social inclusion, market protections, and calls for improved financial market regulation, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports rule of law, civil rights, economic openness, and financial market regulation aimed at enabling market function, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses classical liberal principles, explicitly linking political and economic freedom, and advocating for open markets, competition, and private property.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports liberal democratic principles, including human rights, minority protections, and regulated private-sector participation in healthcare and pension funds.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through judicial independence and anti-discrimination, but takes a more protectionist and regulatory stance on economic and financial matters.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports political liberalism and human rights, but takes a mixed stance on economic liberalism by opposing public-private partnerships and demanding tighter regulation of financial markets.
