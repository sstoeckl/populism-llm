WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly frames the Liberal federal government as corrupt, wasteful, and anti-democratic (patronage, propaganda, fiscal deception), while positioning the Bloc Québécois as the sole defender of the virtuous Québécois people against an elite Ottawa establishment.

chunk 2 · The text frames the federal Liberal government as systematically corrupt and abusive ("abus et de fraude, de propagande et de patronage"), portrays Quebec citizens and workers as victims of elite federal mismanagement, and constructs a moral struggle between the federal establishment and the Quebec people, though the framing is primarily ethno-regional rather than a classic people-vs-elite populist narrative.

chunk 3 · The manifesto criticizes the federal Liberal government as acting with "cynisme destructeur" and being "un gouvernement d'une autre époque," framing it as out of touch with Quebec's needs, but the rhetoric is primarily institutional/jurisdictional rather than a full populist people-vs-elite moral binary.

chunk 4 · The manifesto criticizes the federal Liberal government for neglecting Quebec's interests and farmers, frames the federal government as an adversary of the Québécois people, and invokes consumers and citizens as virtuous actors, but the populist rhetoric is moderate and issue-specific rather than a sweeping people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 5 · The text criticizes the federal Liberal government for patronage appointments and mismanagement, framing it as elite corruption, but the tone is largely policy-focused and technocratic rather than strongly populist; people-centrism and Manichean framing are present but moderate.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly asserts the existence and rights of the Quebec nation, opposes federal government actions, and criticizes elites, showing moderate to strong populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean framing.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the federal government as having excessive fiscal resources leading to abuses and propagandistic use of public funds, reflecting anti-elitism and a people-centric stance through the Bloc Québécois's commitments to denounce these practices.

chunk 3 · The manifesto criticizes the federal government as an elite imposing ineffective policies, highlights the role of 'the people' especially youth in opposing undemocratic globalization, and frames political struggles in moral terms, indicating moderate populist attitudes.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the federal government as anti-Québec and portrays the Québec people as a distinct group facing opposition, indicating anti-elitism and people-centrism with a moralized conflict framing Québec as an 'enemy interior'.

chunk 5 · The manifesto criticizes political appointments as 'copinage libéral' and emphasizes building bridges between peoples, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moral framing of government failures.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text frames the federal Liberal government as corrupt, engaging in patronage, and acting against the democratic will of the Quebec people.

chunk 2 · The text strongly frames the federal government as corrupt, abusive, and out of touch, engaging in propaganda and patronage against the interests of the people.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits mild anti-elitism by framing the federal government's actions as hiding a destructive cynicism, but lacks strong people-centrism or Manichean framing.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the federal Liberal government as a corrupt, out-of-touch elite ('le copinage libéral') that ignores the democratic will of the citizens and treats Quebec as an 'enemy'.

chunk 5 · The text criticizes the ruling Liberal party for political patronage and lack of transparency in administrative appointments.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is a hybrid: left-leaning on redistribution and social solidarity (targeting tax cuts to low/middle incomes, restoring social transfers), but also right-leaning on national/identity protection (French language, Québec sovereignty, cultural distinctiveness), making it primarily a left-nationalist blend.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is primarily left-leaning populist in its ideological orientation, advocating for redistribution, social equity, workers' rights, and protection of the poor and women, while also carrying a strong ethno-regional (Quebec sovereigntist) dimension that partially overlaps with right-leaning identity protectionism.

chunk 3 · The Bloc Québécois combines left-leaning social policy (workers' rights, anti-discrimination, social programs) with a strong Quebec nationalist/identity dimension (cultural protection, provincial jurisdiction), making it primarily left-populist with a significant nationalist overlay rather than right-populist.

chunk 4 · The Bloc Québécois combines left-leaning demands (social clauses in trade agreements, artist income stabilization, agricultural subsidies, fair trade) with Quebec nationalist/identity concerns (cultural protection, immigration control by Quebec), placing it primarily in left-populist territory with a strong nationalist overlay.

chunk 5 · The Bloc Québécois combines left-leaning social concerns (protection of vulnerable immigrants, indigenous rights, social equality) with a Quebec-nationalist/right-leaning emphasis on provincial control over immigration, making it a mixed but predominantly centre-left populist ideology.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text promotes left-leaning economic redistribution, defends Quebec identity and language (right-leaning cultural nationalism), and includes centrist people-elite appeals, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The text shows a left-leaning populist ideology by highlighting social inequalities caused by liberal budget cuts, while also expressing nationalist concerns about federal institutions and policies affecting Quebec, indicating a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 3 · The text shows left-leaning populist ideology through advocacy for redistribution and provincial funding, right-leaning elements in defending cultural identity against federal imposition, and centrist populism in generic people-elite critiques, reflecting a balanced populist ideological stance.

chunk 4 · The text shows a left-leaning populist ideology emphasizing human rights and social justice, a right-leaning nationalist stance opposing federal interference, and a centrist populist appeal to democratic representation and negotiation rights for Québec.

chunk 5 · The text supports immigrant workers' rights and Quebec's control over immigration, showing left-leaning social justice concerns and right-leaning identity protectionism, with centrist appeals to partnership and dialogue.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist discourse is combined with a left-leaning emphasis on social solidarity, public services, and progressive taxation.

chunk 2 · The populist rhetoric is combined with left-leaning demands for social justice, poverty reduction, and protection of vulnerable groups.

chunk 3 · The populist elements are primarily centrist, focusing on regional and provincial autonomy against federal overreach without a strong left-wing or right-wing populist ideology.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology leans left-centrist, emphasizing social rights, environmental clauses in trade, and public services over corporate interests, without right-wing anti-immigrant sentiment.

chunk 5 · The populist critique is centrist, focusing on administrative corruption and political favoritism without a strong left-right ideological framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal signals: it invokes democratic principles and transparency (pro-liberal), proposes progressive tax reform and social spending (moderately liberal), advocates for Québec's cultural and linguistic protection (somewhat restrictive), and raises globalization governance concerns, yielding a middling overall liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal signals: it supports free trade in lumber and competitive financial markets, but also advocates for heavy state intervention in social housing, employment insurance, and banking regulation, with strong emphasis on civil rights and anti-discrimination measures for women and youth workers.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports women's reproductive rights and democratic institutions (rule of law, press freedom), while showing mixed signals on economic liberalism—opposing certain aspects of globalization but supporting provincial autonomy and fiscal transfers; financial market content is limited to anti-money-laundering measures.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports free trade, democratic transparency in treaty-making, and refugee protections including sexual orientation, but also advocates supply management, cultural protectionism, and state subsidies, producing a mixed liberal profile across dimensions.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports civil rights and anti-discrimination norms (refugee protection based on gender/sexual orientation, indigenous rights, francophone minority rights), advocates for transparent and impartial political institutions, and proposes modest fiscal measures, yielding an overall moderately pro-liberal profile.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions and parliamentary respect, social policies aiding families, economic openness, and some financial market regulation enabling market function, indicating moderate classical liberalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto discusses political liberalism through calls for indexed federal transfers and respect for provincial competencies, social liberalism via advocacy for gender equality and social justice, economic liberalism in terms of fiscal policy and investment, and financial market liberalism through detailed discussion of financial institution reforms.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions and provincial rights, minority protections, and economic policies favoring provincial funding and education, but lacks discussion on financial market regulation, indicating moderate liberalism overall.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports political rights and freedoms, social rights for vulnerable groups, and economic liberalization with social and environmental safeguards, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 5 · The manifesto advocates for minority rights, including francophone and refugee protections, supports social justice, and calls for government responsibility and funding, indicating a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions, though financial market issues are not addressed.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, pluralism, and economic competitiveness for small and medium enterprises.

chunk 2 · The text supports classical liberal principles such as free trade, market competition, and provincial political autonomy, alongside progressive social rights.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports liberal democratic institutions, media freedom, and individual social rights such as abortion access, while supporting prudential financial transparency rules.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports political liberalism through democratic oversight of treaties, social liberalism by defending minority rights (LGBTQ+ and women refugees), and economic liberalism via qualified support for free trade.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports liberal values by advocating for human rights, non-discrimination based on gender and sexual orientation, and impartial, rule-of-law-based public institutions.
