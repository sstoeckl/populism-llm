WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: it criticises governing elites and clientelism ("concentración de poder político y económico," "amiguismo político, el clientelismo y la corrupción") and centres the people as virtuous agents ("creemos en la gente"), but frames these critiques within a constructive, dialogue-oriented, institutional reform agenda rather than a sharp Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains mild populist undertones—criticizing privatization as benefiting "unos pocos" and condemning "politiqueras" pressures—but is predominantly a technocratic policy document focused on institutional reform, infrastructure, and environmental management, with no sustained people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: it criticizes corruption and irresponsible elites in infrastructure and politics, and invokes the people's welfare, but frames these within a technocratic, programmatic reform agenda rather than a sharp people-vs-elite moral binary.

chunk 4 · The manifesto contains only mild populist elements: a brief reference to the corrupt "traditional political class" and emphasis on citizen participation, but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than moralized people-vs-elite conflict.

chunk 5 · The text is primarily a policy programme with minimal populist framing; it invokes youth as a virtuous generation and references exclusion from political participation, but does not construct a clear elite-vs-people antagonism or moralized struggle.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elite concentration and clientelism while emphasizing belief in the people and their capacity to influence decisions, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moralized division.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes disproportionate privileges and emphasizes collective interest over individual, indicating moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism, with references to corruption and accountability, reflecting a moderate populist stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows mixed populist elements with critiques of corruption and social confrontation, appeals to citizen participation, and calls for urgent fiscal reforms favoring redistribution.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes corruption among elites and emphasizes active citizen participation and social equity, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral framing consistent with populism.

chunk 5 · No evidence of populist rhetoric or themes was found in the manifesto text.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing traditional politicians and neoliberal elites for exploiting public resources for the benefit of a few, while advocating for citizen participation.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism, criticizing corrupt elites and political clientelism while advocating for citizen participation and the defense of the common people.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing the traditional political class and corruption, though it lacks strong manichean or people-centric rhetoric.

chunk 5 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist flavour leans mildly left-of-centre: the manifesto calls for redistribution ("distribución justa de la riqueza"), a mixed economy, combating poverty and inequality, and social solidarity, while also invoking national sovereignty; it does not exhibit strong right-populist nativist or anti-immigration themes, nor a purely centrist people-vs-elite framing without programmatic content.

chunk 2 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist in its defense of state control over strategic resources (water, electricity, ports), opposition to neoliberal privatization, and emphasis on redistribution and social rights, but lacks strong right-wing or purely centrist populist framing.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology leans mildly left-center: the manifesto emphasizes redistribution, social equity, support for SMEs and cooperatives, and reducing inequality, while also endorsing market openness and foreign investment, placing it in a center-left reformist space without strong right-populist or nativist elements.

chunk 4 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist with a critique of neoliberal policies and emphasis on social solidarity, equity, and redistribution, but the overall ideological tone is centrist-reformist rather than strongly left-populist.

chunk 5 · The text leans mildly left-populist through emphasis on redistribution-adjacent social protections, gender equality, and minority rights, but lacks strong anti-corporate or nationalist rhetoric, placing it closer to centre-left programmatic politics than populism proper.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text promotes progressive taxation and social equity (left), national sovereignty (right), and broad people-elite participation appeals (centrist), indicating a mixed but moderate populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The text advocates for income redistribution, social equality, and citizen participation while emphasizing national strategic interests, showing a mixed but overall moderate left-leaning populist ideology with centrist elements.

chunk 3 · The text combines left populist calls for tax increases on the wealthy and redistribution with right-leaning protection of national producers and centrist appeals to citizen participation and state-private cooperation.

chunk 4 · The text supports left-leaning social solidarity and equitable pension systems, acknowledges immigrant contributions, and stresses broad citizen participation, indicating a mixed but centrist-leaning populist ideology.

chunk 5 · The manifesto does not present any populist ideological content, left, right, or centrist.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on wealth redistribution, social equity, and public services rather than right-wing identity politics.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing, focusing on anti-neoliberalism, social equality, and protecting public services from private corporate interests.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on wealth redistribution, social justice, and protecting small producers against corporate-led globalization.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing, explicitly criticizing neoliberal policies and advocating for a solidary state.

chunk 5 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on programmatic social and economic policies without populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it strongly endorses democratic institutions, division of powers, rule of law, and media/transparency (lib_political); champions gender equity, minority rights, and non-discrimination (lib_social); supports a mixed but competitive economy with private enterprise and trade (lib_economic); and promotes credit/microfinance access while maintaining public regulatory oversight (lib_financial_market), yielding a moderately high overall liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal signals: it supports transparency, rule of law, and competition in financial markets, but also advocates strong state control over strategic sectors (electricity, water, ports), subsidies, and progressive taxation, yielding a moderate overall liberalism score.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political transparency and rule of law, supports trade openness and market competition, and addresses gender discrimination; however, it also advocates state-directed credit, preferential rates, and state bank intervention in financial markets, producing mixed scores on economic and financial dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly liberal: it strongly supports democratic institutions, rule of law, and civil rights including gender and sexual-orientation equality; it endorses market competition in health services and credit access for marginalized groups, though with a strong state-solidarity framing that moderates economic liberalism.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on social dimensions (explicit recognition of LGBTQ+ rights, anti-discrimination, gender equality, parity democracy) and moderately pro-liberal economically (support for micro-enterprise, entrepreneurship, and development banking), reflecting a broadly liberal-democratic orientation.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, political rights, social equality, free enterprise, and transparency in public function, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, civil rights, market competition, economic stability, and financial system efficiency, reflecting a generally pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including democracy, human rights, social protections, free markets, competitiveness, and prudent financial market regulation enabling productive investment.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, minority rights, cultural diversity, and efficient resource allocation, reflecting a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions, though financial market policy is not addressed.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly supports political rights and anti-discrimination laws, promotes social equality and minority protections, and encourages economic support for microenterprises, reflecting a pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic checks and balances and minority rights, while advocating a mixed economy with regulated markets.

chunk 2 · The text presents a mixed approach to liberalism, supporting democratic accountability and financial competition, but strongly opposing neoliberal privatization of public utilities.

chunk 3 · The text supports democratic transparency and social rights, but advocates for a highly regulated economy with state-directed credit and support for social economy enterprises over pure free markets.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic institutions and minority rights, while advocating a regulated, state-supported economic and financial framework.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports classical liberal values, emphasizing minority rights, gender equality, democratic institutions, and market-enabling support for micro-entrepreneurs and credit access.
