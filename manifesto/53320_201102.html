WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate populist tendencies: it criticises Fianna Fáil as a corrupt, discredited elite and frames Labour as serving "the Irish people," but the rhetoric is largely technocratic and policy-focused rather than deeply moralistic or conspiratorial, keeping overall populism scores in the low-to-mid range.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains mild populist elements — blaming Fianna Fáil elites and developers for Ireland's crisis and invoking local communities — but the rhetoric is predominantly technocratic and policy-focused rather than morally Manichean or strongly people-centric.

chunk 3 · The text contains limited populist rhetoric, primarily blaming Fianna Fáil and bank elites for Ireland's damaged reputation, but the overall tone is policy-oriented and institutional rather than morally charged people-vs-elite framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elite corruption and crony capitalism, emphasizing serving the Irish people and proposing constitutional reform, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral struggle themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto criticizes the Fianna Fáil government as an out-of-touch elite and emphasizes empowering local communities, reflecting low to moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism but lacks strong moral dichotomies.

chunk 3 · The manifesto criticizes economic elites for mismanagement, indicating mild anti-elitism, but lacks strong people-centrism or moralized good-vs-evil framing.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the political establishment as cronyist and calling for accountability to the Irish people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the political and financial establishment as having prioritized developers and banks over ordinary citizens.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate anti-elitism by blaming the economic crisis on the recklessness of banking elites and the political establishment, but lacks broader people-centrist or moralizing populist rhetoric.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is clearly left-leaning populist in flavour: it targets financial elites (bondholders, developers, bankers), advocates redistribution through progressive taxation and a jobs fund, and opposes austerity imposed by discredited elites, with no right-wing nativist or identity-based populist content.

chunk 2 · The manifesto leans left-populist in its emphasis on protecting workers (minimum wage), tackling poverty, expanding public services, and criticising developers and banks, but it is a mainstream centre-left social-democratic programme rather than a strongly ideological populist one.

chunk 3 · The text leans mildly left-populist through support for a Tobin Tax, overseas development aid, social Europe, and redistribution toward developing nations, but lacks strong anti-corporate or class-struggle rhetoric typical of high left-populism scores.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · Labour’s platform includes left-leaning social equality measures like a gay marriage referendum, opposition to privatization, and centrist appeals to constitutional renewal and transparency, indicating a mixed but overall moderate populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The manifesto combines left-wing economic policies like minimum wage increases and anti-poverty measures with centrist appeals to local control and a fair immigration system, showing a mixed but overall moderate populist ideological stance.

chunk 3 · The text supports left-leaning equity in taxation and trade, alongside centrist appeals to restoring Ireland's reputation, reflecting a moderate populist ideological stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing, focusing on fair taxation, social fairness, and protecting citizens from corporate and banking failures.

chunk 2 · The populist tone is combined with a left-wing ideology focused on reversing minimum wage cuts and protecting social welfare.

chunk 3 · The populist critique is directed from a center-left perspective, targeting financial elites and advocating for international transaction taxes.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it strongly supports political freedoms (FOI, whistleblowers, democratic reform), social liberalism (gay marriage referendum), and market competition, while taking a mixed stance on financial markets—calling for stronger prudential regulation and burden-sharing with bondholders rather than pure market liberalisation.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political institutions (independent oversight bodies, rule of law, democratic accountability) and social liberalism (same-sex marriage, LGBT rights, gender equality), moderately pro-liberal on financial markets (prudential regulation enabling market function), but tempered on economic liberalism by explicit rejection of pure market provision of public services.

chunk 3 · The text is broadly pro-liberal across political, social, and economic dimensions — supporting democratic accountability, human rights, low corporate tax, and market reform — while the Tobin Tax proposal and calls for financial regulation introduce some mixed signals on financial liberalism.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal democratic institutions, civil rights, market-friendly economic policies, and prudential financial regulation, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political institutions, social equality including LGBT rights, economic regulation favoring market function, and prudent financial market oversight, indicating a robust liberal orientation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports democratic transparency, social rights, market-friendly economic policies, and financial market reforms, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through institutional transparency and gay marriage rights, while taking a mixed, more interventionist stance on economic and financial markets.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic reforms and same-sex marriage rights, while advocating a regulated, non-pure-market approach to public services and housing.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports human rights, democratic transparency, and economic competitiveness, while advocating for financial market regulation and a Tobin tax.
