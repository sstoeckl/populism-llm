WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames politics as a moral struggle between "the people" (workers, the poor, minorities) and corrupt elites/the "golpista" government, invoking Lula as the virtuous popular leader betrayed by an illegitimate coup, scoring high on all three populism sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently frames the Temer government as a "governo golpista" (coup government) that betrayed the people, invokes the poor and working class as the virtuous core constituency, and constructs a Manichean struggle between the corrupt elite/coup plotters and the Brazilian people.

chunk 3 · The text frames land reform as a struggle against elite domination ("dominação da terra pelas elites políticas e econômicas"), invokes the people as beneficiaries of housing and rural policies, and labels the previous government "golpista," indicating moderate populist framing concentrated in specific passages rather than throughout.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elites and emphasizes the sovereignty and virtue of the people, framing a moral struggle against an elite coalition, demonstrating strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean populism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elites and prioritizes the poor and working class, invoking 'the people' as a central political subject and framing political struggle in moral terms against a 'golpista' government, indicating a populist stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto emphasizes 'garantir moradia digna para o povo' and critiques neoliberal elites, showing strong anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moralized conflict references.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text frames Brazilian politics as a struggle between the virtuous majority of the people and a corrupt, privileged elite that executed a coup.

chunk 2 · The text frames political conflict as a moral struggle between the virtuous 'povo' and the corrupt 'elites' or 'governo golpista'.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the historical land ownership as a domination by political and economic elites and contrasting the needs of 'the people' against a 'coup government'.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly left-populist, emphasizing redistribution of wealth and power, anti-corporate elite rhetoric, workers' rights, social equality, and state-led development, with only residual nationalist/sovereignty framing rather than right-populist identity politics.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is firmly left-leaning, emphasizing redistribution, state-led development, anti-privatization, expansion of social programs, workers' rights, and combating inequality across racial, gender, and class lines, with minimal right-wing nationalist content.

chunk 3 · The text is clearly left-populist: it critiques "capitalismo moderno e excludente," advocates redistribution through agrarian reform, social housing, and family farming support, while rejecting the "projeto neoliberal"; right-wing or nativist themes are minimal and secondary.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates progressive taxation, social equality, and political participation, while opposing right-wing figures and emphasizing broad popular involvement, indicating a left-leaning populist ideology with centrist elements.

chunk 2 · The text emphasizes left-wing populist themes such as redistribution, social protection, and anti-privatization, while also showing some right-leaning nationalist economic protectionism by opposing privatization and foreign control of resources, and centrist appeals through calls for federative cooperation and public service expansion.

chunk 3 · The text supports left-leaning populist ideology through calls for democratization of land access and social participation, while also showing centrist appeals and some right-leaning critiques of the 'golpista' government.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist discourse is strongly left-wing, focusing on wealth redistribution, fighting corporate/financial elites, and defending marginalized social groups.

chunk 2 · The populist discourse is strongly left-wing, focusing on class struggle, redistribution, and protecting the poor from elite austerity.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, explicitly criticizing the 'neoliberal project' for subjecting public goods to market interests and advocating for agrarian reform and state-led redistribution.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political democracy and social/civil rights (LGBTI+, racial equality, women's rights), but explicitly anti-liberal economically (halting privatizations, state-led reindustrialization, banking reform targeting "speculation") and anti-liberal on financial markets (opposing market-based finance in favor of state-directed credit).

chunk 2 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it strongly supports social liberalism (LGBTI+ rights, racial equality, democracy) and political liberalism (democratic institutions), but is anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions, favoring state intervention, public banks, anti-privatization, capital flow regulation, and suppression of financial speculation.

chunk 3 · The text shows mixed liberal signals: it supports participatory democracy and some market mechanisms (credit deregulation, export promotion), but explicitly rejects neoliberalism and advocates strong state intervention in land, environment, and housing, yielding a moderate overall liberalism score.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, civil rights, social equality policies, balanced fiscal reforms, and financial market regulation aimed at enabling market function, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal democratic institutions, civil rights including minority and LGBTI+ protections, economic policies favoring market competition and credit access, and financial market reforms to increase competition and reduce oligopoly, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto promotes political participation, human rights protections, and economic policies favoring deregulation and credit simplification, reflecting a pro-liberal stance except for financial markets which are not discussed.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political democracy and social minority rights, but rejects classical economic liberalism by opposing privatization and advocating state intervention in financial markets.

chunk 2 · The text supports democratic institutions and social rights but advocates for heavy state intervention, bank regulation, and public control over key economic sectors.

chunk 3 · The text shows mixed liberalism, supporting democratic participation and minority rights, but advocating for heavy state intervention, regulation of agribusiness, and state-directed credit over free-market mechanisms.
