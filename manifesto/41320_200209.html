WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows minimal populist features: it frames CDU/CSU as obstructionist opponents and occasionally invokes 'the people' and societal consensus, but the dominant tone is technocratic, centrist, and policy-oriented rather than morally dividing society into virtuous people vs. corrupt elites.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is a detailed SPD government programme focused on policy delivery (East German development, social welfare, healthcare, security), with minimal populist framing: no strong elite-bashing, no moralized good-vs-evil narrative, and only mild people-centric rhetoric in passing references to representing citizens' interests.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly supports a centrist populist approach emphasizing 'Politik der Mitte' and contrasts its policies against the CDU/CSU elite opposition, invoking 'Wir in Deutschland!' as a unifying people-centric appeal, and framing a moral struggle for rule of law over 'Recht des Stärkeren'.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mild anti-elitism and moderate people-centrism through emphasis on representing people's interests and local participation, without strong moral polarization.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text contains virtually no populist rhetoric, focusing instead on mainstream governance, consensus-seeking, and institutional reforms.

chunk 2 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is primarily a centre-left social democratic programme: it emphasises social cohesion, workers' rights, regulated markets, and redistribution, while explicitly rejecting both nationalist right-wing positions and unregulated market liberalism, placing it in a moderate left-populist ideological space.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mild left-leaning populist ideology through solidarity-based social policy and redistribution themes, but explicitly positions itself as centrist ("Politik der Mitte") and combines market competition with social protection, with only marginal right-populist immigration-control language.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text promotes a centrist populist ideology with emphasis on social cohesion, opposing nationalist narrow-mindedness, and advocating a balanced middle path ('Politik der Mitte') addressing social and economic alternatives.

chunk 2 · The text supports left-leaning social equality and gender equality, opposes cultural parallel societies (a right-leaning stance), and includes centrist appeals to people's interests, reflecting a mixed but moderate populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · There is no evidence of a populist ideology, as the text rejects anti-establishment framing and positions itself firmly in the political center.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of a populist ideological framework in the text.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political democracy, civil rights (including same-sex partnerships, disability rights, gender equality), and open international trade, while favouring regulated rather than free markets and calling for coordinated financial market oversight, yielding a moderately pro-liberal overall profile.

chunk 2 · The manifesto scores consistently pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it strongly affirms democracy, rule of law, and media freedom; advances gender equality and LGBTQ+ partnership rights; promotes competitive market structures in East Germany and healthcare; and supports investor/consumer protection measures that enable market-based finance.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses liberal principles including democracy and open culture, civil rights such as recognition of same-sex partnerships, economic liberalism via tax reforms benefiting all income groups, and support for stable, functioning financial markets.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic participation, social equality, economic investment promotion, and improved financial market protections, indicating a clear pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal values, including democratic institutions, minority rights, market competitiveness, and stable financial markets.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including democratic participation, minority and LGBTQ+ rights, market competition, and consumer/investor protection in financial services.
