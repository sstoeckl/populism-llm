📄 Full text: the scored text is the Manifesto Corpus version 2026-1, key 23420_201310, available through the Manifesto Project API (free registration), e.g. in R: manifestoR::mp_corpus(data.frame(party = 23420, date = 201310)). The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist features: the DP positions itself as the voice of ordinary citizens against a failing government, criticises the incumbent for opacity and mismanagement, and frames opponents as acting against future generations, but it does not construct a sharp people-vs-elite moral binary or invoke a virtuous homogeneous people in a sustained way.

chunk 2 · The text shows only mild populist tendencies: the incumbent government is occasionally criticized for past decisions and poor management, but there is no sustained anti-elite rhetoric, no invocation of a virtuous 'people' against corrupt elites, and no Manichean good-vs-evil framing; the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-oriented.

chunk 3 · The text criticises the incumbent government for mismanagement (housing, childcare, energy) but does so in technocratic, policy-oriented language without invoking a virtuous 'people' against corrupt elites or framing politics as a moral struggle, yielding very low populism scores across all sub-dimensions.

chunk 4 · The text shows only mild populist features: it criticises past governance failures and a "patriarchal" governing style, but does not construct a sharp people-vs-elite dichotomy, invoke a unified virtuous people, or frame politics in Manichean moral terms; the dominant register is technocratic-liberal reform.

chunk 5 · The text is a technocratic, reform-oriented manifesto focused on public administration modernisation, cultural policy, and foreign affairs; it invokes citizens and transparency but does not frame elites as corrupt enemies of the people, and the only mildly adversarial framing is mild criticism of the incumbent government's reform process.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The DP's populist appeals are centrist-liberal in character: they invoke citizens against an ineffective incumbent government without left-wing anti-corporate redistribution or right-wing nativist themes; the mild anti-casino-capitalism remark is isolated and does not define the programme's ideological thrust.

chunk 2 · The text's populist elements are minimal and ideologically ambiguous: opposition to 'casino capitalism' and calls for clear rules for all financial actors hint at mild left-leaning populist critique, but the overall program is firmly liberal-centrist with strong pro-market, pro-competition, and pro-financial-sector stances.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows mild centre-left leanings (fighting child poverty, social housing, progressive taxation) combined with a liberal emphasis on free choice and private investment, with only a faint nod to national identity (Luxembourgish language), placing it weakly in the left-centrist space with minimal populist ideological charge.

chunk 4 · The manifesto's populist flavour is weakly centrist-liberal: it appeals to good governance and citizen trust without strong left redistributive or right nativist framing, though minor social-equity and integration concerns are present.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is predominantly a centrist-liberal reform programme with no strong left or right populist ideology; it explicitly rejects nationalism and protectionism, supports market economy and democracy, and its people-centric appeals are generic and technocratic rather than ideologically charged.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 2 · The text does not express any clear left, right, or centrist populist ideological positions.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideological stances, left, right, or centrist, in the manifesto text.

chunk 4 · The text shows left-leaning populist concern for social justice in pensions, a rejection of patriarchal governance (right-leaning), and centrist calls for government unity and effective governance, reflecting a mixed but moderate populist ideological stance.

chunk 5 · There is no evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the text; the manifesto focuses on institutional and policy matters without populist framing.

Gemini

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideology, as the text focuses on mainstream programmatic policy proposals.

chunk 3 · The populist elements are very weak and limited to centrist criticisms of government mismanagement without strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

chunk 4 · There is no clear populist ideology present in the text, as the criticisms of the government are framed within standard democratic accountability rather than a populist left, right, or centrist ideology.

chunk 5 · There is no evidence of populist ideology, as the text focuses on standard liberal-democratic governance and administrative reforms.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions rule of law and transparency (political), gender equality and modern family structures (social), competitive taxation and market-oriented fiscal reform (economic), and investor protection, financial market stability, and opposition to financial transaction taxes (financial market).

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it defends press freedom and data protection (political), equal opportunity and non-discrimination (social), competitiveness and labor market flexibility (economic), and opposes the financial transaction tax while supporting investor protection and clear regulatory frameworks for the financial center (financial market).

chunk 3 · The DP manifesto is consistently pro-liberal: it champions free choice in family models, private investment in housing and energy, market-oriented instruments (climate bank, public-private partnerships), and local democratic autonomy, while opposing excessive state intervention in the property market and calling for prudential but market-enabling financial tools.

chunk 4 · The DP manifesto is strongly liberal across political (separation of powers, judicial independence, democratic rights), social (same-sex marriage, abortion rights, anti-discrimination for trans/intersex persons), and economic (moderate social charges, pension reform avoiding contribution hikes) dimensions, with no discussion of financial-market policy.

chunk 5 · The manifesto consistently champions democracy, rule of law, free trade, open markets, individual rights, minority protections, and market-based development tools such as microfinance, reflecting a strongly pro-liberal orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles such as democracy, rule of law, transparency, social solidarity, responsible economic and financial policies, and market-friendly reforms, with explicit references to institutional trust, fiscal responsibility, and financial market stability.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through press freedom, social liberalism via equality of opportunity, economic liberalism by endorsing a social market economy and opposing casino capitalism, and financial market liberalism by advocating for strong regulatory authorities that enable market function.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal political principles such as free choice in family life, equality of opportunity, and economic support for child care and education, indicating a pro-liberal stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal democratic principles such as separation of powers and fundamental rights, promotes social liberalism through anti-discrimination and inclusion policies, and endorses moderate economic liberalism emphasizing social protections and moderate taxation; financial market policy is not addressed.

chunk 5 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including democracy, rule of law, free markets, and social equality, as evidenced by explicit references to the EU as a democratic community, promotion of civil rights, and support for open market access.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly aligns with classical liberalism, advocating for the rule of law, gender equality, fiscal discipline, market competition, and transparent financial market regulation.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including press freedom, data privacy as a fundamental right, market competitiveness, and clear, non-distortionary rules for the financial sector.

chunk 3 · The text strongly advocates for classical liberal principles, including individual freedom of lifestyle choices, private initiative in the economy, and market-oriented financial instruments like a climate bank.

chunk 4 · The text strongly advocates for classical liberal principles, including the separation of powers, state neutrality in religious matters, individual rights, gender and LGBTQ+ equality, and maintaining moderate social charges to preserve economic competitiveness.

chunk 5 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including democracy, human rights, free trade, private property, and market-based economic development.
