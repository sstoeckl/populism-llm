WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames the Spanish state's political and judicial elites as corrupt heirs of Francoism oppressing the Catalan people, invoking a virtuous popular will against an oligarchic establishment, but the people-centrism is primarily ethno-civic (Catalan citizens) rather than a universal populist appeal, and the Manichean framing is moderate rather than conspiratorial.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently frames the Spanish state as corrupt and controlled by extractive elites ("elits extractives"), portrays the people as virtuous victims of oligarchic rule, and draws a sharp moral contrast between the corrupt Spanish regime and the virtuous Catalan republican project.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly frames Spanish economic elites as corrupt heirs of Francoism who captured the state, while invoking 'all of society' as victims paying for elite misdeeds, with a clear good-vs-evil narrative around oligopolies and revolving doors.

chunk 4 · The text strongly frames Spanish state elites, oligopolies, and corrupt parties (PP/PSOE) as extractive enemies of the Catalan people, invoking a clear people-vs-elite moral struggle while centering the virtuous citizenry as the foundation of the desired Catalan Republic.

chunk 5 · The text shows moderate populist features: elites and oligarchies are framed as controlling the state apparatus against the Catalan people, the people are invoked as a unified virtuous collective ("som un sol poble"), and reactionary forces are cast as enemies of the Catalan school model, but the overall tone is more civic-republican and policy-oriented than strongly populist.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elites as corrupt and out of touch, emphasizes a committed and responsible citizenry, and frames the political struggle as a fight against repression and injustice, demonstrating strong populist elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly denounces corruption and institutional decay, highlights conspiracies against political leaders, and emphasizes the defense of citizens' rights, reflecting strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and a moralized struggle against corrupt powers.

chunk 3 · The text explicitly criticizes economic elites as heirs of Francoism and oligarchic structures, emphasizing a moral struggle against corrupt elites and advocating for the people’s role in health and social systems, reflecting strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and a moralized good-vs-evil framing.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes a corrupt regime and elite interests, emphasizing structural corruption and institutional betrayal, reflecting strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized struggle.

chunk 5 · The manifesto does not explicitly discuss populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, providing no direct evidence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text frames the Spanish state and its '78 regime' as a corrupt, repressive, and out-of-touch elite opposing the democratic and peaceful will of the virtuous Catalan people.

chunk 2 · The text strongly frames the Spanish state institutions and military as corrupt, out of touch, and conspiratorial against political leaders, while championing the virtuous citizens.

chunk 3 · The text frames economic and political elites as a corrupt, extractive oligarchy exploiting the working class.

chunk 4 · The text strongly frames the Spanish state and its political-economic elites as a corrupt, speculative, and wasteful regime that exploits the people and peripheral territories.

chunk 5 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing Spanish state elites and oligarchies as oppressive forces denying Catalan identity and rights.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is predominantly left-leaning, emphasising social justice, workers' interests, redistribution, and anti-corporate elite critique, while explicitly rejecting authoritarian nationalism and welcoming diversity, with no meaningful right-populist nativist content.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it attacks corporate elites, the IBEX oligarchy, and capitalist inequality, advocates wealth redistribution, a universal welfare state, feminist and LGBTI rights, and opposes the far right, with no right-wing nativist or anti-immigration populist content.

chunk 3 · The populist framing is predominantly left-wing, targeting corporate elites, demanding redistribution, progressive taxation, workers' rights, and criticizing capital over labour taxation, with only minor Catalan nationalist identity elements that do not constitute right-wing populism.

chunk 4 · The populism is predominantly left-leaning, emphasizing anti-corporate sentiment, redistribution, workers' rights, cooperative economics, and opposition to the "gran capital," with a secondary Catalan nationalist dimension but no significant right-wing nativist or anti-immigration content.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly self-identifies as left-wing and independentist ("republicanisme independentista, i d'esquerres"), combining social-equality and redistribution themes with Catalan national identity politics; right-wing populist elements (migration control) are present but framed in a humanitarian, not exclusionary, manner.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates for protecting popular classes and social justice (left), opposes the far right, and promotes citizen responsibility without clear centrist programmatic stance, reflecting a left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist elements.

chunk 2 · The text advocates for wealth redistribution and social equality, condemns far-right hate discourses, and promotes republican values, indicating a predominantly left-wing populist ideology with centrist appeals and minor right-wing elements.

chunk 3 · The manifesto combines left-wing populist themes of fiscal justice and anti-corporate privilege with some regionalist/centrist elements about citizen participation and critiques of central state dominance, showing a mixed but predominantly left populist ideology.

chunk 4 · The text supports left-leaning redistribution and labor rights, some right-leaning commercial protectionism, and centrist appeals to citizen participation and governance, indicating a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 5 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the text; the manifesto focuses on institutional, cultural, and social policy without populist framing.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist discourse is strongly left-wing, focusing on protecting the popular classes, fighting gender inequality, and opposing corporate/financial elites, while explicitly rejecting right-wing authoritarian nationalism.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on redistribution of wealth, fighting capitalist speculation, and opposing the economic oligarchy of the IBEX companies.

chunk 3 · The populist discourse is strongly left-wing, focusing on anti-corporate sentiment, tax justice, and redistribution.

chunk 4 · The populist discourse is combined with a clear left-wing ideology focusing on social cohesion, fighting inequality, and opposing big corporate capital and oligopolies.

chunk 5 · The populist ideology is explicitly left-wing, combining Catalan pro-independence sovereignty with left-wing social transformation and anti-oligarchic rhetoric.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly champions democratic rights, rule of law, gender equality, and civil liberties, and endorses free competition and meritocracy while criticising crony capitalism and bank-friendly legislation, yielding a moderately pro-liberal overall profile tempered by statist social-policy commitments.

chunk 2 · The manifesto scores high on social liberalism (strong LGBTI, gender, and religious freedom protections), moderately on political liberalism (independent judiciary, democratic rights, but framed within a secessionist republican project), and lower on economic and financial-market liberalism due to its emphasis on redistribution, progressive taxation, and state welfare expansion over market mechanisms.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-market competition and pro-rule-of-law, advocating free competition, regulatory independence, and consumer protection, but also proposes public banking, bank separation, and significant state intervention, yielding a mixed-to-moderate liberal profile.

chunk 4 · The text shows mixed liberal signals: it defends consumer freedom, fair competition, and democratic institutions, but also advocates strong state intervention in labor markets, energy, housing, and tourism, and frames privatization and liberalization as causes of oligopoly and social harm.

chunk 5 · The manifesto scores moderately liberal overall: it strongly supports democratic deepening, pluralism, minority rights, and anti-discrimination (high lib_political and lib_social), but advocates significant state intervention in energy, housing markets, and financial products (rent controls, mortgage caps), pulling lib_economic and lib_financial_market scores toward the centre-low range.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto critiques the authoritarian tendencies and limitations on political freedoms in Spain, supports gender equality and social rights, promotes economic competitiveness with caution against speculation, and criticizes financial market favoritism, indicating a mixed but overall moderate liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports independent justice, political rights, social equality, wealth redistribution, and corporate accountability, showing a strong commitment to political and social liberalism, moderate economic liberalism, and some support for financial market regulation favoring human rights.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions and rights, social protections, economic entrepreneurship, market facilitation, and financial market competition including public banking, indicating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto advocates for political transparency, social equality, economic competition, and financial market regulation enabling market function, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal political values such as democracy, rights, and pluralism; social liberalism through equal rights and interculturalism; and economic liberalism via sustainable, efficient economic infrastructure planning, though it lacks discussion on financial market policies.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through civil rights, gender equality, and democratic freedoms, and advocates for fair economic competition against crony capitalism, though it remains critical of the financial banking elite.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political liberalism (separation of powers, independent judiciary) and social liberalism (LGBTQ+ and gender equality), but leans interventionist on economic matters like rent controls.

chunk 3 · The text supports democratic accountability and minority rights, but advocates a mixed economic model with strong state intervention alongside market competition.

chunk 4 · The text supports democratic transparency, gender equality, and fair market competition, but advocates for strong public regulation and cooperative credit models rather than pure laissez-faire.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through human rights, democracy, and anti-discrimination, but favors state intervention and price controls in the housing market.
