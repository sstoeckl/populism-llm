WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is a detailed technocratic policy programme (climate, mobility, environment, food, health) with minimal populist framing; the rare anti-elite references (privatised nuclear profits, pharmaceutical lobby) are incidental critiques rather than a sustained people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 2 · The text references specific political scandals implicating "partis traditionnels" and calls for restoring citizen trust in institutions, but the overall framing is reform-oriented and technocratic rather than strongly populist; anti-elitism is present but moderate.

chunk 3 · The text shows only mild populist features: it frames wealth concentration and debt interest as a transfer from taxpayers to capital holders, and criticises "nationalistes-populistes," but it does not systematically invoke a virtuous "people" against corrupt elites, nor does it construct a Manichean moral struggle; the overall tone is technocratic and policy-oriented.

chunk 4 · The text shows only mild populist tendencies: there is a brief framing of justice as favouring the powerful over the weak, and some people-centred rhetoric around public consultations and citizens' rights, but the dominant register is technocratic policy prescription rather than a moralized people-vs-elite struggle.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the nuclear sector's privatized profits versus public costs, advocates citizen partnerships in environmental initiatives, and calls for disinvestment from fossil fuels, indicating a moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral framing consistent with populism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly discusses political scandals undermining trust in elites, calls for citizen participation and transparency, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and some moral conflict framing, thus indicating moderate populism overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly discusses anti-elitism through highlighting tax fraud as a major loss to public finances, people-centrism by emphasizing the burden on taxpayers, and a moral struggle against fiscal injustice, reflecting moderate populist themes.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly critiques national-populist currents as contemptuous and highlights societal demands for a more equal, social, and democratic model, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized conflict.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text displays low overall populism, with only mild anti-elitist framing targeting corporate and nuclear profits at the expense of the public.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing traditional party elites involved in scandals and advocating for direct citizen participation to restore trust.

chunk 3 · The text rejects populism, explicitly positioning itself against the 'fantasies' of nationalist-populists.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits low-to-moderate populism, occasionally framing political and economic elites as acting against the weak or waging war on specific groups like artists.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto leans mildly left-green through emphasis on social solidarity, redistribution of healthcare costs, worker protections, and ecological transition, but frames these primarily as technocratic policy proposals rather than ideological populism; no right-wing or nativist elements are present.

chunk 2 · The manifesto leans left-populist with emphasis on redistribution, social equity, and worker protections, but lacks strong right-populist or nativist framing; centrist people-centric appeals are secondary to a programmatic green-left agenda.

chunk 3 · The manifesto leans modestly left-populist through proposals for progressive taxation, equalising capital and labour income taxation, and a wealth levy on fortunes above €1 million, while explicitly rejecting blind protectionism and distancing itself from right-wing populism; centrist populist appeals are minimal.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is clearly left-leaning in its populist flavour, emphasising redistribution, raising minimum wages and social benefits, fighting poverty, and critiquing neoliberal globalisation, while explicitly condemning right-wing national-populism.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text promotes left-leaning populist ideology through calls for disinvestment from fossil fuels, social and environmental justice, and citizen involvement, with some centrist appeals to partnerships and shared responsibility, and minimal right-wing nationalist elements.

chunk 2 · The text advocates social justice, citizen empowerment, and regional autonomy, showing a left-leaning populist ideology with some right-leaning regional identity elements and centrist people-elite appeals.

chunk 3 · The manifesto advocates left-leaning populist ideology with progressive taxation on wealth and capital, some right-leaning concerns about social and environmental dumping, and centrist appeals to fairness in debt and taxation, indicating a mixed but predominantly left populist stance.

chunk 4 · The text emphasizes a left-leaning populist ideology advocating for social equality and solidarity while acknowledging the rise of right-wing national-populist movements, resulting in a mixed but left-leaning populist ideological stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are clearly left-wing, focusing on the socialization of costs and privatization of profits in the energy sector.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing, focusing on reducing fiscal pressure on low-income workers and taxing capital and wealth.

chunk 3 · The text does not adopt a populist ideology, instead criticizing populist rhetoric on migration.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing, focusing on anti-neoliberalism, inequality, and the protection of social rights against corporate and political elites.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports independent oversight bodies and rule-of-law mechanisms (lib_political), gender-sensitive health policy (lib_social), and prudential financial transparency via CO2 stress tests and divestment (lib_financial_market), while mixing market-enabling and market-substituting economic interventions (mandatory targets, public investment, tax reductions) yielding a mixed lib_economic score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic participation, gender parity, and citizen rights (high lib_political and lib_social), while taking a mixed stance on economic liberalism (regulatory state preferred over free markets) and explicitly criticising financial deregulation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores moderately liberal overall: it strongly defends civil rights, gender equality, and democratic rule of law (high lib_social and lib_political), but proposes significant financial-market regulation (strict bank separation, public ownership of Belfius, zero-rate perpetual debt conversion) and mixed economic liberalism (social/environmental tariffs alongside green investment incentives), pulling the overall score toward the middle.

chunk 4 · The text is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (defending democracy, rule of law, access to justice, gender equality, anti-harassment), mixed on economic liberalism (supports social regulation, public subsidies, minimum wage hikes, but also some market-enabling measures), and moderately mixed on financial markets (supports vulture-fund regulation and mortgage credit access, but also proposes a GAFA tax and criticises speculative private pension funds).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political rights and public policy centrality, social rights to healthcare, economic support for market-based energy investments, and financial market mobilization for sustainable initiatives, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports democratic governance reforms, gender parity, strategic economic planning, and acknowledges financial market failures, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political rights through anti-fraud measures, social liberalism via gender equality and anti-discrimination, economic liberalism by promoting sustainable investments and tax reforms, and financial market liberalism through calls for stricter banking regulation and transparency.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political and social principles, emphasizing access to justice as a democratic pillar and anti-discrimination police training, alongside economic measures favoring employment and social welfare, though it lacks explicit financial market policy discussion.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text shows a mixed approach to liberalism, supporting constitutional rights and tax cuts for green investments, but advocating for heavy state intervention and capital restrictions on fossil fuel investments.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic reforms and anti-discrimination, but is highly skeptical of unregulated economic and financial markets.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports social and political liberalism through anti-discrimination and democratic norms, but advocates for heavy state intervention and regulation in economic and financial markets.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic access to justice and gender equality, but opposes economic and financial liberalism by advocating for market regulations and criticizing private pensions.
