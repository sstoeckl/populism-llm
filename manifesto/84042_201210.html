WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames the 2012 elections as a moral struggle of the Georgian people against a "politically and morally bankrupt ruling regime," invoking the people as the sole legitimate source of law while condemning the elite for self-serving authoritarian rule, yielding moderate-to-high populism scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto moderately invokes populist themes by framing the incumbent government as corrupt and serving narrow elite interests ("ელიტარული კორუფცია," high-ranking officials' personal interests), while positioning the bloc as champion of the people's welfare, but the overall tone is programmatic and technocratic rather than strongly Manichean.

chunk 3 · The manifesto exhibits moderate populism: it frames the incumbent government as corrupt and colluding with privileged business circles at the expense of ordinary consumers and small businesses, and invokes a Manichean contrast between elite-connected oligopolists and the broader population, but stops short of a fully moralized good-vs-evil narrative or strong people-as-sovereign rhetoric.

chunk 4 · The text criticises the ruling government for manipulating public opinion, avoiding accountability, politicising the military and education systems, and suppressing civil society, but these critiques are institutional and reform-oriented rather than strongly Manichean or people-centric, yielding a moderate populism score.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the ruling regime as corrupt and power-hungry, emphasizes the unity and majority will of the people, and frames the political struggle as a moral choice between freedom and dictatorship, demonstrating clear populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the oligopolistic economic model controlled by an authoritarian elite, highlighting elite corruption and social grievances, indicating a low populist anti-elitism and people-centrism stance with some moralized critique of the ruling power's actions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elite corruption and cronyism benefiting ruling party officials, indicating a low anti-elitism score and overall weak populism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto criticizes the ruling authorities for manipulating public opinion and shielding themselves from responsibility, while emphasizing peaceful coexistence of ethnic groups and highlighting Russian propaganda and conflict tensions, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict framing.

Gemini

chunk 2 · The text strongly criticizes the ruling elite for autocratic decision-making and elite corruption while positioning the coalition as the true representative of the public interest.

chunk 3 · The text strongly criticizes the ruling elite for corruption, elite cartels, and ignoring the needs of ordinary citizens and small businesses.

chunk 4 · The text strongly criticizes the ruling elite for manipulating public opinion, establishing a police-like regime in schools, and acting in an authoritarian manner, while positioning the coalition's policies as directly aligned with the interests of the Georgian public.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The coalition's populist appeals are primarily centrist-democratic (rule of law, elections, checks and balances) with a mild left-leaning social dimension (social justice, minority rights) and minimal right-wing identity content, placing the overall ideological flavor as centrist-liberal rather than strongly left or right populist.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is primarily left-leaning, emphasizing social justice, poverty eradication, redistribution, and workers' rights, combined with moderate sovereignty/national interest rhetoric, but without strong nativist or anti-immigration content; centrist pragmatism is also present.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is mixed-left-leaning: the manifesto emphasizes poverty reduction, employment, and support for small and medium enterprises as tools of social equity, while also invoking domestic production protection and national economic self-sufficiency, without a strong nativist or identity-based right-wing frame.

chunk 4 · The manifesto (Georgian Dream bloc, 2012) blends mild centre-left themes (equality of opportunity, social guarantees, redistribution via education funding) with civic-national concerns (Georgian language protection, territorial integrity) and a strong centrist reform agenda, without a dominant left or right populist ideological flavour.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-leaning social justice and economic development goals with centrist appeals to democratic principles and some respect for minority rights, reflecting a mixed but overall moderate populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The text combines left-leaning social justice and anti-inequality goals with pragmatic centrist realism in foreign policy, and some right-leaning authoritarian critiques, reflecting a mixed but overall moderate populist ideological stance.

chunk 3 · The text combines concern for social inequality and poverty reduction with national economic protection and generic people-elite divides, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance with left, right, and centrist elements.

chunk 4 · The text shows a left-leaning populist stance through criticism of political manipulation and police-like control in education, a right-leaning stance in highlighting Russian aggression and national security threats, and a centrist populist approach in advocating peaceful conflict resolution aligned with international community policies.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist appeal is centrist, focusing on broad democratic restoration and representing the majority without strong left-wing or right-wing populist exclusions.

chunk 2 · The populist rhetoric has a left-leaning economic flavor, attacking oligopolistic models and market fundamentalism for impoverishing the population.

chunk 3 · The populist framing is centrist, focusing on protecting small and medium enterprises and local farmers against corrupt state-aligned monopolies without a strong left-right ideological polarization.

chunk 4 · The populist rhetoric is centrist, focusing on democratic restoration, anti-authoritarianism, and national unity without strong left-wing economic redistribution or right-wing anti-immigrant themes.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses liberal-democratic institutions—electoral democracy, media freedom, judicial independence, rule of law, minority rights, and free-market economics—while explicitly committing to end authoritarian rule and protect individual constitutional rights, yielding high liberalism scores across political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly endorses free-market principles, media independence, rule of law, democratic institutions, and EU/NATO integration, while also advocating significant state intervention in social policy and agriculture, producing a mixed but overall moderately pro-liberal stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is moderately pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it calls for free competition, an independent judiciary, anti-monopoly legislation, open trade with the EU and US, competitive banking, and investor protections, while also proposing some state interventions (agricultural funds, SME support funds) that temper its overall liberal score.

chunk 4 · The manifesto consistently advocates democratic institution-building, fair elections, parliamentary oversight, civil rights, human rights mechanisms, and mixed public-private financing, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal orientation across political, social, and economic dimensions, while financial-market policy is entirely absent.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal democratic institutions, rule of law, minority rights, market economy principles, and financial property rights, indicating a robust pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, human rights, and rule of law, endorses free market principles with social justice considerations, and discusses financial market credit issues, indicating a generally pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports rule of law reforms, social improvements, free competition, and financial market regulation favoring market functioning, indicating moderate liberalism overall.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports liberal political principles by advocating for improved parliamentary oversight and transparency, liberal social policies ensuring equal educational access, moderate economic liberalism through tax incentives for cultural investment, but lacks evidence on financial market policies.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly advocates for liberal-democratic institutions, minority rights, and free-market principles to replace authoritarian rule.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly endorses classical liberal principles, including the rule of law, media independence, and a free-market economy, while rejecting pseudo-libertarianism.

chunk 3 · The text advocates for classical liberal principles, including an independent judiciary, property rights protection, free market competition, and transparent financial regulations.

chunk 4 · The text advocates for strong liberal-democratic reforms, including parliamentary oversight, institutional checks and balances, gender equality, and tax incentives to encourage private investment.
