🚧 PREVIEW / DRAFT · Working paper, not peer-reviewed. Numbers and methodology may change. Do not cite — sebastian.stoeckl@uni.li.

Country Netherlands · Party D’66 (Democrats‘66) · Date March 2017 · Party family Liberal · Tokens 48,726.75 · Manifesto id 22330_201703

📄 Full text: the scored text is the Manifesto Corpus version 2026-1, key 22330_201703, available through the Manifesto Project API (free registration), e.g. in R: manifestoR::mp_corpus(data.frame(party = 22330, date = 201703)). The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows minimal populist features: it briefly notes declining trust in government and invokes 'all Dutch people' collectively, but explicitly positions itself against populists and frames challenges in technocratic, policy-oriented terms rather than through elite-versus-people antagonism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is a detailed, technocratic policy programme from D66 with virtually no populist rhetoric: there is no sustained anti-elite framing, no invocation of a unified virtuous 'people', and no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative; the few references to challenging incumbents (e.g. banks) are framed in pro-competition, liberal terms rather than populist ones.

chunk 3 · The manifesto contains only faint populist traces — a brief reference to "outsiders" versus a "small, powerful group of insiders" and mild criticism of conservative establishment parties — but the dominant register is technocratic, policy-oriented, and pro-institutional, with no sustained people-vs-elite framing or Manichean moral narrative.

chunk 4 · The text shows minimal populist features: it occasionally references patient interests and criticises pharmaceutical industry influence, but frames these in technocratic, policy-oriented language rather than as a moral struggle between 'the people' and corrupt elites.

chunk 5 · The text explicitly positions itself against populism and intolerance, shows mild criticism of political elites (false promises, 'regenteske reflexen'), and advocates citizen empowerment through institutional reform rather than people-vs-elite framing; overall populism is very low.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto expresses distrust in political elites but emphasizes a society of free people working together, with some moral framing against populists, indicating moderate populist elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains explicit references to politicians as a distinct group and emphasizes connection with 'the people', showing mild anti-elitism and people-centrism, with some moral framing of political attitudes as risky or harmful.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes a small, powerful elite and emphasizes the importance of an inclusive society, reflecting anti-elitism and people-centrism, while also condemning hate and discrimination, indicating a mild moralistic good-vs-evil framing.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the influence of pharmaceutical companies on doctors and patient organizations, indicating anti-elitism, while emphasizing patient rights and decentralization, reflecting people-centrism and centrist populist appeals.

chunk 5 · The manifesto criticizes political elites with phrases like 'Warrige politieke compromissen, regenteske reflexen' indicating anti-elitism, emphasizes trust in people's own strength, and lacks strong moral dichotomy rhetoric, resulting in low populism scores overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text contains very low levels of populism, focusing on institutional reform and restoring trust rather than framing politics as a moral struggle between a corrupt elite and pure people.

chunk 2 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

chunk 3 · The text contains very minimal populist rhetoric, only briefly criticizing a powerful group of insiders in favor of outsiders.

chunk 4 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

chunk 5 · The text contains very minor populist elements, explicitly positioning itself against populism while criticizing 'regents' and political broken promises to advocate for democratic renewal.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto's weak populist signals lean slightly left (addressing inequality, tweedeling, redistribution through education and labour market reform) with virtually no right-populist identity or nativist framing, and only generic centrist appeals to fairness and pragmatic governance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows no right-wing populist ideology (no nativism or anti-immigration framing); mild left-leaning elements appear in redistribution and labour-income equity concerns, but the dominant flavour is centrist liberal-progressive, focused on innovation, market competition, and individual choice rather than class conflict or identity politics.

chunk 3 · The minimal populist flavour is centrist-liberal rather than left or right populist: appeals to "outsiders" are framed in terms of equal opportunity and institutional reform, not anti-corporate redistribution or nativist identity politics, and there is no evidence of right-wing populist ideology.

chunk 4 · The very limited populist signals lean slightly left (e.g., university profits returning to society) and centrist (patient-centred appeals), with no right-populist identity or anti-immigration framing whatsoever.

chunk 5 · The text is explicitly social-liberal (D66), explicitly anti-populist, with mild concern for inequality and some border-management language; it does not fit a strong left or right populist ideology.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-leaning social justice and economic renewal themes with centrist pro-European integration and controlled immigration policies, reflecting a mixed but overall moderate populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The text promotes environmental responsibility and social justice, emphasizing that polluters should pay and advocating for fairness and opportunity, reflecting a centrist-left populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The text shows a left-leaning populist ideology through emphasis on social equality, anti-discrimination, and inclusion, with some centrist appeals to unity and openness, and a rejection of right-wing exclusionary attitudes.

chunk 4 · The text supports redistribution and social equity through university licensing reforms and patient-centered care, indicating left-leaning populist ideology, combined with centrist appeals to patient rights and transparency.

chunk 5 · The text promotes left-leaning environmental and social justice policies, emphasizes individual empowerment without clear right-wing nationalism, and balances centrist appeals to people's own strength, reflecting a moderate left-populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text explicitly positions itself against populists and lacks left-wing or right-wing populist ideology, focusing instead on centrist, progressive-liberal reforms.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on mainstream socio-economic and environmental policies.

chunk 3 · The minor populist framing is centrist, focusing on reform and opening up systems rather than left-wing redistribution or right-wing nativism.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on mainstream progressive-liberal policy proposals.

chunk 5 · The mild anti-establishment critique is centrist and focused on institutional reform rather than left-wing or right-wing populist ideologies.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · D66's manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic institutions and independent media, explicitly endorses gender and LGBTQ+ equality and self-determination, promotes deregulation and market competition for green growth, and supports market-based investment while proposing a public investment bank to crowd in private capital.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democracy and the rule of law, actively combats discrimination and promotes gender/LGBTQ+ equality, advocates free markets, competition, deregulation and entrepreneurship, and explicitly calls for more competition in the financial sector to challenge incumbent banks.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions free internal markets, trade openness, competition, and deregulation (economic); banking competition, FinTech entry, prudential risk-pricing, and a banking union (financial); democratic accountability, rule of law, and independent oversight (political); and full equality for LGBTQ+ persons, gender parity, and anti-discrimination measures (social).

chunk 4 · The text is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic institutions, LGBTQ+ equality and individual autonomy, market competition and deregulation in healthcare, and allows private capital in innovative care while promoting transparency and competition in financial/market arrangements.

chunk 5 · The text is strongly liberal across all dimensions: it champions rule of law, democracy, checks and balances, civil rights for minorities and LGBTQ+, free trade, and access to financial instruments, while opposing tax evasion and advocating transparent markets.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights, market-driven economic policies, and prudent financial market interventions, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, social equality, free markets with environmental responsibility, and financial market innovation through a public investment bank, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political rights, media freedom, social diversity and equality, economic openness, and financial market stability and reform, as evidenced by multiple explicit policy commitments.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports political rights, social equality, economic innovation, and financial market transparency in healthcare, demonstrating a robust liberal stance across all sub-dimensions.

chunk 5 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal democratic institutions, human rights, free trade, and financial inclusion, with explicit references to democracy, rights, trade openness, and financial instruments, indicating a high pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports classical liberal values, including media freedom, minority rights, gender equality, and market-oriented economic policies supported by smart state frameworks.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including market competition, anti-discrimination, the rule of law, and financial sector diversification.

chunk 3 · The text strongly advocates for classical liberal principles, including market competition, reducing regulatory burdens, strengthening the rule of law, and protecting minority rights.

chunk 4 · The text strongly champions classical liberal values, including individual self-determination, civil rights, and market-oriented reforms with regulatory oversight.

chunk 5 · The text strongly champions classical and social-liberal values, emphasizing the rule of law, human rights, open markets, and access to financial instruments.
