WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The text shows mild populist features: the federal government is occasionally framed as dismissive of entrepreneurs and workers, and workers/entrepreneurs are invoked as the virtuous backbone of society, but the rhetoric is primarily technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

chunk 2 · The text contains moderate anti-elitist rhetoric directed at the Di Rupo government and federal parties, framing them as fiscally irresponsible, but this is largely policy critique rather than deep populist moralization; people-centrism is present through Flemish community identity appeals, and Manichean framing is mild.

chunk 3 · The text shows mild populist tendencies through appeals to citizens not getting the policy they vote for and framing the government as serving the people, but it is primarily a detailed policy programme without strong elite-bashing or Manichean framing.

chunk 4 · The text contains moderate populist elements: it criticises political elites for patronage appointments ("koehandel rond politieke benoemingen"), portrays traditional parties as self-serving, and frames the Flemish people as victims of an unjust federal system, but the dominant register is technocratic-institutional reform rather than a sharp people-vs-elite moral narrative.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly encourages rewarding and supporting working and entrepreneurial people while criticizing the government for discouraging them, reflecting a mild anti-elitist and people-centric populist stance with some moral framing about misuse of social security.

chunk 2 · The manifesto criticizes the federal government as corrupt or ineffective, emphasizing fiscal responsibility and accountability, reflecting anti-elitism and people-centrism with a moralized critique of political leadership.

chunk 3 · The manifesto expresses distrust towards federal elites and emphasizes government serving the people and businesses, reflecting low anti-elitism and moderate people-centrism and manichean framing.

chunk 4 · The manifesto criticizes the 'politieke carrousel van de federale diplomatie' indicating anti-elitism and moral critique, while emphasizing focus on 'de burger' showing people-centrism, and framing political appointments as a corrupt cycle reflecting a manichean struggle.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits very low populism, with only minor anti-elitist criticism directed at political interference in state-owned enterprises.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the federal government's failures and contrasting them with the needs of the working and saving population.

chunk 3 · The text does not contain populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil struggles.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the traditional political elite's 'appointment carousel' and 'horse-trading' while advocating for policies rooted in the citizens' will.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The text leans mildly right-populist: it emphasises Flemish identity, mandatory Dutch-language tests for non-native job-seekers, and a strong pro-market/anti-welfare-dependency stance, with no significant left-populist anti-corporate framing.

chunk 2 · The dominant populist ideological flavor is right-leaning, centered on restrictive immigration and integration policies (no collective regularizations, stricter asylum, identity-based community cohesion), with only minor left-populist elements (raising minimum benefits to poverty line).

chunk 3 · The manifesto blends centre-right and Flemish nationalist themes — stricter integration/language conditions for social housing and a confederalist agenda — with some market-liberal economic proposals, placing it primarily in the right-populist ideological space at moderate intensity.

chunk 4 · The populist flavour is predominantly right-leaning: the text centres on Flemish identity, opposition to inter-regional transfers, confederalism, and references to migration/asylum policy, while anti-corporate or redistributive left-populist themes are essentially absent.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-leaning social welfare support with right-leaning emphasis on active migration control and centrist appeals to responsibility and work ethic, showing a mixed but overall moderate populist ideological flavor.

chunk 2 · The text combines left-leaning concerns about tax burden and social justice with right-leaning emphasis on migration control and cultural integration, alongside centrist calls for equal opportunity and rule of law, indicating a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate left populist elements in industrial competitiveness and environmental compensation, right populist elements in regional autonomy and skepticism about federal policies, and centrist populism in general people-empowerment rhetoric.

chunk 4 · The text supports right-leaning populist ideology by emphasizing regional ownership of powers (Vlaanderen and Wallonië), while also showing centrist populist appeals through subsidiarity and some left-leaning concern about social dumping and fraud.

Gemini

chunk 1 · There is no strong populist ideology present, only a mild centrist critique of political interference in public management.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, focusing on strict integration, language requirements, and limiting passive migration.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideology in the provided text.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology leans rightward, emphasizing regional Flemish autonomy, strict migration controls, and linking international aid to the repatriation of undocumented migrants.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across economic and financial dimensions, advocating lower taxes, labour-cost reductions, deregulation, privatisation of state enterprises, independent market regulators, and easier access to capital, while also supporting inclusion of persons with disabilities and independent regulatory institutions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it advocates lower taxes, fiscal discipline, free-market competition, anti-discrimination and LGBTQ+ rights, democratic procedures, and prudential concern about financial market vulnerabilities, reflecting a classical liberal/ordo-liberal orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 3 · The text consistently favours free markets (opposing an energy purchasing cartel as contrary to the "vrijemarktprincipe"), a fully liberalised energy market, parliamentary oversight, and rule of law, while also including some state intervention in social housing and energy, yielding a moderately pro-liberal overall score.

chunk 4 · The text is broadly pro-liberal: it champions the EU internal market, calls for restoring financial market confidence, advocates abolishing compulsory voting and strengthening democratic checks, and promotes diversity policy, though its confederalist and subsidiarity-focused institutional agenda introduces some mixed signals on political liberalism.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism (rights and rule of law), social liberalism (inclusion of disabled persons), economic liberalism (market competition and lowering labor costs), and financial market liberalism (encouraging capital formation), indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, equal rights, simplified and strictly enforced fiscal regulations, market-friendly economic policies, and prudent financial market management, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, social policies on environment and noise, free market energy policies, and military cooperation, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall, though financial market policy is not addressed.

chunk 4 · The manifesto advocates for trust in political institutions, modern diversity policies, reduction of administrative burdens on businesses, and responsible financial market behavior, reflecting a moderate pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal economic principles, including free markets, deregulation, privatization, and reducing the tax burden on labor and enterprise.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including individual rights, private initiative, and fiscal discipline, while maintaining a regulated social framework.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits strong classical liberal economic views supporting free markets, balanced by more restrictive social integration requirements.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including the rule of law, democratic institutions, a functioning market economy, and prudential financial oversight through a European banking union.
