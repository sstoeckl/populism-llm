WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies through its framing of entrenched parliamentary privileges as corrupt and its appeal to "fellow citizens," but the dominant mode is technocratic policy delivery rather than a moralized people-vs-elite struggle.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows limited populist features: it frames construction unions and "강성 택배노조" as corrupt vested interests harming ordinary workers and citizens, and criticises the previous Moon Jae-in government's "ideologically driven" energy policy, but these are targeted critiques rather than a sustained people-vs-elite narrative; the overall tone is technocratic and policy-focused.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies: it criticizes the Moon Jae-in government's policies as producing "dismal results" and frames itself as delivering tailored promises directly to the people ("맞춤 공약 배달합니다"), but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly anti-elite or Manichean.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows minimal populist characteristics; it uses service-delivery framing ("맞춤 공약 배달합니다") and addresses specific vulnerable groups without invoking a unified virtuous people against corrupt elites. The closest populist element is mild criticism of unfair practices targeting desperate youth job-seekers, but this is framed as a regulatory problem rather than an elite-vs-people struggle.

chunk 4 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist flavor is weakly centrist-to-mildly-left, combining welfare expansion for ordinary workers and small businesses with market-friendly deregulation and a conservative national security stance, without a strong ideological anchor in either left or right populism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto leans mildly right-populist in its anti-union rhetoric (cracking down on illegal strikes, construction union abuses) and pro-business deregulation stance, while offering some targeted support for small business owners; there is no significant left-populist redistribution framing.

chunk 3 · The manifesto leans mildly right-populist: it emphasizes the US-Korea alliance, North Korean denuclearization, national security, and conservative governance critiques of the Moon administration, while also incorporating some centrist public-service framing. Left-populist redistribution themes are minimal.

chunk 4 · The manifesto blends moderate left-leaning welfare expansion (support for single-parent families, vulnerable groups, youth housing subsidies) with some right-leaning elements (North Korean human rights advocacy, national security framing), but neither dominates; the overall tone is centrist technocratic policy delivery without strong ideological populist flavor.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text supports left-leaning populist themes like support for middle and lower classes, while also emphasizing national identity and security, and generic people-elite appeals, reflecting a centrist populist ideology overall.

chunk 2 · The text shows a moderate populist ideology with left-leaning support for small businesses and social support, right-leaning criticism of union elites, and centrist appeals to dispute resolution and fairness, indicating a mixed but moderate populist ideological flavor.

chunk 3 · There is no explicit discussion of left, right, or centrist populist ideological themes in the manifesto, thus no evidence of populist ideology.

chunk 4 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the manifesto text, thus no populist ideology is detected.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist appeal is centrist, focusing on anti-privilege political reform and citizen-centric governance without strong left-wing or right-wing ideological framing.

chunk 2 · The populist rhetoric targets militant labor unions and strikes, aligning with a right-wing law-and-order stance.

chunk 3 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on administrative overreach without strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence of populist ideology, as the text focuses entirely on programmatic policy proposals without populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it supports deregulation and regulatory sandboxes (economic), abolition of the financial investment income tax and deposit protection expansion (financial market), constitutional rights and due process (political), and balanced protection of teachers' and students' rights (social), though state intervention in welfare and care is also prominent.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal economically, emphasising deregulation, investment promotion, free-market competition (e.g., abolishing the handset distribution law to spur competition), and prudential financial support for SMEs; it also supports parliamentary accountability through regulatory impact assessments, though social liberalism is entirely absent from the text.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is moderately liberal: it upholds constitutional rights and due process (헌법 제27조), promotes deregulation zones for urban development, respects private autonomy in rental contracts, and supports digital inclusion as a universal right, while also expanding public healthcare and welfare services in ways that partially constrain pure market logic.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it supports parliamentary oversight mechanisms and rule-based governance (lib_political), extensive protections for disabled persons, multicultural families, and gender-specific health rights (lib_social), market-enabling green industry and startup support (lib_economic), and new financial instruments like green bonds and emissions-linked ETFs that expand market-based finance (lib_financial_market).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto contains verified support for political rights expansion, social equality in education and safety, economic liberalization including tax reforms, and financial market regulation enabling investment and protection, indicating a generally pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly discusses political liberalism through regulatory reform and legislative quality, social liberalism via social welfare and healthcare equity, economic liberalism through policy finance support, and financial market liberalism by promoting venture capital and easing investment regulations, supporting an overall pro-liberal stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through expansion of rights and judicial guarantees, social liberalism via emphasis on equality and anti-discrimination, and economic liberalism by advocating regulatory easing and agricultural support; however, it lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles including political rights and institutions, social minority protections, economic market mechanisms, and financial market facilitation, as evidenced by references to human rights reports, disability media access, emissions trading schemes, and green finance initiatives.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports classical liberal principles by advocating for deregulation, tax relief, financial market liberalization, and constitutional rights.

chunk 2 · The text strongly advocates for deregulation, investment promotion, and market-enabling financial reforms.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing private autonomy in contracts and constitutional judicial rights.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports liberal principles through human rights advocacy, minority protection, market-based emissions trading, and green financial market development.
