WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto (SYRIZA, Greece 2012) strongly invokes 'the people' as a virtuous collective against a corrupt two-party establishment and international financial interests, frames the memorandum policies as a 'deadly experiment' against the Greek people, and calls for a broad social alliance of workers and youth against elite-driven destruction.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits very high populism: SYRIZA consistently frames the Greek people as virtuous victims of a corrupt political-economic establishment (PASOK-ND leadership, capital forces), uses moralized good-vs-evil language ("criminal responsibilities," "barbaric attack"), and centers all policy around the survival and will of the Greek people against memorandum-imposed destruction.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populism: it frames Greek foreign policy as captured by powerful allies (USA/NATO/Israel) and imperialist centres, invokes the Greek people as a vigilant collective actor, and frames the struggle against dominant economic interests in moral terms, though the primary focus is policy rather than a pure people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits moderate populism: it frames large business interests and the political establishment as corrupt exploiters of public wealth (anti-elitism), invokes the broad social majority and local communities as the virtuous collective (people-centrism), and frames the struggle against dominant economic interests in moralized terms, though without extreme Manichean rhetoric.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populism is firmly left-wing: the text explicitly identifies SYRIZA as 'the Left of workers and the non-privileged,' calls for redistribution from wealth and high incomes, public ownership of banks, abolition of neoliberal structural reforms, and frames the struggle in class terms against a bourgeois political-economic establishment.

chunk 2 · The populism is overwhelmingly left-wing: the text explicitly invokes redistribution against capital ("αναδιανομής του πλούτου υπέρ των δυνάμεων του κεφαλαίου"), a "socialist horizon," abolition of exploitation, public universal social insurance, and anti-neoliberal framing throughout; right-wing national-identity elements are minimal and secondary.

chunk 3 · The text is clearly left-populist: it explicitly endorses anti-neoliberal and anti-capitalist directions (citing Latin America), calls for abolition of privatisation laws, state ownership of defence industry, and frames capital markets and corporate interests as enemies of the people and environment.

chunk 4 · The text is clearly left-populist: it explicitly identifies as radical left (ΣΥΡΙΖΑ/ΕΚΜ), calls for redistribution of wealth, opposes privatization, champions public goods, and frames neoliberalism as the enemy; right-wing or centrist populist elements are essentially absent.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly anti-liberal economically: it advocates public ownership of recapitalized banks, state-directed investment, freezing of wage cuts, and rejection of neoliberal structural reforms; it shows mixed political liberalism (supporting direct democracy and rule of law while attacking existing institutions); social liberalism is weakly present through equality and solidarity values.

chunk 2 · The text is predominantly anti-liberal economically, explicitly rejecting "neoliberal" policies, advocating state-led redistribution, public ownership of health/education/insurance, and re-regulation of labor markets; it shows mixed political liberalism (pro-democracy/transparency but also majoritarian people-power rhetoric), and limited social liberalism (human rights references without detailed civil-rights agenda).

chunk 3 · The text scores mixed-to-low on liberalism overall: it supports human rights and democratic principles (moderately pro-liberal politically and socially) but is explicitly anti-neoliberal economically, calls for abolition of privatisation measures, state control of key industries, and rejects market mechanisms for carbon trading, yielding low scores on economic and financial-market liberalism.

chunk 4 · The text is broadly anti-liberal economically, opposing privatization of waste management, water services, and public assets, and calling for state control of energy pricing and banking; it shows moderate support for democratic participation and non-discrimination, but overall scores low on classical liberalism due to strong statist and anti-market positions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports democratic rights, human rights, and international cooperation, opposes military alliances like NATO, and promotes social equality and environmental responsibility, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports democratic electoral processes, social rights, local government empowerment, and criticizes financial sector practices, indicating a moderate pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · While supporting democratic institutions and social equality, the text strongly rejects classical liberal economic principles, advocating for state-controlled banking, heavy taxation of wealth, and rejection of market-based structural reforms.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political democracy and social rights, but rejects economic liberalism and market-based financial mechanisms in favor of state intervention.

chunk 3 · The text shows a mixed stance on liberalism, strongly supporting political democracy and human rights while rejecting free-market economics and financial market mechanisms.

chunk 4 · While supporting democratic procedures and minority inclusion, the text strongly rejects market mechanisms, privatization, and financial autonomy in favor of state control.
