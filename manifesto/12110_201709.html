WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows minimal populist features: it briefly acknowledges popular resistance to economic policy and invokes collective ownership ("Fondet eies av alle nordmenn"), but frames politics in technocratic, ecological, and solidaristic terms rather than a people-vs-elite struggle.

chunk 2 · The text shows mild populist tendencies through occasional references to "the people" owning national resources and criticism of multinational tax avoidance, but the overall tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than moralized people-vs-elite framing.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows minimal populist features: it occasionally frames private profit in public welfare as problematic and invokes universal access for "all," but it does not construct a moralized people-vs-elite narrative, relying instead on policy-oriented, pluralist language throughout.

chunk 4 · The text shows minimal populist features: it occasionally references "folk flest" and a "folkelig forankret forsvar," but the dominant tone is policy-oriented, rights-based, and institutionalist, with no sustained elite-bashing or Manichean framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly discusses anti-elitism through calls to combat concentration of power and promote local democracy, people-centrism via mobilizing a global green movement, and a mild manichean framing by highlighting ecological limits and threats from resource overuse.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows moderate anti-elitism through criticism of multinational tax avoidance, emphasizes people-centrism by framing the pension fund as owned by all Norwegians, and presents a mild moral struggle against the oil industry, resulting in a moderate overall populism score.

chunk 3 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles.

chunk 4 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist through redistribution, progressive taxation, and anti-inequality measures, but explicitly rejects narrow nationalism and frames solidarity globally, making right-populist ideology nearly absent.

chunk 2 · The manifesto leans left-populist through redistribution, progressive taxation, and anti-corporate tax avoidance rhetoric, but is primarily an ecological/green party platform rather than a strongly ideological left-populist one; right-populist elements are minimal and limited to agricultural protectionism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist through calls for reduced working hours, strong public welfare, redistribution, and worker protections, but lacks strong anti-corporate or anti-immigration rhetoric; it is primarily a green-progressive policy platform rather than a populist ideological manifesto.

chunk 4 · The text leans mildly left-populist through calls for redistribution, global solidarity, and reducing inequality, but lacks strong anti-corporate or anti-elite rhetoric; right-populist or centrist-populist elements are negligible.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports left-leaning populist ideology with emphasis on redistribution, environmental justice, and social equality, while also showing centrist populist appeals to broad people mobilization without clear right-wing nationalist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports left-leaning populist ideology with progressive taxation and redistribution, includes some right-leaning nationalist elements regarding state ownership and tax sovereignty, and maintains centrist populist appeals to collective ownership, leading to a moderate overall populist ideology score.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideological stances, left, right, or centrist, in the manifesto text.

chunk 4 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances, left, right, or centrist, in the manifesto text provided.

Gemini

chunk 1 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on green politics, ecological sustainability, and democratic reform without populist framing.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is moderately left-leaning, focusing on reducing economic inequality and taxing high incomes and multinational corporations.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideology, as the text focuses on green, social-democratic, and liberal-democratic policy proposals without populist framing.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on green, internationalist, and liberal-democratic policies without populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism (free media, democracy, minority rights, whistleblower protections) and social liberalism (indigenous/minority rights, anti-discrimination), but takes a mixed-to-restrictive stance on economic liberalism (heavy regulation, state ownership, financial transaction taxes) and is moderately anti-liberal on financial markets.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal signals: it supports democratic transparency and pluralism (pro-liberal), but advocates heavy state intervention in the economy, progressive taxation, financial transaction taxes, and capital controls on the sovereign wealth fund, placing it in the moderate range overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly liberal on social dimensions (anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ rights, religious freedom, minority protections) and politically liberal (democracy, rule of law, individual rights), while being moderately mixed on economic liberalism—supporting free competition in some areas but also strong public provision and labor regulation.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across political and social dimensions—emphasizing rule of law, individual rights, due process, LGBTQ+ asylum protections, and privacy—while showing mixed economic liberalism (supporting labour market openness but opposing social dumping) and moderate support for financial transparency.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses liberal principles including political democracy, social inclusion, economic regulation favoring market incentives and redistribution, and prudent financial market management through ethical investment and divestment from fossil fuels.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal political institutions with emphasis on democracy and environmental protection, liberal social policies on education and equality, liberal economic policies favoring progressive taxation and market incentives, and liberal financial market transparency and anti-tax avoidance measures, resulting in a high overall liberalism score.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political rights, anti-discrimination, social equality, free education, and economic policies favoring investment and innovation over speculation, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political principles such as rule of law and individual rights, social liberal values including human rights and personal freedoms, and economic liberalism through support for predictable labor market regulations; however, it lacks explicit discussion of financial market policies.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic participation and minority rights, but takes a highly interventionist, anti-market stance on economic and financial matters to curb consumption and regulate capital.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism, but is highly interventionist in economic and financial markets, advocating for transaction taxes and strong trade protectionism.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, explicitly defending the rule of law, individual rights, and civil liberties, while maintaining a mixed stance on economic regulation.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing the rule of law, individual rights, and minority protections, while maintaining a moderate stance on economic openness.
