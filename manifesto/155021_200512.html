WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate populism: it contrasts the candidate's non-elite origins and citizen-driven candidacy against traditional elites ("Para las élites, escuchar es una señal de debilidad"), invokes 'the people' as the true wealth of Chile, and frames opponents as clinging to the past, but stops short of a fully Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 2 · The text shows minimal populism: it occasionally invokes 'the people' and references powerful interests harming consumers, but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-oriented, framing challenges as national tasks requiring broad coalitions rather than a virtuous people versus corrupt elites.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies, referencing undue elite influence via money and invoking "Chile somos todos" as a people-centrist slogan, but frames these within institutional reform rather than a sharp people-vs-elite moral struggle.

chunk 4 · The text uses inclusive "Chile somos todos" rhetoric and references past injustices, but frames these in a programmatic, institutional, and rights-based manner rather than through elite-versus-people antagonism or Manichean moral struggle, yielding very low populism scores overall.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the traditional elite and emphasizes the strength and centrality of the people, framing a moral struggle against arrogance and fear, demonstrating strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and a moralized good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 2 · The manifesto emphasizes broad social cooperation and inclusion of diverse sectors, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism without strong moral polarization.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly critiques elite influence in politics and emphasizes inclusive national identity, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and strong people-centrism with some moral framing of social issues.

chunk 4 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by contrasting the candidate's non-elite background with the traditional elite and emphasizing a direct connection to the virtuous citizens.

chunk 2 · The text contains almost no populist rhetoric, focusing instead on institutional consensus, public-private partnerships, and technocratic governance, with only minor, standard references to protecting the Chilean people.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits low populism, explicitly rejecting 'populismo' in security policy and focusing on institutional reforms, though it notes unequal influence of moneyed elites.

chunk 4 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is predominantly left-leaning, emphasizing redistribution, social protection, anti-monopoly measures, and explicitly rejecting tax cuts as contradictory to fighting inequality, while right-wing or nativist themes are virtually absent.

chunk 2 · The text's limited populist content leans slightly left-centrist, emphasizing poverty reduction, social protection, and equality of opportunity, but without anti-corporate or anti-immigration rhetoric; the overarching frame is a broad social pact across all sectors.

chunk 3 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist, emphasizing the state's role in correcting market failures and supporting the poor, while explicitly rejecting neoliberalism, but it lacks strong redistributive or anti-corporate rhetoric and is primarily a centrist-reformist program.

chunk 4 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist through emphasis on gender equality, anti-discrimination, indigenous rights, and social inclusion, but is primarily a mainstream centre-left programmatic document without strong populist ideological framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text highlights social inequalities and the need for redistribution and social justice, reflecting left-leaning populist ideology, while also emphasizing grassroots citizen support and a centrist appeal to broad participation.

chunk 2 · The text supports poverty reduction and social inclusion (left), while emphasizing broad political consensus and national pact (centrist), with no strong right-wing populist themes.

chunk 3 · The text supports left-leaning redistribution and social equality policies, acknowledges immigration regulation, and promotes inclusive centrist appeals, indicating a mixed but overall moderate populist ideology.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence of populist ideological content on the left, right, or centrist populism in the text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist tone is combined with a center-left ideology focused on social justice, equality, and state-led social protection.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of a populist ideological framework, as the text rejects anti-elite polarization and instead promotes a broad social pact involving all political and economic actors.

chunk 3 · The mild populist elements lean left, focusing on social inequalities, gender discrimination, and the influence of money in politics.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on institutional reforms and rights without populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it champions democratic consolidation, anti-discrimination, market competition, and prudential pension-fund reform, though it also advocates significant state intervention in social protection, yielding mixed-to-moderate scores on economic and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions macroeconomic stability, free competition, anti-monopoly enforcement, financial market integration, corporate governance reform, and social rights framed as enabling equal opportunity rather than substituting for markets.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across political and social dimensions—championing democratic reform, anti-discrimination, gender equality, and minority rights—while also supporting market competition and credit access, though with significant state intervention in economic and social policy.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across political (democracy, rule of law, human rights institutions), social (anti-discrimination, gender equality, minority rights), and economic (free trade, open markets) dimensions, with only incidental reference to financial markets through private capital flows.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil liberties, social equality, economic growth with equity, and prudent macroeconomic policies, reflecting a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial market dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, social equality, market competition, and financial market integration, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights, social equality, and economic policies aimed at reducing inequality, with no evidence on financial market regulation, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles, emphasizing human rights, anti-discrimination policies, democratic institutions, and economic openness through trade agreements.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports democratic institutions, gender equality, and free-market competition while advocating for state regulation to prevent market concentration.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports classical and ordoliberal principles, emphasizing market competition, macroeconomic stability, independent regulatory institutions, and global financial integration.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including democratic checks and balances, human rights, anti-discrimination protections, and regulated market competition.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing the rule of law, minority rights, and free trade.
