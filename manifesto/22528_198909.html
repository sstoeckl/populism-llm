WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populism

chunk 3 · The text shows minimal populist features: it occasionally invokes resistance to political power abuse (e.g., in the UN) and frames some issues in moral terms, but it does not systematically pit 'the people' against corrupt elites or employ a strong Manichean narrative.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto combines modest right-leaning elements (national sovereignty, strict immigration, traditional family) with some redistributive concern (development aid, protecting the weak), but the dominant frame is Christian-democratic rather than populist-ideological.

chunk 2 · The manifesto's populist-ideological flavour is weakly right-leaning: it promotes voluntary repatriation of foreign workers, opposes state-driven emancipation policy, and defends traditional family roles, but it lacks strong nativist or anti-corporate elite framing typical of full right-wing populism.

chunk 3 · The text leans mildly right-populist through its emphasis on national sovereignty, rejection of European Union, and national identity, but the dominant framing is Christian-democratic and policy-oriented rather than strongly populist-ideological.

chunk 4 · The text mixes a mild national-interest defence of Dutch fishing rights (right-leaning) with a concern about multinational corporate power in biotechnology (left-leaning), but neither strand is strong enough to dominate; overall populist ideology is very weak.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The RPF supports rule-of-law institutions and some economic freedom, but explicitly rejects gender-equality legislation and equal treatment laws, opposes referenda, and advocates strong state moral regulation, producing a mixed-to-low liberalism profile across political and social dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is moderately liberal economically (free enterprise, privatisation, deregulation) and supports a free press and rule of law, but is clearly anti-liberal on social dimensions, explicitly rejecting government emancipation policy, opposing gender-role reform in education, and restricting media content on moral-religious grounds.

chunk 3 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it supports human rights, opposes protectionism and an EU monetary union, and favors free trade for developing countries, but also endorses significant state intervention in agriculture, transport, and education, and restricts social liberalism through religiously grounded norms.

chunk 4 · The text shows a mixed liberal economic stance: it favours private initiative in housing and explores privatisation of fish auctions, but also calls for extensive government regulation in agriculture, biotechnology, fisheries, and housing subsidies, yielding a moderate overall liberalism score.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports political freedoms and rule of law but rejects some social liberal measures and promotes a regulated economy, showing moderate classical liberalism with no evidence on financial market liberalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through defense of educational freedom, social liberalism via gender equality in labor rights, and economic liberalism by advocating for government role in enabling trade and enterprise; however, it lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through emphasis on human rights and democracy, social liberalism via protection of religious and expression freedoms, and economic liberalism by advocating national sovereignty and market principles within European cooperation, though it lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through housing laws and rights, social liberalism via integrated policy approaches including environmental and social concerns, and economic liberalism by promoting home ownership and fiscal incentives, though it lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports classical political freedoms and a healthy business sector, but strongly rejects modern social liberalism and equal treatment legislation.

chunk 2 · The text shows a mixed classical liberal stance, strongly supporting free enterprise, deregulation, and a free press, while rejecting state-driven social progressivism like gender emancipation policies.

chunk 3 · The text supports classical liberal principles such as individual rights, religious freedom, and free enterprise, while opposing centralized European monetary integration.

chunk 4 · The text supports classical liberal principles by advocating for private initiative in housing and the rule of law in enforcing regulations.
