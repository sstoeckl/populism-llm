WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist features: it frames the right-wing government as having failed ordinary citizens and praises honest workers, but it primarily presents a detailed policy programme rather than a Manichean people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 2 · The text contains mild populist elements — framing citizens as the true addressees of policy, criticising police abuse and corruption as threats to democracy — but the overall tone is programmatic and institutional rather than strongly people-vs-elite.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly addresses corruption as a serious societal problem, emphasizes representing ordinary hardworking citizens, and advocates for a state serving public interests, reflecting moderate populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism through emphasis on legal certainty and criticism of elite corruption, with a moralized stance against corruption undermining democracy.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits low overall populism, occasionally contrasting the interests of ordinary working people with the privileges of elites, but without a strong moralizing or anti-establishment focus.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism by framing the people as the sole true recipients of policy and criticizing political manipulation of the police, but lacks a strong moralized good-versus-evil narrative.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist in flavour, emphasising redistribution through progressive taxation, strong social state, worker protections, and opposition to privatisation of public services, with only minimal right-wing or centrist populist signals.

chunk 2 · The text leans left-populist through opposition to privatisation of public services and emphasis on public investment, with a minor right-populist note in preferring immigration from Slavic countries, but the overall ideological flavour is moderate centre-left social democracy.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text promotes a democratic social state model combining economic performance with social equality, opposes extremism, and supports a state serving public good, indicating a left-leaning centrist populist ideology.

chunk 2 · Populist ideology is mixed, with left-leaning distancing from elite financing models, right-leaning preference for Slavic immigration, and centrist appeals to legal certainty and social order.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, emphasizing social justice, progressive taxation, and the defense of the welfare state against right-wing policies.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is primarily left-wing, focusing on stopping the privatization of public services, though it also contains restrictive, culturally selective immigration stances.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political rights (direct democracy, rule of law) and social equality (anti-discrimination), moderately mixed on economic liberalism (endorsing markets but also progressive taxes and state ownership), and mildly anti-liberal on financial markets (government-set interest rate caps on consumer credit).

chunk 2 · The text is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (deepening democracy, fighting discrimination, rule of law), mixed on economic liberalism (opposing privatisation of public services but welcoming private capital in infrastructure), and moderately pro-market on financial matters (PPP projects).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, political rights, social equality, free market reforms, and prudent financial regulation, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through rule of law and democracy, social liberalism via anti-discrimination efforts, and economic liberalism by promoting private-public partnerships, but lacks evidence on financial market liberalism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports classical liberal democratic institutions, human rights, and market mechanisms, but advocates for strong state regulation, social protections, and financial market oversight.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports democratic institutions, direct democracy, and minority protections, while taking a mixed stance on economic liberalism by opposing public service privatization but supporting public-private partnerships.
