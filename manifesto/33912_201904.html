WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames the Spanish state as a corrupt, repressive elite that ignores the legitimate will of the Catalan people, invoking a clear people-vs-elite dynamic with moralized good-vs-evil framing around freedom, repression, and political prisoners.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: it frames the Spanish central state as deliberately strangling Catalonia financially ("ofensiva premeditada d'ofec financer"), uses some people-centred rhetoric, and frames the fiscal relationship as a moral injustice ("presa de pèl"), but the dominant register is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than strongly populist.

chunk 3 · The text frames the Spanish central state as systematically unjust and self-serving toward Catalonia (e.g., "situació absolutament injusta," systematic persecution of Catalan economy), but the critique is primarily institutional/territorial rather than a full people-vs-corrupt-elite populist frame; anti-elitism and Manichean elements are moderate and focused on state-Catalonia relations.

chunk 4 · The manifesto frames the Spanish central state and its governments as disloyal, illegitimate, and prepotent actors who systematically override Catalan democratic will, showing moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism focused on Catalan self-determination rather than a broad populist good-vs-evil narrative.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the Spanish state elites as incapable and repressive, highlights the majority will of the Catalan people, and frames the conflict as a struggle for freedom against repression, demonstrating strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized conflict.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly critiques the unfair financial relationship with the Spanish state and emphasizes a people-centered paradigm, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moralized critique of the state imbalance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto criticizes the centralization and economic policies of the Spanish state as harming Catalonia, invoking the people of Catalonia and framing a conflict with the state, showing moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes central governments as disloyal and arrogant elites, emphasizes the importance of local municipalities as close to the people, and frames the state as disloyal and breaking rules, indicating moderate populist stances across anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean views.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text frames the Spanish state and its institutions as an authoritarian, repressive regime that actively ignores and harms the virtuous Catalan people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the Spanish state's financial and political actions as a deliberate, unfair offensive against the Catalan people and their institutions.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits very low populism, with only minor anti-elitist framing directed at the Spanish central government's tendency to favor large monopolies over small businesses.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits strong populist anti-elitism by framing the Spanish central government and state institutions as corrupt, abusive, and acting with disloyalty and prepotency against the democratic will of the Catalan people.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is primarily left-leaning (welfare state expansion, reducing inequality, social housing, minimum wage increases) combined with a strong ethno-territorial/nationalist dimension defending Catalan identity and self-determination, making it a left-nationalist blend.

chunk 2 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist through its emphasis on reducing wage inequality, protecting workers, expanding social services, and combating precarious employment, combined with a strong Catalan nationalist identity frame; right-populist or nativist elements are essentially absent.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is primarily centrist-territorial (Catalan autonomy/independence framing) with a moderate left-leaning tilt (redistribution, fighting inequality, support for SMEs and workers), but no strong right-wing nativist or anti-immigration content; the dominant frame is Catalonia vs. the Spanish central state.

chunk 4 · The populist appeals are primarily centred on Catalan nationalist self-determination rather than a clear left or right economic programme; there are mild left-leaning elements (green economy, social spending) but the dominant frame is ethno-territorial autonomy, placing the ideology closest to centrist/nationalist populism.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-wing populist themes of social inequality and redistribution with centrist appeals to unity and dialogue, while opposing right-wing extremism, reflecting a mixed but predominantly left-leaning populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The text supports left-leaning social policies and redistribution through social collaboration, while also showing moderate right-leaning concerns about fiscal imbalances, resulting in a centrist populist ideological stance overall.

chunk 3 · The text promotes left-leaning redistribution and social equality policies, criticizes state economic centralization, and calls for defending Catalan interests, with some centrist and right-leaning elements in market unity and identity protection.

chunk 4 · The text shows a left-leaning populist ideology through environmental fiscal policies, a right-leaning stance in rejecting Spanish state interventions like Article 155, and centrist calls for bilateral dialogue, reflecting a mixed but moderate populist ideological profile.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist discourse is combined with left-leaning social demands, such as reducing inequality, increasing the minimum wage, and strengthening the welfare state.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly right-leaning/regionalist, focusing on defending Catalan national identity, language, and educational models against central state interference.

chunk 3 · There is no clear populist ideological framing, as the text focuses on regionalist, pro-market, and social-democratic policy proposals rather than a populist ideology.

chunk 4 · The populist discourse is strongly aligned with right-wing/nationalist regionalism, focusing on Catalan self-determination, sovereignty, and independence from the Spanish state.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto defends democratic rights and Catalan self-determination against state repression (pro-liberal on political rights), advocates for social rights and anti-discrimination, but also promotes significant state intervention in housing, labour markets, and financial oversight of the Generalitat, yielding mixed liberal scores overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly liberal: it strongly champions LGBTQI+ rights and gender equality, supports democratic participation and transparency, advocates competitive and innovative economic growth, and references market access for public financing, though it also favours significant state intervention in labour markets and social services, producing a mixed but overall pro-liberal profile.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports independent regulatory bodies, anti-discrimination, and market competition (pro-liberal), but also advocates a financial transactions tax and state-directed credit allocation, producing a mixed liberal profile overall averaging around 6.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly advocates judicial independence, rule of law, democratic transparency, and human rights for migrants and minorities, while also proposing a financial transaction tax that moderately constrains financial market liberalism, yielding a moderately pro-liberal overall profile.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto evidences limited support for liberal political and social rights, critiques economic and financial constraints imposed by the state, and highlights failures in financial market autonomy, indicating moderate liberalism with critical stance on state intervention.

chunk 2 · The manifesto promotes political liberalism through participatory governance, strong social liberalism via gender equality and anti-discrimination policies, and economic liberalism by advocating competitive and sustainable economic growth, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, anti-fraud measures, social equality, free market competition, and financial market regulation that enables market function, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports political liberalism by advocating judicial independence, social liberalism by emphasizing human rights for migrants and refugees, and economic liberalism through promotion of eco-innovation, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market liberalism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly defends political and civil rights against state overreach, though it expresses skepticism toward economic deregulation in favor of local market protection.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports social equality, gender rights, and democratic parliamentary consensus, alongside a mixed economic approach that values private-public collaboration and entrepreneurship.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal and ordoliberal principles, advocating for market competition, financial market regulation to protect consumers, and individual civil rights.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles such as the separation of powers, judicial independence, and human rights, while also endorsing some market regulations like financial transaction taxes.
