WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: it criticises private banks and holding companies for socialising losses while privatising profits, invokes collective interests against private elites, and frames the Walloon industrial crisis as a result of deliberate blocking by Flemish political actors, but it does so in a technocratic, programmatic register rather than a strongly Manichean people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 2 · The text frames Flemish political actors as monopolizing financial power and deliberately disadvantaging Wallonia, with some anti-elite and Manichean framing, but the primary thrust is technocratic regionalization policy rather than classic people-vs-elite populism.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes unemployment as a market failure and emphasizes solidarity and workers' readiness to make sacrifices, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral framing of social issues.

chunk 2 · Populism is moderately present, evidenced by criticism of elite control over agriculture and calls for regional empowerment, but lacks strong people-centrism or moral dichotomy.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing private banking and energy interests as acting against the collective interest of the people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild anti-elitism by criticizing the political dominance and 'grip' of the CVP party over state departments, though it lacks a generalized moralized people-vs-elite framework.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist in orientation: it advocates redistribution, public industrial initiative, social security preservation, tax justice targeting large-scale fraud, and workers' rights, while explicitly rejecting Thatcherite/Reaganite supply-side cuts; right-populist or centrist-populist elements are minimal.

chunk 2 · The text is predominantly left-populist, advocating for a strong public credit sector, regionalization of economic resources, and redistribution of investment toward Wallonia, with only minor identity-based (Flemish vs. Walloon) framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates for reducing fiscal inequalities and social justice, opposes right-wing attempts to weaken Brussels, and proposes a comprehensive strategy for employment, indicating a left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows a left-leaning populist ideology through advocacy for economic federalization and public credit control, with moderate centrist populist appeals but no right-wing nationalist themes.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on anti-corporate sentiment, public industrial initiatives, and resisting the privatization of social security.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, advocating for a socialized and regionalized credit sector to counter private financial power.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports gender equality and workers' rights (moderately pro-liberal socially), but advocates extensive state intervention in industry, banking controls, public enterprise expansion, and financial regulation that restricts market-based pricing, yielding below-average scores on economic and financial-market liberalism.

chunk 2 · The text is predominantly anti-liberal in economic and financial terms, advocating for a strong regionalized public credit sector, capital controls, and state-directed investment, while showing only moderate support for independent oversight institutions in the political dimension.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democratic political institutions, social protections, rational economic policies, and financial market oversight that enable market function, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · Liberalism is moderately supported via calls for regional political participation in EU policy and recognition of economic investment dynamics, though social and financial market liberalism are not addressed.

Gemini

chunk 1 · While supporting democratic representation and social rights, the text is highly anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions, advocating for state intervention, public banking vetoes, and public industrial initiatives.

chunk 2 · The text is highly anti-liberal, advocating for state-controlled, socialized credit institutions, regionalized economic planning, and strict controls on capital flight.
