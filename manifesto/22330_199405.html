WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows minimal populist features: mild criticism of the "rituele touwtrekkers op het middenveld" and a technocratic, pragmatic tone that explicitly rejects ideological rhetoric, with no strong people-vs-elite framing or Manichean narrative.

chunk 2 · The text shows mild populist tendencies through criticism of opaque bureaucratic "bolwerks" that sideline citizens and politicians, and calls for more direct democratic participation, but explicitly avoids Manichean framing (the government is "not truly malicious") and does not construct a virtuous people vs. corrupt elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto from D66 (Netherlands, 1994) focused on administrative reform, justice, integration, welfare, health, and education. There is minimal populist rhetoric: no strong anti-elite framing, no moralized good-vs-evil narrative, and only mild people-centric language around local democracy. The slight anti-elitism is limited to criticism of unaccountable administrative structures.

chunk 4 · The text contains minimal populist content: a brief reference to a "worrying gap" between management and students/staff in higher education, and a passing mention of "populist leaders" abroad as a negative phenomenon, but no sustained people-vs-elite framing or Manichean moral struggle.

chunk 2 · The manifesto criticizes the government as an unwieldy 'reus' and calls for more direct influence of the people in politics, reflecting mild anti-elitism and people-centrism but lacks strong moral dichotomies.

chunk 4 · The manifesto does not provide explicit content related to populism dimensions such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or manichean worldviews.

Gemini

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing the 'political elite' and advocating for direct democratic reforms like electing the prime minister directly.

chunk 3 · The text contains very minimal populist rhetoric, only expressing mild concern over democratically unaccountable administrative bodies without framing politics as a moral struggle.

chunk 4 · The text contains very minimal populism, only noting a worrying gap between university management and students/staff, but lacks broader anti-elitist or people-centric framing.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto blends modest left-leaning social justice concerns with centrist pragmatism and market-oriented reforms, without a strong populist ideological anchor on either left or right.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are primarily centrist-reformist (democratic reform, direct elections, decentralization) with minor left-leaning environmental justice themes (fairer distribution of environmental space), and no right-wing nativist or anti-immigration content.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is primarily a centrist-liberal reform programme (D66). It contains mild left-leaning elements (anti-discrimination labour law, integration policy, welfare) and mild right-leaning elements (discouraging illegal immigration), but the dominant ideological flavour is centrist pro-democracy reform with no strong populist ideological charge.

chunk 4 · The text is not ideologically populist; it combines moderate centre-left elements (poverty reduction, development aid, women's rights) with pro-market and pro-free-trade positions, and explicitly criticises populist nationalism, placing it firmly in a liberal-democratic, centrist-reformist tradition (D66).

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of a populist ideological framing, as the text focuses on institutional reforms and policy details rather than populist mobilization.

chunk 4 · There is no distinct populist ideology present in the text, as the document focuses on mainstream programmatic policies.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it upholds the rule of law and rechtsstaat, promotes anti-discrimination, advocates market competition and deregulation (e.g., ending PTT monopoly, reducing administrative burdens), and supports expanded bank participation in capital markets.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal politically (direct election of PM, referenda, checks and balances, rule of law), moderately pro-liberal socially (affirmative action for minorities, women, disabled), and mixed economically (free market in agriculture but extensive environmental regulation and state intervention in energy/transport).

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across political and social dimensions: it advocates directly elected mayors, referenda, constitutional review, registered partnership for homosexuals, anti-discrimination laws, and individual rights protections. Economic liberalism is present but more mixed, with significant state roles in healthcare, education, and welfare. Financial market policy is not discussed.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic governance and parliamentary control, free international trade, market-based development, capital access for private investment, and civil/human rights including women's and minority rights, reflecting D66's classical liberal-democratic orientation.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, social justice, market renewal, and prudent financial participation, indicating a robust classical liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through calls for transparency and open government, social liberalism via support for minority and disadvantaged groups, and economic liberalism by advocating market liberalization in housing; financial market issues are not addressed.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including local democracy, minority rights, social integration, market mechanisms, and political freedoms, as evidenced by direct quotes on municipal democracy, anti-discrimination, and fiscal policy.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through emphasis on democratic processes and individual rights in education, social liberalism via pluralism and tolerance, and economic liberalism by endorsing market-oriented knowledge institutions; no evidence on financial market liberalism was found.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports classical and social-liberal principles, emphasizing the rule of law, equal rights, market competition, and financial modernization.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including democratic reforms, individual freedom rights, and transitioning agricultural sectors to a free market.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing individual legal protections, civil rights such as same-sex partnerships, and democratic checks and balances.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including democratic accountability, minority rights, and free international trade.
