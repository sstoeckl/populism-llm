WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly frames elites (private banks, transnational corporations, corrupt politicians) as having plundered the public good, invokes 'the people' and communities as the virtuous sovereign, and constructs a Manichean struggle between a corrupt neoliberal system and the suffering majority, scoring high on all three populism sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto frames past governments and neoliberal elites as corrupt and destructive, invokes communities and 'the people' as virtuous protagonists, and uses moralized language ("políticas suicidas," "partidos tradicionales") to draw a clear good-vs-evil divide, yielding moderate-to-high populism scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows moderate populism: it frames established elites and neoliberal governments as having betrayed the people's welfare, invokes the common good of "the majority," and draws a moral contrast between capital's gains and workers' wellbeing, though the tone is programmatic rather than stridently Manichean.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes corruption as systemic and highlights political sectors exploiting the poor, reflecting strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized good-vs-evil struggle.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes 'los grupos en el poder' and emphasizes 'las comunidades locales' participation, reflecting anti-elitism and people-centrism, while also framing environmental policies as a struggle against 'políticas suicidas', indicating a moderate moralized conflict.

chunk 3 · The manifesto expresses moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism by emphasizing economic justice and respect for human rights, while framing external trade policies as detrimental to national interests, indicating a moderate populist stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames the political and economic elites as corrupt and self-serving while championing the virtuous power of the people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing previous neoliberal governments as corrupt or self-serving and contrasting them with the needs of the 'great majorities'.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing traditional parties for neglecting the youth and framing the economy as serving capital over the people.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is overwhelmingly left-populist: it explicitly blames neoliberalism and private capital (banks, transnationals, large exporters) for inequality and poverty, advocates redistribution, progressive taxation, strengthening public institutions, cooperatives, and social economy, with only a marginal nativist element regarding foreign land ownership.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly left-populist, emphasizing redistribution, social economy, cooperatives, fair trade, anti-corporate elite critique, and state control of strategic sectors, with only marginal national-sovereignty framing and an explicit rejection of penal populism.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is clearly left-leaning, emphasizing progressive taxation, redistribution of wealth, workers' rights, social programs, and skepticism of free-trade agreements and speculative capital, with only marginal nationalist/right-populist elements.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports left-wing populist ideology through calls for progressive taxation, wealth redistribution, and prioritizing marginalized groups, with moderate centrist populist appeals but no right-wing populism.

chunk 2 · The text promotes 'justa distribución de la riqueza' and social solidarity, supports protection of traditional communities, and emphasizes community participation, showing a left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist elements and moderate right-leaning identity protection.

chunk 3 · The text supports left-leaning populist ideology through calls for progressive taxation and economic redistribution, with some right-leaning skepticism of free trade agreements, and centrist appeals to human rights and dignity, reflecting a mixed populist ideological profile.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist discourse is explicitly left-wing, focusing on anti-neoliberalism, redistribution, and fighting capital accumulation by privileged sectors.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on wealth redistribution, social justice, and opposition to neoliberalism and corporate privatization.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on anti-corporate sentiment, redistribution, and criticizing neoliberal economic policies.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores moderately on political liberalism (strong democratic participation, anti-corruption, rule of law) and social liberalism (gender equality, sexual diversity, minority rights), but is explicitly anti-neoliberal on economic liberalism (rejecting free trade and privatization) and anti-liberal on financial markets (proposing Tobin tax, heavy bank taxation, capital controls), yielding a mixed overall liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, gender equality, and minority rights (moderately pro-liberal on political and social dimensions), but is explicitly anti-privatization, favors state control of energy and banking, and redirects commercial credit toward social ends, yielding low scores on economic and financial-market liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores high on social liberalism (strong LGBTQ+, gender, and minority rights) and political liberalism (human rights, democracy), but is mixed on economic liberalism (progressive taxation and state intervention alongside some market support for SMEs), and anti-liberal on financial markets (capital controls, restrictions on speculative flows, state-directed interest rates).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto endorses democratic participation, gender equality, and social inclusion, while showing mixed views on economic liberalism due to criticism of neoliberalism and mixed financial market practices.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports 'democracia del siglo XXI', gender equality, defends free trade with safeguards, and calls for transparency and fair financial resource use, indicating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto endorses liberal principles in political rights, social equality, economic market support, and financial regulation enabling market function, though with some regulatory emphasis, resulting in a moderate pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports democratic institutions and social diversity but strongly rejects free-market economics and financial liberalization in favor of state intervention.

chunk 2 · While supporting democratic institutions and minority rights, the text strongly opposes economic liberalism by advocating for state-controlled energy, public banking, and market interventions.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through human rights and press freedom, but rejects economic and financial liberalism by advocating for capital controls and state intervention.
