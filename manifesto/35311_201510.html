The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames the right-wing coalition government as corrupt and deceitful ("burla política", "fanatismo ideológico"), appeals to the majority of Portuguese people as victims of elite misrule, and draws a moral contrast between the PS and the governing right, but stops short of full Manichean populism, remaining largely within a programmatic social-democratic register.

chunk 2 · The text criticises the right-wing coalition government (PSD/CDS) repeatedly for austerity, disrespect for social partners, and mismanagement, but frames this as a programmatic policy critique rather than a deep people-vs-corrupt-elite populist narrative; people-centrism is present but instrumental, and Manichean framing is mild.

chunk 3 · The text criticises the incumbent PSD/CDS government for mismanaging the NHS and cancelling adult education programmes, but the critique is policy-focused and institutional rather than a moralistic people-vs-elite frame; populist elements are mild and incidental.

chunk 4 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto (Portuguese Socialist Party, 2015) focused on maritime economy, territorial development, energy transition, and agriculture; it contains only mild criticism of the incumbent PSD-CDS government (e.g., "manifestamente hostil às energias renováveis") without constructing a sustained people-vs-elite narrative, yielding very low populism scores across all sub-dimensions.

chunk 5 · The text contains mild anti-elite rhetoric directed at the incumbent PSD/CDS government (accused of destroying science policy, exploiting the crisis, and harming vulnerable citizens), but this is standard partisan criticism rather than deep populist framing; there is no strong people-vs-elite moral dichotomy or invocation of a unified virtuous people.

chunk 6 · The text contains mild partisan criticism of the PSD/CDS government for cutting social protection, and frames the PS as defender of the poor and workers, but the rhetoric is policy-focused rather than strongly populist; there is no sustained people-vs-elite narrative or Manichean framing.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist appeals are predominantly left-leaning, focusing on workers' rights, combating precarity, redistribution, and social equality against corporate and political elites, with no significant right-wing nativist or anti-immigration content and only mild centrist people-centric framing.

chunk 2 · The mild populist appeals lean left, emphasising social cohesion, reducing inequality, combating poverty, and restoring public services, with no significant right-populist nativist or anti-immigration content beyond routine border-management references.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is predominantly centre-left, emphasising social equality, poverty reduction, public services, and inclusive immigration policy, with no nativist or right-populist content and only weak generic centrist appeals.

chunk 4 · The faint populist signals lean slightly left (opposing privatisation of EGF, supporting small farmers, redistributive subsidies) rather than right or centrist, but the overall ideological tone is mainstream social-democratic rather than populist.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is a mainstream centre-left social-democratic programme (PS Portugal) focused on social protection, redistribution, public investment in science/culture, and combating poverty and inequality, with no nationalist or right-populist elements.

chunk 6 · The text leans mildly left-populist through its emphasis on restoring social protection for the poor, combating wage inequality, and criticising the incumbent right-wing government's cuts, but the overall tone is mainstream social-democratic rather than strongly ideological populism.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently supports democratic institutions, fundamental rights, rule of law, and the Constitutional Court; promotes civil rights and freedom of movement; advocates market competition, deregulation for businesses (Simplex), and capital market development; and endorses the Banking Union and Single Capital Market as pro-liberal financial measures.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it defends separation of powers, rule of law, and democratic accountability (lib_political); opposes racism and xenophobia and supports victim protections (lib_social); promotes competitiveness and legislative simplification for businesses (lib_economic); and supports prudential financial regulation, regulator independence, and consumer protection in financial markets (lib_financial_market).

chunk 3 · The text consistently supports democratic citizenship, equal access to public services, social inclusion, and market-based housing/rental mechanisms, reflecting moderate classical-liberal values across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly pro-market in energy (competition, liberalisation, market pricing of renewables) and economic policy (export orientation, entrepreneurship, deregulation/simplification), while also endorsing some state intervention (halting privatisation of EGF, public control of water services), producing mixed but moderately liberal scores across dimensions.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it supports market unbundling in energy, media ownership transparency, democratic institutions, and strong civil rights/equality provisions, while also advocating state intervention in social protection and public investment, yielding a moderately pro-liberal overall profile.

chunk 6 · The text is strongly pro-liberal on social dimensions (LGBTQ+ rights, gender equality, religious freedom, disability inclusion) and political dimensions (democracy, human rights, rule of law), moderately liberal on economic openness within the Lusophone space, and only tangentially touches on financial markets through references to pan-African banking platforms and capital market instruments for TAP.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, social rights, economic liberalization, and financial market development, emphasizing a modern, transparent state and market-friendly policies, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic participation, social rights, public health access, social equality, and economic policies promoting urban rehabilitation and market efficiency, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through municipal autonomy and governance, social liberalism via biodiversity conservation and minority protections, and economic liberalism by promoting innovation, knowledge, and sustainable economic development; however, it lacks explicit discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly supports constitutional democracy, equality and non-discrimination, and economic innovation and competitiveness, reflecting a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 6 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal political values such as democracy, human rights, and rule of law; promotes social liberalism through equality and anti-discrimination; endorses economic liberalism by strengthening state instruments for economic projection; but lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports liberal democratic institutions, constitutionalism, and regulated market mechanisms, while opposing the right-wing government's constitutional violations.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports liberal democratic principles, including the separation of powers, institutional transparency, and prudential regulation of financial markets to prevent systemic risk.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports social and political liberalism through democratic citizenship and equal access to public services, while maintaining a regulated market economy that utilizes private enterprise and banking guarantees.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits moderate classical liberal tendencies by advocating for administrative simplification (Simplex), reducing bureaucracy, and facilitating market access to credit, while maintaining a strong role for public planning and environmental regulation.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports liberal democratic values, non-discrimination, and competitive public tenders, though it does not address financial market policies.

chunk 6 · The text strongly supports liberal values, advocating for robust civil rights, LGBTQ+ equality, democratic principles, and market-friendly economic integration within the Lusophone space.
