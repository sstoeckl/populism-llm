WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames politics as a struggle between ordinary people/workers and capitalist elites/overclass, invoking people-power slogans ("Folkemakt, ikke pengemakt") and condemning capital owners who put their interests above the collective.

chunk 2 · The text shows mild populist features: property speculators are briefly cast as villains, and the welfare state is framed as serving "the whole population," but the dominant register is technocratic social-democratic policy rather than a moralized people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The text shows mild populist features: corporate/business interests are occasionally framed as threats to public goods (research independence, energy resources), and the people are invoked as owners of natural resources, but the overall tone is programmatic and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows moderate populist tendencies: it frames global capitalism as exploitative ("rovdrift på mennesker og miljø"), criticises concentrated power among elites, and calls for expanding democracy to counter elite control, but does so within a programmatic left-wing framework rather than a purely people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 5 · The text invokes "vanlige mennesker" with limited power against concentrated elites, calls for radical democratic expansion, and frames financial speculation and corporate power as threats to the common good, reflecting moderate populist themes without a sharp Manichean conspiracy narrative.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the overclass and market elites, emphasizing strengthening folkestyret and fellesskapet against market forces, reflecting strong anti-elitism and people-centrism with a moral critique of market liberalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly critiques market liberalism as threatening people's security and emphasizes collective welfare solutions, indicating moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and a mixed moral struggle framing, resulting in an overall moderate populism score.

chunk 3 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

chunk 4 · Manifesto critiques capitalist elites and imperialist powers, emphasizing people's needs and moral struggles, reflecting moderate populist themes.

chunk 5 · The manifesto emphasizes concentration of power among few elites and advocates expanding democratic control and public ownership, reflecting strong anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moral framing of power imbalances.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text frames a struggle between the common people and a wealthy overclass or financial elite.

chunk 2 · The text contains very limited populist rhetoric, with only minor anti-elite framing directed at property speculators.

chunk 3 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil struggles.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits very low populism, focusing on structural socialist critiques rather than a moralized struggle between a corrupt elite and a pure people.

chunk 5 · The text exhibits mild populism by framing power as concentrated in the hands of a few and advocating for a broader 'folkestyre' (people's rule).

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist appeals are firmly left-wing: the manifesto explicitly calls for socialism, redistribution of wealth and property, anti-privatisation, strong public ownership, and opposition to market liberalism, with no right-wing nativist or anti-immigration content.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist in orientation, opposing privatisation, championing public ownership, strong unions, redistribution, and social equality, with no nativist or right-populist content; immigration is framed positively as a labour-market necessity.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist in orientation, advocating active state ownership, redistribution, workers' rights, gender equality, and public control of key industries, with only minor nationalist/identity elements around resource sovereignty.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing: the manifesto advocates public ownership, opposes privatisation, calls for redistribution, frames capitalism as exploitative, and supports workers' control — with only marginal nationalist/right-populist elements (EU scepticism framed in democratic rather than identity terms).

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly labels its program "sosialistisk finanspolitikk," advocates public ownership over key industries and financial institutions, redistribution of workplace power to employees, and reversal of privatization, placing it firmly in the left-populist tradition.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports left-wing populist ideology with calls for radical redistribution, anti-capitalist rhetoric, and anti-racism, while rejecting right-wing nationalist themes and showing some centrist populist appeals.

chunk 2 · The text clearly opposes privatization and competition in welfare services, reflecting a strong left-wing populist ideology, with no evidence for right or centrist populist ideology.

chunk 3 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the manifesto text provided.

chunk 4 · The text supports left-wing populist ideas like redistribution and anti-imperialism, while opposing right-wing exclusionary policies, showing a centrist populist appeal to fairness and peace.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports left-wing populist ideology through calls for increased public ownership, democratic control over economy, and opposition to privatization, with moderate centrist populist appeals and no right-wing populist content.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist appeal is explicitly socialist and left-wing, targeting capitalism, market forces, and corporate interests.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on anti-privatization, public welfare, and redistribution.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideology in the text, as it focuses on programmatic policy proposals without populist framing.

chunk 4 · The populist elements are strongly left-wing, targeting global capitalism, corporate power, and economic elites while advocating for redistribution and workers' control.

chunk 5 · The populist elements are explicitly embedded in a left-wing socialist ideology that seeks to expand public ownership and worker control over capital.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores low on economic and financial-market liberalism (opposing privatisation, taxing stock transactions, advocating state banking control) but high on social liberalism (LGBTQ+ rights, anti-discrimination) and moderately on political liberalism (expanded democracy), yielding a mixed overall score.

chunk 2 · The text scores high on social liberalism (anti-discrimination, minority rights, gender equality) and political democracy, but is strongly anti-liberal economically—explicitly opposing privatisation, competition tendering, and market principles in health and welfare—and introduces price controls and state intervention in housing/financial markets.

chunk 3 · The text scores moderately on political and social liberalism (democracy, equal rights, minority inclusion) but is anti-liberal economically, explicitly rejecting market-neutral policies, favouring state ownership, opposing liberalisation of postal services, and advocating politically set interest rates.

chunk 4 · The manifesto scores high on social liberalism (strong anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ rights, gender equality) and moderately on political liberalism (democracy, civil society), but is explicitly anti-liberal on economic and financial-market dimensions, opposing privatisation, advocating capital controls, and calling for nationalisation of financial institutions.

chunk 5 · The text scores moderately on political and social liberalism (free speech, gender representation, religious pluralism, civil rights), but strongly anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions, explicitly opposing privatization, advocating state control of financial institutions, and calling for reversal of deregulation.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto advocates for democratic control, civil rights protections, and a regulated economy with public ownership, showing strong political and social liberalism, moderate economic liberalism, and mixed but critical stance on financial markets.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports democratic political control of institutions, strong anti-discrimination laws, public regulation and ownership, and coordinated market interventions, demonstrating a strong classical liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports democratic governance of educational institutions, gender equality, and active state involvement in economic development, reflecting strong classical liberal values in political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports political and social liberal rights, advocates for fair economic policies, and calls for regulated financial markets to enable equitable development.

chunk 5 · The manifesto supports liberal political and social rights explicitly, but opposes economic liberalism by favoring increased public ownership and control over finance, reflecting a mixed stance on liberalism overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political democracy and social minority rights, but rejects free-market economics and financial deregulation.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political democracy and social minority rights, but rejects economic liberalism and market-based solutions in favor of state control and public services.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political democracy and social human rights, but explicitly rejects free-market economic liberalism in favor of state intervention and public ownership.

chunk 4 · The text is highly supportive of political democracy and social minority rights, but strongly opposes classical economic and financial liberalism, advocating instead for state ownership, market regulation, and capital controls.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through human rights and state-church separation, but strongly rejects economic and financial liberalism in favor of state ownership and market regulation.
