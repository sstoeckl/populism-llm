WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames Labor as a corrupt, union-controlled threat to ordinary Australians, but the anti-elitism is directed at a rival party rather than a systemic elite, and people-centric appeals are moderate and embedded in mainstream centre-right economic rhetoric.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains mild partisan attacks framing Labor as fiscally reckless and anti-enterprise, with occasional 'fair go' people-centric rhetoric, but the dominant tone is technocratic policy delivery rather than populist mobilisation against corrupt elites.

chunk 3 · The text contains moderate populist elements primarily through repeated contrasts between the Morrison Government's competence and Labor's failures, invoking ordinary Australians (farmers, regional communities, young people) as virtuous beneficiaries, but stops short of framing elites as corrupt — the anti-Labor rhetoric is partisan rather than deeply anti-establishment.

chunk 4 · The manifesto contains moderate populist elements primarily directed against Labor and unions as self-serving elites betraying farmers and workers, with some good-vs-evil framing ("economy-wrecking plans"), but the dominant tone is policy-focused and technocratic rather than strongly populist; people-centrism is present but limited to specific constituencies (farmers, regional communities) rather than a unified "the people."

chunk 5 · The text frames Labor as incompetent and union-captured rather than as a corrupt elite oppressing the people; anti-elitism is mild and directed at a rival party, people-centrism is present but instrumental, and the good-vs-evil framing is moderate partisan rhetoric rather than deep Manichean populism.

chunk 6 · The manifesto shows only mild populist features: it frames Labor as fiscally incompetent and morally callous ("heartless tax grab"), invokes a "fair go for all," and uses some good-vs-bad framing, but the dominant register is mainstream centre-right policy advocacy rather than a sustained people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 7 · The manifesto contains mild populist elements — primarily a government-vs-opposition framing that casts Labor as reckless and out of touch with regional Australians, with some good-vs-evil moral contrast ("envy rather than aspiration"), but the rhetoric is largely policy-focused and institutional rather than a sustained people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 8 · The text exhibits mild populism: it frames Labor as corrupt and incompetent ("Labor has a scam"), invokes ordinary Australians ("Every Australian child") as beneficiaries, and uses some good-vs-evil rhetoric against Labor, but the dominant tone is policy-focused and partisan rather than deeply populist.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The document is primarily a mainstream centre-right governing-party manifesto emphasising lower taxes, free enterprise, fiscal discipline and border security, with only mild populist flavouring directed against Labor and unions rather than a coherent left- or right-populist programme.

chunk 2 · The manifesto leans mildly right-populist with emphasis on border security, anti-Labor partisanship, and small business/tax-cut rhetoric, but the overall ideological flavour is mainstream centre-right rather than strongly populist.

chunk 3 · The populist flavour is weakly right-leaning, with references to population management, national defence sovereignty, and protecting regional/rural communities, but the document is predominantly a mainstream centre-right government policy manifesto without strong ideological populist framing.

chunk 4 · The manifesto's populist flavour is predominantly right-leaning, emphasising protection of farmers' property rights, national agricultural identity, free trade agreements, and opposition to union influence and environmental regulation, with no redistributive or anti-corporate left-populist content.

chunk 5 · The populist flavour is predominantly right-leaning, centred on strict border protection and anti-immigration rhetoric, combined with pro-business, low-tax and free-trade positions; left-populist redistribution themes are absent.

chunk 6 · The limited populist framing leans mildly right — emphasising national security, economic management, and opposition to Labor's tax agenda — rather than left-wing redistribution or strong nativist identity politics; centrist "fair go" appeals are present but secondary.

chunk 7 · The manifesto's populist ideological flavour is predominantly right-leaning, centred on immigration restriction, border security, and national identity protection, with no anti-corporate or redistributive left-populist content.

chunk 8 · The populist elements are weak and do not align strongly with left or right populism; the text is a centre-right government manifesto emphasising fiscal responsibility, national interest, and regional access, with only mild anti-Labor partisan framing rather than a clear populist ideological program.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses free markets, lower taxes, deregulation, trade liberalisation, competitive finance, rule of law over union power, and gender pay-gap reduction, placing it firmly in the pro-liberal quadrant across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently endorses classical liberal positions: rule of law, democratic freedoms, equal opportunity regardless of background, tax cuts and deregulation for small business, and market-based finance through competitive lending and equity funds.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it supports market-based skills development, individual choice in disability services, free trade and agricultural competitiveness, and prudential/concessional finance mechanisms, reflecting a classical liberal orientation tempered by targeted government intervention.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal economically, strongly emphasising free trade agreements, market competition (ACCC reviews, export market development), and deregulation ("removing red tape"), while also supporting rule-of-law institutions (independent statutory reviews, ACCC oversight) and making modest gestures toward Indigenous inclusion; it does not advocate capital controls or state-directed pricing.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal economically (free trade, tax cuts, competitive finance, deregulation) and moderately pro-liberal politically (transparency, rule of law online) and socially (gender equality, women's safety programs), with no anti-liberal financial-market positions evident.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions gender equality as a human right (lib_social), defends the rule of law against union power (lib_political), promotes free enterprise, entrepreneurship and trade openness (lib_economic), and supports women's access to finance and start-up capital (lib_financial_market).

chunk 7 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it explicitly endorses liberal democracy, rule of law, and individual rights; champions free trade, competition, and deregulation; supports civil rights and LGBTI protections internationally; and promotes market-based housing finance mechanisms.

chunk 8 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it champions freedom of speech on campuses, opposes tax increases, promotes competition and performance-based funding in higher education, and supports access for disadvantaged groups, reflecting classical liberal values in political, social, and economic dimensions.
