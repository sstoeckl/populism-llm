WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: it criticises the incumbent "blauwroodgroen" coalition for broken promises and poor governance, invokes ordinary families and people as the central moral reference, and frames politics as a choice between honest CD&V and a self-serving establishment, but stops short of a fully Manichean or conspiratorial narrative.

chunk 2 · The text criticises the incumbent "blauwroodgroen" government for self-interest and policy failures, but does so in a programmatic, party-political manner rather than through strong people-vs-elite populist framing; anti-elitism and Manichean rhetoric are mild and targeted at a specific coalition rather than a corrupt elite class.

chunk 3 · The manifesto repeatedly criticises the incumbent "blauwroodgroen" government for incompetence and deception (e.g., manipulating crime statistics, failing on debt reduction, drug tolerance), framing CD&V as the defender of ordinary people and victims, but the rhetoric stays largely technocratic and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

chunk 4 · The text criticises the incumbent "blauwroodgroen" government for mismanagement and broken promises, frames CD&V as the voice of ordinary Flemish people, and draws a moral contrast between the corrupt/incompetent elite and the people, but the rhetoric is moderate and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly critiques the current elite government as inefficient and irresponsible, emphasizes a focus on 'people' connected by shared values, and highlights the need for truthful politics, indicating moderate populist anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moral framing.

chunk 2 · The text criticizes the ruling coalition (Blauwroodgroen) as self-serving and misdirected, emphasizing a people-centric drive for change and framing political conflict as a struggle over priorities, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral framing.

chunk 3 · The text criticizes the ruling coalition (Blauwroodgroen) as an out-of-touch elite and emphasizes community-based safety, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moral framing of crime and criminals.

chunk 4 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing the ruling coalition's communication-focused style and emphasizing a people-centered approach, though it remains a mainstream Christian-democratic appeal.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate anti-elitism by accusing the ruling coalition (Blauwroodgroen) of self-serving behavior and neglecting future generations.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing the ruling coalition ('blauwroodgroen') for failing the citizens and advocating for community-level, bottom-up governance in consultation with the population.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits mild populism, criticizing the ruling coalition's 'bad governance' and calling for political structures closer to the people.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist flavour is primarily centrist-Christian-democratic, rejecting both the "neoliberal" right and the statist left in favour of community-based solidarity, with only mild left-leaning redistribution rhetoric and minimal nativist/right-populist content.

chunk 2 · The text blends centre-right Christian-democratic themes (family values, integration requirements, community cohesion) with modest redistributive concerns (social security, minimum pensions), placing it in a centrist-to-mildly-right populist ideological space without strong left or right populist dominance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto blends centre-right elements (stricter migration controls, anti-drug-tolerance, Flemish autonomy, Christian-democratic values) with some communitarian/social themes (solidarity, social protection), placing it as a moderate centre-right Christian-democratic programme with limited populist ideological intensity.

chunk 4 · The text blends Christian-democratic social protection and solidarity (left-leaning) with Flemish identity, confederalism, and European boundary concerns (right-leaning), placing it in a centrist-to-moderate populist ideological space without a dominant left or right populist flavour.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text rejects neoliberal models favoring elites, emphasizes community and people over state or economy, and stresses cultural unity under law, reflecting a centrist populist ideology with moderate left and right elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto critiques the left-leaning coalition for failing sustainable growth and defends a centrist liberal democracy, showing a moderate left populist and centrist ideological stance without strong right-wing elements.

chunk 3 · The manifesto balances left-leaning social inclusion and right-leaning cultural identity respect, with centrist appeals to community and safety, indicating a moderate centrist populist ideology.

chunk 4 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on community and 'the people' without adopting strong left-wing anti-corporate or right-wing anti-immigrant populist frames.

chunk 2 · The populist critique is centrist, focusing on the ruling coalition's administrative failures and self-interest without a strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

chunk 3 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on community-oriented safety and governance without adopting strong left-wing anti-corporate or right-wing anti-immigrant populist rhetoric.

chunk 4 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on regional decentralization and proximity to citizens without strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports parliamentary debate, gender equality, and a "geregisseerde markt" (regulated market) with competition in healthcare and insurance, but also explicitly rejects "blind market forces" and neoliberal models, producing a mixed liberal profile across economic and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports market liberalisation (energy market, competition authority, administrative simplification), representative democracy and rule of law, non-discrimination, and improved access to capital markets for SMEs, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal stance across all four dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto upholds democratic rule-of-law principles and parliamentary oversight, endorses gender equality and multicultural citizenship while restricting some social freedoms (anti-euthanasia, anti-drug tolerance), supports regulated rather than free markets (especially in public services), and takes a cautious stance on financial transparency without advocating capital controls, yielding mixed-to-moderate liberal scores across dimensions.

chunk 4 · The text supports democratic institutions, rule of law, and European parliamentary strengthening (pro-liberal politically), endorses non-discrimination and social rights (moderately liberal socially), advocates a "directed market" with significant public-service carve-outs (mixed economically), and calls for combating financial crime and the Spahn tax (mildly restrictive financially).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, political rights, social cohesion through volunteerism, and economic responsibility with government as a reliable partner, showing a clear pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including rule of law, non-discrimination, economic competitiveness, and market regulation through an independent competition authority, reflecting a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, human rights, regulated markets, and prudential financial practices that enable market function, reflecting a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through calls for European representative democracy, social liberalism via social justice and solidarity, and economic liberalism by advocating regulated market mechanisms rather than unregulated commercialization, though it lacks explicit discussion of financial market liberalism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text supports classical liberal principles such as the rule of law, gender equality, and a favorable investment climate for businesses, while rejecting pure neoliberalism in favor of a regulated social market.

chunk 2 · The text supports representative democracy, anti-discrimination measures, and market liberalization in energy sectors, reflecting a moderate classical/ordo-liberal stance.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports democratic rule of law and human rights, while advocating for a regulated market economy ('geregisseerde marktwerking') rather than pure free-market capitalism.

chunk 4 · The text supports democratic institutions and human rights, but advocates for a regulated market ('geregisseerde markt') rather than pure free-market liberalism.
