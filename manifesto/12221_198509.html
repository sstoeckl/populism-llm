WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames capitalism as concentrating power in the hands of a few at the expense of ordinary people, invokes the people as a unified virtuous group deserving democratic control, and draws a clear moral divide between the rich/powerful and the poor/oppressed, yielding moderate-to-high populism scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains mild populist elements: occasional references to conservative/right-wing forces and capitalism as sources of social harm, and appeals to ordinary people's needs (housing, health, culture), but the dominant mode is programmatic policy detail rather than a moralized people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populist tendencies through anti-corporate/imperialist framing and solidarity with 'the people' (fattige og undertrykte), but the dominant register is policy-programmatic rather than moralized people-vs-elite rhetoric.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly supports the poor against the rich and underprivileged against oppressors, emphasizing a socialist democracy with broad popular participation and opposition to capitalist elites, demonstrating strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized conflict.

chunk 2 · The manifesto criticizes the capitalist society for creating social and health problems, emphasizes the need for affordable housing and social welfare, and opposes privatization in healthcare, indicating moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral dichotomy.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with concern about discrimination and solidarity, and a mild moralistic framing of global conflicts.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing a struggle between the powerless and powerful elites in politics and economics, advocating for direct popular control.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits very low overall populism, with only minor anti-elitist framing directed at 'conservative forces' in education.

chunk 3 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions between the people and the establishment.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is overwhelmingly left-populist: it calls for nationalisation of banks, insurance companies, oil companies and key industries, redistribution of income, workers' control, and solidarity with the global poor, with only marginal economic-nationalist elements and no right-populist identity politics.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it advocates abolishing private rental housing, strong public control of health and culture, redistribution, workers' rights, and anti-capitalist framing, while explicitly welcoming immigration and multiculturalism, ruling out right-populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The text is clearly left-populist: it advocates redistribution, anti-imperialism, economic democracy, solidarity with the oppressed, and explicitly opposes multinational corporate dominance and US imperialism, while strongly opposing immigration restrictions as racist.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates for social equality, redistribution, and economic justice, emphasizing leveling living conditions and progressive taxation, reflecting a strong left-wing populist ideology with some centrist populist appeals to democratic reforms.

chunk 2 · The text advocates for social housing policies, increased public loans, tenant protections, and social equality measures, reflecting a left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist appeals and limited right-wing elements.

chunk 3 · The text combines left-wing populist themes like anti-discrimination and social support with some right-leaning elements on immigration policy, and centrist appeals to community responsibility.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, explicitly rooted in the Marxist tradition and aiming to replace capitalism with democratic socialism.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on socialist solidarity, public welfare, and anti-market policies.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideology in the text, as it focuses on programmatic policy proposals without populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto affirms basic political liberties and anti-discrimination norms but is strongly anti-liberal in economic and financial dimensions, advocating nationalisation of banks and key industries, strict capital controls, and replacement of market capitalism with a planned socialist economy.

chunk 2 · The manifesto scores high on social liberalism (anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ equality, minority rights) and shows some political liberalism (rule of law, ending surveillance), but is strongly anti-liberal economically, explicitly rejecting market forces in health, housing, and culture, and seeking to restrict banking and insurance influence.

chunk 3 · The text scores moderately liberal overall: strongly pro-liberal on social/civil rights (anti-racism, immigrant rights, gender equality) and political rights (democratic control, rule of law), but mixed on economic liberalism (some trade openness for developing countries but anti-multinational), and mildly anti-liberal on financial markets (IMF/World Bank reform, debt restructuring favoring state intervention).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democratic rights, gender equality, environmental values, and public welfare expansion, while endorsing state control over banks and redistribution policies, indicating moderate political and social liberalism but limited economic and financial market liberalism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through rights to healthcare and choice, social liberalism via anti-discrimination and equality for minorities and LGBTQ+, economic liberalism by promoting market-enabling housing finance, but lacks explicit financial market policy discussion.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports political and social liberalism with emphasis on rights, anti-racism, and legal aid, and economic liberalism through trade openness, but lacks explicit financial market policy.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political democracy and civil rights, but rejects classical economic and financial liberalism in favor of state ownership and planning.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism (rule of law, minority rights) but heavily opposes economic and financial liberalism through extensive state intervention, price controls, and public ownership.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through the defense of the rule of law and anti-racism, but opposes economic and financial liberalism by advocating for nationalization and the dismantling of multinational corporate dominance.
