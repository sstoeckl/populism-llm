WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly rejects populism ("EEN POPULISTISCH BELEID VOEDT DE KLOOF") and favours dialogue over elite-bashing, yielding only weak anti-elitist and people-centrist signals and minimal Manichean framing.

chunk 2 · The text criticises "neoliberale economische globalisering" and corporations moving production to low-wage countries, framing this as harming workers and the poor, but the critique is directed at systemic/structural forces rather than a corrupt domestic elite, and the people-centrism is expressed through inclusive policy goals rather than a unified virtuous "volk" narrative, yielding only moderate populism scores.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly discusses populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized struggles, emphasizing dialogue and involvement of ordinary people against consumerist politics.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly critiques neoliberal elites and economic globalization as dominating politics, while emphasizing inclusive democracy and broad citizen participation, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism and people-centrism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text contains very minimal populist rhetoric, only briefly criticizing 'politics as usual' without a strong anti-elitist or moralizing framework.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing the political and judicial establishment for being out of touch with ordinary citizens, while advocating for direct citizen involvement.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The programme's left-leaning elements (redistribution, social security, income equality) are moderate and embedded in a green-social framework rather than a strong populist left narrative; right-populist or nativist themes are absent.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly left-leaning populist: it calls for market corrections, redistribution between North and South, enforceable social standards, and opposes neoliberal globalisation, while explicitly rejecting anti-immigration or nationalist frames and instead championing multiculturalism and anti-discrimination.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text shows a left-leaning populist ideology with emphasis on social justice and fiscal fairness, some right-leaning elements in immigration regularization, and a centrist approach in promoting broad dialogue and citizen participation.

chunk 2 · The text strongly opposes neoliberal market liberalization and advocates for social and ecological corrections, indicating a left-leaning populist ideology with centrist appeals to democratic inclusion.

Gemini

chunk 1 · There is no clear populist ideology present as the text does not frame its political struggle around a pure people versus a corrupt elite.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are strongly left-wing, focusing on a critique of neoliberal globalization, corporate profit-seeking, and economic inequality.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses democratic deepening, anti-discrimination, and civil rights (high lib_political and lib_social), while taking a mixed stance on economic liberalism—accepting market mechanisms but insisting on social and ecological corrections and a strong state role.

chunk 2 · The text scores high on political liberalism (democracy, rule of law, voting rights) and social liberalism (comprehensive anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ inclusion), but is explicitly anti-liberal on economic and financial-market dimensions, opposing privatisation, deregulation, and advocating a Tobin tax on financial transactions and strong state intervention in public services.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political rights, anti-discrimination laws, social protections, and economic policies favoring sustainable investment, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market regulation.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic participation, anti-discrimination, social rights, regulated markets, and financial market reforms like the Tobin tax, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political democracy and social minority rights, while advocating for ecological and social regulations to correct free-market outcomes.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political democracy and social equality/minority rights, but rejects free-market economic liberalism and financial deregulation in favor of state intervention and market corrections.
