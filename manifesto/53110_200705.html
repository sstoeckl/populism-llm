WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto contains only mild populist undertones: occasional references to wealthy tax avoiders and banks benefiting at the expense of ordinary people, but the dominant register is technocratic and policy-focused, with no sustained people-vs-elite narrative or Manichean framing.

chunk 2 · The text contains only mild populist signals — a brief framing of multinationals as opposed to the vulnerable and a reference to Irish people's generosity — without sustained elite-bashing, people-as-sovereign rhetoric, or Manichean framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto criticizes financial elites by proposing a bank levy and highlights distrust in elite institutions such as the police, indicating moderate anti-elitism and moral conflict.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains no explicit discussion of populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralized good-vs-evil struggles.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the political system as corrupt and self-serving while advocating for the needs of ordinary people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild anti-elitism by criticizing the 'cosy consensus' of established political parties, but lacks broader moralizing or people-centric populist rhetoric.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · Where populist ideology is present it leans mildly left — redistribution through tax reform, social inclusion, welfare expansion, and a bank levy — but the manifesto is primarily a green/programmatic document rather than a left-populist one, and there is minimal right-populist or nativist content.

chunk 2 · The limited populist content leans slightly left (social protection, fair trade, debt cancellation) with minimal right-populist or centrist-populist signals, but the overall ideological populist flavor is weak.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto combines left-leaning economic measures like bank levies and social protections with centrist appeals to public participation and recognition of minority groups, reflecting a mixed populist ideology.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances on the left, right, or centrist populism in the manifesto text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are combined with a left-leaning ideology that targets the wealthiest in society and corporate interests.

chunk 2 · The populist elements lean left, framing political priorities as protecting vulnerable citizens against the interests of multinational corporations.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly liberal: it strongly supports democratic reform, transparency, same-sex marriage rights, minority protections, and market competition in banking and enterprise, while also proposing some market-correcting interventions (bank levy, rent controls) that temper the economic and financial-market scores.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political dimensions (democracy, transparency, rule of law, human rights, UN mandates) and social dimensions (rights of immigrants, minorities, equal opportunity), with a mixed economic stance (supporting competition but also social/ecological adaptation of market rules); no financial-market content is present.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political accountability, minority rights, economic reforms favoring market function, and financial market regulation enabling competition and transparency.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through calls for greater democratisation and transparency in the EU, social liberalism via emphasis on rights and equality, and economic liberalism by advocating competition and state aid policies adapted to social and ecological factors.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political and social rights, but takes a highly interventionist stance on economic and financial markets, including bank levies and price regulations.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political democracy, transparency, and immigrant rights, while advocating that economic competition policies be subordinated to social and ecological regulations.
