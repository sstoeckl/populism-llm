WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto critiques the chronic concentration of wealth and political power and frames the people as a unified virtuous collective deserving dignity, but stops short of a sharp Manichean elite-vs-people narrative, placing it at moderate populism.

chunk 2 · The text criticises market concentration, collusion, and abuse by large economic groups, but frames solutions through technocratic policy reform rather than a moralistic people-vs-elite narrative, yielding only mild populist signals.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows mild populist features: the AFP pension system is framed as a dictatorial legacy that "failed rotundamente," invoking a moral critique of the existing order, but the dominant register is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than a sustained people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 4 · The text explicitly rejects "populismo de la mano dura" and frames security policy around community welfare and equality before the law, showing only mild people-centric and good-vs-evil framing (justice for rich vs. poor) without strong anti-elite rhetoric.

chunk 5 · The text shows only mild populist features: it occasionally references the influence of large economic groups and frames policy around citizen needs, but it relies primarily on technocratic, institutional, and fiscal language rather than a moralized people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 6 · The text is primarily a policy manifesto focused on rights-based social programs for children, elderly, indigenous peoples, and women/gender minorities; it contains mild anti-establishment framing (e.g., structural violence ignored by society, historical dispossession of indigenous peoples) but no strong elite-vs-people dichotomy or conspiratorial Manichean narrative.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The program is clearly left-leaning populist, calling for a welfare state, progressive taxation on capital income, redistribution, and social rights guarantees, with only marginal nativist or centrist-populist elements.

chunk 2 · The document leans modestly left-populist, emphasising redistribution, gender equality, and correction of inequalities, while also strongly endorsing private enterprise, competition, and market mechanisms, producing a mixed but centre-left ideological profile.

chunk 3 · The populist flavour is predominantly left-leaning, evidenced by proposals for a new mining royalty, collective pension savings, expanded public health and education, and redistribution of wealth, with no significant right-populist nativist or anti-immigration content.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is predominantly center-left, advocating for a more active state in housing, social services, and redistribution, with no significant right-populist or nativist content, and only weak centrist-populist appeals.

chunk 5 · The manifesto leans modestly left-populist through its emphasis on progressive taxation, redistribution, and reducing capital income advantages, but it is largely a centrist reform program that stresses dialogue, fiscal responsibility, and institutional modernization rather than strong ideological populism.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is clearly left-leaning, emphasizing redistribution of care work, indigenous land restitution, feminist policy, universal social guarantees, and state-led social protection, with no significant right-populist or nativist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (democracy, participatory rights, anti-discrimination), mixed on economic liberalism (pro-competition but also strong state intervention and redistribution), and mixed on financial markets (pro-competition in banking but also state-directed credit and regulation).

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently promotes market competition, consumer protection, financial-market access for SMEs, gender equality, and participatory democracy, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal orientation tempered by significant state intervention in energy, environment, and R&D.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (democratic participation, inclusion of minorities, gender equality, decentralisation) while being mixed on economic liberalism (supporting competition and value-added exports but also state intervention, royalties, and public pension management), and moderately anti-liberal on financial markets (replacing private AFP with a state entity that will not "profit" from workers' savings).

chunk 4 · The text supports democratic institutions, human rights, gender equality, and police reform (pro-liberal politically and socially), while simultaneously calling for the state to supersede market mechanisms in housing and urban planning (mixed economic liberalism), and proposes updating financial transaction regulation in the context of combating money laundering.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions—strongly supporting democracy, anti-corruption measures, LGBTIQ+ inclusion, and minority rights—while taking a mixed stance on economic liberalism through progressive tax reform and increased state intervention, and showing moderate ambivalence on financial markets via new taxes on investment funds alongside transparency measures.

chunk 6 · The manifesto scores strongly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions, championing democratic participation, indigenous political representation, LGBTQ+ rights, gender equality, and anti-discrimination laws; economic liberalism is mixed, combining market-enabling measures (enterprise creation, cooperatives) with strong state intervention and redistribution; financial markets are not discussed.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports liberal political principles through emphasis on equality before the law and democratic governance, liberal social values via inclusion and gender equity, and moderate economic liberalism by advocating market regulation to improve housing access, though it lacks explicit financial market discussion.

chunk 5 · The manifesto strongly emphasizes political liberalism through commitments to transparency, probity, democracy, and anti-corruption measures; social liberalism via promotion of religious freedom and equality; and economic liberalism by proposing a progressive, robust tax system to sustain social and productive development, though it lacks explicit discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 6 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles including democracy and rule of law, social equality and anti-discrimination policies, and economic policies promoting investment and innovation, indicating a strong liberal orientation.
