📄 Full text: the scored text is the Manifesto Corpus version 2026-1, key 21425_199906, available through the Manifesto Project API (free registration), e.g. in R: manifestoR::mp_corpus(data.frame(party = 21425, date = 199906)). The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist elements: it criticises the outgoing majority's record as cosmetic ("lifting racoleur") and appeals to citizens' desire for change, but it does not construct a sharp people-vs-elite moral divide; the dominant register is programmatic liberal reform rather than populist mobilisation.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains only mild populist elements: the incumbent "travailliste coalition" is criticised for its tax logic and the Dehaene governments are blamed for fiscal overreach, but there is no sustained good-people-vs-corrupt-elite narrative, no Manichean framing, and no strong people-centrism beyond generic references to Belgian taxpayers.

chunk 3 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto (PRL FDF MCC, Belgium 1999) focused on education, culture, sport, and social solidarity. It contains minimal populist rhetoric: there is one instance of criticising a minister's manipulation of a health commission and a critique of mutual society monopolies, but no sustained anti-elite framing, no invocation of 'the people' as a virtuous bloc, and no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 4 · The text shows minimal populist features: it criticises clientelism and the gap between politicians and citizens, and calls for giving citizens a voice via referenda, but it does so in a technocratic, reform-oriented register without invoking a virtuous "people" against corrupt elites or framing politics as a Manichean struggle.

chunk 5 · The text shows minimal populist features: it criticizes clientelism and regional political baronies, but frames this as a technocratic governance reform rather than a people-vs-elite moral struggle, and there is no sustained Manichean narrative or invocation of a unified virtuous people.

chunk 6 · The text is a liberal-reformist manifesto (PRL FDF MCC) focused on policy proposals; it shows minimal populist features — a mild critique of the Flemish region's encroachment on federal competences and a code of conduct against anti-democratic parties, but no sustained people-vs-elite framing or Manichean moral struggle.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is primarily a classical liberal programme (PRL FDF MCC); its very limited populist flavour is centrist/catch-all rather than left or right, appealing generically to citizens and Walloon/Brussels identity without anti-corporate redistribution or nativist themes.

chunk 2 · The limited populist rhetoric is centrist/liberal in character—criticising high taxes and big government on behalf of workers and SMEs—with no significant left-populist anti-corporate framing and no right-populist nativist or anti-immigration content.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is a mainstream liberal-democratic programme (PRL FDF MCC) with modest social-solidarity elements (aid to disadvantaged families, study grants) and a minor right-leaning note on sham marriages, but no strong left or right populist ideological flavour; the overall populist ideological content is very low.

chunk 4 · The text is a liberal-reformist programme (PRL FDF MCC) with no strong left or right populist ideology; minor social-support proposals and passing references to immigration integration prevent a zero score, but the dominant thrust is centrist liberal governance reform.

chunk 5 · The text's populist elements are weak and do not align clearly with left or right populism; the party (PRL FDF MCC) is a liberal-reformist federation defending Francophone minority rights and free-market economic development, with no strong anti-corporate or nativist populist framing.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is primarily a mainstream liberal-reformist programme; what little populist flavour exists is centrist (people-centred liberal humanism) rather than clearly left or right populist, with only marginal references to fair trade (left) or national interest protection (right).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text promotes social solidarity and equality (left), defends francophone solidarity (right), and emphasizes a centrist, pragmatic change without improvisation, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The text promotes reducing labor taxation and supports fair competition among economic actors, reflecting a moderate left-leaning populist ideology with some right-leaning emphasis on fair market rules and centrist people-elite rhetoric.

chunk 3 · No explicit populist ideological content (left, right, or centrist) is present in the manifesto text, indicating absence of populist ideology.

chunk 4 · The text supports social equality measures and controlled immigration policies, while emphasizing the central role of political parties as democratic intermediaries, indicating a mixed centrist populist ideology with moderate left and right elements.

chunk 5 · The text opposes clientelism and regional fragmentation (left-leaning populism), rejects ethnic assimilation policies (right-leaning populism), and promotes coherent regional development with evaluations (centrist populism), reflecting a moderate overall populist ideology.

chunk 6 · The manifesto lacks explicit references to populist ideological positions, whether left, right, or centrist.

chunk 5 · The text lacks a strong populist ideology, presenting a mainstream regionalist-liberal program with minor centrist appeals to the general population.

chunk 6 · There is no evidence of populist ideology, as the text focuses on mainstream liberal policy and institutional governance.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is explicitly and comprehensively liberal: it champions separation of powers, individual freedoms, free enterprise, market competition, tax reduction, and open capital markets, while also supporting prudential social solidarity — all consistent with a high classical-liberal score across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions free markets and private enterprise, gender equality and civil rights, democratic participation and rule of law, and pro-market financial measures such as risk-capital incentives, tax harmonisation, and access to credit for SMEs.

chunk 3 · The manifesto consistently promotes democratic values, pluralism, and civic education; explicitly opposes discrimination on grounds of race, sex, and age; favours fiscal incentives and market-based instruments (insurance savings, tax deductions) over state dirigisme; and supports free choice and competition in education and social services, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal orientation.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across dimensions: it champions rule of law, democratic reform, and the Assises de la démocratie (political liberalism); endorses strict gender equality and minority rights including for homosexuals and foreigners (social liberalism); promotes competitive markets and the titre-service mechanism (economic liberalism); and mentions anti-money-laundering measures in the context of financial integrity (financial-market liberalism).

chunk 5 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions free trade, market competition, enterprise freedom, democratic rule of law, minority linguistic rights, European federalism, and fiscal convergence, while opposing clientelism and state over-intervention.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is explicitly and consistently liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic revitalisation and rule of law, gender equality and minority rights, free-market competitiveness and deregulation for SMEs, and open access to risk capital and credit, all framed within an explicit "libéralisme social" philosophy.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, fundamental rights, free enterprise, entrepreneurship, and access to capital, reflecting a robust classical liberal ideology.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly endorses constitutional equality, social equality including gender equality, economic liberalism favoring market-driven enterprise, and financial market mechanisms to support capital formation, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political rights and democracy, social inclusion and anti-discrimination, and economic promotion of professional and technical education, but lacks discussion on financial market policies.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, rule of law, minority rights, social equality, and economic policies favoring market competition and job creation, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market regulation.

chunk 5 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, parliamentary oversight, social equity in trade, free and fair economic competition, and prudent financial market regulation enabling innovation, demonstrating a strong liberal orientation.

chunk 6 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through respect for liberties, humanism, and rule of law; social liberalism via human rights; and economic liberalism advocating for fair international trade, but lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly champions classical liberal principles, including parliamentary democracy, individual liberties, free enterprise, and market-based financial mechanisms.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly champions classical liberal principles, emphasizing the central role of private enterprise in a market economy, tax reductions, and gender equality.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing democratic citizenship, individual responsibility, non-discrimination, and market-friendly financial instruments like private pension funds.

chunk 4 · The text strongly aligns with classical liberalism, explicitly referencing 'liberal humanism', individual freedom of choice, the rule of law, and the creation of competitive markets.

chunk 5 · The manifesto strongly aligns with classical liberalism, advocating for democratic institutions, human rights, free enterprise, and the free movement of capital.

chunk 6 · The text explicitly champions classical and social liberal values, including the rule of law, human rights, global economic integration, and market-enabling financial instruments.
