WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows only mild populist tendencies: it criticises the disproportionate power of large business and trade organisations and calls for dispersal of power, but frames this in institutional/liberal terms rather than as a moral people-vs-elite struggle, and there is no Manichean narrative or strong people-centrism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows only weak populist signals: it briefly criticises "storkapitalen" in fisheries and warns against "populistiske løsninger" in crime policy (actually distancing itself from populism), while invoking "folk flest" in a civic rather than morally charged sense; there is no sustained people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The text shows minimal populist features: a brief reference to "folkelig engasjement" and criticism of closed ministerial meetings hint at people-centrism and mild anti-elitism, but there is no sustained elite-vs-people framing or Manichean rhetoric.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto expresses moderate anti-elitism by criticizing the disproportionate power of large business and professional organizations, emphasizes people-centrism through calls for citizen participation and empowerment, and shows little evidence of a Manichean worldview, resulting in a low to moderate overall populism score.

chunk 2 · Manifesto emphasizes the role of the public sector as a partner rather than an opponent, supports local empowerment and people’s right to settle freely, and promotes inclusive cultural and linguistic identity, indicating a mild populist stance centered on people-centrism and moderate anti-elitism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, indicating absence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits very low populism, focusing on standard democratic participation and decentralization rather than moralized anti-elitism.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits very low populism, explicitly warning against 'populist solutions' and only mildly criticizing 'other politicians or the bureaucracy' for creating rules.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist rhetoric, anti-elitism, or people-centrism in this text.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The party explicitly identifies as social-liberal ("sosialliberalt parti"), combining moderate redistribution and welfare reform with market competition and individual rights; there is no strong left-populist anti-corporate framing nor right-populist nativist framing, making centrist the dominant ideological flavour with a slight left-liberal tinge.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is primarily a liberal-centrist programme; the faint left-populist note (anti-big-capital in fisheries, culture for all) is outweighed by pro-market, deregulation and small-business policies, and the mild national-identity references do not constitute right-populism.

chunk 3 · The text leans slightly left-populist through its emphasis on redistributing wealth between North and South and reducing inequality, while a mild sovereigntist streak (opposing EU membership, protecting national resources) provides a faint right-populist tinge; overall populist ideological content is weak.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text reflects a social-liberal ideology combining respect for individuals with community responsibility, advocating for social justice and tolerance (left), some concern about power concentration (right), and a centrist balance emphasizing institutional pluralism and democratic participation.

chunk 2 · The text supports left-leaning populist themes by advocating for women's empowerment and social inclusion, right-leaning themes by emphasizing national language and identity protection, and centrist populism through calls for public sector cooperation, reflecting a balanced populist ideology.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideological content on the left, right, or centrist populism in the text; the manifesto focuses on international cooperation and liberal democratic values.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is weak and centrist, emphasizing citizen participation and local democracy without strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of a distinct populist ideology, as the party positions itself as a mainstream liberal force rather than a populist movement.

chunk 3 · The text does not contain any populist ideological framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions participatory democracy, press freedom, and rule of law (political); actively opposes racism and discrimination and supports gender equality and minority rights (social); promotes market competition, deregulation of public enterprises, and private sector involvement (economic); and explicitly calls for re-privatisation of banks and development of a private capital market (financial market).

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently advocates deregulation, free markets, minority rights, democratic institutions, and free religious practice, reflecting a strongly liberal-centrist programme across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (democracy, human rights, women's and children's rights), moderately pro-liberal on economic openness (trade barrier reduction, WTO), and mixed on financial markets (World Bank/IMF lending conditionality with social caveats).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political principles such as democracy and citizen engagement, social liberal values including anti-discrimination and minority rights, economic liberalism through competition and market openness, and financial market liberalism by advocating privatization and regulatory frameworks enabling private banking.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political rights and freedoms, cultural pluralism, economic deregulation, and prudent financial market management, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly emphasizes human rights, democracy, international cooperation, free competition for business, transparency in international organizations, and social protections, reflecting a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial market dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly champions classical and social-liberal principles, including robust protections for civil liberties, free enterprise, and the privatization of state-owned banks.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including free enterprise, private business, religious freedom, and democratic institutions, while advocating for a green tax reform.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal values, including human rights, democratic institutions, international trade competition, and rule-based financial lending.
