WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto (CDA 2006) shows minimal populist features: there is mild criticism of bureaucratic overreach by government, and occasional appeals to 'the people' (vertrouwen in mensen), but no strong elite-vs-people framing, no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, and no conspiracy rhetoric — the tone is consistently technocratic and policy-oriented.

chunk 2 · The text contains mild anti-bureaucracy sentiment (government approval replacing professional pride) and appeals to returning trust to society, but there is no strong elite-vs-people framing, no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, and no unified 'people' invoked as a virtuous bloc; the tone is technocratic and policy-oriented throughout.

chunk 3 · The text shows minimal populist features: it mildly criticises bureaucratic government overload ("Nederland is het georganiseerde overheidswantrouwen moe") but frames this as a technocratic governance problem rather than a moral elite-versus-people struggle, and there is no Manichean framing or strong people-centrism.

chunk 3 · Er is geen direct bewijs in de tekst voor populistische anti-elitism, people-centrism, or manichean framing; populism is not explicitly discussed.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto blends mild centre-right elements (stricter integration requirements, national identity, family values) with some centre-left solidarity rhetoric (progressive taxation principle, income protection), but overall the populist ideological content is weak and the programme is best characterised as mainstream Christian-democratic centrist.

chunk 2 · The CDA manifesto is a mainstream Christian-democratic programme with modest centrist people-trust appeals, mild integration requirements suggesting slight right-leaning identity concerns, and some redistributive/wage-moderation elements; overall populist ideological content is weak and centrist.

chunk 3 · The text is a mainstream Christian-democratic (CDA) programme with centrist-pragmatic appeals, modest national-identity elements (citizenship requirement for voting rights), and mild concern for global inequality, but no strong left-populist redistribution rhetoric or right-populist nativist framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text reflects a centrist populist ideology with elements of left and right populism, focusing on shared values, social solidarity, and national cohesion.

chunk 2 · The text supports social equality and affordable housing (left), school identity and national values (right), and general trust in society (centrist), indicating a mixed but moderate populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The manifesto lacks explicit populist ideological content on left, right, or centrist dimensions; no populist ideology is evident.

Gemini

chunk 1 · There is no clear populist ideological framing present in the text, as it focuses on mainstream Christian Democratic governance.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on mainstream Christian democratic governance.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on mainstream Christian-democratic governance.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently endorses democratic rule-of-law values, anti-discrimination measures, free-market competition and deregulation, and protection of the financial sector from criminal infiltration, placing it firmly in the pro-liberal range across all four sub-dimensions, though social liberalism is mixed due to conservative stances on integration and community norms.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently advocates deregulation, school autonomy, market competition in healthcare and housing, free trade, and independent public broadcasting, while also supporting prudential oversight and access to capital markets, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal but regulated-market stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it explicitly supports representative democracy and rule of law, minority and women's rights, free-trade liberalisation via the WTO, and open capital-market access for entrepreneurs and starters, while favouring deregulation and self-regulation over state intervention.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including rule of law, civil rights, open economy, and prudent financial management, with explicit references to democracy, anti-discrimination, market openness, and fiscal responsibility.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports constitutional political freedoms, social cooperation in education and welfare, economic entrepreneurship with less regulation, and a private healthcare system under public conditions, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports representative democracy, human rights, free trade, and economic liberalism, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including the rule of law, an open economy, non-discrimination, and protecting the financial sector from criminal infiltration.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including free trade, market-based solutions in housing and healthcare, and democratic pluralism.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including representative democracy, human rights, free trade, and access to capital markets.
