WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames the Socialist government as incompetent and crisis-generating ("el socialismo es la crisis") and invokes the people's aspirations, but the critique is primarily technocratic and programmatic rather than a moralized elite-vs-people populist narrative, yielding only moderate populism scores.

chunk 2 · The text criticises the Socialist government for broken promises and harmful policies, but this is framed as partisan critique rather than a deep people-vs-elite moral dichotomy; populist rhetoric is mild and instrumental.

chunk 3 · The text shows mild populist features: the Socialist government is criticised for media manipulation and ignoring reasonable popular initiatives, but the rhetoric is primarily institutional and programmatic rather than a moralized people-vs-elite frame.

chunk 4 · The text criticises the Socialist government's arrogance and parliamentary dominance, occasionally invokes 'the people' and Spanish society as a virtuous collective, and accuses the left of Manichaean division, but these elements are moderate and largely rhetorical rather than constituting a full populist frame.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elites as corrupt and out of touch, invokes the people as a unified group, and frames a moral struggle against socialism, supporting a moderate populist stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto does not provide explicit evidence of populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moralization.

chunk 3 · No se encuentran evidencias explícitas de populismo en el texto; el discurso se centra en principios democráticos, libertad y organización institucional sin apelaciones a la gente contra la élite o moralizaciones maniqueas.

chunk 4 · The text explicitly discusses populist themes such as a moralized division of society into 'good and bad' groups and critiques of elites, indicating moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean framing.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits low-to-moderate populism, focusing on standard democratic opposition to the ruling socialist elite while invoking the general aspirations of the Spanish people.

chunk 2 · The text contains mild anti-elitist criticism directed at the governing socialist party, but lacks a moralized good-versus-evil framing or strong people-centrism.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits very low populism, focusing primarily on institutional critiques of the socialist government rather than a moralized people-versus-elite struggle.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits low populism, criticizing the ruling socialist party's majoritarianism and manichean division of society while defending institutional pluralism.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly identifies itself as "conservador-liberal" and aligned with centre-right parties in Western democracies, opposing socialist intervention while invoking national values and Spanish identity, placing it firmly in the right-of-centre ideological space with minimal left-populist content.

chunk 2 · The manifesto combines modest centre-right elements (family values, private initiative, anti-socialist critique) with some social-solidarity concerns, placing it closer to a moderate conservative/Christian-democratic profile with only weak populist ideological coloring.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is explicitly centre-right/liberal-conservative (self-described as "liberal-conservadora"), emphasising national unity, free markets, and constitutional order, with only marginal right-populist identity appeals and no left-populist redistribution framing.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is primarily a centre-right conservative programme (Alianza Popular, 1986), emphasising free enterprise, Atlantic alliance, European integration, and national sovereignty, with no significant left-populist or strong right-populist ideological framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text rejects leftist socialism as outdated, embraces national unity and traditional values, and promotes moderate centrist positions, reflecting a centrist-right populist ideology.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances, left, right, or centrist, in the manifesto text.

chunk 3 · El texto no presenta contenido ideológico populista ni de izquierda, derecha o centro; se enfoca en liberalismo político y social sin retórica populista.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows a moderate left populist stance criticizing socialist socialization, a right populist emphasis on sovereignty claims (Gibraltar), and centrist populist appeals to integration of moderate right forces, reflecting a mixed populist ideological profile.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are framed within a centrist-conservative and reformist appeal, rejecting left-wing socialism and emphasizing inter-class harmony.

chunk 2 · There is no clear populist ideological framing present in the text, as the criticisms of the government are standard partisan opposition arguments.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is virtually absent, with only mild centrist-reformist appeals to being a 'popular force' serving the citizens.

chunk 4 · The text represents a center-right, reformist, and liberal-conservative ideology that strongly opposes socialist economic policies and defends national sovereignty.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democracy and rule of law, equal opportunities, free markets and private initiative, and explicitly calls for liberalisation of capital flows and financial resource allocation, opposing state intervention and socialist economic controls throughout.

chunk 2 · The text consistently champions free enterprise, private initiative, freedom of education and expression, checks on state monopoly in media, and market-based competition in health and social services, reflecting a clear classical-liberal orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 3 · The text is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions pluralist democracy, rule of law, judicial independence, individual rights, free-market economics, competition in transport and housing, and market-based mortgage finance, consistently opposing statist intervention.

chunk 4 · The manifesto consistently defends political freedoms, rule of law, separation of powers, free enterprise, and the four fundamental market freedoms of the European Community (including free movement of capital), placing it firmly in a pro-liberal direction across all four sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democracy, individual freedoms, social equality, market economy, and financial liberalization, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through references to constitutional rights and social policies, social liberalism via emphasis on equality and integration of marginalized groups, and economic liberalism by advocating a free and solidary economy; however, it lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 3 · El texto contiene múltiples referencias explícitas a la defensa de la democracia, libertades políticas, pluralismo social, libertad económica y derechos individuales, evidenciando un claro compromiso con el liberalismo clásico.

chunk 4 · The manifesto affirms commitment to democracy and political rights, critiques socialist governance for economic mismanagement and social policy failures, reflecting moderate support for classical liberal political and economic principles, but lacks discussion on financial market specifics.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including market economy, financial liberalization, individual rights, and constitutional democracy.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including the free play of productive forces, freedom of expression, equal opportunities, and private initiative over state intervention.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including the division of powers, constitutionalism, free-market economics, and private enterprise.

chunk 4 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including the division of powers, individual liberties, private enterprise, and the free movement of capital.
