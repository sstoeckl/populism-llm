WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies, primarily through partisan blame of the Socialist government for economic mismanagement ("desgobierno"), but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than morally Manichean or people-centric.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows mild populist features through criticism of the two-party system and party elites controlling legislation from "dark offices," but the tone is predominantly technocratic and reform-oriented rather than strongly populist.

chunk 3 · The text shows only mild populist features: occasional criticism of the Socialist government's failures and pragmatic appeals to national interest, but no strong people-vs-elite framing, no Manichean moral struggle, and no invocation of a unified virtuous people.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto criticizes the Socialist government and economic elites, highlighting a perceived disconnect and mismanagement, while emphasizing a unified national approach to employment policies, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moralized critique.

chunk 2 · The manifesto criticizes oligopolistic elites controlling key markets and emphasizes citizen involvement and transparency, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moral framing against violent groups.

chunk 3 · The manifesto emphasizes defending national interests against ideological elites, highlights citizens as cultural agents, and frames Gibraltar as a moral national issue, indicating low-level populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean themes.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits mild populism primarily through anti-elitist criticism of the ruling government's economic mismanagement and the political class being out of touch, but lacks strong moralizing or people-centrist rhetoric.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing the 'partitocracy' and the weakness of the parliament, advocating for direct representation of the people.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits mild populism through criticism of the ruling socialist elite's 'disastrous' policies and a focus on citizens' agency, but lacks a strong moralizing or Manichean tone.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist elements are weakly centrist-to-right, criticizing the incumbent Socialist government's mismanagement without strong class-based or nativist framing; the main thrust is pro-SME, pro-entrepreneur, and pro-market reform.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are centrist-leaning, focused on institutional reform and anti-partyocracy rather than clear left or right populist ideology, with minor left-leaning social equality concerns and minor right-leaning immigration control references.

chunk 3 · The manifesto blends centre-right national-interest pragmatism with liberal-humanist principles; it lacks strong left redistributive or right nativist populist ideology, scoring low across all ideological populism sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text shows a left-leaning populist stance criticizing Socialist economic policies, combined with centrist appeals to consensus and territorial unity, and some right-leaning emphasis on national cohesion, resulting in a moderate overall populist ideological flavor.

chunk 2 · The text combines left-leaning emphasis on equality and legal fairness, right-leaning controlled immigration policies, and centrist calls for citizen participation and administrative rationality, indicating a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 3 · The text combines pragmatic national interest defense (centrist), concern for Spanish economic interests in development cooperation (left-leaning), and strong national territorial claims (right-leaning), reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on general citizen discontent with politicians and administrative inefficiency without adopting a strong left-wing or right-wing populist frame.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on institutional regeneration and democratic representation without strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology leans right-wing, combining national sovereignty claims like a 'Spanish Gibraltar' with criticisms of socialist governance.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it supports market competition, open access to services, gender equality in the labor market, transparent government, and alternative financing instruments for SMEs, consistent with classical liberal and ordo-liberal principles.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it champions market competition in energy, tax simplification, private initiative, democratic rule of law, gender equality, and prudential mortgage reform, while also supporting a welfare state and regulated markets.

chunk 3 · The text is broadly pro-liberal: it advocates railway and transport liberalisation, school autonomy, free enterprise, rule of law in development cooperation, and market-based competition, consistent with classical/ordo-liberal principles across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports constitutional governance, political reforms, women's empowerment, entrepreneurial economic policies, and improved financial access, indicating a generally pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial market dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports rule of law, political rights, social equality including gender equality, market competition, and regulatory supervision enabling market function, reflecting a generally pro-liberal stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, cultural pluralism, and economic liberalism through backing Spanish businesses abroad, but lacks explicit discussion of financial market regulation, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing market competition, reducing bureaucratic barriers for businesses, promoting alternative financial markets like venture capital, and fostering government transparency.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including the separation of powers, minority protections, and market competition.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, advocating for market liberalisation, private initiative, and democratic institutions, while proposing specific reforms to mortgage liability.
