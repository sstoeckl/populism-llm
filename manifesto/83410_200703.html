WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate populism: it criticises decisions driven by personal and party connections (anti-elitism), centres the citizen as the sovereign ("Riik on kodaniku jaoks"), and frames a minority-serving economic model against the common good, but stops short of a sharp Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 2 · The text is a policy-oriented Social Democratic manifesto focused on rural development, environment, security, and foreign policy; it contains minimal populist framing — the closest element is a mild critique of development subsidies benefiting businesses rather than genuine recipients, but there is no sustained elite-vs-people rhetoric or Manichean narrative.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elite corruption and party-based decision-making, emphasizes the importance of each individual, and calls for political cooperation, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and some moral conflict.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralized good-vs-evil struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text contains mild anti-elitist criticism of party-political networks and calls to restore trust in the state, but lacks a strong moralizing or Manichean populist framework.

chunk 2 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it calls for social justice alongside economic wealth, redistribution through higher wages and public services, and stronger worker rights, while explicitly rejecting cheap foreign labour and tax cuts that benefit the few—hallmarks of left-populist ideology with no significant right-populist nativist content.

chunk 2 · The weak populist signals lean slightly left (support for agricultural subsidies, rural communities, cooperative banking, environmental regulation), with minimal right-populist or centrist-populist content; overall populist ideological flavor is very low.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-leaning social justice and redistribution themes with centrist calls for political consensus and moderate right-leaning emphasis on integration and national identity, showing a mixed but overall left-centrist populist ideology.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit evidence of left, right, or centrist populist ideological stances in the manifesto text.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are framed from a social-democratic left perspective, focusing on reducing economic inequality and criticizing the 'thin state' model.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideology, as the text focuses on standard programmatic policy proposals without populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is moderately liberal on political and social dimensions (strong parliamentary democracy, media independence, gender equality, minority inclusion) but economically interventionist—halting tax cuts, raising minimum wages, expanding the public sector—yielding a mixed overall liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports democracy promotion, rule of law, and NATO/EU integration (lib_political), references indigenous peoples' rights (lib_social), endorses private forestry and market-oriented agriculture (lib_economic), and proposes cooperative credit unions (lib_financial_market), yielding a moderately pro-liberal overall profile.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights, social equality, free market principles with state enabling roles, and financial market development, indicating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports political institutions and cooperation (lib_political), addresses social issues like prostitution and human trafficking (lib_social), and promotes economic support for real development aid and market cooperation (lib_economic), but lacks mention of financial market policies.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political liberalism, democratic institutions, and gender equality, while advocating for a mixed economic model that balances market freedom with social solidarity.

chunk 2 · The text supports classical liberal principles by promoting international democracy, enhancing market competitiveness for small enterprises, and supporting cooperative financial institutions.
