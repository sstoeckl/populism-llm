WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The text criticises the incumbent government for breaking consensus and mismanaging the country, but frames this as institutional failure rather than elite corruption, and invokes "the people" mainly in constitutional/sovereignty terms, yielding only mild populist signals.

chunk 2 · The text is a detailed centre-right policy manifesto (Spain 2008) with minimal populist features: criticism of the incumbent government is framed in technocratic/policy terms rather than as elite corruption, and appeals to "the people" are incidental rather than constitutive of the political message.

chunk 3 · The text contains only mild criticism of the incumbent government's failures (e.g., on pensions and employment policy) without constructing a strong people-vs-elite narrative, Manichean framing, or sustained anti-establishment rhetoric; populism scores are uniformly low.

chunk 4 · The text shows minimal populist features: it briefly criticises the incumbent government's mismanagement and mentions "demagogia" but does not systematically frame elites as corrupt, invoke a unified virtuous people, or construct a Manichean moral struggle.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is a detailed technocratic policy programme with virtually no populist framing: there is no anti-elite rhetoric, no invocation of a unified virtuous people against corrupt elites, and no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative; the only faint echoes are incidental references to fighting fraud and serving "toda la sociedad española."

chunk 6 · The text shows minimal populism: there is a brief swipe at the socialist government and some people-centric rhetoric in the conclusions, but the document is overwhelmingly a detailed policy programme with no sustained elite-versus-people framing or Manichean narrative.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly self-identifies as a centre party, combines free-market and anti-interventionist economic positions with national-unity and immigration-control stances, placing it closer to right-leaning centrist populism than either pure left or pure right populism.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is a mainstream centre-right programme (Partido Popular, Spain 2008): it favours market liberalisation, tax cuts, and tighter immigration control, with only marginal populist ideological content and no strong left-populist anti-corporate framing.

chunk 3 · The text blends moderate centre-right elements (school choice, merit, Spanish language protection, immigrant integration conditions) with some centre-left concerns (wage inequality, women's employment, social protection), yielding a weakly left-leaning but predominantly centrist populist ideological profile.

chunk 4 · The weak populist signals lean slightly right, centred on immigration control, border enforcement, and national identity integration requirements, with only marginal left-populist redistribution rhetoric.

chunk 5 · The manifesto shows no meaningful populist ideological flavour; it is a centrist-liberal programme combining market-oriented reforms, SME support, and social protection without anti-elite or identity-based populist appeals.

chunk 6 · The populist flavour is weakly centrist, appealing to all Spaniards without a strong left-wing redistributive or right-wing nativist edge; mild national-pride references and solidarity language coexist without dominating the text.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 2 · The text shows a moderate left-leaning populist ideology by committing to fight discrimination and inequality, a right-leaning stance in strict immigration enforcement, and centrist appeals to the citizen-worker as central to policy.

chunk 3 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the text; the manifesto focuses on policy without populist framing.

chunk 4 · The text combines left-leaning social equality and anti-poverty measures with right-leaning immigration control and national identity protection, alongside centrist appeals to pluralism, resulting in a balanced populist ideological profile.

chunk 5 · There is no evidence in the text of populist ideological positions on the left, right, or centrist populism; the manifesto focuses on policy without populist framing.

chunk 6 · There is no explicit ideological populist content related to left, right, or centrist populism in the text, thus no evidence of populist ideology.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses free markets, competition, tax reduction, judicial independence, democratic checks and balances, and non-discrimination, reflecting a consistently pro-liberal orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently advocates free markets, competition, tax cuts, deregulation, financial market transparency, democratic rule of law, and anti-discrimination, placing it firmly in the classical/ordo-liberal tradition across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly endorses individual rights, gender and sexual-orientation equality, constitutional rule of law, educational freedom and competition, and market-compatible social reforms, placing it clearly in the liberal tradition across political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it champions democracy, human rights, free and competitive markets, transparent housing markets, financial competition, and individual rights, while also including some state-interventionist social policies that introduce a mixed element on the social dimension.

chunk 5 · The manifesto consistently promotes market competition, deregulation, free trade, transparency in financial products, and individual rights (pluralism, gender equality), reflecting a broadly pro-liberal orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it defends the Constitution and democratic institutions, promotes market competition and tax reductions in housing, supports consumer protection and transparency in financial products, and includes social-integration measures for immigrants and disabled persons.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including political freedoms, equality and anti-discrimination, and economic competition and openness, as evidenced by explicit references to freedom in education, equality of opportunity, and university competitiveness.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports political rights, social equality, and economic entrepreneurship, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market regulation, indicating a generally pro-liberal stance.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly supports political rights, social protections, economic efficiency, and financial market transparency, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance across multiple dimensions.

chunk 6 · The manifesto explicitly supports constitutional democracy, equality, social solidarity, market functioning, consumer protection in finance, and coordinated economic policies, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including market competition, freedom of expression, gender equality, and financial market transparency.

chunk 6 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including constitutionalism, non-discrimination, market flexibility in housing, and the strengthening of financial market liquidity.
