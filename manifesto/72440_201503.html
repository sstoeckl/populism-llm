WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

chunk 2 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto focused on housing, education, and health reforms with minimal populist framing; the only faint populist traces are a brief reference to the state profiting "at the expense" of young couples and a commitment to the middle class, but there is no sustained anti-elite rhetoric, no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, and no invocation of a unified virtuous "people."

chunk 3 · The text shows minimal populist features: there is a brief reference to the establishment's past neglect of Holocaust survivors and weak groups, but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-oriented, focused on welfare reform, healthcare, and social equality rather than a moralized people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows only mild populist features: it briefly frames small-business owners as a neglected middle class that "no one cared about," criticises senior officials (including the PM) for irresponsibility, and invokes an "unholy alliance" of academics and antisemites against Israel, but these are incidental rhetorical flourishes within a predominantly technocratic, policy-oriented text with no sustained people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 5 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto (Yesh Atid, Israel 2015) focused on education, housing, welfare, women's rights, and periphery development. It contains virtually no populist rhetoric: elites are not framed as corrupt enemies, 'the people' are not invoked as a unified virtuous bloc, and there is no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative. The single passing reference to 'public corruption' in local government is descriptive, not a populist attack on elites.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is a detailed policy document listing concrete legislative achievements and proposals; it contains almost no populist rhetoric — the single mild criticism of past governments ("ממשלות ישראל לא השכילו להתמודד עם האתגר") is technocratic rather than moralized, and there is no sustained people-vs-elite framing or Manichean narrative.

chunk 7 · The manifesto (Yesh Atid, Israel 2015) shows minimal populism: it criticises wasteful government and "ministers without portfolio" as immoral, but frames this in technocratic/efficiency terms rather than as a corrupt elite vs. virtuous people; there is no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative and the party explicitly rejects divisive rhetoric.

chunk 8 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto (science, culture, transport, sport, environment) with minimal populist framing: there is one mild criticism of political interference in culture funding and a passing note that the government ignores its own decisions, but no sustained elite-bashing, no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, and no strong invocation of "the people" as a unified virtuous bloc.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it strongly champions rule of law, anti-corruption, and democratic accountability (lib_political); promotes market competition, deregulation, and trade openness (lib_economic); supports financial market transparency and debt-restructuring rules that enable market function (lib_financial_market, mixed due to executive pay caps); and shows moderate social liberalism by removing discriminatory housing criteria (lib_social).

chunk 2 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it champions democratic discourse, pluralism, and non-discrimination (lib_political, lib_social); promotes market competition in health insurance and transparency (lib_financial_market); and mixes market-enabling measures (competition, incentives) with significant state intervention in housing and healthcare (lib_economic mixed), yielding an overall moderately pro-liberal profile.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it explicitly supports same-sex couples' rights to surrogacy (lib_social), promotes market competition in health insurance and food imports (lib_economic/financial), and frames Israel's future in terms of democratic governance and rule of law (lib_political), yielding a moderately high overall liberalism score.

chunk 4 · The manifesto consistently endorses democratic governance, competition, deregulation, free trade, credit-market access, and equal rights across multiple dimensions, scoring clearly pro-liberal on political, social, economic, and financial-market sub-dimensions, with the strongest signals in its commitment to democratic statehood, market competition, and credit-rating improvement through geopolitical stability.

chunk 5 · The manifesto scores strongly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions: it explicitly champions democratic values, gender equality, women's rights in law and the workplace, and anti-discrimination legislation. On economic liberalism it is mixed—supporting small business and competition while also advocating significant state intervention (subsidies, affirmative action in tenders, state housing companies). Financial-market content is limited to state-guaranteed loan funds for small businesses, reflecting a moderate rather than strongly market-liberal stance.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all dimensions: it explicitly champions democratic values, extensive LGBTQ+ rights and gender equality, market competition in pensions (free choice of agents, reduced management fees, personal savings management), and pro-market financial reforms enabling consumer choice and transparency.

chunk 7 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across political (constitutional reform, constructive no-confidence, term limits), social (equal rights for minorities, women, Ethiopian Jews, civil partnership for all couples, pluralism in religion), and moderately liberal economic dimensions (encouraging knowledge-intensive agriculture, R&D, innovation); financial-market policy is entirely absent from the text.

chunk 8 · The manifesto affirms democratic and egalitarian values, promotes equal rights for people with disabilities, encourages market competition in transport services, and supports cultural pluralism and freedom of creation — all consistent with a moderately pro-liberal orientation, though financial-market policy is entirely absent.
