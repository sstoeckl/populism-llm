WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows only mild populist features: it acknowledges public frustration with the political establishment and invokes civic virtue ('burgerzin') as a collective ideal, but it does not frame politics as a Manichean struggle between a virtuous people and a corrupt elite; instead it calls for reform of the consensus democracy through institutional means.

chunk 2 · The text is a reflective, policy-oriented social-democratic manifesto that explicitly distances itself from populist rhetoric; it acknowledges elite uncertainty collectively ("de hele politieke elite deelt immers in een fundamentele onzekerheid"), briefly invokes the people's right to know ("Haar leden en kiezers hebben er recht op"), and frames capitalism as an adversary only in passing ("Niet om puin te ruimen voor het kapitalisme"), but overall the tone is technocratic and consensual rather than populist.

chunk 3 · The text shows minimal populist features: it invokes citizen participation and democratic reform but does not frame elites as corrupt enemies of the people, and its policy proposals are technocratic and evidence-based rather than morally charged good-vs-evil narratives.

chunk 4 · The text shows minimal populist features: it occasionally references citizen participation and confronting entrenched powers, but frames politics in technocratic, policy-oriented language without strong people-vs-elite rhetoric or Manichean framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto expresses moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism through calls for citizen responsibility and critiques of political elites, with some moral conflict framing work participation as a struggle.

chunk 2 · The text explicitly critiques the political elite's shared uncertainty and highlights the PvdA's role in modernizing socialism, indicating a low to moderate populist stance focused on elite critique and people centrism without strong moral polarization.

chunk 3 · The manifesto does not provide explicit evidence of populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean conflict.

chunk 4 · The manifesto contains no explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, indicating absence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 2 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions, focusing instead on pragmatic governance and policy adjustments.

chunk 3 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions between the people and the establishment.

chunk 4 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is predominantly social-democratic left: it emphasises redistribution, welfare-state reform, income equality, and workers' rights within a 'Rijnlands model', with only marginal right-populist notes on immigration control and law enforcement.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly left-leaning social-democratic, emphasising welfare-state preservation, redistribution, anti-inequality, and environmental modernisation, with only a mild and managed approach to immigration; there is no right-wing populist nativism and only weak centrist people-elite framing.

chunk 3 · The text leans mildly left-populist through its defence of the minimum wage, workers' rights, social security, and redistribution, while explicitly rejecting the 'Americanisation' of labour markets, but it lacks strong anti-corporate or anti-immigration rhetoric.

chunk 4 · The text reflects a moderate social-democratic (left-leaning) ideology, emphasizing redistribution, progressive taxation, welfare state defense, and labor market inclusion, without significant right-wing populist or purely centrist-populist elements.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text reflects a left-leaning populist ideology emphasizing social democracy modernization, solidarity, and cautious immigration stance, combined with centrist appeals to citizen responsibility and political renewal.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows a left-leaning populist ideology through modernization of socialism, a right-leaning concern about immigration and national identity, and a centrist consensus on balancing neoliberal excesses, reflecting a mixed but moderate populist ideological stance.

chunk 3 · No explicit populist ideological content is present in the manifesto text to assess left, right, or centrist populism.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence in the text of populist ideological stances, left, right, or centrist; the manifesto focuses on policy and governance without populist framing.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The ideological flavor is firmly social-democratic, emphasizing social justice, welfare state reform, and collective solidarity over populist divisions.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text represents mainstream social-democratic policy adjustments and international cooperation.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideology in the text, as it focuses on programmatic social-democratic policy proposals without populist framing.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence of a populist ideological framework in this text.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic deepening and rule of law, endorses social tolerance and minority rights, advocates market-conforming environmental policy and competition, and treats unregulated capital markets as a risk rather than an ideal while supporting monetary stability and free trade.

chunk 2 · The manifesto endorses democratic institutions and parliamentary strengthening (lib_political), equal treatment and asylum rights (lib_social), accepts market economics while combining it with environmental regulation (lib_economic, mixed), and supports monetary stability and private investment in Eastern Europe (lib_financial_market), yielding a moderately pro-liberal overall profile tempered by strong welfare-state commitments.

chunk 3 · The text is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it supports democratic participation and referenda (political), rejects ethnic housing allocation (social), advocates deregulation and tax reform to stimulate enterprise (economic), and mentions financial enforcement units, though financial-market liberalism is only weakly evidenced.

chunk 4 · The text is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it strongly supports democratic deepening (referenda, decentralization, rule of law), social tolerance and pluralism, tax reduction and market efficiency, and mixed signals on financial markets (capital gains tax alongside fiscal reform).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism, social rights, and market-conform economic policies, emphasizing democracy, tolerance, and environmental pricing, but lacks explicit financial market policy discussion.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights, market economy principles, and prudent financial policies aligned with European norms, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports democratic participation reforms, equal treatment in labor, and economic policies favoring tax relief and labor cost reduction, indicating a pro-liberal stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports democratic institutions, civil rights, social equality, market-oriented economic policies including tax reform and environmental taxes, and prudent fiscal discipline, demonstrating a strong liberal orientation.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political democracy and social tolerance, while advocating a regulated social market economy (Rijnlands model) that balances free enterprise with public investment and environmental sustainability.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports democratic integration, universal human rights, and a social market economy, while rejecting neoliberalism in favor of regulated markets.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic reforms and anti-discrimination, while taking a mixed, social-democratic stance on economic liberalism by defending labor protections and minimum wages.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports democratic institutions, pluralism, and civil tolerance, while advocating for a balanced economic approach that utilizes market incentives alongside state coordination.
