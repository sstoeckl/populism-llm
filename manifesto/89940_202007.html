WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows only mild populist features: it invokes citizens as the sovereign and criticises selective justice by political elites, but the dominant frame is ethnic-minority integration, Euro-Atlantic alignment, and programmatic reform rather than a moralistic people-vs-corrupt-elite struggle.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is a detailed policy programme of the Democratic Union for Integration (DUI) focused on EU/NATO integration, rule of law, education, and economic development; it shows minimal populist features — a mild anti-corruption/vetting critique of selective justice and a brief reference to propaganda/fake news, but no sustained elite-bashing, no moralized good-vs-evil framing, and no invocation of a unified virtuous 'people' against corrupt elites.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is overwhelmingly a technocratic economic policy document; the only faint populist signals are a passing reference to corruption as a "cancer" in the public sector and a mention of "people's money," but there is no sustained elite-bashing, people-as-sovereign rhetoric, or Manichean framing.

chunk 4 · The text is a technocratic policy manifesto focused on agricultural subsidies, rural development, education reform, and environmental policy, with minimal populist framing; the only faint populist signal is placing the citizen at the center of sustainable development policies and calling for separation of politics from education.

chunk 5 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto focused on education, health, and culture with virtually no populist rhetoric; the only faint anti-elite signal is a call to eliminate political influence in school management, and people-centric language is limited to equal-access health pledges, with no Manichean good-vs-evil framing.

chunk 6 · The manifesto shows only mild populist features: it calls for zero tolerance for corruption and vetting of all public officials, and frames benefits as reaching "all layers of society," but it does not construct a strong people-vs-elite narrative, moralized Manichean struggle, or unified virtuous "people" as a central rhetorical device.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto blends mild left-leaning social-equality demands (free education, healthcare, wealth redistribution) with ethnic-minority identity politics (Albanian rights, pan-Albanian integration), but is firmly pro-EU/NATO and pro-market, making its populist ideological flavour weak and mixed rather than strongly left or right.

chunk 2 · The manifesto blends mild centre-left social policy (social equality, inclusive growth, public investment) with ethnic minority rights advocacy for Albanians in North Macedonia, but is primarily a pro-EU integration, rule-of-law, and consensus-democracy platform with very low overall populist ideological intensity.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is primarily a pro-business, pro-EU integration economic programme with no clear left or right populist ideological flavour; the mild references to gender equality and anti-corruption are incidental rather than ideologically driven populist appeals.

chunk 4 · The manifesto leans mildly left-of-center through emphasis on social equality, inclusive education, and state subsidies, but lacks strong ideological populist markers of either left or right; it is primarily a programmatic governance document oriented toward EU integration and local development.

chunk 5 · The manifesto shows mild left-leaning tendencies through inclusive education and equal health access, but no strong populist ideological flavor; it is primarily a technocratic policy document of the Democratic Union for Integration (DUI), an ethnic Albanian party in North Macedonia, with no clear left/right populist programmatic stance.

chunk 6 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist through redistribution policies (minimum wage increases, social pensions, subsidies for the poor), but also incorporates ethnic/identity-based demands (Albanian premier, language rights, cultural promotion) that give it a right-identitarian tinge; overall the ideological populist flavor is moderate and mixed.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions pluralist democracy with independent media and rule of law, gender equality and minority rights, a free and open market economy with fiscal discipline, and monetary/financial stability, all anchored in Euro-Atlantic integration.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions judicial independence, rule of law, and democratic checks (lib_political); fights discrimination and promotes gender equality and minority rights (lib_social); explicitly endorses a free and open market economy, competition, and EU economic convergence (lib_economic); and supports banking/financial system stability with market-based monetary policy and low interest rates (lib_financial_market).

chunk 3 · The manifesto consistently promotes market liberalisation, privatisation, EU alignment, fiscal transparency, gender equality, and a stable financial system, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal orientation across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it endorses democratic and multicultural values, gender equality and inclusive education, market competition and EU-standard economic development, and explicitly calls for more liberal and transparent borrowing conditions for municipalities accessing capital markets.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it upholds constitutional values including non-discrimination and student rights (lib_political/lib_social), aligns vocational education with market needs and supports private-sector health providers (lib_economic), but contains no discussion of financial markets or banking policy (lib_financial_market = no_evidence).

chunk 6 · The manifesto consistently supports democratic freedoms (free media, freedom of expression, rule of law, vetting), social inclusion (disability, gender equality, minorities), market liberalization (TV signal market, SME support, competition), and market-enabling financial instruments (interest-free credit, bank guarantees), yielding a moderately pro-liberal overall profile.
