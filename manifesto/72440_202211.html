WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it explicitly champions liberal democracy and fights populism (political), commits to LGBTQ+ rights and minority welfare (social), promotes import reform, competition, and deregulation (economic), and supports capital-market access for small businesses (financial).

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions rule of law and anti-corruption (political), gender equality and women's rights (social), deregulation and competition for SMEs (economic), and opening credit markets to non-bank competitors (financial market).

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across political (constitutional reform, checks and balances, term limits), social (civil marriage, LGBTQ+ equality, adoption rights), and economic (competition, deregulation in tourism/hospitality) dimensions, explicitly describing Israel as a "democratic, liberal" state and committing to a written constitution and civil rights expansion.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across political and social dimensions—explicitly championing checks and balances, judicial independence, equal rights for all citizens, civil marriage, pluralism in religious services, and women's representation—while also promoting market competition (kashrut reform) and decentralisation; the absence of any financial-market discussion prevents a full four-dimension score, but the three evidenced dimensions average to a clearly pro-liberal overall score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it champions individual rights and accessibility (lib_social/lib_political), explicitly calls for deregulation to enable ride-sharing markets (lib_economic), and frames internet access as a human right, while financial-market policy is entirely absent from the text.

chunk 6 · The manifesto scores consistently liberal across all four dimensions: it explicitly commits to full civil and political equality for all citizens (quoting the Declaration of Independence), promotes minority inclusion and gender equality, calls for deregulation and reduced bureaucracy to enable business, and proposes capital market regulatory reforms oriented toward consumer protection and market transparency rather than state control.

chunk 7 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four sub-dimensions: it promotes deregulation and barrier removal for renewable energy entrepreneurs, anti-discrimination and minority integration, credit-market competition and financial reform, and free-market competition for small businesses, reflecting a consistent classical-liberal orientation.

chunk 8 · The manifesto consistently promotes democratic discourse, pluralism, and tolerance (lib_political); explicitly endorses equality regardless of religion, race, sex, disability, and sexual identity (lib_social); and advocates fair market competition aligned with OECD standards and support for small businesses (lib_economic); financial-market policy is entirely absent from the text.
