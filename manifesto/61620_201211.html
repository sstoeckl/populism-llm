WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The platform invokes "the people" as virtuous and self-reliant against a bloated, overreaching federal government and liberal elites, but frames this primarily through constitutional and economic arguments rather than a fully moralized good-vs-evil populist narrative.

chunk 2 · The text contains moderate populist elements—framing the current Administration and Congressional Democrats as blocking the people's will and burdening taxpayers—but the rhetoric is primarily policy-programmatic rather than moralized people-vs-elite, keeping overall populism at a moderate level.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populist tendencies primarily through criticism of the "current Administration" as undermining collaborative relationships and through occasional appeals to "the American people" and "taxpayer dollars," but the rhetoric is largely policy-focused rather than deeply populist in framing elites as corrupt or the people as a unified virtuous bloc.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes a big government entitlement society and emphasizes the people's courage and need for trustworthy leadership, reflecting moderate to strong populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes corrupt elites, emphasizes government close to the people, and frames a moral struggle against enemies, indicating moderate populist themes.

chunk 3 · The manifesto criticizes the current Administration as an out-of-touch elite harming innovation and regulatory collaboration, invokes 'the American people' as a priority, and frames threats in a moralized good-vs-evil struggle, supporting moderate populism overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing liberal elites as out of touch and advocating for trusting the people over government bureaucracy.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing corrupt foreign elites and advocating for government closest to the people, but lacks a strong moralized good-versus-evil narrative.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits low-level populism, primarily criticizing political earmarks as self-serving and invoking the defense of the American people against external enemies.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The platform's populist appeals lean right, emphasizing national economic sovereignty, opposition to redistribution, traditional values, and selective immigration, with anti-elite rhetoric directed at Washington bureaucrats and liberal cultural elites rather than corporate elites.

chunk 2 · The text's populist ideology is predominantly right-leaning, combining strong anti-illegal-immigration stances, national sovereignty rhetoric, and cultural conservatism (traditional marriage, English as official language), with only generic anti-government-bloat appeals that lack a clear left or centrist populist program.

chunk 3 · The populist flavor is predominantly right-leaning, emphasizing American sovereignty against international bodies (UN, ICC), national security, resistance to foreign influence, and opposition to what is framed as statist or corrupt foreign governance, with minimal left-populist redistribution themes.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines centrist appeals to trust the people and limit government with right-wing stances on traditional marriage and rejection of income redistribution, showing a mixed but predominantly right-leaning populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The text combines right-wing anti-illegal immigration and centrist appeals to local governance with some left-leaning welfare reform proposals, reflecting a mixed populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The text combines right-leaning national security and strength rhetoric with some centrist people-elite appeals and mild left critiques of regulatory overreach, indicating a mixed but predominantly right-leaning populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, emphasizing national sovereignty, traditional values, and strict distinctions between citizens and illegal aliens.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are combined with a right-wing focus on national sovereignty, border security, and opposition to illegal immigration.

chunk 3 · The populist elements lean right, focusing on national sovereignty, defense, and opposition to international regulatory bodies.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The platform is strongly pro-liberal economically (free markets, low taxes, deregulation, free trade) and moderately pro-liberal politically (rule of law, checks and balances), but scores lower on social liberalism due to opposition to same-sex marriage and abortion rights, and shows mixed financial-market liberalism through support for prudential regulation alongside opposition to Dodd-Frank.

chunk 2 · The text is strongly pro-economic liberalism (free markets, deregulation, competition in healthcare and financial services, transparency) and moderately pro-political liberalism (constitutional rights, federalism), but scores low on social liberalism due to explicit opposition to same-sex marriage and restrictive stances on reproductive rights and immigration.

chunk 3 · The text is broadly pro-liberal in the classical sense, championing free enterprise, rule of law, democracy promotion abroad, and open markets, while showing mixed signals on social liberalism (supporting religious freedom but opposing LGBTQ+ rights and certain international conventions on women's and children's rights).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports constitutional governance, individual rights, free markets, and financial prudence, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance in political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports rule of law, judicial restraint, free markets, reasonable financial oversight, and individual rights, demonstrating a moderately strong classical liberal stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, religious freedoms, market competition, and criticizes heavy regulation that harms innovation, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports classical economic liberalism and the rule of law, while presenting a mixed stance on social liberalism by opposing same-sex marriage.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports free-market economics, deregulation, and financial transparency, while taking a socially conservative stance on marriage and abortion.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports free enterprise, deregulation, and democratic governance, though it holds conservative stances on social issues like DOMA.
