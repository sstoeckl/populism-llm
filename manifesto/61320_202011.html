WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The platform frames the economy as "rigged" against ordinary Americans by wealthy elites and big corporations, invokes "the American people" as a unified moral community deserving a "new social and economic contract," and casts Trump and Republicans as corrupt adversaries, yielding moderate populist scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The text shows mild populist tendencies, primarily framing the Democratic agenda against wealthy elites and corporations and criticizing Trump's governance, but the dominant mode is programmatic policy advocacy rather than a moralized people-vs.-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The text contains mild populist elements—occasional anti-corporate rhetoric and appeals to American workers—but is predominantly a policy-oriented platform document focused on institutional reform, diplomacy, and equity, without sustained people-vs-elite framing or Manichean moral narratives.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The platform explicitly criticizes elites and the Republican Party as rigging the economy against working families, emphasizes a contract with 'the American people,' and portrays a moral struggle against Trump’s leadership failures, demonstrating anti-elitism, people-centrism, and a Manichean worldview.

chunk 2 · The text explicitly criticizes elites, emphasizes unity of 'the American people', and highlights moral struggles such as police brutality, indicating moderate populist themes.

chunk 3 · The text contains no explicit references to anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the political and economic system as rigged by corrupt elites against the virtuous American people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits low-to-moderate populism, primarily targeting the Trump administration's perceived cronyism and political interference rather than a systemic elite conspiracy against a pure people.

chunk 3 · The text contains very low levels of populism, focusing primarily on partisan criticism of the Trump administration's corruption and foreign policy rather than systemic anti-elitism or moralized good-versus-evil struggles.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The platform's populism is predominantly left-leaning, emphasizing redistribution, workers' rights, union organizing, racial equity, and anti-corporate concentration, with minimal nativist or right-populist content and only weak centrist people-vs-elite framing without programmatic specificity.

chunk 2 · The populist flavor leans left, with calls to end private prisons, expand public services, and redistribute resources toward marginalized communities, while explicitly rejecting right-wing nativist positions on immigration and border walls.

chunk 3 · The text leans left-populist in its emphasis on redistributive education funding, student debt relief, worker rights, and anti-corporate rhetoric, but the overall ideological flavor is moderate-progressive rather than strongly populist, with no significant right-populist or nativist content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The platform advocates a left-leaning populist ideology focused on economic redistribution, workers’ rights, racial equity, and opposition to corporate power, while also appealing broadly to 'the American people' in a centrist tone and contrasting with right-wing policies.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports left-leaning populist policies like ending private prisons and racial profiling bans, while also emphasizing unity and centrist appeals, reflecting a mixed populist ideology with left dominance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto does not present clear populist ideological stances on the left, right, or centrist populism; no evidence found.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist rhetoric is strongly left-wing, focusing on economic inequality, corporate concentration, and the rigging of the economy in favor of the wealthy.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are framed from a left-progressive perspective, criticizing private profit motives in public services and advocating for marginalized groups.

chunk 3 · There is no clear populist ideological framing, as the text relies on standard programmatic policy proposals rather than populist rhetoric.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The platform scores high on political and social liberalism (voting rights, democratic institutions, LGBTQ+/civil rights), but is mixed-to-low on economic liberalism (opposing privatization, favoring heavy regulation and public investment) and anti-liberal on financial markets (Glass-Steagall revival, curbing Wall Street, restricting investment banking).

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions—emphasizing voting rights, civil rights, anti-discrimination, and rule of law—while showing mixed economic liberalism through support for public goods and regulation alongside some market-enabling stances on financial disclosure and competition.

chunk 3 · The text is strongly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions—emphasizing democratic institutions, civil rights, LGBTQ+ protections, and minority rights—while showing mixed economic liberalism (supporting public investment and regulation alongside some market openness), and endorsing financial transparency measures that enable rather than restrict market function.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The platform strongly supports liberal principles including democracy and voting rights, civil rights protections for minorities and marginalized groups, economic policies promoting fair competition and regulation, and financial market reforms enabling market function.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including voting rights, anti-discrimination, economic equity, and financial transparency, with multiple explicit references to these liberal values.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights, free markets, and financial transparency, as evidenced by explicit commitments to education, civil rights protections, trade enforcement, and anti-corruption financial measures.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through civil rights and democratic reforms, while advocating for a more regulated, interventionist economic and financial framework.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through civil rights, judicial independence, and democratic reforms, while favoring state-led regulation and public investment over free-market economic liberalism.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including democratic institutions, civil rights protections for minorities, and transparent, regulated financial and economic markets.
