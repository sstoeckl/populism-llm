WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The PSP consistently frames the existing capitalist order and government as serving "de werkelijke machthebbers" at the expense of ordinary people, invoking democratic grassroots power-building as the remedy, with a clear moral struggle between exploiters and the exploited.

chunk 2 · The PSP manifesto frames corporate elites and the capitalist system as corrupt forces exploiting the common people, calling for community control over production and nationalization of key industries, while invoking the people's right to direct democratic control over their living environment.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populist tendencies: it frames agribusiness as a conspiratorial "komplot" exploiting farmers, invokes "the people's culture" as oppressed by a ruling class, and calls for liberation of subordinated groups, but the overall tone is primarily programmatic and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly critiques elite power structures and capitalist exploitation, emphasizing the principled equality of all people and advocating for radical societal change against established elites.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly critiques the insufficiency of parliamentary democracy and highlights a disconnect between the people and elites, indicating moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict themes.

chunk 3 · The manifesto does not provide explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, thus no evidence of populism is found.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text contains mild populist elements, framing the state as a bureaucratic monster out of touch with the people, but focuses primarily on structural socialist critique rather than pure populism.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the political elite as offering a 'fopspeen' (pacifier) of participation while ignoring the actual will of the people.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the culture of the ruling class and invoking the liberation of the people's culture, alongside a conspiratorial reference to the agribusiness lobby.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly left-populist, calling for socialization of the means of production, nationalization of banks, radical redistribution of labor and income, and anti-imperialism, while explicitly rejecting nationalism, racism, and right-wing identity politics.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist, explicitly endorsing socialism, nationalization of energy and housing sectors, redistribution, and anti-corporate rhetoric, with no right-wing nationalist or anti-immigration elements—indeed it strongly advocates for migrants' equal rights.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is clearly left-populist: it advocates communal land ownership, redistribution, cooperative farming, anti-agribusiness measures, and liberation of oppressed groups, with no right-wing nationalist or anti-immigration content — foreign youth are explicitly granted equal rights.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports left-wing populist ideology, focusing on anti-capitalism, social equality, and opposition to racism and patriarchy, while rejecting right-wing nationalist elements and showing some centrist populist appeals regarding people's self-governance.

chunk 2 · The text advocates for community ownership of housing and critiques monarchy and elite power, reflecting a left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist and right-leaning elements.

chunk 3 · There is no explicit evidence in the text of populist ideological stances on the left, right, or centrist populism; the manifesto focuses on policy issues without populist framing.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are entirely embedded in a radical left-wing ideology that targets capitalism, multinational corporations, and militarism.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on the struggle of the community and workers against capital, corporate interests, and real estate speculators.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, advocating for the socialization of land and collective ownership over private enterprise.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The PSP scores high on social liberalism (anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ rights, women's liberation) but is strongly anti-liberal economically and financially, advocating nationalization of banks, socialization of industry, abolition of private enterprise forms, and comprehensive state planning.

chunk 2 · The PSP scores high on social liberalism (anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ rights, minority protections) and moderately on political liberalism (democratic expansion, rule of law), but scores low on economic and financial-market liberalism due to explicit advocacy for nationalization, state-set interest rates on bank lending, and rejection of market-based housing allocation.

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores high on political and social liberalism (strong democratic rights, minority protections, decentralisation), but is markedly anti-liberal economically, advocating communal ownership of land and cable networks, banning commercial exploitation, and state-directed production planning.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto advocates for democratic governance, social rights protections, economic policies favoring public ownership and social control, and financial sector nationalization under worker control, reflecting a strong pro-liberal stance in political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports strong parliamentary oversight, anti-discrimination, community ownership, and democratic control, reflecting a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions, though financial market policy is not addressed.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political rights (e.g., universal suffrage from age 16), social rights (e.g., rights and inclusion of people with disabilities), and economic policies favoring structural reform and market-oriented solutions, indicating a high overall liberalism score.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political and social liberalism (civil rights, anti-discrimination, transparency) but completely rejects economic and financial liberalism in favor of state nationalization and worker control.

chunk 2 · The text is highly supportive of social and political rights (anti-discrimination, privacy) but strongly anti-liberal on economic and financial matters, advocating for nationalization and state-mandated non-commercial lending.

chunk 3 · While highly supportive of minority and social rights, the text is strongly anti-liberal on economic and financial matters, advocating for state control, the elimination of commercial markets, and the nationalization of land.
