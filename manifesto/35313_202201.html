The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies through criticism of political-financial elite promiscuity and oligarchies, but explicitly rejects populism as a democratic perversion, keeping anti-elitism moderate and manichean framing minimal.

chunk 2 · The text shows minimal populist features: it mildly criticises the incumbent Socialist government as "errática, inconsequente e propagandística" but does not frame politics as a Manichean struggle between a virtuous people and a corrupt elite; the dominant register is technocratic and policy-oriented.

chunk 3 · The text is a detailed centre-right policy manifesto (PSD, Portugal) focused on environment, energy, agriculture, health and social policy; populist signals are minimal—there is one passing reference to elites being culturally exclusive and mild criticism of the outgoing socialist government, but no sustained people-vs-elite framing or Manichean narrative.

chunk 4 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto from PSD (Portugal's centre-right Social Democratic Party) with minimal populist rhetoric; there is one passing reference to culture going beyond elite circles, a brief invocation of 'the Portuguese people' wanting social contract preservation, and a single exclamatory criticism of state inefficiency, but no sustained anti-elite framing, people-centrism, or Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 5 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto from PSD (Portugal's centre-right party) focused on defence, security, and public finances; it contains only mild criticism of the incumbent socialist government and occasional references to 'the Portuguese people', but lacks sustained populist framing, moralized good-vs-evil rhetoric, or strong people-vs-elite dichotomies.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The PSD presents itself as a centrist social-democratic party invoking national interest above partisan or corporate interests, with no strong left-redistributive or right-nativist populist ideology, making centrist populist framing the dominant (but weak) flavor.

chunk 2 · The text blends centre-right economic liberalism (competitiveness, tax cuts, FDI attraction) with modest identity-adjacent concerns (controlled immigration, demographic decline), placing it weakly on the right of the populist spectrum, but the overall populist ideological intensity is very low.

chunk 3 · The very limited populist signals lean neither strongly left nor right; the manifesto briefly invokes redistributive fairness and national food independence but is primarily a technocratic, market-oriented centre-right programme without a clear populist ideological flavour.

chunk 4 · The manifesto's populist elements are very weak and do not align clearly with left or right populism; the PSD explicitly identifies itself as a broad cross-class ('interclassista') centre-right social democratic party, with mild redistributive language and pragmatic immigration framing, placing it at most as weakly centrist-populist.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is primarily a mainstream centre-right policy document; the limited populist elements lean mildly right (border/security agency restoration, criticism of the socialist government) but are embedded in technocratic, institutional language rather than strong ideological populism.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines a centrist commitment to national interest and compromise with left-leaning social equality concerns and right-leaning national prioritization, reflecting a mixed but moderate populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The text combines left-leaning social justice and redistribution themes with right-leaning immigration control and national identity concerns, alongside centrist appeals to decentralization and governance reform, reflecting a mixed populist ideological orientation.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideological content on the left, right, or centrist spectrum in the manifesto text.

chunk 4 · The text supports redistributive policies and social solidarity (left-leaning populism) and emphasizes inclusive societal participation without clear right-wing nationalist themes, indicating a centrist-left populist ideology overall.

chunk 5 · There is no textual evidence of populist ideological positions on the left, right, or centrist populism in the manifesto, thus no populist ideological stance is present.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are centrist and institutional, focusing on anti-clientelism and transparency without adopting left-wing anti-corporate or right-wing nativist populist ideologies.

chunk 2 · The text does not contain populist ideological framing, focusing instead on mainstream policy proposals.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideology, as the text focuses on mainstream policy proposals without populist framing.

chunk 4 · The populist elements are centrist, appealing to the general Portuguese population's desire for a modern social contract without strong left-wing anti-corporate or right-wing anti-immigrant rhetoric.

chunk 5 · The populist elements are centrist and institutional, focusing on administrative efficiency and political accountability rather than left-wing or right-wing populist ideologies.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across political (judicial independence, rule of law), social (gender equality, anti-discrimination, inclusive society), and economic (tax cuts, competitiveness, deregulation) dimensions, with only passing reference to financial market globalization.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic institutions, anti-discrimination and immigrant integration (lib_social/political), free-market competitiveness, deregulation and tax reduction (lib_economic), and efficient, open financial markets (lib_financial_market).

chunk 3 · The manifesto consistently favours market competition, regulatory independence, private-sector participation in public services, deregulation/simplification of licensing, and prudential oversight of utilities, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal (classical/ordo-liberal) orientation across economic, financial, political and social dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto consistently endorses liberal values across all four dimensions: it champions democracy and rule of law, gender equality and minority inclusion, free enterprise and market-led growth, and market-based financing instruments such as venture capital and fiscal incentives for R&D, placing PSD firmly in the pro-liberal centre-right tradition.

chunk 5 · The manifesto consistently advocates for tax reduction, market-enabling financial regulation, completion of the EU Banking Union and Capital Markets Union, depoliticisation of public administration, and whistleblower protections — all hallmarks of classical liberal policy orientation across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, social inclusion without discrimination, economic deregulation, and independent financial agencies, reflecting a generally pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles including rule of law, victim protection, economic competitiveness, regulatory reform, and financial market interfaces, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through emphasis on maintaining and modernizing health structures, social liberalism via promotion of healthy lifestyles, and economic liberalism by advocating increased production and competitiveness; however, it lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism (democracy, rights), social liberalism (gender equality), economic liberalism (free initiative, entrepreneurship), and financial market liberalism (regulatory independence), reflecting a broadly pro-liberal stance.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal political institutions through proposals for depoliticized appointments, advocates economic liberalism via tax reductions and incentives for savings, and endorses financial market liberalism by emphasizing the completion of the Banking Union and Capital Markets Union.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports classical liberal principles, including the rule of law, tax reductions, free enterprise, and social equality.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including judicial reform, anti-discrimination measures, corporate tax reductions, and capital market integration.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing the mobilization of private initiative, constitutional rights, and non-discrimination.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing free enterprise, private initiative, and the rule of law, while advocating for state regulation that enables market functions.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, advocating for depoliticized public administration, tax reductions for businesses, and the integration of financial and capital markets.
