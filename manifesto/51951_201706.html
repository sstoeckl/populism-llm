WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · UKIP consistently frames the political establishment and EU elites as corrupt betrayers of the British people's democratic will, invokes the 17 million Leave voters as a unified sovereign people, and casts Brexit opponents in a moralized good-vs-evil struggle, yielding a moderately high populism score.

chunk 2 · UKIP consistently frames the political establishment as corrupt and self-serving ("An unaccountable elite revels in mutual back-scratching and cronyism"), positions itself as the sole defender of ordinary working people, and draws sharp moral contrasts between itself and all other parties.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the political establishment as a 'tyranny of the status quo' and frames Brexit as a movement to 'pull Britain Together,' indicating strong anti-elitism and people-centrism, with moralized language about betrayal by political leaders.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes an unaccountable elite and emphasizes power for the people, showing moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized struggle against the political establishment.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits strong populism by framing the political establishment as betraying the people and calling for a fight against the tyranny of the status quo.

chunk 2 · The text strongly frames politics as a struggle between an unaccountable, corrupt elite and the virtuous British people.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is predominantly right-wing populist, centred on nativist immigration restriction, national sovereignty, and cultural protectionism, with a secondary left-populist strand targeting corporate tax dodgers and big business, making right-populism the dominant ideological flavour.

chunk 2 · The populism is overwhelmingly right-wing, centred on nativist law-and-order policies, deportation of foreign nationals, opposition to Islam, Brexit nationalism, and anti-immigration sentiment, with only marginal rhetorical nods to working-class economic concerns.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto combines right-wing populist themes such as reducing net migration to zero and immigration control with some centrist appeals to principles and left-leaning economic concerns about tax cuts and worker incentives, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The text combines right-wing stances on immigration and foreign criminals with centrist appeals to the people and some left-leaning economic redistribution skepticism, reflecting a mixed but predominantly right-leaning populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is heavily right-wing, focusing on national sovereignty, strict border controls, and reducing net migration to zero.

chunk 2 · The populist rhetoric is heavily combined with right-wing nationalist themes, focusing on anti-immigration, deporting foreign criminals, and protecting British values.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is economically mixed-liberal (pro-low tax and deregulation but with protectionist 'British jobs' rules and state housing schemes), socially illiberal (burqa ban, anti-multiculturalism, restrictive immigration), politically mixed (democratic reform rhetoric but opting out of ECHR and European Arrest Warrant), and financially mildly illiberal (attacking PFI/private finance while not proposing market-enabling reforms).

chunk 2 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it advocates free trade, low taxes, and deregulation post-Brexit, but simultaneously proposes repealing human rights legislation, restricting civil liberties (Stop and Search, postal voting), and reviewing utility ownership, producing a below-average overall liberal score.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democratic mandates and political rights but shows limited commitment to social liberalism and moderate economic liberalism, with some support for market incentives and tax cuts, while endorsing stricter financial market regulation to prevent tax avoidance, indicating a moderate liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports democratic reforms, political rights, religious freedoms, and a low-tax, low-regulation economic approach, but lacks discussion on financial market regulation, indicating moderate classical liberalism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text shows weak support for classical liberalism, advocating for state bans on religious face coverings and major institutional overhauls while supporting small business tax cuts.

chunk 2 · The text shows mixed liberal stances, supporting free trade and low regulation while opposing international human rights frameworks and restricting minority religious practices.
