WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto contains mild populist elements—criticising "new regents" and established interests—but frames this in a liberal-democratic, pro-market reform language rather than a sharp people-vs-elite moral binary; people-centrism is present but inclusive and procedural rather than exclusionary.

chunk 2 · The manifesto (D66, Netherlands 2006) shows mild populist tendencies: it criticises entrenched interests and bureaucratic structures ("wurgende houdgreep") and invokes citizen self-determination, but frames these in a liberal-democratic, technocratic register rather than a moralized people-vs-elite struggle, and lacks Manichean or conspiratorial rhetoric.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes entrenched interests and populist egoism, emphasizes the importance of equal opportunity and people’s self-determination, and highlights social divisions as sources of conflict, reflecting moderate populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto criticizes entrenched interests and bureaucratic delays, emphasizes citizen self-determination, and highlights government shortcomings, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and some moral critique.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing 'new regents' and bureaucratic elites who restrict citizens' freedom, while advocating for direct democratic reforms to give power back to the people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing established interests and bureaucracy while advocating for direct citizen empowerment through referendums.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · D66 explicitly distances itself from both left and right populism ("populisten, links en rechts"), positioning itself as a centrist, pro-market, pro-openness liberal party; its mild anti-establishment rhetoric targets entrenched interests across the spectrum rather than a class or national enemy.

chunk 2 · D66 is a centrist liberal-democratic party; its mild people-centric appeals are framed around democratic reform and individual autonomy rather than left redistribution or right nativist populism, placing it closest to centrist populist ideology at a low intensity.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-leaning critiques of entrenched interests and social inequality with centrist appeals to individual responsibility and democratic participation, and some right-leaning concerns about exclusion and preservation of acquired positions, indicating a mixed but moderate populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The text advocates for a small, efficient government with citizen involvement, rejects coercive integration policies, and stresses political trust, indicating a centrist populist ideology with mild left and right elements.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text explicitly rejects both left-wing and right-wing populism, positioning itself as a centrist, reform-oriented force against established interests.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on democratic reform and citizen autonomy without strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · D66's manifesto is strongly liberal across all dimensions: it champions direct democracy and individual rights, endorses self-determination on abortion/euthanasie/homohuwelijk, advocates market liberalisation and removal of labour-market barriers, and supports open financial services as tools for empowerment.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it advocates direct democratic elections and constitutional courts (political), full equality regardless of sexual orientation and minority rights (social), privatisation conditioned on competition and deregulation (economic), and market liberalisation in healthcare purchasing (financial market).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, civil rights including self-determination on social issues, economic dynamism with government enabling individual creativity, and prudent market oversight, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, human rights, social emancipation, and a lean market-friendly government, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market regulation, indicating a broadly pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly champions classical liberal values, including individual self-determination, civil rights, free-market competition, and deregulation, while opposing state-driven market distortions.

chunk 2 · The text strongly champions classical liberal values, including the rule of law, individual rights, social tolerance, and market-based solutions in public services.
