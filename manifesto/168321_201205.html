The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames the incumbent PLD government and its officials as corrupt elites betraying the people, invokes "el pueblo" as a virtuous collective deserving better governance, and draws a moral contrast between the corrupt establishment and the PRD's mission to liberate citizens from crime, corruption, and mismanagement.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows moderate populism: it contrasts the virtuous PRD/people against the negligent PLD government, frames the incumbent as corrupt and indifferent to citizens' welfare, and centers policy on 'the people,' but stops short of a fully Manichean or conspiratorial narrative.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populist features: it criticizes the ruling PLD government and "dominant groups" for neglecting ordinary citizens and small producers, invokes the people's needs and dignity, and frames the incumbent as corrupt and indifferent, but stops short of a fully Manichean or people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 4 · The text shows moderate populist features: it criticizes the ruling PLD government as corrupt and serving elites ("sectores pudientes"), invokes the people and small enterprises as the backbone of the economy, and frames the electricity crisis as a moral failure of the governing elite, but stops short of a fully Manichean or people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 5 · The text criticizes the ruling PLD government for corruption, incompetence, and neglect of key sectors, framing the incumbent administration as failing ordinary citizens (workers, SMEs, youth), but the rhetoric is primarily technocratic and policy-oriented rather than strongly populist; anti-elitism is directed at a specific party government rather than a generalized corrupt elite, and people-centrism is moderate.

chunk 6 · The text shows only mild populist features: it criticises past executive power concentration under Balaguer and invokes collective wisdom and solidarity, but does not frame politics as a sharp people-vs-elite moral struggle, keeping overall populism low.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes corrupt elites and emphasizes the people's welfare and moral struggle against societal ills, reflecting strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the social policy as a clientelist 'repartición de favores' and emphasizes prioritizing 'las personas' and their quality of life, showing anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moral framing of social injustice.

chunk 3 · The text explicitly criticizes corrupt public administration and government neglect, while emphasizing the people's demand for ethical governance and unity, indicating moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict typical of populism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the high taxes paid by the majority and their use to subsidize wealthy sectors, indicating anti-elitism but lacks broader populist themes, resulting in a low overall populism score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto does not contain explicit references to populist themes such as anti-elitism, people-centrism, or Manichean moral struggles, thus no evidence of populism is found.

chunk 6 · No explicit populist rhetoric or themes of anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moral dichotomies are present in the text.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the ruling PLD government as corrupt and out of touch, while contrasting it with the virtuous struggles of the Dominican people and diaspora.

chunk 5 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the corrupt ruling elite and framing the struggle as a defense of the virtuous people against systemic injustices.

chunk 6 · The text exhibits mild populism by criticizing the current central government's attitude toward local municipalities and emphasizing collective wisdom.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly identifies with "socialismo democrático" and frames its program around redistribution, poverty elimination, and human development against a "neoliberal" rival, placing it clearly on the left of the populist spectrum, though with moderate rather than radical left intensity.

chunk 2 · The PRD explicitly identifies as a 'partido socialista democrático' opposing 'neoliberalism,' emphasizing redistribution, human development, poverty reduction, and social equity, placing it clearly on the left of the populist spectrum with minimal right-populist or centrist-populist content.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology leans mildly left-of-center, emphasizing solidarity pensions for the disabled and poor, support for small and medium enterprises, rural development, and redistribution of state resources toward marginalized groups, with only minor nationalist-protectionist elements.

chunk 4 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist, emphasizing redistribution toward the poor and middle class, criticism of elite-benefiting subsidies, and support for small enterprises, but it also stresses public-private partnership and consensus, giving it a centrist-pragmatic flavor rather than a strongly ideological left-populist one.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is grounded in Democratic Socialism (explicitly named), critiques neoliberalism and savage capitalism, and advocates redistribution, gender equity, and social inclusion, placing it clearly on the left of the ideological spectrum with only marginal nationalist/territorial references.

chunk 6 · The dominant ideological flavour is centre-left, emphasising solidarity economy, cooperativism, support for the poor and SMEs, with only marginal right-populist signals around border/migration control.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text affirms commitment to democratic socialism and social traditions, includes national security concerns, and pragmatic leadership appeals, indicating a left-leaning populist ideology with some centrist and mild right elements.

chunk 2 · The text promotes left-leaning populist themes such as poverty reduction and social equity, while also emphasizing institutional strengthening and people-centered wealth generation, reflecting a mixed but overall centrist-left populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows left-leaning populist elements through anti-corruption and social justice concerns, right-leaning elements in concerns about insecurity and protection, and centrist populism in appeals to unity and democratic participation, reflecting a mixed populist ideology.

chunk 4 · The text criticizes economic inequity and subsidies favoring wealthy sectors, reflecting a left-leaning populist ideology, but lacks right or centrist populist elements.

chunk 5 · No explicit populist ideological content (left, right, or centrist) is present in the manifesto text, indicating absence of populist ideology.

chunk 6 · The text lacks clear populist ideological stances on left, right, or centrist dimensions, focusing instead on institutional and developmental policies.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is explicitly framed around democratic socialism and the legacy of José Francisco Peña Gómez, focusing on poverty reduction and social equity.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is explicitly left-wing, framing the party as democratic socialist in opposition to the neoliberal model of the ruling party.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology leans left, criticizing 'dominant groups' and 'privileged classes' who build wealth at the expense of the poor and small businesses.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology leans left, focusing on the economic struggles of the poor and middle class against corrupt elites, without right-wing nationalist or anti-immigration rhetoric.

chunk 5 · The populist ideology is explicitly left-wing, grounded in democratic socialism and critiques of wild capitalism and neoliberalism.

chunk 6 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on institutional friction and local community empowerment without strong left-wing or right-wing ideological framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports constitutional rule of law and judicial independence, references gender equity, promotes free competition via the Ley de Defensa de la Competencia, and explicitly endorses free capital movement and private-investment-led growth, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal orientation across all four dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, rule of law, non-discrimination, and export-oriented economic openness, while also advocating significant state intervention in health, education, and social policy, yielding a mixed but moderately pro-liberal overall profile.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is moderately liberal: it endorses democratic participation, gender equality, market openness and international trade, and competitive credit markets, while also proposing significant state intervention in agriculture, banking, and industry, producing a mixed but net-positive liberal profile.

chunk 4 · The manifesto consistently promotes public-private partnerships, private investment incentives, decentralization, and rule of law compliance, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions, though it also envisions a strong state regulatory and facilitating role.

chunk 5 · The manifesto consistently supports democratic rule of law, gender equality and civil rights, a mixed economy with a primary role for private investment in mining and tourism, and promotes capital market development for mineral products, yielding a moderately pro-liberal overall profile tempered by strong state regulatory and redistributive roles.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is moderately liberal: it promotes gender equality and minority rights, invites private-sector investment in culture, proposes cooperative banking, and upholds democratic governance, but also relies heavily on state-led investment and regulation, producing mixed-to-moderate liberal scores across all four dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports constitutional democracy, judicial independence, social protections against violence, free competition, and financial market development enabling productive credit, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism with emphasis on democracy, rule of law, and accountability, social liberalism with rights and anti-discrimination, and economic liberalism with sustainable wealth generation, but lacks explicit discussion on financial market policies.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports political liberalism through democratic participation, social liberalism via social programs for vulnerable groups, economic liberalism by promoting soft financing and market facilitation for SMEs, and financial market liberalism through banking coordination and advisory services, indicating an overall pro-liberal stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports democratic municipal decision-making, improving quality of life for lower-income populations, and facilitating economic activity, indicating moderate classical liberalism in political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through emphasis on human rights and democratic principles, social liberalism via women's rights and social security, and economic liberalism by promoting government role in economic investment; however, it lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 6 · The manifesto explicitly supports democratic governance, social equality including gender rights, and economic development through private sector support and cultural industries, indicating a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including the independence of public powers, the rule of law, and the promotion of free and fair market competition.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports democratic institutions, rule of law, and non-discrimination, while advocating for state-supported entrepreneurship and fiscal responsibility.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, emphasizing democratic participation, individual rights, and market-based financial and economic development.

chunk 4 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including democratic local governance, private investment support, and market-based financial mechanisms like stock market financing.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including the rule of law, gender equality, private investment security, and market-based financial mechanisms.

chunk 6 · The text strongly supports liberal principles through commitments to transparency laws, gender equality, private sector investment incentives, and mixed financial markets.
