WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows minimal populism: it briefly criticises large business and labour organisations for accumulating disproportionate power, but frames this in terms of democratic balance rather than a corrupt elite vs. virtuous people, and there is no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 2 · The text is a detailed liberal policy manifesto (Venstre/Norway 2005) with virtually no populist framing: elites are not attacked, 'the people' are not invoked as a unified virtuous bloc, and there is no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative; the mild concern about fishing-rights rents being 'drained' from coastal communities is the closest hint of anti-elite sentiment but is framed as a technical policy concern.

chunk 3 · The text is a policy-oriented liberal manifesto with minimal populist framing; the only quasi-populist rhetoric appears in a quoted epigraph contrasting the powerful and powerless, while the body text focuses on rule-of-law, rights, and technocratic environmental and justice policy without invoking a virtuous 'people' against corrupt elites.

chunk 4 · The text shows minimal populism: it briefly frames large global corporations as wielding excessive power and criticises rich-country dominance of world trade, but does not invoke a virtuous 'people' against corrupt domestic elites, and the overall tone is policy-oriented and institutionalist rather than Manichean.

chunk 5 · The text contains only a faint populist trace — a single mild contrast between large music companies and consumers — with no sustained elite-bashing, no moralized good-vs-evil framing, and no invocation of a unified virtuous people against corrupt elites.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The weak populist signals lean slightly left (redistribution, social equality, welfare state) and centrist (individual-centred appeals), with no right-populist nativist or anti-immigration content; overall populist ideological flavour is very low.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is primarily social-liberal (Venstre's self-described 'sosialliberal' tradition), combining modest redistribution proposals (basic income, child benefit redistribution) with strong pro-market, pro-competition, and pro-immigration stances; left-populist elements are minimal and right-populist elements are essentially absent.

chunk 3 · The faint populist signals lean mildly left through calls for global resource redistribution and anti-discrimination, but the overall ideological frame is explicitly social-liberal rather than left-populist, with no right-populist or nativist content.

chunk 4 · The text's mild populist flavour is centrist-liberal rather than left or right: it invokes market economics as a tool for the least well-off and calls for global competition oversight, without anti-immigration rhetoric or strong redistributive class framing.

chunk 5 · The text is a cultural-policy chapter of a liberal (Venstre) manifesto; it mixes modest public-spending proposals with market-friendly language and minority-rights commitments, producing no clear left- or right-populist ideological profile.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democracy, rule of law, checks and balances, and media freedom (lib_political); gender equality, anti-discrimination, and religious freedom (lib_social); free markets, competition, deregulation, and entrepreneurship (lib_economic); and transparency/standardisation in financial services to enable market comparison (lib_financial_market).

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across political (democratic control, rule of law, asylum rights), social (same-sex marriage equality, anti-discrimination, multicultural pluralism), and economic (market competition, privatisation, deregulation) dimensions; financial-market content is limited to pension-fund consolidation and competitive shipping tax regimes, yielding a mixed but moderately pro-liberal signal.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across political (democracy, rule of law, human rights), social (anti-discrimination, women's rights, minority integration), and economic (liberal immigration/labour regime, market-based environmental pricing) dimensions, while financial-market policy is entirely absent from the text.

chunk 4 · The manifesto is strongly liberal across all four sub-dimensions: it champions human rights, democracy, free expression, women's rights, open world trade, removal of export subsidies, and micro-credit for development, reflecting a consistent classical-liberal and social-liberal orientation.

chunk 5 · The manifesto consistently favours open democratic structures, minority and linguistic rights, market-based competition in broadcasting and cinema, and market-based financing of digital TV infrastructure, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal stance across all four sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, rule of law, civil rights, market economy with political and legal frameworks, and prudential financial regulation enabling market function, reflecting a high pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles such as stronger democratic control, equal rights for minorities including LGBTQ+ people, market-oriented economic policies, and infrastructure competition, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal principles such as rule of law, individual rights, anti-discrimination, and economic self-reliance, as evidenced by explicit quotes on legal security, anti-discrimination, and work as a principle for immigrants.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political rights (freedom of speech), social rights (human rights and equality), economic liberalism (fair trade and market openness), and financial market liberalism (ethical investment and transparency), reflecting a high overall liberalism score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly supports political rights and democratic structures through cultural infrastructure and public support, social liberalism via copyright and minority cultural protections, and economic liberalism by promoting market-based cultural production and creative industries, though it lacks discussion on financial market policies.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly champions classical social-liberal principles, including robust defenses of democracy, individual liberty, free-market competition, and minority rights.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal values, including democratic oversight, equal rights for LGBTQ+ individuals, and market-oriented economic reforms.

chunk 3 · The text strongly advocates for classical liberal values, including the rule of law, individual rights, minority protections, and market-based green taxation.

chunk 4 · The text strongly advocates classical liberal principles, including human rights, democracy, free trade, and market-based solutions like microcredit.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports liberal values through democratic structures, minority rights, and market competition by opposing monopolies and parallel import bans.
