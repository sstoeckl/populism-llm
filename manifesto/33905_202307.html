WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames the Spanish state and its monarchy as a corrupt, oligarchic "régim del 78" that oppresses the Catalan people, invoking a clear elite-vs-people dynamic, though the primary driver is national self-determination rather than pure populist mobilisation.

chunk 2 · The manifesto frames the Spanish state apparatus, police, and establishment parties (PP, PSOE) as corrupt and repressive elites acting against the Catalan people, while invoking the citizenry as a virtuous collective deserving democratic security and rights, with a clear moral contrast between the repressive Spanish model and the democratic Catalan model.

chunk 3 · The manifesto frames the Spanish state and its governing elites as corrupt protectors of privilege ("elits extractives", "règim del 78"), while invoking the people's sovereignty and a moral struggle between republican values and elite-serving politics, yielding moderate populist scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto of Esquerra Republicana exhibits moderate populism, framing economic elites (IBEX35 banks, energy oligopolies) as corrupt extractors who socialize costs while privatizing gains, while invoking workers and citizens as the virtuous base of the Republic, though the Manichean framing is more structural-economic than morally conspiratorial.

chunk 5 · The manifesto (ERC, Catalonia 2023) exhibits moderate populism: it frames PP and PSOE governments as corrupt plunderers of the Social Security fund ("saqueig"), invokes the working class as a virtuous collective, and draws a moral contrast between ordinary workers and self-serving elites, though the rhetoric remains largely programmatic rather than purely Manichean.

chunk 6 · The manifesto frames the Spanish state's cultural and linguistic elites/oligarchies as corrupt gatekeepers suppressing Catalan identity, invokes the Catalan people as a unified virtuous bloc, and casts political opponents as reactionary forces, producing a moderate-to-strong populist register.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist framing is predominantly left-leaning, emphasising wealth redistribution, social rights, anti-corporate critique (IBEX 35 privileges), and feminist/LGBTQ+ advances, while explicitly rejecting right-wing populism and far-right ideology.

chunk 2 · The populist framing is predominantly left-leaning, emphasizing anti-militarism, redistribution of competences to autonomous communities, workers' and migrants' rights, feminist demands, and condemnation of arms industry subsidies, with no meaningful right-populist nativist or anti-immigration content.

chunk 3 · The populist framing is clearly left-leaning, centred on redistribution of wealth, taxing corporate and banking profits, fighting fiscal inequality, and expanding social rights, with explicit opposition to right-wing and far-right parties rather than any nativist or identity-protectionist agenda.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is predominantly left-leaning, combining anti-corporate elite rhetoric, demands for progressive fiscal reform, redistribution from capital to labour, and permanent taxes on banks and energy companies, with a strong Catalan national-sovereigntist dimension that does not fit a right-wing nativist frame.

chunk 5 · The populist ideology is clearly left-leaning: the text calls for redistribution and progressivity in taxation, repeal of labour reforms, expansion of public pensions, workers' rights, and solidarity economy, while national/identity themes are secondary and limited to Catalan self-governance rather than anti-immigration sentiment.

chunk 6 · The manifesto's populist content is predominantly left-leaning, criticising bank bailouts over families, speculative investment funds in housing, and fiscal privileges for the wealthy, while also carrying a strong ethno-territorial (Catalan nationalist) dimension that adds a mild right-populist flavour.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly endorses political liberalism (rule of law, separation of powers, democratic rights) and social liberalism (anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ equality), while taking a mixed stance on economic liberalism (entrepreneurship alongside redistribution) and a mildly restrictive stance on financial markets (financial-transaction taxes, opposition to private pension funds).

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports civil and political rights, minority protections, gender equality, and anti-discrimination measures (high lib_social, lib_political), while showing mixed signals on economic liberalism (criticizing arms industry subsidies but not advocating free-market deregulation broadly) and only marginally touching financial markets through a transparency demand on banks' arms investments.

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores high on social liberalism (strong LGBTI+ and women's rights advocacy) and political liberalism (voting rights, democratic participation), but is mixed on economic liberalism (market regulation in the public interest, rent controls, labour protections) and moderately anti-liberal on financial markets (permanent windfall taxes on banks, financial transaction taxes, opposition to fiscal engineering).

chunk 4 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports competition policy, digital rights, and anti-discrimination measures (pro-liberal), but also advocates permanent windfall taxes on banks, financial transaction taxes on derivatives, state-directed investment, and public banking (anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions), yielding a middling overall score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports LGBTQ+ non-discrimination and democratic participation (pro-liberal socially/politically), but advocates heavy labour-market re-regulation, wage controls, public ownership of energy networks, and hostility to speculative investment funds, placing it below the midpoint on economic and financial-market liberalism.

chunk 6 · The manifesto scores moderately liberal on political freedoms and anti-discrimination, but takes interventionist stances on housing markets (rent controls, public housing parks, mortgage regulation) and financial assets (control of securitisations, recovery of SAREB flats), pulling the economic and financial-market scores below the liberal midpoint.
