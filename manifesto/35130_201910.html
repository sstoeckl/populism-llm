WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows only mild populist features: it frames large fortunes and unregulated capitalism as threats to democracy and invokes redistribution for 'the people', but explicitly rejects populism ('rejeita o populismo') and frames its project as progressive, cosmopolitan, and institutionally grounded rather than as a Manichean people-vs-elite struggle.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains mild populist elements — criticising political cartelisation and unregulated capitalism — but frames these within a programmatic, policy-rich, pluralist discourse rather than a sharp people-vs-elite binary, keeping populism scores low.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elite capture and promotes mobilizing people cooperatively against threats of egoism and fear, reflecting moderate to strong populist themes.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly critiques economic monopolization and political cartelization, emphasizes inclusive politics where everyone counts, and highlights a struggle against dehumanizing forces, reflecting moderate populist anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits low overall populism, rejecting populist tactics while occasionally criticizing the capture of politics by large fortunes and calling for the financial system to serve the people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism, criticizing the cartelization and clientelism of the political establishment while advocating for citizen empowerment.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The dominant ideological flavour is left-populist: the manifesto calls for redistribution, a Green New Deal, worker protections, anti-corporate taxation, and social equality, while explicitly distancing itself from right-wing nationalism and identity-based populism.

chunk 2 · Where populist flavour exists, it leans left — criticising unregulated capitalism, corporate monopolies, and financial sector power — while explicitly rejecting nationalist/right-wing rhetoric, placing the manifesto firmly in left-populist territory at a moderate intensity.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-wing populist themes of wealth redistribution and social justice with critiques of right-wing xenophobia and a centrist rejection of populist tactics, indicating a mixed but predominantly left populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The text combines left-leaning economic critiques of monopolies and cartelization with criticism of nationalist rhetoric harming migrants, alongside centrist appeals to inclusive politics, indicating a mixed but overall moderate populist ideological stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on taxing large fortunes, combating tax evasion, and redistributing wealth, while explicitly rejecting right-wing xenophobia and nationalism.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are strongly left-wing, framing the struggle against an unregulated capitalist system that commodifies workers.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political rights (defending the rule of law and democracy) and social liberalism (extensive LGBTQ+, gender, and anti-racism provisions), but is anti-liberal on economic and financial-market dimensions, advocating state ownership of key sectors, financial-transaction taxes, capital controls on tax havens, and separation of commercial and investment banking.

chunk 2 · The manifesto scores high on political and social liberalism (strong democracy, pluralism, anti-discrimination), but is mixed-to-low on economic liberalism (favouring state intervention, public enterprise, and re-regulation of energy and financial markets over free-market solutions).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, social rights, a mixed economy with public and cooperative sectors, and financial market reforms aimed at fighting tax evasion, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through transparency and democratic rights, social liberalism via cultural and social cohesion, economic liberalism by opposing monopolies, and financial market liberalism by advocating regulatory frameworks that enable market function, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic rule of law and minority rights, but scores low on economic and financial liberalism due to its opposition to marketization, support for state intervention, and strict financial regulations.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism (democracy, human rights, anti-discrimination) but rejects economic and financial liberalism in favor of heavy state regulation and public ownership.
