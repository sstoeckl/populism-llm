WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mild populist tendencies: it frames Berlin decision-makers as out of touch with the region ("über unsere Köpfe hinweg getroffen") and invokes "the people" of Schleswig-Holstein as a unified community, but it lacks strong Manichean good-vs-evil framing or sharp anti-elite rhetoric, focusing instead on pragmatic regional policy advocacy.

chunk 2 · The manifesto contains mild redistributive rhetoric (taxing the wealthy more, relieving the middle class) and occasional references to the "working middle" as a burdened group, but there is no sustained anti-elite framing, no Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, and no strong people-centrism; the overall tone is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than populist.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly opposes populism and extremism as threats to parliamentary democracy, frames no elite-vs-people dichotomy, and scores very low on all three populism sub-dimensions.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto expresses distrust of distant elites making decisions over the people, emphasizes a strong regional and minority-focused people-centric approach, and highlights external threats to minorities, reflecting moderate populist elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly critiques short-term political interests and emphasizes ethical values and solidarity, indicating low anti-elitism and moderate people-centrism, with limited moral polarization, reflecting a low overall populism score.

chunk 3 · Das Manifest enthält keine Hinweise auf populistische Rhetorik oder Themen wie Anti-Elitismus, Menschenzentrierung oder moralische Schwarz-Weiß-Darstellungen.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits mild regional populism by framing Berlin decision-makers as ignoring the local population, though it lacks a strong moralizing or Manichean tone.

chunk 2 · The text contains no populist rhetoric, anti-elitist framing, or moralized good-versus-evil divisions.

chunk 3 · The text rejects populism and extremism, framing them as threats to parliamentary democracy rather than adopting a populist stance.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The SSW's populist ideology leans left: it advocates a Scandinavian-style welfare state, redistribution, universal social insurance, and workers' rights, while explicitly rejecting right-wing nationalist and anti-immigration positions; its regional-minority focus adds a centrist-regionalist dimension but the dominant programmatic thrust is centre-left.

chunk 2 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist through calls for wealth taxes, higher taxation of large corporations, stronger workers' co-determination, and redistribution toward lower incomes, but lacks strong nationalist or identity-based right-populist content; the overall ideological flavor is centre-left reformist rather than strongly populist.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows mild left-leaning tendencies through emphasis on social justice, equality, and workers' rights, but explicitly rejects right-wing populism and nationalism; overall populist ideological content is minimal.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text supports left-leaning social welfare and solidarity, criticizes right-wing nationalist exclusionary policies, and shows centrist pragmatism in coalition-building, indicating a mixed but overall moderate populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The text advocates for social justice, fair taxation, and a modern state providing services, showing a left-leaning populist ideology with centrist elements but no right-wing populism.

chunk 3 · Es fehlen jegliche Hinweise auf populistische Ideologie, weder links-, rechts- noch zentristisch geprägte populistische Inhalte sind erkennbar.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist elements are combined with a strong regionalist focus on Schleswig-Holstein and left-leaning social justice demands.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text focuses on regional, environmental, and social-democratic policy proposals without populist framing.

chunk 3 · There is no evidence of populist ideology as the text explicitly positions itself against populist movements.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The SSW scores high on political and social liberalism (strong minority rights, democracy, rule of law) but is mixed-to-low on economic and financial-market liberalism, favouring state control over healthcare, public housing, and a Scandinavian welfare model that replaces market mechanisms with universal public provision and solidarity-based financing.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is strongly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (defending democracy, rule of law, minority rights, EU values), mixed on economic liberalism (supporting SME competition but also strong state intervention, public ownership of utilities, and anti-privatisation stances), and moderately anti-liberal on financial markets (advocating a financial transaction tax and opposing privatisation of savings banks).

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly endorses democratic institutions, rule of law, civil rights, minority protections, LGBTQ+ equality, and anti-discrimination measures, reflecting a high degree of political and social liberalism, with moderate economic liberalism through support for fair competition and open borders.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, minority rights, social equality, and a welfare state, alongside economic policies favoring social justice and market mechanisms, reflecting a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic values, social equality, economic cooperation, and financial market regulation within the EU context, reflecting a high overall liberalism score.

chunk 3 · Das Manifest betont demokratische Legitimität, Pluralismus, Gleichheit, offene Märkte und Schutz der EU-Binnenmarktprinzipien, was auf eine starke liberale Ausrichtung hinweist.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political democracy and minority rights, but advocates for extensive state intervention and regulation over free-market economic principles.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political liberalism, minority rights, and rule of law, while advocating a mixed economic approach with state-backed social protections and financial incentives for private retail investment.

chunk 3 · The text strongly champions liberal political and social values, including minority rights, gender equality, and democratic legitimacy, while supporting regulated fair competition.
