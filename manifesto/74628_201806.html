WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently champions rule of law, separation of powers, individual rights and freedoms, open and competitive markets, and prudential financial regulation, placing it firmly in the pro-liberal range across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The text explicitly endorses democracy, fundamental rights, rule of law, and checks on security services; promotes equal access to education for disabled and disadvantaged groups; and encourages private-sector participation in education and vocational training, reflecting a moderately pro-liberal stance across political, social, and economic dimensions, with no discussion of financial markets.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows moderate liberal scores across political (lowering voting age, expanding rights), social (lifting headscarf ban, gender equality laws, anti-violence measures), and economic (public-private partnerships, entrepreneurship incentives) dimensions, while financial-market policy is entirely absent from the text.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly endorses democracy, rule of law, free markets, open economy, financial market deepening, and disability/minority rights conventions, consistently framing liberal institutions as prerequisites for economic development, yielding moderately high liberalism scores across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 5 · The manifesto strongly endorses free markets, open trade, privatization, capital market deepening, prudential banking regulation, and rule of law as prerequisites for economic development, placing it firmly in the pro-liberal camp across all four sub-dimensions.

chunk 6 · The manifesto is strongly pro-market: it highlights major corporate tax cuts, removal of minimum capital requirements for foreign investment, deregulation of business formation, promotion of capital markets and financial instruments, and extensive investment incentives—all consistent with classical liberal economic policy. Social liberalism is partially present through emphasis on women's and disabled persons' labor market inclusion.

chunk 7 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal signals: it endorses privatization, market competition, and sector liberalization (aviation, telecoms) but simultaneously promotes heavy state-directed industrial policy, import substitution, subsidized credit, and state-set agricultural prices, yielding a mid-range overall liberalism score.

chunk 8 · The manifesto shows consistent pro-liberal economic orientation through liberalization of aviation and telecoms sectors, opening postal services to private competition, and promoting market competition; political liberalism is partially present through accountability and decentralization measures, though the dismissal of elected mayors and appointment of trustees tempers the score.

chunk 9 · The manifesto invokes rule of law, transparency, and democratic ideals in foreign policy and governance contexts, and supports entrepreneurship and market-based regional development, suggesting moderate liberal orientation; however, the OHAL (state of emergency) measures and strong state-led development programs temper the liberal score.

chunk 10 · The manifesto expresses pro-democratic rhetoric (UN reform, rule of law, minority rights for Turkic communities, expanding freedoms), but simultaneously emphasizes national security, counter-terrorism, and state-led development, producing a mixed liberal signal; financial market policy is entirely absent from this chapter.

chunk 11 · The manifesto is primarily a technical infrastructure document; it shows moderate liberal economic tendencies through repeated use of Build-Operate-Transfer (YİD) and public-private partnership models for major projects, while justice system digitization suggests procedural rule-of-law support, and social programs for disadvantaged groups reflect limited but present social inclusion orientation.
