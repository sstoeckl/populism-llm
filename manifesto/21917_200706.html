WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames Belgian elites (Francophone politicians, the royal court, party establishment) as corrupt and self-serving, invokes the Flemish people as a unified virtuous group whose wealth is being stolen, and portrays the conflict between Flanders and the Belgian establishment in stark moral terms ("regelrechte diefstal van Vlaams geld").

chunk 2 · The text frames established parties as systematically deceiving and neglecting the Flemish people on crime, immigration, and economic transfers, while invoking 'the people' (volk) as the virtuous victim of elite betrayal, though the populist framing is secondary to the nationalist-identitarian program.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populist features: it invokes the Flemish people as a virtuous collective deserving protection, criticises incumbent socialist governance for poverty growth, and frames asylum seekers/foreigners as unfairly privileged over native Flemings, but the dominant register is policy-programmatic rather than strongly anti-elite or conspiratorial.

chunk 4 · The text frames EU institutions as undemocratic, distant bureaucracies that override the will of peoples (Denmark, Ireland referenda), invoking citizens and sovereign nations against an unresponsive elite, but the tone is more institutional-critical than strongly Manichean.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly frames elites and establishment as corrupt and oppressive, emphasizes the sovereignty and will of 'the people', and portrays a moral struggle over the theft of Flemish wealth, demonstrating strong populist rhetoric.

chunk 2 · The text explicitly criticizes established parties and elites, framing them as out of touch and opposing immigrant voting rights to protect 'our own citizens', showing anti-elitism, people-centrism, and a moral struggle against perceived threats to the community.

chunk 3 · The manifesto expresses moderate anti-elitism through concern about growing inequality and poverty, invokes 'the people' in cultural and educational contexts emphasizing assimilation, and frames societal issues in a moral struggle against multiculturalism, indicating a low to moderate populist stance overall.

chunk 4 · The text criticizes EU elites as a 'slecht werkende en logge bureaucratie' and emphasizes respect for 'de eigenheid en het zelfbeschikkingsrecht van de volkeren', showing anti-elitism and people-centrism, with moralized blame in 'zelfverloochening van Europeanen'.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames the Belgian political elite as corrupt and out of touch while championing the Flemish people's sovereignty against systemic exploitation.

chunk 2 · The text strongly frames the established political parties as corrupt, deceptive, and out of touch, while positioning the party as the defender of the virtuous Flemish people.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the state and progressive elites as imposing top-down multiculturalism and 'state dirigisme' against the identity and values of the Flemish people.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits strong populist elements by criticizing the European elites ('bewindvoerders') for ignoring democratic referendums and widening the gap with ordinary citizens.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is overwhelmingly right-wing populist: it combines ethno-nationalist Flemish independence demands, strict anti-immigration positions (immigration stop, forced assimilation, withdrawal of Islam recognition), and identity-protectionist rhetoric, with only marginal redistributive elements framed in ethnic rather than class terms.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is firmly right-wing, centered on anti-immigration, national identity protection, compulsory assimilation, voting rights restricted to citizens, and opposition to multicultural society, with strong Flemish ethno-nationalist framing throughout.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is predominantly right-wing: the manifesto centres on Flemish ethno-national identity, restricts social housing by ethnic quota, opposes multicultural society, and demands immigration controls, while social-welfare demands are secondary and framed in nationalist rather than class terms.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is clearly right-wing: the text emphasizes national/ethnic identity, opposes Turkish EU membership on cultural grounds, advocates strict immigration controls, and frames Flemish national sovereignty and European identity as core values.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text strongly advocates for right-wing populist themes such as national self-determination, strict immigration control, cultural assimilation, and opposition to multiculturalism, while showing limited left-wing populist economic concerns.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly advocates for strict immigration controls, cultural assimilation, and national identity protection, reflecting a right-wing populist ideology.

chunk 3 · The text combines left-leaning calls for redistribution and social support with strong right-leaning nationalist and anti-multiculturalism rhetoric, reflecting a mixed but overall centrist-to-right populist ideology.

chunk 4 · The manifesto rejects a European superstate, favors confederalism, excludes Turkey from EU membership, and criticizes globalism's economic effects, indicating a right-leaning populist ideology with centrist elements.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is heavily right-wing, focusing on national identity protection, anti-immigration policies, and cultural assimilation.

chunk 2 · The populist appeal is deeply embedded in a right-wing nationalist ideology focused on strict immigration controls, assimilation, and the preservation of Western values.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, focusing heavily on preserving Flemish national identity, opposing multiculturalism, and restricting social benefits and housing for foreigners.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, focusing heavily on national sovereignty, anti-immigration measures, and rejecting Turkey's EU membership.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it defends free speech, separation of powers, and some economic liberalization, but is strongly anti-liberal on social dimensions (immigration stop, forced assimilation, anti-multiculturalism, opposition to minority rights) and ambiguous on political liberalism (champions democratic rights for Flemish citizens while restricting them for foreigners and minorities).

chunk 2 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it endorses free markets and tax reduction (pro-liberal economically) but strongly restricts civil liberties for minorities and immigrants, opposes voting rights for non-citizens, advocates banning headscarves, and supports extensive state intervention in social and economic policy, yielding a below-average overall liberal score.

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores mixed-to-low on liberalism: it invokes democratic critique of the EU but opposes multicultural pluralism and minority equality (anti-liberal on social dimension), supports some market-friendly fiscal incentives but also state intervention in housing and broadcasting, and mentions interest-rate preferences for families rather than open capital markets.

chunk 4 · The text shows mixed liberal signals: it calls for democratic openness and subsidiarity but simultaneously advocates strict immigration controls, opposes Turkish membership on cultural grounds, and frames free trade as incompatible with national identity, yielding a below-average overall liberalism score.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows limited support for liberal principles, with some emphasis on political rights and freedoms but also endorses restrictive social policies and state intervention in economic and financial matters, reflecting a low overall liberalism score.

chunk 2 · The text supports a regulated market economy with government intervention to prevent abuses, criticizes political appointments, and opposes financial policies seen as harmful, indicating moderate liberal economic and political views but limited social liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports political liberalism through defense of educational freedom, social liberalism by advocating improved access for disabled persons, and economic liberalism with pension system reforms, but lacks explicit discussion of financial market liberalism, resulting in a moderate overall liberalism score.

chunk 4 · The text critiques EU decision-making as 'verre van democratisch', calls for respect of human rights, and discusses economic inequalities and market issues, reflecting moderate liberal political, social, and economic concerns but lacks financial market discussion.

Gemini

chunk 1 · While supporting basic economic freedoms and free enterprise, the text rejects social liberalism and minority rights, particularly regarding immigrants and religious symbols.

chunk 2 · The text supports a corrected free-market economy but rejects social liberalism by advocating for the restriction of political rights to citizens and opposing multiculturalism and religious pluralism.

chunk 3 · The text shows mixed classical liberal elements, supporting private property and some market mechanisms, but strongly rejects social liberalism and opposes free-market pricing in education and pensions.

chunk 4 · The text shows a highly restrictive stance on social liberalism (immigration and asylum) and expresses skepticism toward global free trade, while advocating for democratic decision-making closer to the citizens.
