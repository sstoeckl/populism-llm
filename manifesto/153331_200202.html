WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames the political establishment ("argollas, monopolios de poder, compadrazgos") as corrupt and opposed to the people, while centering "la gente" as the rightful sovereign, yielding moderate-to-high populism scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows moderate populism: it attacks political clientelism and corrupt elites managing public funds, invokes Costa Rican citizens as the rightful beneficiaries of state resources, and frames agricultural policy as a struggle against international and domestic "lies," but stops short of a fully Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populist tendencies: it criticizes decisions made "a espaldas de la población" (behind the people's back) and frames the people as the rightful sovereign in trade negotiations, but stops short of strong elite-demonization or Manichean framing, focusing instead on procedural transparency and civic participation.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elite monopolies and compadrazgos, emphasizes the party as 'the party of the people', and condemns political lies and corruption, showing moderate to strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized conflict.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes clientelism and political corruption harming the poor, emphasizes the role of 'the people' especially youth in development, and highlights moral struggles against corruption, supporting moderate populist scores.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes elite negotiation secrecy and emphasizes respect and direct engagement with the people, reflecting moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism, with some moral framing of social issues.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames politics as a struggle between 'the people' and corrupt, secretive elites ('argollas' and 'monopolios de poder') who make pacts behind the backs of citizens.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by attacking corrupt political clientelism and 'politiquería' while championing the dignity of the virtuous Costa Rican people.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing previous governments for making decisions behind the backs of the people and demanding that pacts be made with the citizens first.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is primarily centrist-reformist with mild left-leaning elements (solidarity with the poor, combating poverty, state role in redistribution), but explicitly rejects ideological dogmatism and embraces private enterprise, placing it closer to centrist than left populism.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is mixed-centrist with left-leaning elements (progressive taxation, subsidized credit, poverty reduction) and some right-leaning protectionism (agricultural tariffs, food self-sufficiency), but the overall programmatic thrust is pragmatic and centrist rather than strongly ideological.

chunk 3 · The PAC manifesto leans mildly left-populist, emphasizing social equity, workers' rights, anti-privatization of health, and solidarity with the poor, while also incorporating centrist civic-dialogue themes; right-populist elements are minimal and limited to immigration control framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text promotes solidarity, universal access to wealth creation, and a people-centered approach without right-wing nationalist or anti-immigration rhetoric, indicating a left-leaning and centrist populist ideology.

chunk 2 · The text supports left-leaning populist ideology through emphasis on universal access to wealth creation tools and social justice, right-leaning elements via agricultural protectionism, and centrist appeals through ethical governance and people-elite divides, justifying a balanced populist ideological score.

chunk 3 · The text shows a centrist populist ideology through calls for broad societal participation and dialogue, with left-leaning emphasis on solidarity and peaceful negotiation, and some right-leaning concern about immigration issues.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology leans left-centrist, emphasizing social solidarity, public services, and citizen participation over right-wing identity or anti-immigration themes.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on state intervention, agricultural subsidies, protectionism, and anti-neoliberal critiques of structural adjustment.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology leans left-centrist, combining strong opposition to privatization and support for social welfare with some protectionist concerns regarding foreign labor.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal: it champions democratic participation, rule of law, minority rights, gender equality, private enterprise, and free competition, while also assigning an active redistributive role to the state and proposing a Development Bank, producing a mixed but overall moderately pro-liberal profile.

chunk 2 · The manifesto is moderately liberal: it endorses democratic institutions, human rights, gender equality, and minority rights (pro-liberal), affirms private enterprise as the best wealth creator, but simultaneously advocates state subsidies, below-market interest rates, agricultural protectionism, and state control of key sectors, producing a mixed economic and financial-market liberalism score.

chunk 3 · The manifesto scores moderately pro-liberal overall: it strongly endorses democracy, human rights, press freedom, minority rights, and gender equality (lib_political/lib_social); it supports trade openness and competitiveness but with social safeguards (lib_economic mixed); and it promotes financial modernization and efficiency (lib_financial_market), while opposing health privatization.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political rights, minority protections, free enterprise, and financial reforms enabling market access, reflecting a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto advocates for democratic institutions, civil rights, free enterprise, and a financial system that supports productive sectors, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly supports liberal political and social values including democracy, human rights, gender equality, and minority protections, alongside economic liberalism focused on competitiveness and financial market modernization.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports democratic institutions, minority rights, and the role of private enterprise, while advocating for a regulated social market economy and inclusive financial access.

chunk 2 · While supporting democratic institutions and minority rights, the text rejects free-market economic liberalism in favor of state-directed protectionism and subsidized credit.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports classical liberal values, including human rights, minority protections, democratic transparency, and structural economic competitiveness through efficient financial and public services.
