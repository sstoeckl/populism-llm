WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto frames a narrow business elite as controlling society and moving away from the people, invokes 'folkestyre' (people's rule) as a central value, and opposes market liberalism and privatization, but stops short of a fully Manichean good-vs-evil narrative.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows only mild populist features: it calls for rebalancing power toward small independent businesses and protecting workers, but frames this in policy terms rather than through sharp elite-vs-people rhetoric or Manichean moral conflict.

chunk 3 · The text shows mild populist tendencies through occasional references to corporate short-termism and capital concentration harming communities, but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-focused rather than moralized people-vs-elite rhetoric.

chunk 4 · The text shows mild populist tendencies through appeals to "the people" and local communities against concentrated capital and multinational ownership, but the discourse is primarily programmatic and policy-oriented rather than strongly populist.

chunk 5 · The manifesto frames multinational corporations and Western elites as winners exploiting the poor, invokes 'folkestyre' (people's rule) as a core value, and draws a moral contrast between powerful elites and ordinary/poor people, but does so in a policy-oriented rather than strongly demagogic manner.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The dominant populist ideological flavor is left-leaning, emphasizing redistribution, combating poverty, strong public services, and taming market forces, with a secondary agrarian/nationalist strand (anti-EU, national sovereignty) but no strong right-populist nativist content.

chunk 2 · The populist flavour is weakly left-leaning (opposition to commercialisation of welfare, worker protections) with a minor right-leaning element (insistence on Norwegian wage conditions for labour immigrants), but neither strand is dominant enough to classify the manifesto as strongly ideological-populist.

chunk 3 · The populist flavour is weakly left-leaning (redistribution, taxing capital income, state ownership of resources) combined with agrarian/nationalist elements (national ownership, local community), but neither dimension is strongly populist in character.

chunk 4 · The text blends left-leaning redistribution (taxing capital income more heavily) with right-leaning national ownership and resource protectionism, alongside a strong centrist/agrarian focus on local communities and decentralization, reflecting Senterpartiet's characteristic agrarian-centrist populism.

chunk 5 · The manifesto leans left-populist with calls for redistribution, Tobin tax, debt relief, and political control of capital flows, but also has agrarian/centrist elements (rural development, national sovereignty, local governance) and mild right-leaning national sovereignty themes, making it a mixed but predominantly centre-left populist ideology.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines left-leaning concerns about economic inequality and social justice with right-leaning emphasis on national sovereignty and opposition to EU membership, alongside centrist calls for local democracy and decentralization, showing a mixed but overall populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the text; thus, no populist ideology is detected.

chunk 3 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the manifesto text.

chunk 4 · The text supports left populist themes through anti-corporate taxation stance, right populist themes via national resource control, and centrist populism by valuing traditional Norwegian social values, reflecting a balanced populist ideology.

chunk 5 · The text supports left-leaning populist ideology through emphasis on local resource control and fair distribution, with moderate centrist appeals to folkestyre and some right-leaning calls for institutional reforms.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is centrist-to-left, combining strong anti-globalization and anti-corporate rhetoric with a focus on social equality, mixed economy, and national sovereignty.

chunk 2 · There is no evidence of populist ideology in the text, as it focuses on standard programmatic policy proposals without populist framing.

chunk 3 · The text does not contain populist ideological framing or appeals to 'the pure people' against 'the corrupt elite'.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is a blend of agrarian-right protectionism (national ownership, anti-globalization) and left-leaning tax policies targeting capital income.

chunk 5 · The populist elements are centrist and agrarian, focusing on decentralization and local sovereignty without strong left-wing socialist or right-wing nativist framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is moderately pro-liberal on political rights and social equality (supporting democratic participation, anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ rights), but explicitly anti-liberal on economic and financial-market dimensions, calling for capital flow restrictions, opposing privatization, and advocating state control over markets.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports a free and independent press and equality legislation (pro-liberal), but consistently opposes privatisation and marketisation of health, care, and media services, yielding a mixed overall liberalism score; financial-market policy is entirely absent.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is mixed on liberalism: it supports democracy and equal educational access (pro-liberal) but advocates state ownership of energy resources, Tobin tax on capital flows, opposition to privatisation of Statkraft/Statnett, and state-directed industrial policy (anti-liberal on economic and financial dimensions).

chunk 4 · The text is mixed on liberalism: it supports local democracy and market transparency (stock market oversight, anti-insider trading), but explicitly rejects industry neutrality, favors active state ownership, national ownership requirements, and capital controls, placing it in a moderate, mixed-liberal position.

chunk 5 · The manifesto supports democracy-building and anti-discrimination (moderately pro-liberal politically and socially), but explicitly calls for political control of markets and capital flows, a Tobin tax on currency transactions, binding periods for foreign investments, and subordination of WTO to the UN, all of which are anti-liberal economically and financially.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports political liberalism through calls for open government and transparency, social liberalism via equal rights and anti-discrimination, economic liberalism with some critique of market excesses, and a nuanced stance on financial markets acknowledging the need for regulation but wary of unfettered capital flows.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through advocacy for a free and independent press and media pluralism, social liberalism via multicultural tolerance and anti-racism, and economic liberalism by promoting balanced power in business favoring smaller enterprises; however, it lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports political liberalism through emphasis on democratic institutions and economic liberalism via support for market-based economic development and decentralization, while also endorsing social liberalism by valuing diversity and specialization in education.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows strong support for political liberalism via NATO and international cooperation, social liberalism through animal welfare and equality, economic liberalism by promoting market-friendly tax policies, and financial market liberalism by advocating clearer insider trading laws and investor protections.

chunk 5 · The manifesto affirms respect for human rights, political freedoms, market importance with political oversight, and calls for political control of financial markets, indicating a moderately pro-liberal stance overall.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political democracy and social equality, but rejects market liberalism and free capital flows in favor of state regulation and a mixed economy.

chunk 2 · The text supports political liberalism through media freedom and social liberalism through minority rights, but scores low on economic liberalism due to strong opposition to privatization and market competition in public services.

chunk 3 · The text supports democratic institutions and cultural pluralism, but strongly opposes economic liberalism by advocating state ownership, market regulation, and capital transaction taxes.

chunk 4 · The text supports local democracy and equal rights but strongly rejects economic liberalism by advocating for state intervention, protectionism, and rejecting market neutrality.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports political democracy and human rights, but rejects economic liberalism and free financial markets in favor of heavy state intervention, protectionism, and capital controls.
