WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly frames the ruling regime as corrupt, oligarchic, and criminal ("autoritarnog, retrogradnog, kriminogenog i oligarhijskog režima"), invokes the people's interest against elite capture, and presents a Manichean choice between a "captive and poor" versus "free and prosperous" Montenegro.

chunk 2 · The manifesto exhibits strong anti-elitism by directly accusing the ruling regime of collusion with organized crime and acting against the public interest, while invoking citizen sovereignty and framing politics as a moral struggle between a corrupt elite and the people.

chunk 3 · The manifesto strongly frames the ruling regime as corrupt, oligarchic, and authoritarian ("kriminogenog i oligarhijskog režima"), portrays citizens as victims of elite betrayal, and draws a stark moral binary between the captive present and a free future Montenegro, yielding high populism scores across all three sub-dimensions.

chunk 4 · The manifesto frames bad privatizations as having made workers the "biggest victims of transition" and explicitly identifies a criminal nexus at the top of power, combining anti-elite rhetoric with a Manichean good-vs-evil framing, while invoking citizens' sovereignty as the normative anchor.

chunk 5 · The text criticises nepotism, clientelism and political patronage in public institutions and frames the ruling regime as persecuting genuine civil-society activists, but the anti-elite framing is moderate and policy-focused rather than strongly moralistic or conspiratorial.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes a corrupt, authoritarian regime and calls for the people to confront the truth and make decisions in the public interest, framing a moral struggle between a captive impoverished state and a free prosperous one, demonstrating strong anti-elitism, people-centrism, and manichean populist themes.

chunk 2 · The text explicitly criticizes the ruling regime as self-serving elites and highlights the need for a trusted parliament representing the people, indicating moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism with some moral conflict framing.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes a corrupt, authoritarian, and oligarchic regime, invoking the people’s choice between oppression and freedom, and frames societal issues in moral terms, demonstrating strong populist rhetoric.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the regime as self-serving elites and highlights the need for a state where every citizen feels secure, indicating moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism, alongside references to conspiratorial elite collusion.

chunk 5 · The text does not contain explicit references to anti-elitism, people-centrism, or moralized good-vs-evil struggles, indicating no evidence of populism.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly attacks the ruling elite as a corrupt, criminogenic oligarchy that has captured state institutions, while framing the upcoming election as a moral choice between a captive and a free Montenegro.

chunk 2 · The text strongly attacks the ruling regime as corrupt, captive, and colluding with organized crime, while advocating for the sovereignty of citizens.

chunk 3 · The text strongly frames the ruling elite as a corrupt, criminogenic oligarchy that has captured state institutions, contrasting them with the public interest.

chunk 4 · The text strongly frames the ruling regime as corrupt, greedy, and out of touch, while positioning the workers and citizens as victims of transition.

chunk 5 · The text criticizes the ruling elite for political clientelism, nepotism, and favoring privileged cultural creators close to the government.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is predominantly left-leaning, emphasizing workers as victims of privatization, redistribution, social protection, and state ownership of key industries, while also invoking national identity concerns and condemning "neoliberal economic ideology," placing it as left-populist with minor right-populist nationalist undertones.

chunk 2 · The populist appeals are primarily centrist-reformist, focused on regime change and institutional reform rather than a clear left or right ideological program; some mild left-leaning social welfare measures and mild conservative family-values rhetoric are present but neither dominates.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is primarily left-leaning (workers as victims of privatization, strong social protection demands, state ownership of key industries) with secondary anti-elite/centrist appeals and only minor nationalist-right elements (regime nationalism mentioned critically), placing the overall ideological flavor in a moderate left-populist register.

chunk 4 · The dominant populist ideological flavor is left-leaning: the text centers on workers' rights, redistribution, social housing, pension protection, and state guarantees against market abuses, with only minor nativist/identity elements.

chunk 5 · The populist flavour is mild and centrist-technocratic: the text calls for expertise over political patronage and depoliticisation of public institutions, with some left-leaning social-spending proposals (higher wages, universal healthcare, free education), but no nationalist/identity or strongly anti-corporate rhetoric.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text advocates for breaking harmful contracts and illegal deals, indicating left-populist anti-elite economic stances, while also condemning regime nationalism and ethnic divisions, reflecting right-populist identity concerns; it balances these with centrist appeals to truth and responsibility, showing a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto shows a moderate left populist stance against corruption and elite capture, some centrist appeals to democratic institutions, and mild right-leaning emphasis on regional stability and national interests.

chunk 3 · The text combines left-wing critiques of corruption and clientelism with right-wing nationalist elements and centrist ambiguity about EU integration, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 4 · The text shows left-leaning populist concern for workers harmed by privatization, some right-leaning nationalist symbolism emphasis, and centrist calls for broad consensus and strategic governance.

chunk 5 · There is no explicit discussion of left, right, or centrist populist ideological themes in the text, thus no evidence of populist ideology.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-leaning, explicitly criticizing neoliberal economic ideology and calling for state ownership of key industries and extensive social protections.

chunk 2 · The populist framing is combined with conservative and traditional identity appeals, such as introducing the traditional tricolor flag and elevating the status of the Serbian language.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is combined with left-leaning socio-economic demands, such as protecting workers, establishing social safety nets, and state intervention in key industries.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is predominantly left-wing, focusing heavily on workers' rights, social protection, and anti-privatization, with minor traditionalist/nationalist elements.

chunk 5 · The populist critique is centrist, focusing on political corruption, nepotism, and elite control of public institutions without a strong left-wing or right-wing ideological framing.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows mixed liberal signals: it endorses rule of law, democracy, and EU integration (pro-liberal), but also advocates extensive state ownership of energy and industry, nationalization of funds, and strong state intervention in markets, tempering its overall liberal score to the middle range.

chunk 2 · The manifesto broadly supports rule of law, judicial independence, anti-discrimination, minority rights, and free enterprise with tax incentives, reflecting moderate classical-liberal commitments, though it also calls for significant state intervention in social and economic policy.

chunk 3 · The manifesto endorses rule of law, democratic change, and anti-discrimination (pro-liberal on political and social dimensions), while mixing market-economy rhetoric with extensive state ownership proposals and a development bank (mixed on economic and financial-market liberalism), yielding a moderate overall liberalism score.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly endorses rule of law, judicial independence, minority rights, and anti-discrimination measures (pro-liberal), while simultaneously advocating state intervention in housing, pensions, and labor markets and treating financial-market activity with suspicion (mixed/moderately anti-liberal on economic dimensions).

chunk 5 · The manifesto supports institutional autonomy from political interference, encourages private-sector participation in education and culture, and calls for inclusion of persons with disabilities, reflecting a moderate pro-liberal stance across political, social and economic dimensions, while financial-market policy is entirely absent.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto calls for strengthening democratic institutions, rule of law, and transparency, supports inclusive social rights and equality, demands comprehensive economic reforms for a sustainable market economy, and emphasizes improving financial market regulation to enable market function, reflecting a broadly pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto discusses the need for rule of law, human rights, social equality, economic development, and addresses financial market corruption, reflecting a moderate pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto acknowledges institutional capture and political repression (lib_political), notes discrimination issues (lib_social), supports economic reforms and market growth (lib_economic), and calls for improved financial market regulation (lib_financial_market), indicating moderate liberal commitments.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports rule of law and human rights, acknowledges shortcomings in social rights and economic reforms, but lacks discussion on financial market liberalism, resulting in a moderate liberalism score.

chunk 5 · The manifesto emphasizes autonomy of educational institutions from political influence, access to education in native languages, support for private educational institutions, and reforms promoting market-oriented education policies, indicating a moderately pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · While the text supports the rule of law and democratic institutions, it advocates for heavy state intervention, nationalization, and price controls, which limits its overall classical liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The text strongly advocates for the rule of law, judicial independence, human rights, and democratic reforms to transition to a market economy.

chunk 3 · The text advocates for democratic reforms, rule of law, and a sustainable market economy, while also proposing state-backed financial institutions and social protections.

chunk 4 · The text supports pluralistic democracy, human rights, and minority protections, while advocating for a transition to a market economy alongside strong social safety nets.

chunk 5 · The text strongly supports liberal principles including institutional autonomy from political influence, minority/disability rights, and fostering private competition.
