WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently frames the current government as corrupt and serving power rather than the people ("demokracia slúži moci a nie ľuďom"), invokes the Slovak nation as a unified virtuous subject whose interests SNS alone defends, and portrays politics as a moral struggle between the honest people and self-serving elites ("vševediace politické elity").

chunk 2 · The text criticises the current government for reducing state support and pursuing a policy of "social ignorance," but the anti-elite framing is moderate and policy-focused rather than morally Manichean; people-centrism is present but expressed through programmatic social-policy proposals rather than a unified virtuous-people narrative.

chunk 3 · The text shows moderate populist tendencies: it frames special courts and high legal fees as tools that serve the powerful at the expense of ordinary citizens, invokes equal access to justice for all citizens, and criticizes political elites for subordinating law to politics, but does not construct a strong Manichean people-vs.-corrupt-elite narrative throughout.

chunk 4 · The text frames foreign and domestic elites (lobbyist groups, incompetent politicians) as corrupt actors undermining national sovereignty, invokes the nationally-oriented people as the virtuous reference point, and draws a moral contrast between genuine national interest and the manipulative will of outside forces.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the ruling elites as corrupt and irresponsible, emphasizes the defense of the national people as a unified group, and describes a moralized political struggle, supporting a moderately high populism score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the current political elite's social neglect and emphasizes the state as a community of responsible citizens, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral conflict.

chunk 3 · The manifesto expresses moderate anti-elitism through criticism of government reforms and judicial inefficiencies, emphasizes people-centrism by focusing on judicial actors and legal processes, and shows a mild manichean view by condemning special courts as political dominance over law.

chunk 4 · The text expresses distrust of external elites controlling key sectors and emphasizes national unity and defense, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moralized threats.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly frames the ruling political elites as corrupt, out of touch, and acting against the interests of the Slovak nation, while positioning the party as the sole defender of the virtuous Slovak people.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the ruling coalition as socially ignorant and corrupting the division of powers, while positioning the party as the defender of the common Slovak citizen.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the ruling elite's governance and advocating for the common citizens against moral corruption.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing incompetent and arrogant politicians while framing national defense as a struggle against foreign control and domestic lobby groups.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is predominantly right-wing populist: it centres on national identity, sovereignty, hostility to the Hungarian minority party's political influence, and protection of Slovak national interests, while combining this with social-solidarity rhetoric that adds a secondary left-populist flavour.

chunk 2 · The SNS manifesto blends left-leaning social-welfare demands (higher state spending, VAT reductions for the poor, stronger pension solidarity) with right-wing national-identity themes (Slovak language enforcement, rejection of Hungarian minority coalition policies, Christian cultural heritage), placing it in a mixed but moderately right-nationalist populist ideological space.

chunk 3 · The SNS manifesto blends right-wing national-populist ideology (strong emphasis on Slovak national state, identity protection, resistance to Hungarian minority demands, Slavic solidarity) with some left-leaning social-state elements (solidarity, inheritance tax, equal access to justice), making the right-populist strand dominant.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is predominantly right-wing, emphasising national sovereignty, Christian-national identity, territorial integrity, and resistance to foreign influence, with no significant left-wing redistributive or anti-corporate framing.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text combines nationalistic, social, and centrist populist elements, emphasizing national interests, social solidarity, and a unique political identity, justifying a moderate to high populist ideology score across left, right, and centrist dimensions.

chunk 2 · The text shows a moderate left-leaning populist ideology through calls for redistribution and social support, combined with right-leaning national identity protection and centrist appeals to citizen responsibility.

chunk 3 · The text strongly supports national state sovereignty and identity (right-wing populism), advocates social justice and tax fairness (left-wing elements), and includes centrist appeals to balance freedom, economy, and social solidarity, reflecting a mixed populist ideology.

chunk 4 · The manifesto combines nationalistic defense emphasis with calls for political control and some anti-elite economic concerns, indicating a centrist-right populist ideology with mild leftist economic critique.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is heavily right-wing nationalist, focusing on protecting the Slovak nation against the Hungarian minority and foreign cultural influences, combined with left-wing economic protectionism against privatization.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, focusing heavily on protecting Slovak national identity against Hungarian assimilation, combined with left-wing economic demands like higher taxes on the rich.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, emphasizing the preservation of the national state and identity, combined with some left-wing welfare state rhetoric.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, focusing on national sovereignty, Christian traditions, and protecting the nation-state from external globalizing forces.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto accepts market economy and rule of law in principle but consistently advocates strong state intervention in privatisation (51% state ownership in strategic firms), differentiated VAT, state-directed capital allocation, and protection of national assets from foreign sale, yielding a mixed-to-low liberal score across all dimensions.

chunk 2 · The manifesto endorses democracy and rule of law in principle but simultaneously advocates state monopoly over health insurance, higher taxes on dividends, strong state direction of culture and education, and enforcement of Slovak language norms, producing a mixed liberal profile that leans moderately anti-liberal in economic and financial-market dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto affirms constitutional equality, rule of law, and democratic principles, but tempers economic liberalism with social-state corrections and market intervention; on social liberalism it formally rejects discrimination while simultaneously opposing positive discrimination for Roma and framing minority rights in nationalist terms, producing a mixed overall liberal profile.

chunk 4 · The text supports civilian political oversight of the military (moderately liberal) but strongly emphasises state control over the economy, energy, finance, and communications, and frames foreign financial flows as a security threat, reflecting a statist rather than liberal economic orientation.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto supports democratic principles, social welfare, market economy development, and targeted financial policies enabling market function, reflecting a moderate pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 2 · The manifesto supports democratic institutions, social equality for vulnerable groups, economic measures like tax adjustments, and prudent financial market oversight, indicating a moderately pro-liberal stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports political liberalism through emphasis on rights and media freedom, social liberalism by rejecting discrimination and supporting minority rights, economic liberalism by advocating tax reform and market clarity, but lacks evidence on financial market policies.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports political control, national defense, and some economic concerns but lacks discussion of financial market liberalism, resulting in a moderate liberalism score.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits low support for classical liberalism, advocating for state intervention in markets, opposing privatization of strategic enterprises, criticizing minority political representation, and expressing skepticism toward European financial integration.

chunk 2 · The text criticizes the current state of the rule of law and rejects market-based pension privatization, advocating instead for state-controlled social-market policies and a single state-run health insurance company.

chunk 3 · The text shows a mixed stance on liberalism, supporting basic constitutional rights but advocating for state intervention in markets and the abolition of special courts.

chunk 4 · The text opposes liberal principles by advocating for state control over key economic and financial sectors to prevent foreign ownership, while emphasizing traditional Christian values over pluralism.
