📄 Full text: the scored text is the Manifesto Corpus version 2026-1, key 180240_201410, available through the Manifesto Project API (free registration), e.g. in R: manifestoR::mp_corpus(data.frame(party = 180240, date = 201410)). The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: it criticizes entrenched political elites for patrimonialism and corruption, invokes the Brazilian people's dreams as the lodestar of policy, and frames the current political system as a moral failure, but it does so in a technocratic, reform-oriented register rather than a sharply Manichean one.

chunk 2 · The text is primarily a technocratic policy manifesto focused on agrarian reform, credit markets, infrastructure, and energy; it contains only mild anti-elite framing (criticising discretionary credit to large firms and political capture of Incra) and occasional references to population welfare, but lacks strong Manichean or people-vs-elite rhetoric.

chunk 3 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto focused on environmental, educational, and cultural policy; it contains only mild anti-elite rhetoric (e.g., power concentrated in few hands at Ancine, stagnation under recent governments) and appeals to broad social inclusion, but lacks strong populist framing of a virtuous people versus a corrupt elite or Manichean good-vs-evil narratives.

chunk 4 · The text shows mild populist tendencies: it criticizes lobbies and concentrated corporate power (e.g., large companies dominating cultural incentives), but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than a strong people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 5 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto focused on health, urban planning, sanitation, mobility, and public security; it invokes the people's needs and social inequalities but does not frame elites as corrupt enemies or construct a strong Manichean good-vs-evil narrative, resulting in very low populism scores across all sub-dimensions.

chunk 6 · The text shows mild populist features: it criticizes the chronic incapacity of state institutions and highlights the people's role as protagonists of democracy, but frames issues primarily through rights-based and policy-reform language rather than a sharp elite-vs-people antagonism.

chunk 7 · The text invokes popular movements and the working poor as virtuous protagonists against concentrated wealth and speculative elites, with moderate but consistent people-centric and anti-elite framing, though it stops short of a fully Manichean conspiracy narrative.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The coalition explicitly identifies with "socialismo democrático" and emphasizes redistribution, land reform, and social justice, placing it on the center-left; it explicitly rejects old polarization and frames itself as a cross-partisan reformist force, with minimal right-populist or nativist content.

chunk 2 · The manifesto blends centre-left social policy (Bolsa Família expansion, minimum wage, land reform) with market-oriented proposals (PPPs, free energy market, credit reform), placing it closer to a moderate centre-left than a strongly populist ideological position; there is no right-wing nativist or identity-based populism.

chunk 3 · The limited populist elements lean mildly left, emphasizing social equity, redistribution through education funding, and combating ethnic and social inequalities, with no significant right-wing nativist or identity-protectionist framing.

chunk 4 · The manifesto leans left-of-center, emphasizing redistribution, reduction of inequality, universal public services, and social programs, without nationalist or identity-based right-wing populist elements.

chunk 5 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist through its emphasis on reducing social inequalities, universal public health, and redistribution of resources to the poor, but it also embraces market participation in housing and sanitation, making the overall ideological flavor moderate and mixed rather than strongly left-populist.

chunk 6 · The manifesto leans left-populist in its emphasis on reducing economic and social inequalities, supporting vulnerable groups, affirmative action, and social movements, with no significant right-populist (nativist/anti-immigration) content.

chunk 7 · The text is clearly left-leaning populist, championing land reform, wage protection, union rights, and redistribution for workers, women, Afro-Brazilians, and LGBT groups, with no right-wing nativist or anti-immigration content.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across all four dimensions: it champions democratic deepening and rule of law, endorses gender/ethnic/LGBTQ+ equality and pluralism, proposes tax simplification, deregulation, and market-oriented reforms, and calls for central bank independence and market-reflective pricing by state enterprises.

chunk 2 · The manifesto consistently favours market competition, PPPs, credit market liberalisation (eliminating mandatory credit directives, reducing spreads, expanding the free energy market), and fiscal transparency, while retaining targeted subsidies for agriculture and social programmes, yielding a moderately pro-liberal overall stance.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal across dimensions: it endorses democratic governance, social diversity and pluralism, market competition in forestry and creative industries, and public credit mechanisms to enable market participation, though it also advocates significant state intervention in education and culture, producing mixed scores.

chunk 4 · The text supports participatory democracy, transparency, and digital inclusion (lib_political); promotes diversity and inclusivity (lib_social); shows mixed signals on economic liberalism by combining market references with strong state intervention; and endorses risk capital and innovation financing mechanisms (lib_financial_market).

chunk 5 · The manifesto supports civil rights, anti-discrimination, and human rights (lib_social, lib_political), endorses private sector participation in housing and sanitation alongside public investment (lib_economic mixed), and mentions combating financial circuits of organized crime but does not substantively address financial market liberalization (lib_financial_market weak), yielding a moderate overall liberalism score.

chunk 6 · The text is strongly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions, championing civil rights, anti-discrimination, LGBT rights, indigenous rights, and democratic participation; it shows moderate economic liberalism through entrepreneurship promotion, while financial-market policy is entirely absent.

chunk 7 · The text strongly supports democratic participation, civil rights, and social pluralism (LGBT, minorities, children's rights), while being skeptical of pure labour-market deregulation, yielding a moderately pro-liberal overall profile with no evidence on financial markets.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal political values such as freedom of expression and cultural autonomy, social liberal values including protection of traditional knowledge, and economic liberalism through support for creative economy and intellectual property rights, but lacks discussion on financial market liberalism.

chunk 4 · The manifesto supports constitutional rights, social protections, economic stimulation of microenterprises, and acknowledges fiscal policies, indicating moderate classical liberalism in political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 5 · The manifesto explicitly supports liberal principles such as constitutional guarantees for political rights and urban social function, social security and safety, and economic policies promoting housing programs with market participation, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 6 · The manifesto strongly emphasizes political and social liberal values such as citizenship, human rights, anti-discrimination, and social inclusion, with verified quotes supporting liberal political and social dimensions; economic liberalism is moderately supported, but financial market liberalism is not addressed.

chunk 7 · The text affirms liberal political values through democratic development, social liberalism via child rights and anti-discrimination, economic liberalism in transport proposals, but lacks financial market discussion.
