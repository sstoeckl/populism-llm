WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto contains moderate populist elements: it frames established lobbying interests and financial elites as corrupt obstacles to progress, invokes the people's collective wellbeing, and uses moral language ("klopt niet en het deugt niet"), but the overall tone is programmatic and policy-focused rather than strongly populist.

chunk 2 · The text shows moderate populist tendencies: it frames lobbyists, bureaucrats and "political backrooms" as corrupt insiders opposed to the people, invokes "the people" as the rightful owners of government, and casts financial markets and multinationals as illegitimate rulers — but these appeals are embedded in a programmatic, institutional reform agenda rather than a purely Manichean populist narrative.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes the established elite's influence and emphasizes unity of 'the people' against inequality and division, reflecting anti-elitism and people-centrism, with some moralized conflict about societal threats.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly criticizes governance by lobbyists and bureaucrats, emphasizes that the government belongs to the people, and highlights increasing political and social divisions, indicating moderate to strong populist themes.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits moderate populism by framing the political and economic status quo as dictated by lobbyists of the established order, while advocating for a unified society.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing the rule of lobbyists and bureaucrats while advocating for the return of power to the people.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is clearly left-leaning, emphasizing redistribution, anti-corporate elite sentiment, labor over capital, and social equality, with explicit rejection of right-wing nativist positions on immigration and refugees.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are clearly left-leaning: the text targets corporate elites, financial markets, and multinationals, advocates redistribution and social solidarity, opposes TTIP/CETA for undermining social and environmental standards, and calls for a "social Europe" — all hallmarks of left-populist ideology.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports left-wing populist themes like fighting tax avoidance, reducing inequality, and social justice, while acknowledging some right-wing populist concerns about societal fears and identity, and centrist appeals to unity and fairness.

chunk 2 · The text strongly opposes financial markets and multinationals controlling governance, supports solidarity, and emphasizes government accountability to the people, reflecting a left-leaning populist ideology with centrist appeals.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on reducing economic inequality, taxing wealth, and opposing corporate interests and neoliberal policies.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing its critique on financial markets and multinational corporations rather than national identity or immigration.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto scores high on political and social liberalism (strong democracy, anti-discrimination, LGBTQ+ rights), but is mixed-to-low on economic and financial market liberalism, advocating significant state intervention, financial transaction taxes, bank separation, and public control over money creation.

chunk 2 · The text is strongly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions (robust democracy, rule of law, digital rights, LGBTQ+ protections), mixed on economic liberalism (supports open trade in principle but opposes TTIP/CETA and calls for social/environmental conditionality), and moderately anti-liberal on financial markets (frames financial markets as a threat to democratic governance).

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto promotes democratic institutions, civil rights, social equality, a green and inclusive economy, and regulated financial markets that serve society, demonstrating a broadly pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democracy, rule of law, civil rights, social equality, free trade with social safeguards, and prudent financial cooperation within the EU, demonstrating a robust liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democratic rights and anti-discrimination, but rejects economic and financial liberalism in favor of heavy state intervention and regulation.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through robust protections for the rule of law, privacy, and minority rights, while taking a highly interventionist and critical stance on economic and financial markets.
