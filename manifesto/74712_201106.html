📄 Full text: the scored text is the Manifesto Corpus version 2026-1, key 74712_201106, available through the Manifesto Project API (free registration), e.g. in R: manifestoR::mp_corpus(data.frame(party = 74712, date = 201106)). The position given with each quote (“at about N% of the text”) refers to that text.

WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto invokes the Turkish nation as a unified virtuous people whose will MHP channels, frames global elites and AKP as corrupt adversaries, but also presents detailed programmatic governance proposals that moderate pure populist rhetoric.

chunk 2 · The text contains mild populist elements—invoking the nation/people as the rightful owner of the state and framing corrupt officials as enemies of the public good—but the dominant register is technocratic and policy-oriented, with detailed institutional reform proposals rather than a sustained people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The text is a detailed policy manifesto focused on science, technology, industry, energy, agriculture, transport, and social welfare; it contains minimal populist rhetoric — a brief reference to "rant ekonomisi" (rent economy) as negative and occasional appeals to the nation/people, but no sustained elite-bashing, Manichean framing, or people-as-sovereign discourse.

chunk 4 · The text is primarily a detailed policy manifesto with minimal populist framing; there is modest people-centric language around the nation and terror, a brief anti-elite reference regarding misuse of zoning authority, and a Manichean framing of the terror struggle, but no sustained elite-vs-people narrative.

chunk 5 · The text invokes the nation (millet) as a unified virtuous collective and frames Turkey's security and sovereignty in terms of protecting the people, but anti-elitism is limited to implicit criticism of submissiveness and foreign interference rather than explicit domestic elite-bashing, yielding a moderate overall populism score.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text exhibits strong people-centrism and anti-elitism by framing the ruling party as a corrupt, anti-democratic 'civil dictate' and positioning the party as the voice of the national conscience.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits mild populism by framing corrupt elites as 'hortumcular' (exploiters) and emphasizing the defense of the nation's trust against moral decay.

chunk 3 · The text contains minimal populist rhetoric, focusing on national values and the nation without explicit anti-elitist or moralizing good-versus-evil framing.

chunk 4 · The text does not contain any populist rhetoric, anti-elitist claims, or moralized good-versus-evil framing.

chunk 5 · The text focuses heavily on national security and the defense of the nation, invoking the nation's peace and security without explicit anti-elitist or moralizing populist rhetoric.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The dominant ideological flavor is right-wing nationalist populism, emphasizing Turkish national identity, unitary state, single language, anti-separatism, and civilizational mission, with secondary social-welfare redistribution themes that do not reach left-populist levels.

chunk 2 · The manifesto (MHP, Turkey 2011) blends right-leaning economic nationalism—protecting the 'national character' of financial markets, reducing foreign dominance, and a 'national production economy'—with moderate left-leaning social-welfare concerns (fairer income distribution, protecting the poor), placing its populist ideology primarily on the right-nationalist side with some left-redistributive elements.

chunk 3 · The manifesto blends mild centre-right nationalist themes (Turkish language, national culture, Islamic education, national innovation system) with redistributive social-welfare pledges (poverty relief, agricultural subsidies, disability allowances), placing it in a moderate right-leaning populist ideological space without strong left or centrist populist dominance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto leans toward right-leaning populist ideology through strong emphasis on national unity, Turkish cultural identity, Islamic values in education, and anti-separatist framing, while social-democratic elements (labor rights, anti-discrimination) are present but secondary.

chunk 5 · The manifesto is predominantly right-wing nationalist populist, emphasizing national unity, territorial integrity, anti-terrorism, Turkish world solidarity, and sovereign independence, with only marginal redistributive (left) or generic centrist people-elite appeals.

chunk 3 · Metin populist ideolojiye dair sol, sağ veya merkezci programatik ifadeler içermemektedir.

chunk 4 · There is no explicit evidence of populist ideological stances (left, right, or centrist) in the manifesto text.

chunk 5 · There is no explicit evidence of left, right, or centrist populist ideological content in the manifesto; it focuses on security, development, and foreign policy without populist framing.

chunk 2 · The populist elements are centrist, focusing on clean governance and serving the collective will of the nation without strong left-wing or right-wing populist framing.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology leans right-wing, emphasizing the preservation of national culture, moral values, and the Turkish language.

chunk 4 · There is no evidence of populist ideology in the text, as it focuses on standard policy proposals without populist framing.

chunk 5 · The populist ideology is strongly right-wing, emphasizing national culture, territorial integrity, and the unitary state structure against external and internal threats.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto consistently endorses rule of law, judicial independence, separation of powers, free-market competition, and basic rights protections, but tempers liberal pluralism with strong nationalist restrictions on minority rights and ethnic autonomy, yielding a mixed-to-moderate liberal overall score.

chunk 2 · The manifesto broadly supports democratic rule of law, competitive market economy, and prudential financial regulation, but tempers liberal openness with economic nationalism (protecting the 'national character' of banks, restricting foreign dominance in capital markets, and interventionist currency/debt management), yielding a mixed-to-moderate liberal profile overall.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports competitive markets, private-sector investment, and deregulation in energy and telecoms (pro-liberal), but also advocates extensive state intervention — price guarantees for agricultural products, state-directed R&D, public procurement as industrial policy, and media content regulation — producing a mixed liberal profile averaging around 5.

chunk 4 · The manifesto shows moderate liberal orientation: it promotes democratization of higher education, removal of anti-democratic restrictions (headscarf ban), private sector incentives, and market-based pricing mechanisms, but also features significant state intervention in health, education, and social services, yielding a mixed-to-moderate liberal profile.

chunk 5 · The text gives mixed liberal signals: it endorses democratic standards, rule of law, and market economy in principle (especially for Turkic republics), but also stresses strong state security, national sovereignty, and restrictions on political actors supporting terrorism, producing mid-range scores across political, social, and economic dimensions with no financial-market content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto advocates for democratic governance, rule of law, social rights including gender equality, a competitive market economy, and state regulatory functions that enable market operations, indicating a pro-liberal stance overall with some mixed views on financial market regulation.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, transparency, civil rights, market economy principles, and financial market regulation that enables market function, demonstrating a pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · Metin, özel hayatın gizliliğinin korunması, medya denetimi, rekabetçi sanayi politikaları gibi liberal değerlere yönelik açık destek içermektedir.

chunk 4 · The manifesto explicitly supports democratic reforms in higher education, gender equality measures, and economic policies promoting fair wages and tax reductions, indicating a pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions.

chunk 5 · The manifesto emphasizes rule of law in counter-terrorism, protection of citizens' rights including minorities abroad, and economic development programs, indicating a moderately pro-liberal stance in political, social, and economic dimensions, though financial market issues are not addressed.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles including the separation of powers, individual rights, and a competitive free-market economy.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports classical liberal principles, including the rule of law, market economy rules, preventing monopolies, and strengthening financial markets and investor protections.

chunk 3 · The text supports classical liberal economic principles such as privatization, deregulation, and market-based financial instruments like futures exchanges, alongside basic protections for individual privacy and disadvantaged groups.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits a mixed classical liberal stance, supporting private enterprise and university autonomy while restricting minority language education.

chunk 5 · The text supports classical liberal principles like the rule of law and market economy, though these are subordinated to national security and state integrity.
