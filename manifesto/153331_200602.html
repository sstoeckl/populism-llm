WarningHow to read this page

The scores are holistic judgements. Each model read the whole manifesto text (in chunks of up to 20,000 tokens) and rated every dimension from the text as a whole. The passages quoted further down are examples the model cited, not the basis of the score: a score that rests on two paragraphs cannot be reduced to a few words, and a quoted passage alone will often look too weak, or too strong, for the number next to it. Read the models’ own explanations first; they state the reasoning behind each block of scores. The quotes then show where in the text the models looked.

Populism, populist ideology and liberalism

Thirteen dimensions scored on 0–10 by three model families from the full manifesto text, each with a verbatim evidence quote. Scores are aggregated as in Rode & Stöckl (2026): a chunk counts if the model marks its evidence as verified and the quote is found verbatim in the manifesto chunk (and is not copied from the prompt); confidence-weighted mean within model, plain mean across models.

What the models say about this manifesto

Each model’s own explanation of its scores, one per block of dimensions, written together with the scores.

Populism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto shows moderate populist tendencies: it criticizes prior governments for undermining the state and favoring inoperative development models, invokes 'the people' and citizen action as the central force for change, and frames politics in terms of honesty vs. corruption/deception, but stops short of a full Manichean good-vs-evil narrative or strong elite-vs-people dichotomy.

chunk 2 · The text shows only mild populist tendencies: it criticizes political clientelism and corruption by elites, invokes citizen participation, and frames some issues as conflicts between public interest and powerful financial interests, but does not construct a strong Manichean people-vs-elite narrative.

chunk 3 · The manifesto shows moderate populist features: it frames powerful economic groups as exploiting public debt resources and criticises "neoliberals" in past governments, while invoking the welfare of "el pueblo de Costa Rica," but the dominant tone is technocratic and policy-oriented rather than strongly Manichean.

chunk 4 · The text shows moderate populist features: it criticizes political clientelism and corruption among public officials (anti-elitism), invokes citizen participation and community solidarity (people-centrism), but stops short of a strong Manichean good-vs-evil framing, focusing instead on technocratic reform proposals.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto explicitly calls for national consciousness and citizen responsibility, invoking 'the people' as a unified group, critiques corruption among elites, and frames development as a struggle against obstacles, reflecting moderate populist elements.

chunk 2 · The manifesto explicitly critiques the absence of a concrete political project and calls for national unity and an alternative national project, indicating moderate anti-elitism and people-centrism, with some moralized critique of corruption.

chunk 3 · The manifesto explicitly discusses anti-elitism through calls for citizen control of corruption and despoliticization, people-centrism by guaranteeing citizen access to public services, and a moral stance against speculation and abuse in fuel pricing, indicating moderate populist elements.

chunk 4 · The text explicitly criticizes public officials and groups of pressure acting for their own benefit, emphasizes democratic participation and control, and calls for elimination of corruption, reflecting moderate anti-elitism, people-centrism, and moral struggle themes.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text frames politics as a struggle of the virtuous citizenry against corrupt practices, clientelism, and political vices.

chunk 2 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing political corruption, clientelism, and the influence of private interests over the public good, while emphasizing citizen participation and community empowerment.

chunk 3 · The text exhibits moderate populism by contrasting the virtuous 'pueblo' with corrupt or 'neoliberal' elites who have blocked public investment.

chunk 4 · The text exhibits moderate populism by criticizing traditional politicians and clientelism while advocating for citizen-led, decentralized decision-making.

Populist ideology

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto leans moderately left-populist, emphasizing redistribution, solidarity, state-led social services, and criticism of structural adjustment programs that harmed workers and producers, while also invoking national sovereignty and borders; it explicitly rejects dogmatism and proposes a mixed economy, placing it closer to center-left than hard left.

chunk 2 · The manifesto leans mildly left-populist, emphasizing fair trade, workers' wages, poverty elimination, and state control of natural resources, while also invoking citizen participation broadly; right-populist elements (immigration framing) are minimal and incidental.

chunk 3 · The populist flavour is predominantly left-leaning, emphasising subsidised credit for weaker productive sectors, redistribution, state monopolies (RECOPE, ICE), social economy, and solidarity, with only minor nationalist/sovereignty references and no significant right-populist identity politics.

chunk 4 · The populist ideology leans mildly left, emphasizing equitable wealth distribution, social economy cooperatives, subsidized credit, and solidarity-based enterprise, while also incorporating some centrist anti-corruption and efficiency themes without strong right-wing nationalist or anti-immigration content.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The text promotes social equity and redistribution, defends national sovereignty and borders, and appeals broadly to citizen action without extreme ideological polarization, indicating a centrist populist ideology with left and right elements.

chunk 2 · The text advocates for fair trade, just wages, and revising trade agreements that harm sovereignty, reflecting a mixed populist ideology with leftist economic justice and rightist sovereignty concerns, alongside centrist calls for unity.

chunk 3 · The text shows left-leaning populist ideology via support for subsidies and protection for farmers, right-leaning elements in controlling foreign land acquisition, and centrist appeals through emphasis on citizen participation and solidarity, reflecting a mixed populist ideological stance.

chunk 4 · The manifesto highlights the democratization of economic and social sectors, as well as a pluralistic society with active citizen participation, indicating a left-leaning and centrist populist ideology without right-wing nationalist elements.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The populist ideology is predominantly left-wing, emphasizing wealth redistribution, social inclusion, and opposition to neoliberal market-driven policies.

chunk 2 · The populist ideology is clearly left-wing, focusing its criticism on neoliberalism, privatization, and corporate/financial elites while advocating for state control of resources and social solidarity.

chunk 3 · The populist ideology is strongly left-wing, focusing on progressive taxation, anti-corporate/transnational stances, and state-led social solidarity.

chunk 4 · The populist tone is left-leaning, focusing on social economy, equitable distribution, and cooperative solidarity rather than right-wing identity politics.

Liberalism

Sonnet

chunk 1 · The manifesto is broadly pro-liberal on political and social dimensions—strongly endorsing democracy, rule of law, human rights, gender equity, and minority protections—while taking a mixed stance on economic liberalism (advocating a mixed economy with both public and private sectors) and showing mild skepticism toward unregulated capital flows and free trade agreements that harm national producers.

chunk 2 · The manifesto strongly endorses democracy, rule of law, and human rights (lib_political high), includes gender perspective (lib_social moderate-high), but explicitly rejects neoliberal privatization and favors state control of strategic resources (lib_economic mixed-low), with only limited and state-directed credit access mentioned for financial markets.

chunk 3 · The manifesto is moderately liberal: it supports transparency, accountability, and democratic participation (lib_political), includes disability inclusion (lib_social), endorses conditional trade openness while defending state monopolies and subsidies (lib_economic mixed), and backs capital-market development alongside subsidised credit and state banking roles (lib_financial_market mixed).

chunk 4 · The text is moderately liberal: it supports judicial independence, democratic pluralism, and human rights (political/social liberalism), endorses private sector growth and market competitiveness, but also advocates subsidized interest rates and state-directed financial channeling, tempering financial market liberalism.

GPT-4.1-mini

chunk 1 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, political rights, social equity including gender and minority rights, a mixed economy with plural ownership, and prudent public investment, demonstrating a clear pro-liberal stance.

chunk 2 · The manifesto emphasizes promotion of human rights, democracy, rule of law, ethical governance, fair trade, and adequate financing for environmental laws, showing a clear pro-liberal stance across political, social, economic, and financial dimensions.

chunk 3 · The manifesto supports liberal principles including political transparency and accountability, social inclusion of disabled persons, economic modernization and regulation of public services, and strengthening financial market supervision, indicating a moderately pro-liberal stance overall.

chunk 4 · The manifesto strongly supports democratic institutions, judicial independence, social pluralism, economic development with social equity, and financial policies enabling social enterprises, demonstrating a broadly pro-liberal stance.

Gemini

chunk 1 · The text strongly supports democratic institutions, human rights, and gender equality, but advocates for a mixed economy with state intervention rather than pure free-market liberalism.

chunk 2 · The text strongly supports political and social liberalism through democracy, human rights, and the rule of law, but takes a highly skeptical, interventionist stance on economic and financial markets.

chunk 3 · The text supports democratic institutions and social inclusion, but takes a highly interventionist, mixed stance on free markets and financial systems through credit subsidies and state monopolies.

chunk 4 · The text supports classical liberal institutions like judicial independence and private sector growth, but favors state-guided social finance and subsidized interest rates.
